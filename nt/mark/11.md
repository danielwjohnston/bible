## Clear & Faithful

**1** As they drew near to Jerusalem—at Bethphage and Bethany, right beside the Mount of Olives—Jesus dispatched two of his disciples
**2** and instructed them, "Go into the village just ahead of you, and as soon as you enter it, you will find a young donkey tied up, one that no human being has ever ridden; untie it and bring it here.
**3** If anyone asks you, 'Why are you doing this?' reply, 'The Lord has need of it, and he will send it straight back here.'"
**4** So they went off and found a colt tied near a doorway outside on the curving street, and they untied it.
**5** Some of the bystanders asked them, "What are you doing, untying that colt?"
**6** They answered them just as Jesus had said, and the people let them go.
**7** They brought the colt to Jesus, threw their cloaks over it, and he sat on it.
**8** Many spread their cloaks along the road, while others spread leafy branches cut from the surrounding fields.
**9** Both those marching ahead and those trailing behind shouted, "Hosanna! Blessed is the one who comes in the name of the Lord!
**10** Blessed is the coming kingdom of our father David! Hosanna in the highest heavens!"
**11** Jesus entered Jerusalem and went into the temple; after looking around at everything, since the hour was already late, he walked back out to Bethany with the Twelve.
**12** The next morning, as they left Bethany, he felt hungry.
**13** Noticing a fig tree in leaf from a distance, he went over to see if he might find any fruit on it; yet when he reached it, he found nothing but leaves, because it was not the season for figs.
**14** Addressing the tree, he said, "May no one ever eat fruit from you again!" And his disciples heard him.
**15** They arrived in Jerusalem. Entering the temple courts, he began driving out those who were buying and selling inside the temple, and he overturned the tables of the money changers and the benches of those selling doves;
**16** nor would he permit anyone to carry merchandise through the temple precinct.
**17** Then he taught them, saying, "Does it not stand written, 'My house shall be called a house of prayer for all nations'? Yet you have turned it into a den of bandits!"
**18** When the chief priests and the scribes heard this, they searched for a way to destroy him; for they feared him, because the entire crowd was captivated by his teaching.
**19** Whenever evening fell, Jesus and his disciples went back outside the city.
**20** Early the next morning, as they passed by, they saw the fig tree withered from its very roots.
**21** Remembering what had happened, Peter said to him, "Rabbi, look! The fig tree you cursed has withered away!"
**22** Jesus replied to them, "Hold firm faith in God.
**23** Truly I tell you, whoever says to this mountain, 'Be lifted up and hurled into the sea,' and does not waver in their heart, but believes that what they say will happen, it will be done for them.
**24** Therefore I tell you, everything you pray and ask for, believe that you have already received it, and it will be yours.
**25** Whenever you stand in prayer, forgive anyone against whom you hold a grievance, so that your Father in heaven may pardon your own offenses as well."
**27** Once more they entered Jerusalem. While Jesus was walking about in the temple, the chief priests, scribes, and elders confronted him
**28** and demanded, "By what authority are you doing these things? Who gave you this authority to act like this?"
**29** Jesus answered them, "Let me pose a single question to you; give me your answer, and then I will inform you by what authority I act:
**30** John's baptism—did it come from heaven or from human origin? Answer me."
**31** They deliberated among themselves: "What should we say? If we say, 'From heaven,' he will ask, 'Why then did you not believe him?'
**32** Yet should we say, 'From human origin'?"—they were afraid of the crowd, because everyone truly held that John was a prophet.
**33** So they replied to Jesus, "We cannot say." And Jesus responded to them, "Neither will I inform you by what authority I act."

## Everyday English

**1** As Jesus and his followers approached Jerusalem, coming near Bethphage and Bethany by the Mount of Olives, he sent two of his disciples on ahead.
**2** He told them, "Head into that village right across from you. As soon as you walk in, you'll spot a young donkey tied up—one nobody has ever ridden. Untie it and bring it back here.
**3** If anyone asks you, 'Hey, what are you doing?' just tell them, 'The Lord needs it, and he'll send it right back here soon.'"
**4** They walked off and found the donkey colt tied outside a doorway on the street corner, and they started untying it.
**5** A few bystanders asked, "What do you think you're doing untying that colt?"
**6** The two disciples gave them the exact answer Jesus had told them, and the people let them take it.
**7** They brought the colt to Jesus, draped their coats across its back, and he climbed on.
**8** Lots of people spread their coats down on the road in front of him, while others laid down leafy branches they'd cut from the fields.
**9** Both the crowds walking ahead and the crowds trailing behind cheered loudly: "Hosanna! God bless the one coming in the Lord's name!
**10** God bless the coming kingdom of our ancestor David! Hosanna in highest heaven!"
**11** Jesus rode into Jerusalem and stepped inside the temple grounds. He looked over everything there, and because it was already getting late, he walked back out to Bethany with the Twelve.
**12** The next morning, as they walked back from Bethany, Jesus felt hungry.
**13** Spotting a fig tree covered with leaves off in the distance, he walked over to check if there was any fruit on it. When he got there, all he found were leaves, since it wasn't fig season yet.
**14** So Jesus told the tree, "Nobody will ever eat fruit from you again!" And his disciples heard every word.
**15** Then they walked into Jerusalem. Going straight into the temple courtyards, Jesus started chasing out the merchants and shoppers, flipping over the money changers' tables and the stools of the dove sellers.
**16** He wouldn't even let anyone use the temple courtyard as a shortcut carrying supplies.
**17** And he taught them plainly: "Doesn't Scripture say, 'My house will be called a house of prayer for every nation'? Yet you've turned it into a hideout for thieves!"
**18** When the chief priests and legal scholars heard what he'd done, they looked for a way to get rid of him. They were scared of him because the whole crowd was fascinated by his teaching.
**19** Whenever evening came, Jesus and his disciples left the city.
**20** The next morning, as they walked past that same spot, they noticed the fig tree had dried up all the way down to its roots!
**21** It clicked for Peter, and he exclaimed, "Teacher, look! That fig tree you cursed has completely withered!"
**22** Jesus told them, "Trust in God!
**23** Believe me, if anyone tells this mountain, 'Lift yourself up and dive into the sea!' without doubting inside, and trusts that what they're saying will happen, God will do it for them.
**24** That's why I'm telling you: whatever you pray for and ask God to do, trust that you've already received it, and it will be yours.
**25** And whenever you stand up to pray, let go of any grudge you hold against anybody, so your Father in heaven will also forgive your wrongs."
**27** They returned to Jerusalem once more. While Jesus was strolling through the temple courts, the chief priests, legal experts, and elders confronted him
**28** and asked, "What gives you the right to act like this? Who gave you authority to do these things?"
**29** Jesus answered, "Let me ask you just one question. Answer me, and I'll tell you where I get my authority for doing this:
**30** When John baptized people, did that authority come from heaven or from humans? Answer me!"
**31** They huddled together and talked it over: "What are we supposed to say? If we say, 'From heaven,' he'll shoot back, 'Then why didn't you believe him?'
**32** Yet can we dare say, 'From humans'?"—they were terrified of the crowd, because everybody was convinced John was a genuine prophet.
**33** So they told Jesus, "We don't know." And Jesus replied, "Then I won't tell you where my authority comes from either."

## Crossings & Lexical Notes

- **v. 9 `Ὡσαννά` (*Hōsanna*):** Greek transliteration of Aramaic/Hebrew *hôshî'â-nnā* ("Save, we pray!" from Ps 118:25), which had evolved into a shout of royal acclamation and praise.
- **v. 15 `κολλυβιστῶν` (*kollybistōn*):** Money changers who exchanged Greek and Roman coinage bearing pagan imagery for Tyrian shekels accepted for the annual temple tax.
- **v. 17 `σπήλαιον λῃστῶν` (*spēlaion lēstōn*):** *Lēstēs* means "bandit / robber / violent insurrectionist," echoing Jeremiah 7:11's critique of treating the sanctuary as a safe hideout for unjust deeds.
- **v. 22 `πίστιν θεοῦ` (*pistin theou*):** Objective genitive ("faith directed toward God" / "trust in God"), grounding mountain-moving prayer in God's faithfulness rather than human willpower.
- **v. 26 `Εἰ δὲ ὑμεῖς οὐκ ἀφίετε…` (*Ei de hymeis ouk aphiete…*):** Omitted in SBLGNT and Nestle 1904 (`WH` / `Treg` / `NA28` / earliest Alexandrian witnesses). Byzantine manuscripts (`RP`) add v. 26 ("And if you do not forgive, neither will your Father in heaven forgive your trespasses"), assimilated from Matthew 6:15.
- **v. 31 `Τί εἴπωμεν;` (*Ti eipōmen;*):** SBLGNT includes "What shall we say?" (`Holmes`, attested in Codex Bezae `D` and several early witnesses), whereas `WH`, `Treg`, `NA28`, and `RP` omit the explicit question.

## Supplemental Texts & Allusions

- **Zechariah 9:9** (echoed in Mark 11:1–10): *"Look, your king is coming to you; righteous and victorious is he, humble and riding on a donkey, on a colt, the foal of a donkey."*
- **Psalm 118:25–26** (quoted in Mark 11:9–10): *"Save us, we pray, O LORD! ... Blessed is he who comes in the name of the LORD!"*
- **Isaiah 56:7 & Jeremiah 7:11** (quoted in Mark 11:17): *"My temple will be known as a house of prayer for all nations"* paired with *"Has this temple, bearing my name, turned into a hideout for bandits in your sight?"*
- **Jeremiah 8:13 & Hosea 9:10** (background to Mark 11:12–21): Barren fig tree imagery symbolizes judgment upon covenant fruitlessness.
