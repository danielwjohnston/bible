## Clear & Faithful

**1** Now the Pharisees gathered around him along with certain scribes who had come down from Jerusalem.
**2** They noticed that some of his disciples were eating their meals with ritually defiled—that is, unwashed—hands.
**3** (For the Pharisees, and indeed all Judeans, do not eat unless they wash their hands ceremonially up to the wrist, holding fast to the tradition of the elders;
**4** and coming back from the marketplace they do not eat unless they wash themselves, along with many other practices they have inherited and keep: ritual washings of cups, pitchers, bronze pots, and dining couches.)
**5** So the Pharisees and scribes questioned him, "Why do your disciples not live according to the tradition of the elders, instead eating their bread with unwashed hands?"
**6** He answered them, "Isaiah prophesied accurately about you hypocrites, as it is written: 'This people honors me with their lips, yet their heart remains far away from me;
**7** in vain do they worship me, teaching human rules as divine doctrines.'
**8** You abandon God's commandment and cling to human tradition."
**9** And he told them, "How skillfully you set aside God's commandment in order to preserve your own tradition!
**10** For Moses declared, 'Honor your father and your mother,' and, 'Anyone who curses father or mother must surely die.'
**11** Yet you declare that if a person says to father or mother, 'Whatever benefit you might have received from me is Corban'—meaning dedicated to God—
**12** then you no longer allow that person to do anything for father or mother,
**13** nullifying the word of God by the tradition you have passed down. And you do many similar things."
**14** Summoning the crowd again, he told them, "Listen to me, everyone, and understand:
**15** nothing entering a person from outside can defile them; rather, what comes out of a person is what defiles them."
**17** Once he had stepped inside a house away from the crowd, his disciples asked him about this parable.
**18** He said to them, "Are you also so slow to grasp it? Do you not realize that nothing going into a person from outside can make them unclean,
**19** since it does not enter the heart, but passes into the stomach and goes out into the latrine?"—in saying this, he declared all foods clean.
**20** He continued, "What comes out of a person—that is what defiles a person.
**21** For from within, out of the human heart, come evil schemes: sexual immorality, theft, murder,
**22** adultery, greed, malice, deceit, shamelessness, envy, slander, pride, and folly.
**23** All these evils emerge from within and defile a person."
**24** Rising from there, Jesus set out into the territory of Tyre. He entered a house wanting nobody to know he was there, yet he could not remain hidden.
**25** Straightaway a woman whose young daughter had an unclean spirit heard about him, came in, and fell at his feet.
**26** The woman was Greek, Syrophoenician by birth, and she kept begging him to expel the demon from her daughter.
**27** He told her, "Let the children be fed first, for it is not right to take the children's bread and toss it to the house dogs."
**28** She answered him, "Lord, even the dogs under the table eat the children's crumbs."
**29** Then he said to her, "Because of that reply, go your way; the demon has left your daughter."
**30** Returning to her home, she found the child lying peacefully on the bed, and the demon gone.
**31** Departing again from the region of Tyre, he traveled by way of Sidon toward the Sea of Galilee, straight through the territory of the Decapolis.
**32** People brought to him a man who was deaf and scarcely able to speak, begging him to lay his hand on him.
**33** Taking him aside privately from the crowd, Jesus placed his fingers into the man's ears, spat, and touched his tongue;
**34** then looking up to heaven, he sighed deeply and said to him, "Ephphatha," meaning, "Be opened!"
**35** Instantly his ears were opened, the restraint on his tongue was untied, and he began speaking plainly.
**36** Jesus strictly ordered them not to tell anyone; yet the more he charged them to keep quiet, the more widely they proclaimed it.
**37** Overwhelmed with amazement, people kept saying, "He has done everything well! He makes even the deaf hear and the mute speak!"

## Everyday English

**1** Some Pharisees and legal experts who had traveled down from Jerusalem gathered around Jesus.
**2** They noticed a few of his followers eating a meal with ritually "unclean" hands—meaning they hadn't performed the ceremonial handwashing.
**3** (You see, the Pharisees and observant Jewish people never eat without washing their hands up to the wrist in a special way, following the rules handed down by their ancestors.
**4** And when they come home from the market, they won't eat until they wash thoroughly. They also follow plenty of other traditional rules about rinsing cups, jugs, bronze pots, and dining benches.)
**5** So the Pharisees and legal experts confronted Jesus: "Why don't your followers live by the traditions of our elders? Why do they eat bread with unwashed hands?"
**6** Jesus answered them, "Isaiah was spot-on when he prophesied about you hypocrites, just as Scripture says: 'These people praise me with their words, while their hearts stay miles away from me.
**7** Their worship of me is completely empty, because they teach mere human rules as if they came from God.'
**8** You've walked away from God's command just to hang onto human traditions!"
**9** He went on, "You're experts at tossing out God's command so you can guard your own customs!
**10** Look at what Moses commanded: 'Honor your father and mother,' and 'Anyone who curses their father or mother must be executed.'
**11** Yet you tell people, 'If you say to your father or mother, "Sorry, whatever support I could've given you is Corban"'—meaning set aside as a gift to God—
**12** 'then you're off the hook from doing anything more for your parents.'
**13** By passing down that kind of rule, you wipe out God's own word! And you do plenty of other things just like it."
**14** Then Jesus called the crowd back over and said, "Listen closely, everyone, and take this in!
**15** Nothing outside you that goes into your body can make you spiritually dirty. It's what comes out from inside you that makes you unclean."
**17** Later, once he left the crowd and went inside a house, his disciples asked him what that saying meant.
**18** Jesus replied, "Don't even you get it yet? Can't you see that nothing you eat from outside can stain your heart?
**19** Food doesn't go into your heart—it goes into your stomach and passes out through the toilet." (By saying that, Jesus showed that all foods are clean.)
**20** Then he explained, "It's what pours out of a person that stains them.
**21** Because deep inside, from the human heart, come crooked thoughts: sexual sin, stealing, murder,
**22** cheating on a spouse, greed, cruelty, trickery, shameless behavior, jealousy, insulting talk, arrogance, and foolishness.
**23** Every one of those ugly things comes from inside, and that's what truly ruins a person."
**24** Leaving that area, Jesus traveled into the region around Tyre. He stayed in a house and didn't want anyone to know he was there, though it proved impossible to stay hidden.
**25** Right away a woman whose little daughter was controlled by an evil spirit heard about him, came inside, and fell at his feet.
**26** She was Greek-speaking, born in Syrian Phoenicia, and she kept begging him to chase the demon out of her daughter.
**27** Jesus said to her, "Let the kids eat until they're full first; it isn't right to take the children's bread and toss it to the house puppies."
**28** She shot back, "Sir, even the puppies under the table get to eat the crumbs the kids drop!"
**29** Jesus smiled and told her, "Because of that answer, go on home—the demon has already left your daughter."
**30** When she walked back into her house, she found her little girl resting calmly on the bed, completely free of the demon.
**31** Leaving the Tyre district, Jesus went north through Sidon and looped back down through the Ten Towns region to Lake Galilee.
**32** Some people brought him a man who was deaf and had severe trouble speaking, and they begged Jesus to place his hand on him.
**33** Jesus led him off away from the crowd for some privacy, put his fingers into the man's ears, spat, and touched his tongue.
**34** Looking up toward heaven, he let out a deep sigh and said, "Ephphatha!"—which means, "Open up!"
**35** Immediately the man's ears opened, whatever held his tongue back broke loose, and he started talking clearly.
**36** Jesus warned them not to breathe a word about it to anyone; yet the harder he tried to keep them quiet, the louder they spread the news everywhere.
**37** People were totally blown away and kept saying, "Everything he does is wonderful! He even gives hearing to the deaf and speech to people who couldn't talk!"

## Crossings & Lexical Notes

- **v. 3 `πυγμῇ` (*pygmē*):** Literally "with a fist" (denoting rubbing one clenched fist into the palm of the other, or washing hands up to the wrist before meals as practiced in Pharisaic purity traditions).
- **v. 11 `Κορβᾶν` (*Korban*):** Transliteration of Hebrew/Aramaic *qorbān* ("dedicated offering/gift"), explained by Mark for Greek readers as *dōron* ("a gift to God"), used here as a vow formula that shielded assets from supporting aging parents.
- **v. 16 `Εἴ τις ἔχει ὦτα ἀκούειν ἀκουέτω` (*Ei tis echei ōta akouein akouetō*):** Omitted in SBLGNT and Nestle 1904 (`WH` / `NA28` / earliest Alexandrian witnesses). `Treg` and Byzantine manuscripts (`RP`) include v. 16 ("If anyone has ears to hear, let them hear"), likely assimilated from Mark 4:9, 23.
- **v. 19 `καθαρίζων πάντα τὰ βρώματα` (*katharizōn panta ta brōmata*):** The nominative masculine singular participle *katharizōn* ("cleansing") refers back to Jesus' speech in v. 18/20 ("he said... thus declaring all foods clean"), rather than agreeing with the neuter subject "food" (`RP` shifts to neuter *katharizon*).
- **v. 27 `τοῖς κυναρίοις` (*tois kynariois*):** Diminutive of *kyōn* ("little dogs / household pets"), distinct from wild scavenger dogs; the woman seizes on the household imagery ("under the table") in v. 28.
- **v. 32 `μογιλάλον` (*mogilalon*):** "Speaking with difficulty / tongue-tied," a rare term echoing the Septuagint of Isaiah 35:6 (where the tongue of the *mogilalos* sings joyfully).
