## Clear & Faithful

**1** Jesus left that place and returned to his hometown, accompanied by his disciples.
**2** When the Sabbath arrived, he began teaching in the synagogue, and many listeners were astounded, asking, "Where did this man get all this? What is this wisdom granted to him, and how are such mighty deeds taking place through his hands?
**3** Is not this the carpenter, the son of Mary and brother of James, Joses, Judas, and Simon? And are not his sisters right here among us?" So they took offense at him.
**4** Jesus said to them, "A prophet is never without honor except in his own hometown, among his relatives, and in his own household."
**5** He could perform no mighty deed there, apart from laying his hands on a few sick people and healing them;
**6** and he marveled at their lack of faith. Then he traveled around the surrounding villages, teaching.
**7** Calling the Twelve to himself, he began sending them out two by two and gave them authority over the unclean spirits.
**8** He instructed them to carry nothing for the journey except a staff—no bread, no pack, no copper coins in their belts—
**9** instead wearing sandals on their feet and not putting on an extra tunic.
**10** He told them, "Whenever you enter a house, stay there until you leave that place.
**11** And if any locality refuses to welcome you or listen to you, shake the dust off the soles of your feet as you leave, as a testimony against them."
**12** So they went out and proclaimed that people should repent;
**13** they expelled many demons and anointed many sick people with olive oil and healed them.
**14** King Herod heard about this—for Jesus' name had become well known—and people were saying, "John the Baptizer has been raised from the dead, and that is why these miraculous powers are active in him."
**15** Others insisted, "He is Elijah," while still others said, "He is a prophet, like one of the prophets of old."
**16** Yet when Herod heard it, he kept saying, "John, whom I beheaded—he has been raised!"
**17** For Herod himself had sent men to arrest John and bind him in prison on account of Herodias, his brother Philip's wife, because he had married her;
**18** and John had been telling Herod, "It is not lawful for you to have your brother's wife."
**19** Herodias held a fierce grudge against him and wanted him put to death, yet she could not manage it,
**20** because Herod stood in awe of John, knowing him to be a righteous and holy man, and kept him protected; listening to John left him deeply unsettled, yet he gladly listened to him.
**21** Then an opportune day arrived when Herod hosted a birthday banquet for his nobles, military commanders, and the leading figures of Galilee.
**22** Herodias's own daughter entered and danced, delighting Herod and his dinner guests. The king said to the girl, "Ask me for whatever you want, and I will give it to you,"
**23** and he swore an oath to her, "Whatever you ask of me I will grant you, up to half of my kingdom."
**24** She stepped out and asked her mother, "What should I request?" Her mother replied, "The head of John the Baptizer."
**25** Hurrying straight back to the king, she made her demand: "I want you to give me right now, on a platter, the head of John the Baptist."
**26** The king was deeply distressed, yet because of his oaths and his dining guests, he was unwilling to refuse her.
**27** Immediately the king dispatched an executioner with orders to bring John's head. The soldier went off, beheaded him in the prison,
**28** brought his head on a platter, and gave it to the girl; and the girl handed it to her mother.
**29** When John's disciples heard of it, they came, took his body, and laid it in a tomb.
**30** The apostles gathered back around Jesus and reported to him everything they had done and taught.
**31** He said to them, "Come away by yourselves to a quiet, deserted place and rest awhile." For so many people were coming and going that they did not even have time to eat.
**32** So they set out in the boat by themselves toward a remote spot.
**33** Yet many saw them leaving and recognized them; from all the towns people hurried on foot to that place and arrived ahead of them.
**34** Stepping ashore, Jesus saw a vast crowd and felt deep compassion for them, because they were like sheep without a shepherd; so he began teaching them many things.
**35** When the hour had grown late, his disciples came to him and said, "This is a remote place, and the hour is already late.
**36** Dismiss the crowd so they can go into the surrounding farms and villages to buy something to eat."
**37** He answered them, "You give them something to eat." They asked him, "Are we to go and buy two hundred denarii worth of bread and give it to them to eat?"
**38** He asked them, "How many loaves do you have? Go and check." Once they found out, they reported, "Five loaves, and two fish."
**39** Then he directed them to have everyone sit down in dining groups on the green grass.
**40** They settled down in neat rows by hundreds and by fifties.
**41** Taking the five loaves and the two fish, he looked up toward heaven, spoke a blessing, broke the loaves into pieces, and kept handing them to his disciples to set before the people; and he divided the two fish among them all.
**42** Everyone ate and was completely satisfied,
**43** and they gathered up twelve wicker baskets packed full of broken pieces of bread and of the fish.
**44** Now those who had eaten the loaves numbered five thousand men.
**45** Right away Jesus urged his disciples to board the boat and cross ahead toward Bethsaida while he dismissed the crowd.
**46** After saying farewell to them, he went up onto the mountain to pray.
**47** When evening fell, the boat was out in the middle of the sea, while Jesus was alone on the shore.
**48** Seeing them straining at the oars—for the wind was blowing dead against them—around the fourth watch of the night he came toward them walking on the sea, intending to pass by them.
**49** When they saw him walking on the sea, they thought it was a ghost and cried out in terror,
**50** for they all saw him and were shaken. At once he spoke to them and said, "Take heart! It is I; do not be afraid."
**51** Then he climbed into the boat with them, and the wind died down. They were utterly astonished beyond words,
**52** for they had not grasped the meaning of the loaves; instead, their hearts remained hardened.
**53** Having crossed over, they came to land at Gennesaret and moored the boat.
**54** As soon as they stepped ashore, people immediately recognized him,
**55** scurried through that entire countryside, and began carrying the sick on mats wherever they heard he was.
**56** Wherever he entered—villages, towns, or open countryside—people laid the sick in the marketplaces and begged him just to let them touch the fringe of his cloak; and all who touched him were healed.

## Everyday English

**1** Jesus left that area and went back to his hometown of Nazareth, with his followers coming along.
**2** When the Sabbath came, he began teaching in the local synagogue. Lots of people listening were amazed and asked, "Where did this guy learn all of this? How did he get such wisdom, and how is he pulling off miracles like these?
**3** Isn't he just the local carpenter—Mary's son, and the brother of James, Joses, Judas, and Simon? Don't his sisters live right here in town with us?" They couldn't get past how ordinary he seemed, and they rejected him.
**4** Jesus told them, "A prophet is respected everywhere except in his own hometown, among his own relatives, and inside his own family."
**5** He couldn't do any major miracles there, except placing his hands on a handful of sick folks and healing them.
**6** Their stubborn refusal to believe shocked him. After that, he walked from village to village in the surrounding area, teaching.
**7** Then Jesus called the Twelve together and started sending them out in pairs, giving them power over evil spirits.
**8** He told them not to pack anything for the trip except a walking stick—no food, no travel bag, no spare change tucked in their belts.
**9** They could wear sandals, he said, but shouldn't pack an extra shirt.
**10** He added, "Whenever someone welcomes you into their home, stay right there until you leave that town.
**11** If any town won't welcome you or listen to your message, walk out and shake the dust off your feet as a clear warning to them."
**12** So the disciples headed out and urged people everywhere to turn back to God.
**13** They forced out lots of demons and rubbed olive oil on many sick people, healing them.
**14** Soon King Herod heard all about Jesus, because his reputation had spread everywhere. Some people were saying, "John the Baptizer has come back to life! That's why these miraculous powers are working through him."
**15** Others claimed, "He's Elijah!" Still others said, "He's a real prophet, just like the prophets of long ago."
**16** When Herod heard these rumors, he kept insisting, "It's John! I had his head cut off, and now he's come back from the dead!"

**24** She stepped outside and asked her mother, "What should I ask him for?" Her mother answered, "The head of John the Baptizer."
**25** The girl hurried straight back to the king and demanded, "I want you to hand me John the Baptist's head on a platter right this minute!"
**26** The king felt sick at heart, yet because he'd sworn an oath in front of all his dinner guests, he couldn't bring himself to say no to her.
**27** So right away the king sent a guard with orders to bring John's head. The soldier went to the prison, beheaded John,
**28** carried the head back on a platter, and handed it to the girl, who carried it straight to her mother.
**29** When John's followers heard what had happened, they came, claimed his body, and buried it in a tomb.
**30** Meanwhile, the apostles met back up with Jesus and told him everything they'd done and taught.
**31** Since crowds kept streaming in and out so constantly that they couldn't even grab a bite to eat, Jesus told them, "Come away by yourselves to a quiet spot and get some rest."
**32** So they climbed into the boat and sailed off by themselves toward an empty stretch of shore.
**33** Plenty of people saw them leaving and recognized them, so crowds from every nearby town ran along the shoreline and actually beat them to the landing spot!
**34** When Jesus stepped out of the boat and saw the huge crowd waiting, his heart went out to them because they looked like sheep without a shepherd. So he began teaching them many things.
**35** Late in the afternoon, his disciples walked up and said, "We're way out in the middle of nowhere, and it's getting late.
**36** Send these people away so they can walk to the nearby farms and villages and buy themselves something to eat."
**37** Jesus answered, "You feed them." "What?" they replied. "Should we go spend two hundred silver coins on bread to feed all these people?"
**38** Jesus asked, "How many loaves of bread do you have? Go look." They checked and came back saying, "Five loaves and two fish."
**39** Jesus told them to seat everybody in groups on the green grass.
**40** The crowd sat down in tidy rows of fifties and hundreds.

**46** Once he told everyone goodbye, he climbed up into the hills by himself to pray.
**47** By nightfall the boat was out in the middle of the lake while Jesus stood alone on shore.
**48** He saw his disciples straining hard at the oars against a strong headwind. Around three in the morning, he walked straight out across the water toward them, looking as though he meant to walk right past them.

## Crossings & Lexical Notes

- **v. 3 `ὁ τέκτων` (*ho tektōn*):** Broadly a skilled artisan working in wood, stone, or building construction ("craftsman / builder / carpenter"). Notice the hometown neighbors identify Jesus as "the son of Mary" rather than of Joseph.
- **v. 8 `πήραν` (*pēran*):** A traveler's leather knapsack or beggar's provision bag; prohibiting bread, bag, and copper coins (*chalkon*) enforces radical hospitality dependence.
- **v. 20 `ἠπόρει` (*ēporei*):** Adopted by `WH` and `NA28` ("was deeply perplexed / torn"), whereas `Treg` and `RP` read `ἐποίει` (*epoiei*, "was doing many things" on John's advice).
- **v. 22 `τῆς θυγατρὸς αὐτῆς τῆς Ἡρῳδιάδος` (*tēs thygatros autēs tēs Hērōdiados*):** SBLGNT (`Treg RP`) reads "her daughter, Herodias's," while `WH` and `NA28` read `αὐτοῦ` ("his daughter Herodias").
- **v. 27 `σπεκουλάτορα` (*spekoulatora*):** Latin loanword (*speculator*), designating a military courier, scout, or imperial executioner.
- **v. 39–40 `συμπόσια συμπόσια … πρασιαὶ πρασιαὶ` (*symposia symposia … prasiai prasiai*):** Hebraistic distributive repetitions meaning "dining company by dining company" and "garden-plot by garden-plot" (like orderly beds of vegetables on the green grass).
- **v. 50 `ἐγώ εἰμι` (*egō eimi*):** Literally "I am," echoing both ordinary self-identification ("It is I") and the divine self-revelation of Isaiah 43:10–11 and Exodus 3:14, right after Jesus "intended to pass by them" (*parelthein*, cf. Exod 33:19–22).

## Supplemental Texts & Allusions

- **Numbers 27:17 & Ezekiel 34:5–15** (echoed in Mark 6:34): Moses prays that Israel will not become *"a flock lacking any shepherd,"* and Ezekiel promises that the LORD himself will shepherd and feed his scattered flock on green pastures.
- **2 Kings 4:42–44** (background to Mark 6:37–44): Elisha feeds a hundred people with twenty barley loaves and leaves leftovers according to the word of the LORD.
- **Exodus 33:19–22 & Job 9:8** (echoed in Mark 6:48–50): God alone *"strides across the crests of the sea"* and "passes by" Moses in self-disclosure.

**33** They saw them going, and many recognized him and ran there on foot from all the cities. They arrived before them and came together to him.
**34** Jesus came out, saw a great multitude, and he had compassion on them because they were like sheep without a shepherd; and he began to teach them many things.
**35** When it was late in the day, his disciples came to him and said, “This place is deserted, and it is late in the day.
**36** Send them away, that they may go into the surrounding country and villages and buy themselves bread, for they have nothing to eat.”
**37** But he answered them, “You give them something to eat.” They asked him, “Shall we go and buy two hundred denarii worth of bread and give them something to eat?”
**38** He said to them, “How many loaves do you have? Go see.” When they knew, they said, “Five, and two fish.”
**39** He commanded them that everyone should sit down in groups on the green grass.
**40** They sat down in ranks, by hundreds and by fifties.
**41** He took the five loaves and the two fish; and looking up to heaven, he blessed and broke the loaves, and he gave to his disciples to set before them, and he divided the two fish among them all.
**42** They all ate and were filled.
**43** They took up twelve baskets full of broken pieces and also of the fish.
**44** Those who ate the loaves were five thousand men.
**45** Immediately he made his disciples get into the boat and go ahead to the other side, to Bethsaida, while he himself sent the multitude away.
**46** After he had taken leave of them, he went up the mountain to pray.
**47** When evening had come, the boat was in the middle of the sea, and he was alone on the land.
**48** Seeing them distressed in rowing, for the wind was contrary to them, about the fourth watch of the night he came to them, walking on the sea; and he would have passed by them,
**49** but they, when they saw him walking on the sea, supposed that it was a ghost, and cried out;
**50** for they all saw him and were troubled. But he immediately spoke with them and said to them, “Cheer up! It is I! Don’t be afraid.”
**51** He got into the boat with them; and the wind ceased, and they were very amazed among themselves, and marveled;
**52** for they hadn’t understood about the loaves, but their hearts were hardened.
**53** When they had crossed over, they came to land at Gennesaret and moored to the shore.
**54** When they had come out of the boat, immediately the people recognized him,
**55** and ran around that whole region, and began to bring those who were sick on their mats to where they heard he was.
**56** Wherever he entered—into villages, or into cities, or into the country—they laid the sick in the marketplaces and begged him that they might just touch the fringe of his garment; and as many as touched him were made well.
