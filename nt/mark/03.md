## Clear & Faithful

**1** Again Jesus entered a synagogue, and a man was there with a withered hand.
**2** They watched him closely to see whether he would heal him on the Sabbath, so they could bring an accusation against him.
**3** He said to the man with the withered hand, "Stand up in the center."
**4** Then he posed the question to them, "What is permitted on the Sabbath—doing good or doing harm, rescuing a life or taking it?" Yet they stayed silent.
**5** Looking around at them with anger, deeply grieved by the callousness of their hearts, he told the man, "Stretch out your hand." He stretched it out, and his hand was completely restored.
**6** The Pharisees went straight out and began plotting with the Herodians against him on how to destroy him.
**7** Jesus withdrew with his disciples toward the sea, and a large crowd from Galilee followed him, along with people from Judea,
**8** from Jerusalem, from Idumea, from beyond the Jordan, and from the region of Tyre and Sidon; a huge multitude came to him after hearing everything he was doing.
**9** He told his disciples to keep a small boat ready for him because of the crowd, so that they would not press in on him;
**10** for he had healed so many that everyone suffering from painful ailments kept surging forward to touch him.
**11** Whenever the unclean spirits caught sight of him, they collapsed before him and shrieked, "You are the Son of God!"
**12** Yet he sternly warned them not to make him publicly known.
**13** Then he went up into the hill country and summoned those whom he himself chose, and they came to him.
**14** He appointed twelve to stay with him and to be sent out to proclaim the message,
**15** and to possess authority to drive out demons.
**16** Thus he appointed the twelve: he gave Simon the name Peter;
**17** James, Zebedee's son, and John, James's brother, to whom he gave the surname Boanerges (meaning "Sons of Thunder");
**18** Andrew, Philip, Bartholomew, Matthew, Thomas, Alphaeus's son James, Thaddaeus, Simon the Cananaean,
**19** and Judas Iscariot, who also betrayed him.
**20** Then he entered a house, and once again the crowd gathered so thickly that they could not even eat a meal.
**21** When his own family heard about it, they went out to take charge of him, for people were saying, "He has lost his mind."
**22** Meanwhile, the scribes who had come down from Jerusalem were asserting, "He is possessed by Beelzebul," and, "By the ruler of the demons he drives out demons."
**23** Calling them over, he began speaking to them in parables: "How can Satan drive out Satan?
**24** Whenever a kingdom splits into factions against itself, that realm cannot endure.
**25** And if a household is divided against itself, that household will not be able to stand.
**26** So if Satan has risen up against himself and is divided, he cannot stand—his end has arrived.
**27** Rather, no one can break into a strong man's house and carry off his possessions unless he first ties up the strong man; only then can he plunder his house.
**28** Truly I tell you, all sins and whatever blasphemies people utter will be forgiven the children of humanity;
**29** yet whoever blasphemes against the Holy Spirit never has forgiveness for all eternity, but stands guilty of an everlasting sin"—
**30** because they were claiming, "He has an unclean spirit."
**31** Then his mother and his brothers arrived; standing outside, they sent word inside to summon him.
**32** A crowd was seated all around him, and they told him, "Look, your mother and your brothers are outside asking for you."
**33** He replied to them, "Who are my mother and my brothers?"
**34** Gazing around at those sitting in a circle about him, he declared, "Here are my mother and my brothers!
**35** Whoever carries out the will of God—that person is my brother and sister and mother."

## Everyday English

**1** Jesus went back into a synagogue, where a man with a crippled, shriveled hand was sitting.
**2** Certain onlookers kept a sharp eye on Jesus to see if he'd heal the man on the Sabbath, hoping to catch him in a charge they could use against him.
**3** Jesus spoke to the man with the paralyzed hand: "Get up and step out into the middle."
**4** Then he challenged the room: "What does our law permit on the Sabbath—doing good or doing harm? Saving a life or ending one?" Nobody answered a word.
**5** Jesus looked around at all of them, angry and deeply hurt by how stubbornly hardhearted they were. Then he told the man, "Hold out your hand." The man reached it out, and his hand was healed right there.
**6** The Pharisees walked straight out and immediately started scheming with Herod's supporters on how to get rid of Jesus.
**7** Meanwhile, Jesus pulled back toward the lake with his followers, and massive crowds trailed after him from Galilee, Judea,
**8** Jerusalem, Idumea, across the Jordan River, and even the coastal areas near Tyre and Sidon. Word of everything he was doing brought huge crowds flocking to him.
**9** Because the press of people was so tight, Jesus asked his followers to have a small boat standing by so the crowd wouldn't crush him.
**10** He had cured so many people that everyone battling severe illnesses kept pushing forward just to touch him.
**11** Whenever evil spirits saw him, they threw themselves down at his feet and screamed, "You are the Son of God!"
**12** But Jesus strictly ordered them not to tell anyone who he was.
**13** Next, Jesus climbed up into the hills and called to himself the exact people he wanted, and they joined him.
**14** He chose twelve of them to stay close beside him, so he could send them out to preach
**15** and give them power to force out demons.
**16** These are the twelve he appointed: Simon, whom he nicknamed Peter;
**17** Zebedee's sons James and John, whom he nicknamed Boanerges (meaning "Sons of Thunder");
**18** Andrew, Philip, Bartholomew, Matthew, Thomas, Alphaeus's son James, Thaddaeus, Simon the Zealot-patriot,
**19** and Judas Iscariot, the one who later turned him over to his enemies.
**20** Afterward Jesus went into a house, and once again such a huge crowd jammed inside that Jesus and his followers couldn't even sit down to grab a bite to eat.
**21** When his relatives heard what was happening, they came to grab hold of him and bring him home, because people were saying, "He's out of his mind!"
**22** At the same time, the religious scholars who had come down from Jerusalem were claiming, "Beelzebul is inside him! He's driving out demons using the ruler of demons!"
**23** Jesus called them over and answered them using comparisons: "How could Satan possibly kick out Satan?
**24** If a nation splits into warring sides against itself, that nation can't survive.
**25** If a family tears itself apart from within, that family falls to pieces.
**26** So if Satan is fighting himself and divided, he can't hold together—he's finished!
**27** In fact, nobody can break into a strong man's house and haul away his belongings unless they tie up the strong man first. Once he's tied up, they can empty his house.
**28** Listen carefully to me: people can be forgiven for every kind of sin and every insult they speak,
**29** but anyone who slanders the Holy Spirit will never be forgiven—that person is stuck with a guilt that lasts forever."
**30** Jesus said this because they kept accusing him, "He's possessed by an evil spirit."
**31** Just then Jesus' mother and brothers arrived. They stood outside and sent someone in to call him out.
**32** A large crowd was sitting all around him, and they passed along the message: "Look, your mother and your brothers are waiting outside asking for you."
**33** Jesus responded, "Who really is my mother, and who are my brothers?"
**34** Sweeping his eyes across the people sitting in a circle around him, he said, "Look right here—these are my mother and my brothers!
**35** Anyone who does what God wants is my brother, my sister, and my mother."

## Crossings & Lexical Notes

- **v. 5 `πωρώσει` (*pōrōsei*):** From *pōros* (callus or hardened stone); denotes moral and spiritual insensibility—a heart grown thick and unresponsive.
- **v. 10 `μάστιγας` (*mastigas*):** Literally "whips" or "scourges," used metaphorically for severe, tormenting physical afflictions or plagues.
- **v. 14 `ἵνα ἀποστέλλῃ` (*hina apostellē*):** Verb *apostellō* ("send forth as an authorized envoy"). While SBLGNT (`Treg RP`) ends v. 14 at *kēryssein*, Westcott-Hort and `NA28` add `οὓς καὶ ἀποστόλους ὠνόμασεν` (*hous kai apostolous ōnomasen*, "whom he also named apostles").
- **v. 18 `Καναναῖον` (*Kananaion*):** Transliterates Aramaic *qan'ānā* ("zealous one" or "Zealot," equivalent to Greek *zēlōtēs* in Luke 6:15), rather than referring to the town of Cana or Canaan.
- **v. 21 `οἱ παρʼ αὐτοῦ` (*hoi par' autou*):** Idiomatic for "those belonging to him," i.e., his relatives/family (anticipating vv. 31–35), who attempt to restrain (*kratēsai*) him because of rumors that *exestē* ("he is beside himself / out of his mind").
- **v. 29 `αἰωνίου ἁμαρτήματος` (*aiōniou hamartēmatos*):** "Eternal sin" (`WH Treg NA28`), contrasting with `RP`'s `κρίσεως` (*kriseōs*, "judgment"). Attributed in v. 30 specifically to calling the Holy Spirit's liberating work an "unclean spirit."

## Supplemental Texts & Allusions

- **Deuteronomy 5:12–15 & Exodus 20:8–11** (background to Mark 3:2–5): Sabbath rest was given as liberation from bondage, underscoring Jesus' principle that preserving human life fulfills rather than violates the Sabbath.
- **Isaiah 49:24–25 & 53:12** (echoed in Mark 3:27): *"Can plunder be taken from a mighty warrior, or captives rescued from a tyrant? Yet the LORD declares: even the captives of the mighty will be taken away, and the tyrant's spoil rescued."*
- **Zechariah 13:2** (alluded to in Mark 3:22–27): God promises to remove the "unclean spirit" (*pneuma akatharton*) from the land.
