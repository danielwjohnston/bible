## Clear & Faithful

**1** In those days, when a large crowd had gathered again and had nothing to eat, Jesus called his disciples to him and said,
**2** "My heart aches with compassion for the crowd, because they have stayed with me three days already and have nothing left to eat.
**3** If I send them away hungry to their homes, they will collapse along the road, for some of them have come from a long distance."
**4** His disciples answered him, "Where could anyone possibly find enough bread out here in the wilderness to fill all these people?"
**5** He asked them, "How many loaves do you have?" "Seven," they replied.
**6** Directing the crowd to sit down on the ground, he took the seven loaves, offered thanks, broke them, and kept handing them to his disciples to distribute; and they distributed them to the crowd.
**7** They also had a few small fish; after blessing these, he told the disciples to distribute them as well.
**8** The people ate and were completely satisfied, and the disciples picked up seven large hamper-baskets full of leftover fragments.
**9** Around four thousand people were present. Then Jesus dismissed them,
**10** boarded the boat immediately with his disciples, and sailed to the district of Dalmanutha.
**11** The Pharisees came out and began arguing with him, demanding from him a sign from heaven in order to put him to the test.
**12** Sighing deeply within his spirit, he asked, "Why does this generation demand a sign? Truly I tell you, no sign whatsoever will be given to this generation!"
**13** Leaving them behind, he climbed back into the boat and crossed to the opposite shore.
**14** Now the disciples had forgotten to bring bread, having only a single loaf with them in the boat.
**15** He warned them, "Watch closely—beware of the yeast of the Pharisees and the yeast of Herod."
**16** They began discussing among themselves why they had no bread.
**17** Aware of their reasoning, Jesus asked them, "Why are you debating about having no bread? Do you still neither perceive nor understand? Are your hearts still hardened?
**18** Having eyes, do you not see? Having ears, do you not hear? Do you not remember?
**19** When I broke the five loaves for the five thousand, how many wicker baskets full of leftovers did you gather?" "Twelve," they answered.
**20** "And when I broke the seven loaves for the four thousand, how many hamper-baskets full of leftovers did you gather?" "Seven," they told him.
**21** Then he asked them, "Do you still not understand?"
**22** They arrived at Bethsaida, where people brought a blind man to Jesus and begged him to touch him.
**23** Taking the blind man by the hand, he led him outside the village; he spat onto his eyes, laid his hands on him, and asked him, "Do you see anything?"
**24** Looking up, the man replied, "I see people—they look like trees walking around."
**25** Next, Jesus placed his hands upon the man's eyes a second time; the man stared intently, his sight was completely restored, and he saw everything distinctly.
**26** Jesus sent him away toward his home, instructing him, "Do not even go into the village."
**27** Then Jesus and his disciples set out for the villages around Caesarea Philippi; along the road he asked his disciples, "Who do people say that I am?"
**28** They answered him, "John the Baptist; others say Elijah; and still others say one of the prophets."
**29** Then he pressed them, "But you—who do you say that I am?" Peter spoke up and answered him, "You are the Christ."
**30** Jesus strictly warned them not to speak about him to anyone.
**31** Then he began teaching them that the Son of Man must suffer many things, be rejected by the elders, the chief priests, and the scribes, be put to death, and rise again after three days.
**32** He stated this message quite openly. Peter took him aside and began to rebuke him.
**33** Turning around and looking directly at his disciples, Jesus rebuked Peter, saying, "Get behind me, Satan! For your mind is not set on God's purposes, but on human concerns."
**34** Summoning the crowd along with his disciples, he declared to them, "Whoever desires to follow behind me must deny themselves, take up their cross, and follow me.
**35** For whoever wants to save their own life will lose it, yet whoever loses their life for my sake and for the sake of the good news will save it.
**36** What good does it do a person to gain the entire world and forfeit their own soul?
**37** Indeed, what could anyone ever give in exchange for their life?
**38** Whoever feels ashamed of me and my words in this unfaithful and sinful generation—the Son of Man will likewise be ashamed of that person when he comes in the glory of his Father with the holy angels."

**2** "I feel deeply for these people. They've been staying with me for three whole days now and have nothing left to eat.

**8** Everyone ate until they were full, and afterward the disciples collected seven big baskets overflowing with leftovers.

**11** When they arrived, some Pharisees came out and started arguing with him, demanding a miraculous sign straight from heaven to test him.
**12** Jesus let out a deep sigh and said, "Why does this generation keep demanding a sign? Listen to me—this generation isn't getting any sign at all!"
**13** He walked away from them, climbed right back into the boat, and sailed to the far shore.
**14** On the trip, the disciples realized they'd forgotten to pack food—all they had with them in the boat was one single loaf of bread.
**15** Jesus warned them, "Keep your eyes wide open! Watch out for the yeast of the Pharisees and the yeast of Herod!"
**16** The disciples started muttering to each other, "He's saying that because we forgot to bring bread."

**18** You've got eyes—why can't you see? You've got ears—why can't you hear? Have you already forgotten?
**19** When I broke five loaves for five thousand people, how many baskets full of leftovers did you pick up?" "Twelve," they answered.
**20** "And when I broke seven loaves for four thousand people, how many large hampers of leftovers did you gather?" "Seven," they told him.
**21** "And you still don't get it?" he asked.
**22** When they reached Bethsaida, some folks brought a blind man to Jesus and begged him to touch him.
**23** Jesus took the blind man by the hand and led him outside the village. He spat on the man's eyes, placed his hands on him, and asked, "Can you see anything?"
**24** Looking up, the man said, "I can make out people, though they look like trees walking around."
**25** So Jesus placed his hands over the man's eyes again. This time the man opened his eyes wide, his vision cleared up completely, and he saw everything sharply.
**26** Jesus sent him straight home, saying, "Don't even go back into the village."

**28** They replied, "Some say John the Baptizer, others say Elijah, and others say you're one of the prophets."
**29** Then Jesus asked them directly, "What about you? Who do you say I am?" Peter spoke up: "You are the Messiah!"
**30** Jesus strictly warned them not to tell anybody about him.
**31** Then he began explaining openly that the Son of Man was destined to suffer terribly, be rejected by the elders, chief priests, and legal experts, be executed, and rise back to life three days later.

**33** Jesus turned around, looked straight at his disciples, and scolded Peter right back: "Get out of my way, Satan! You're looking at things from a human point of view, not God's!"
**34** Calling the whole crowd over along with his disciples, Jesus announced, "If any of you want to come after me, you have to say no to yourself, shoulder your cross, and follow me.
**35** Anyone who tries to cling to their own life will lose it, while anyone who gives up their life for me and for the good news will truly save it.

## Crossings & Lexical Notes

- **v. 8 `σπυρίδας` (*spyridas*):** Large woven reed hampers (distinct from the twelve Jewish hand-baskets *kophinous* in 6:43 and 8:19, a distinction Jesus himself highlights in vv. 19–20).
- **v. 12 `εἰ δοθήσεται` (*ei dothēsetai*):** Literally "if a sign will be given!"—a classic Semitic oath-ellipse omitting the main curse clause ("May God do so to me if..."), functioning as an emphatic negative ("Certainly no sign will be given!").
- **v. 15 `ζύμης` (*zymēs*):** Yeast or leaven, symbolizing a small, hidden influence (political power or hypocritical tradition) that permeates the whole community.
- **v. 25 `τηλαυγῶς` (*tēlaugōs*):** From *tēle* ("far") + *augē* ("brightness/ray"), meaning "clearly even at a distance" or "in sharp focus," marking the completion of the only two-stage healing in the Gospels.
- **v. 29 `ὁ χριστός` (*ho Christos*):** Greek equivalent of Hebrew *Mashiach* ("Anointed One / Messiah"), royal title immediately reinterpreted by Jesus in v. 31 through suffering and resurrection.
- **v. 35 `ψυχήν` (*psychēn*):** Spans physical life, inner self, and enduring soul—creating a deliberate paradox in vv. 35–37 between clinging to self-preservation and gaining true life.

- **Jeremiah 5:21 & Ezekiel 12:2** (echoed in Mark 8:18): *"Hear this, O foolish and senseless people, who have eyes yet do not see, who have ears yet do not hear."*
