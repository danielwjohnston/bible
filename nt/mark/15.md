## Clear & Faithful

**1** As soon as morning broke, the chief priests held a consultation with the elders, the scribes, and the entire council; they bound Jesus, led him away, and handed him over to Pilate.
**2** Pilate questioned him, "Are you the King of the Jews?" Jesus answered him, "You say so."
**3** The chief priests leveled many accusations against him,
**4** so Pilate asked him again, "Have you no reply at all? See how many charges they bring against you!"
**5** Yet Jesus made no further reply, leaving Pilate amazed.
**6** Now at each festival Pilate used to release for them one prisoner whom they requested.
**7** A man known as Barabbas was locked up with the insurrectionists who had committed murder during the uprising.
**8** The crowd marched up and began asking him to do as he customarily did for them.
**9** Pilate asked them, "Do you want me to release for you the King of the Jews?"—
**10** for he realized that the chief priests had handed him over out of envy.
**11** But the chief priests stirred up the crowd to demand that he release Barabbas to them instead.
**12** Responding once more, Pilate asked them, "What then do you want me to do with the one you call the King of the Jews?"
**13** They shouted back, "Crucify him!"
**14** Pilate protested to them, "Why? What evil has he done?" Yet they roared all the louder, "Crucify him!"
**15** Wanting to satisfy the crowd, Pilate released Barabbas to them; and after having Jesus flogged, he handed him over to be crucified.
**16** The soldiers led him inside the courtyard—that is, the governor's headquarters—and assembled the entire cohort.
**17** They dressed him in purple, twisted together a crown of thorns, and placed it on his head,
**18** and they began saluting him: "Hail, King of the Jews!"
**19** They beat his head with a reed staff, spat on him, and knelt down in mock homage before him.
**20** When they finished mocking him, they stripped off the purple robe, dressed him back in his own clothes, and led him out to crucify him.
**21** They forced a passerby coming in from the countryside—Simon of Cyrene, the father of Alexander and Rufus—to carry his cross.
**22** They brought him to the place called Golgotha, which translates as "Place of a Skull."
**23** They offered him wine mixed with myrrh, yet he refused to take it.
**24** Then they crucified him and divided his clothing among themselves, casting lots over the pieces to decide who would take what.
**25** It was the third hour of the morning when they nailed him to the cross.
**26** The written notice stating the charge against him read: "THE KING OF THE JEWS."
**27** Alongside him they crucified two bandits, one on his right and one on his left.
**29** Passersby hurled insults at him, wagging their heads and jeering, "Aha! You who destroy the temple and rebuild it in three days—
**30** save yourself by coming down from the cross!"
**31** In the same way, the chief priests and scribes mocked him among themselves, saying, "He saved others; he cannot save himself!
**32** Let the Christ, the King of Israel, come down right now from the cross so we can see and believe!" Even those crucified alongside him heaped insults on him.
**33** When the sixth hour arrived, darkness fell across the entire land until the ninth hour.
**34** At the ninth hour Jesus cried out with a loud voice, "Eloi, eloi, lema sabachthani?" which translates as, "My God, my God, why have you forsaken me?"
**35** Hearing this, several bystanders remarked, "Listen, he is calling for Elijah!"
**36** One of them ran, soaked a sponge in sour wine, mounted it on a reed, and held it up for him to drink, saying, "Wait—let us see whether Elijah comes to take him down."
**37** Then Jesus let out a loud cry and breathed his last.
**38** And the curtain of the sanctuary was torn in two from top to bottom.
**39** When the centurion standing directly opposite him saw how he breathed his last, he declared, "Truly this man was God's Son!"
**40** Women were also watching from a distance, among them Mary Magdalene, Mary the mother of James the younger and Joses, and Salome,
**41** who had followed him and cared for his needs while he was in Galilee, along with many other women who had come up with him to Jerusalem.
**42** As evening had already come—since it was Preparation Day, the day before the Sabbath—
**43** Joseph of Arimathea, a respected member of the council who was himself eagerly awaiting the kingdom of God, summoned his courage, went in before Pilate, and requested Jesus' body.
**44** Pilate was surprised to hear he was already dead; summoning the centurion, he asked whether he had died some time earlier.
**45** Once assured by the centurion, he granted the corpse to Joseph.
**46** Joseph bought a linen shroud, took Jesus down, wrapped him in the linen, laid him in a tomb carved out of solid rock, and rolled a stone across the entrance of the tomb.
**47** Meanwhile, Mary Magdalene and Mary, Joses's mother, carefully observed the spot where he was placed.

## Everyday English

**1** First thing at dawn, the chief priests, elders, legal scholars, and the entire council reached their decision. They tied Jesus up, marched him over, and handed him over to Governor Pilate.
**2** Pilate asked him directly, "Are you the King of the Jews?" "You're the one saying so," Jesus replied.
**3** The chief priests kept piling accusation after accusation on him.
**4** So Pilate questioned him again: "Aren't you going to say anything? Look at how many charges they're throwing at you!"
**5** Yet Jesus stayed completely silent, which amazed Pilate.
**6** Now every year at Passover, Pilate made a habit of releasing whichever prisoner the crowd asked for.
**7** There was a man named Barabbas sitting in chains alongside some rebels who had committed murder during an uprising.
**8** A crowd came up to Pilate and began asking him for the usual holiday pardon.
**9** Pilate asked them, "Do you want me to set the King of the Jews free for you?"—
**10** because he knew perfectly well the chief priests had only turned Jesus in out of jealousy.
**11** However, the chief priests riled up the crowd to ask for Barabbas to be released instead.
**12** Pilate tried again: "Then what do you want me to do with the man you call the King of the Jews?"
**13** "Crucify him!" they yelled back.
**14** "Why?" Pilate asked. "What crime has he committed?" But they screamed even louder, "Crucify him!"
**15** Eager to keep the crowd happy, Pilate released Barabbas to them, ordered Jesus brutally whipped, and handed him over to be crucified.
**16** The Roman soldiers marched Jesus inside the palace courtyard—the Praetorium—and called out the whole battalion.
**17** They threw a purple cloak around him, twisted thorny branches into a crown, and shoved it onto his head.
**18** Then they mocked him with royal salutes: "Long live the King of the Jews!"
**19** They whacked him on the head with a stick, spat in his face, and dropped to their knees pretending to bow before him.
**20** Once they got tired of mocking him, they yanked off the purple cloak, put his own clothes back on him, and marched him outside to be crucified.
**21** Along the way they forced a passerby walking in from the countryside—Simon from Cyrene, the father of Alexander and Rufus—to carry Jesus' cross.
**22** They brought Jesus to a spot called Golgotha, which means "Place of a Skull."
**23** They offered him wine mixed with myrrh to dull the pain, yet he turned it down.
**24** Then they nailed him to the cross and divided up his clothes by rolling dice to see who got each piece.
**25** It was nine o'clock in the morning when they crucified him.
**26** A sign nailed over him listed the charge: "THE KING OF THE JEWS."
**27** They also crucified two criminals right beside him, one on his right and one on his left.
**29** People walking past insulted him, shaking their heads and mocking, "Ha! You're the guy who destroys the temple and rebuilds it in three days—
**30** climb down off that cross and save yourself!"
**31** The chief priests and legal experts joined right in, laughing among themselves: "He saved plenty of others, yet he can't save himself!
**32** Let this Messiah, this 'King of Israel,' step down off the cross right now so we can see it and believe him!" Even the two men crucified beside him hurled insults at him.
**33** At noon, darkness blanketed the whole land and lasted until three in the afternoon.
**34** At three o'clock Jesus screamed at the top of his lungs, "Eloi, eloi, lema sabachthani?"—which means, "My God, my God, why have you abandoned me?"
**35** A few bystanders heard him and muttered, "Listen, he's calling for Elijah!"
**36** One man sprinted over, soaked a sponge in cheap sour wine, stuck it on a pole, and held it up for Jesus to drink, saying, "Hold on, let's see if Elijah actually shows up to pull him down."
**37** With one final loud cry, Jesus took his last breath and died.
**38** Instantly, the heavy curtain inside the temple ripped straight in two from top to bottom.
**39** When the Roman army captain standing right in front of the cross saw the way Jesus died, he exclaimed, "This man truly was God's Son!"
**40** Several women stood watching from a distance, including Mary Magdalene, Mary (the mother of young James and Joses), and Salome.
**41** They had followed Jesus and helped take care of him back in Galilee, alongside many other women who'd made the trip with him to Jerusalem.
**42** Evening was approaching fast, and since it was Preparation Day—right before the Sabbath began—
**43** Joseph from Arimathea, a well-respected council member who was eagerly waiting for God's kingdom, worked up his courage, went straight to Pilate, and asked for Jesus' body.
**44** Pilate was surprised to hear Jesus was already dead, so he summoned the Roman captain to check if he'd really been dead long enough.
**45** Once the captain confirmed it, Pilate let Joseph take the body.
**46** Joseph bought a linen burial sheet, took Jesus down from the cross, wrapped him in the sheet, laid him inside a tomb cut straight into solid rock, and rolled a heavy stone across the doorway.
**47** Mary Magdalene and Mary the mother of Joses watched carefully to see where he was placed.

## Crossings & Lexical Notes

- **v. 16 `πραιτώριον … σπεῖραν` (*praitōrion … speiran*):** *Praitōrion* is a Latin loanword (*praetorium*) for the Roman governor's headquarters; *speira* translates the Latin *cohors* (a military battalion of roughly 600 soldiers).
- **v. 21 `ἀγγαρεύουσιν` (*angareuousin*):** Originally a Persian loanword denoting the legal right of imperial troops to requisition civilians and pack animals for forced labor.
- **v. 28 `Καὶ ἐπληρώθη ἡ γραφὴ ἡ λέγουσα…` (*Kai eplērōthē hē graphē hē legousa…*):** Omitted in SBLGNT and Nestle 1904 (`WH` / `NA28` / earliest Alexandrian witnesses `א`, `B`, `A`, `C`, `D`). `Treg` and Byzantine manuscripts (`RP`) add v. 28 ("And the Scripture was fulfilled which says, 'And he was counted among lawless ones'"), assimilated from Luke 22:37 / Isaiah 53:12.
- **v. 34 `Ἐλωῒ ἐλωῒ λεμὰ σαβαχθάνι` (*Elōi, elōi, lema sabachthani*):** Mark transcribes Jesus' Aramaic cry (*'Elāhî, 'Elāhî, lemā šebaqtanî*, quoting Ps 22:1) and translates it for Greek readers.
- **v. 38 `τὸ καταπέτασμα` (*to katapetasma*):** The heavy inner veil shielding the Holy of Holies; *eschisthē* ("was torn apart") uses the exact verb from Jesus' baptism (Mark 1:10).
- **v. 39 `κεντυρίων` (*kentyrion*):** Latin *centurio* ("commander of a hundred soldiers"). Notice his confession ("Son of God") lacks the definite article in Greek (`υἱὸς θεοῦ`), echoing Mark 1:1's title.
