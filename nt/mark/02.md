## Clear & Faithful

**1** Some days later, when Jesus returned to Capernaum, word got out that he was at home.
**2** So many people crowded together that there was no space left, not even around the doorway, and he kept speaking the word to them.
**3** Then people arrived carrying a paralyzed man, lifted by four bearers.
**4** Finding no way to get him close to Jesus because of the crowd, they dismantled the roof directly above where he was, dug an opening through, and lowered the mat on which the paralyzed man was lying.
**5** Seeing their faith, Jesus said to the paralyzed man, "My child, your sins are forgiven."
**6** Now some of the scribes were sitting there, questioning silently in their hearts:
**7** "Why does this fellow talk like that? He is blaspheming! Who can forgive sins except God alone?"
**8** Immediately perceiving in his spirit what they were debating inside themselves, Jesus asked them, "Why are you entertaining such thoughts in your hearts?
**9** Which is easier to say to this paralyzed man: 'Your sins are forgiven,' or to say, 'Get up, pick up your mat, and walk'?
**10** Yet so that you may know that the Son of Man holds authority on earth to forgive sins"—he turned to the paralyzed man—
**11** "I tell you: stand up, lift your mat, and go home."
**12** At once the man stood up, picked up his mat, and walked out in plain sight of everyone, leaving them all astonished and praising God as they exclaimed, "We have never seen anything like this!"
**13** Once more Jesus went out along the lakeshore; the whole crowd kept coming to him, and he taught them.
**14** As he walked along, he saw Levi son of Alphaeus seated at the tax-collection booth and said to him, "Follow me." Levi got up and followed him.
**15** Later, as Jesus was reclining at table in Levi's house, many tax collectors and sinners were dining alongside him and his disciples, for there were many who followed him.
**16** When the scribes belonging to the Pharisees noticed that he was eating with sinners and tax collectors, they asked his disciples, "Why does he share meals with tax collectors and sinners?"
**17** Hearing this, Jesus answered them, "Healthy people do not need a doctor; sick people do. I did not come to invite the righteous, but sinners."
**18** Now John's disciples and the Pharisees were observing a fast. Some people came and asked him, "Why do John's disciples and the disciples of the Pharisees fast, while your disciples do not?"
**19** Jesus replied to them, "Can the wedding guests fast while the bridegroom is still with them? As long as they have the bridegroom present, they cannot fast.
**20** But days will come when the bridegroom is taken away from them, and on that day they will fast.
**21** No one stitches a patch of unshrunk cloth onto an old cloak; otherwise the new patch pulls away from the old garment, leaving an even worse tear.
**22** Nor does anyone pour fresh wine into old wineskins; otherwise the wine will burst the skins, and both the wine and the skins are ruined. Instead, fresh wine belongs in new wineskins."
**23** One Sabbath as he was passing through the grainfields, his disciples began clearing a path by plucking heads of grain.
**24** The Pharisees protested to him, "Look! Why are they doing something forbidden on the Sabbath?"
**25** He answered them, "Have you never read what David did when he was in need and hungry, along with his companions—
**26** how he entered God's house when Abiathar was high priest and ate the consecrated bread of the Presence, which no one except the priests is permitted to eat, and even shared it with the men with him?"
**27** Then he told them, "The Sabbath was created for humanity, not humanity for the Sabbath;
**28** consequently, the Son of Man is Lord even over the Sabbath."

## Everyday English

**1** A few days later Jesus went back to Capernaum, and word spread fast that he was staying in a house there.
**2** So many people packed inside that there wasn't even standing room outside the front door. Jesus kept sharing God's message with them.
**3** Just then four people came up carrying a man who couldn't walk.
**4** Since the crowd blocked any way in, they climbed up onto the flat roof right over Jesus, tore away the roofing material, and lowered the man down on his sleeping mat.
**5** When Jesus saw how much trust they had, he told the paralyzed man, "Son, your sins are forgiven."
**6** Some teachers of the law were sitting nearby, and they started thinking to themselves,
**7** "How dare this guy talk like that? That's an insult to God! Nobody can forgive sins except God himself!"
**8** Right away, Jesus sensed inside himself what they were thinking and asked them, "Why are you brooding over this in your minds?
**9** Which one is easier to say to this paralyzed man—'Your sins are forgiven,' or 'Stand up, grab your mat, and walk around'?
**10** I'm going to prove to you that the Son of Man has the authority right here on earth to forgive sins." So he looked at the man who couldn't walk and said,
**11** "I'm telling you—get up, pick up your mat, and head home!"
**12** The man jumped right up, grabbed his mat, and walked straight out in front of everybody. Everyone stood amazed and thanked God, saying, "We've never seen anything remotely like this!"
**13** Jesus walked out along the lake again. Huge crowds kept coming to him, and he taught them there.
**14** As he was walking by, he spotted Levi, the son of Alphaeus, sitting at his tax-collecting booth. "Come follow me," Jesus told him. Levi stood up and followed him.
**15** Later on, Jesus was having dinner at Levi's home, and plenty of tax collectors and social outcasts were eating with him and his followers—there were a lot of them following him around.
**16** When the legal experts among the Pharisees saw Jesus eating with outcasts and tax collectors, they questioned his followers: "Why does he sit down to eat with tax collectors and sinners?"
**17** Jesus overheard them and answered, "Healthy folks don't need a doctor—sick people do. I didn't come to invite people who think they're already good; I came for sinners."
**18** At that time, John's followers and the Pharisees were fasting. Some people walked up and asked Jesus, "Why do John's followers and the Pharisees' followers go without food, while yours don't?"
**19** Jesus answered, "Wedding guests don't fast while the groom is still at the party, do they? Of course they can't fast while the groom is right there with them!
**20** Someday the groom will be snatched away from them, and that's when they'll fast.
**21** Nobody sews a piece of brand-new, unwashed cloth onto an old coat. If you do, the new patch shrinks and rips away from the old fabric, making the hole even bigger.
**22** And nobody pours freshly pressed wine into worn-out leather wineskins. If you do, the fermenting wine bursts the skins, and you lose both the wine and the containers. Fresh wine goes into brand-new wineskins!"
**23** One Sabbath day, Jesus was walking along the edge of some grainfields, and his followers began picking heads of grain as they made their way through.
**24** The Pharisees called out to him, "Hey, look at what they're doing! Why are they breaking the law on the Sabbath?"
**25** Jesus replied, "Haven't you ever read what David did when he and his men were desperate and hungry?
**26** He walked right into God's sanctuary during the time of Abiathar the high priest, ate the sacred bread set out before God—bread only priests were allowed to eat—and even handed it out to his men!"
**27** Then Jesus added, "God made the Sabbath to benefit people; he didn't make people to serve the Sabbath.
**28** And that means the Son of Man is master even of the Sabbath."

## Crossings & Lexical Notes

- **v. 4 `τὸν κράβαττον` (*ton krabatton*):** A colloquial Koine loanword (from Latin *grabatus*) referring to a simple woven pallet or sleeping mat easily carried by four friends and rolled up by the healed man (v. 12).
- **v. 10 `ὁ υἱὸς τοῦ ἀνθρώπου` (*ho huios tou anthropou*):** Literally "the Son of the Human" (translating Aramaic *bar enasha* / Hebrew *ben adam*). In Daniel 7:13–14 it denotes a heavenly figure invested with royal authority; Mark uses it here for the first time in Jesus' mouth to link earthly forgiveness and healing with divine authority.
- **v. 22 `νέον … καινούς` (*neon … kainous*):** Greek distinguishes two words for "new": *neos* (freshly made/recent in time, "new wine") and *kainos* (renewed/fresh in quality and capacity, "fresh wineskins").
- **v. 26 `ἐπὶ Ἀβιαθὰρ ἀρχιερέως` (*epi Abiathar archiereōs*):** Literally "in the time of Abiathar the high priest." First Samuel 21:1–6 names Ahimelech (Abiathar's father) as the priest at Nob, while Abiathar became David's prominent high priest shortly after (1 Sam 22:20; 2 Sam 15). Note also that `πῶς` ("how") is read by `WH Treg RP NA28` and omitted by a few manuscripts/witnesses.

## Supplemental Texts & Allusions

- **Psalm 103:3** (echoed in Mark 2:5–12): *"He pardons all your guilt and cures every one of your illnesses."*
- **Daniel 7:13–14** (echoed in Mark 2:10, 28): *"With the clouds of heaven came one like a son of man... to him were given dominion, honor, and kingship."*
- **1 Samuel 21:1–6 & Leviticus 24:5–9** (cited in Mark 2:25–26): David received the twelve consecrated loaves of the Bread of the Presence from the priest at Nob when he and his companions were starving, though Leviticus normally restricted them to Aaron's priestly sons.
