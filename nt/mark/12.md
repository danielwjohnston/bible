## Clear & Faithful

**1** Jesus began speaking to them in parables: "A man planted a vineyard, surrounded it with a protective wall, dug out a pit for the winepress, built a watchtower, leased it to tenant farmers, and went away on a journey.
**2** When harvest season arrived, he sent a servant to the tenants to collect his share of the vineyard's fruit.
**3** Yet they seized him, beat him severely, and sent him back empty-handed.
**4** Again he sent another servant to them; that one they struck on the head and treated shamefully.
**5** He sent yet another, and him they killed—along with many more, beating some and killing others.
**6** He still had one left: his beloved son. Finally he sent him to them, thinking, 'They will surely respect my son.'
**7** But those tenants said to one another, 'This is the heir! Come on, let us kill him, and the inheritance will become ours.'
**8** So they seized him, killed him, and threw his body outside the vineyard.
**9** What will the owner of the vineyard do? He will come, destroy those tenants, and hand the vineyard over to others.
**10** Have you never read this Scripture: 'The very stone that the builders rejected has become the cornerstone;
**11** this came about from the Lord, and it is wondrous in our eyes'?"
**12** They looked for a way to arrest him, yet they feared the crowd, because they realized he had told this parable against them. So they left him and went away.
**13** Next, they sent some of the Pharisees and Herodians to him in order to trap him in his own words.
**14** Approaching him, they said, "Teacher, we know that you are truthful and show deference to no one, for you do not judge by human status, but teach God's way in truth. Is it lawful to pay the imperial poll tax to Caesar or not? Should we pay, or should we not?"
**15** Perceiving their hypocrisy, he asked them, "Why are you testing me? Bring me a denarius to look at."
**16** They brought him one. He asked them, "Whose portrait is this, and whose inscription?" "Caesar's," they answered him.
**17** Jesus told them, "Give back to Caesar the things that belong to Caesar, and to God the things that belong to God." And they marveled in utter astonishment at him.
**18** Then Sadducees, who maintain there is no resurrection, came to him and posed this question:
**19** "Teacher, Moses wrote for us that if a man's brother dies leaving behind a widow without a child, his brother must marry the widow and raise up offspring for his brother.
**20** Now there were seven brothers. The first married a woman and died without leaving offspring.
**21** The second married her and died, leaving no offspring either; and the third did likewise.
**22** None of the seven left any offspring. Last of all, the woman also died.
**23** In the resurrection, once they have risen, whose wife will she be? After all, all seven men married her."
**24** Jesus replied to them, "Is not this the exact reason you are astray—because you know neither the Scriptures nor the power of God?
**25** Because once people rise from death, they neither marry nor are given in marriage; instead, they live like angels in the heavens.
**26** As for the dead being raised, have you not read in the book of Moses, in the passage about the burning bush, how God spoke to him, saying, 'I am the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob'?
**27** He is not God over corpses, rather over the living! You are deeply mistaken."
**28** One of the scribes walked up, having heard them debating; recognizing that Jesus had answered them well, he asked him, "Which commandment is foremost of all?"
**29** Jesus answered, "Foremost is this: 'Hear, O Israel: the Lord our God, the Lord is one;
**30** and love the Lord your God from your whole heart, your whole soul, your whole mind, and your whole strength.'
**31** The second stands beside it: 'Love your neighbor just as yourself.' No other commandment outranks these."
**32** The scribe said to him, "Well spoken, Teacher! You have stated truthfully that he is one and there is no other besides him;
**33** and to love him with all one's heart, all one's understanding, and all one's strength, and to love one's neighbor as oneself, counts for far more than all burnt offerings and sacrifices."
**34** Seeing how thoughtfully he had answered, Jesus said to him, "You are not far from the kingdom of God." After that, no one dared to question him anymore.
**35** While teaching in the temple, Jesus took up the issue and asked, "How can the scribes claim that the Christ is the son of David?
**36** David himself declared by the Holy Spirit: 'The Lord said to my Lord, "Sit at my right hand until I place your enemies beneath your feet."'
**37** David himself calls him 'Lord'; how then can he be his son?" And the large crowd listened to him with delight.
**38** In the course of his teaching he warned, "Beware of the scribes, who love parading around in long robes, receiving respectful greetings in the marketplaces,
**39** claiming the seats of honor in the synagogues and the places of distinction at banquets,
**40** while devouring widows' households and reciting lengthy prayers for show; these men will receive a far stricter sentence."
**41** Sitting down across from the temple treasury, Jesus watched how the crowd dropped copper coins into the offering boxes, and many wealthy people were putting in large sums.
**42** Then one poor widow came up and dropped in two tiny copper lepta, amounting to a quadrans.
**43** Calling his disciples over, he told them, "Truly I say to you, this poor widow has put in more than all the others contributing to the treasury;
**44** for everyone else gave out of their surplus, whereas she, out of her poverty, put in everything she possessed—all she had to live on."

## Everyday English

**1** Jesus began teaching them with another story: "A landowner planted a vineyard, built a stone wall around it, dug out a pit for crushing grapes, and put up a watchtower. Then he rented it out to tenant farmers and left on a long trip.
**2** When grape harvest came around, he sent a servant to the farmers to collect his share of the crop.
**3** Instead, the farmers grabbed the servant, beat him up, and sent him away with nothing.
**4** So the owner sent another servant; they bashed him on the head and humiliated him.
**5** He sent a third servant, and they murdered him! He kept sending many more—some they beat up, and others they killed.
**6** He had just one person left to send: his own dearly loved son. He sent him last of all, thinking, 'At least they'll respect my son.'
**7** Instead, those farmers whispered to each other, 'Here comes the heir! Come on, let's kill him, and the whole estate will be ours!'
**8** So they grabbed him, killed him, and tossed his body outside the vineyard.
**9** Now what do you think the vineyard owner is going to do? He'll come back, wipe out those farmers, and lease the vineyard to new tenants!
**10** Haven't you ever read this verse in Scripture: 'The stone the builders tossed aside as useless has become the main cornerstone;
**11** the Lord himself made this happen, and it looks amazing to us'?"
**12** The religious leaders wanted to arrest Jesus right on the spot because they knew the story was aimed straight at them, yet they were afraid of the crowd. So they walked away and left him alone.
**13** Later, they sent a group of Pharisees and Herod's supporters to trap Jesus into saying something incriminating.
**14** They walked up and flattered him: "Teacher, we know you're an honest man who doesn't bend to anybody's pressure. You don't play favorites with important people; you truly teach what God wants. Tell us: is it right under our law to pay taxes to Caesar or not? Should we pay or refuse?"
**15** Seeing right through their fake praise, Jesus said, "Why are you trying to bait me? Hand me a silver coin so I can look at it."
**16** They brought him one, and he asked, "Whose face and title are stamped on this coin?" "Caesar's," they answered.
**17** Jesus told them, "Well then, pay back to Caesar what belongs to Caesar, and give to God what belongs to God." They were left completely speechless.
**18** Next, some Sadducees—who claim nobody ever rises from the dead—came over with a riddle:
**19** "Teacher, Moses wrote a law for us that if a married man dies without any kids, his brother has to marry the widow and have children to carry on his brother's family line.
**20** Suppose there were seven brothers. The oldest married a woman and died without children.
**21** The second brother married her and also died childless, and the same thing happened with the third.
**22** In fact, all seven brothers married her without leaving any children, and finally the woman died too.
**23** Now in the resurrection, when they all come back to life, whose wife will she be? Every single one of the seven was married to her!"
**24** Jesus answered them, "That proves how off-track you are—you don't understand either the Scriptures or God's power!
**25** When people rise from the dead, they don't get married anymore; they're like the angels in heaven.
**26** As for the dead actually rising, haven't you ever read in the book of Moses, in the story of the burning bush, what God told Moses: 'I am the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob'?
**27** God isn't the God of dead corpses—he's the God of the living! You couldn't be more wrong."
**28** One of the legal scholars had been listening to their debate. Impressed by how sharply Jesus answered them, he stepped up and asked, "Out of all the commands, which one is the most important?"
**29** Jesus replied, "The number-one command is: 'Listen, Israel! The Lord our God is the one and only Lord.
**30** Love the Lord your God with every bit of your heart, your whole soul, your entire mind, and all your strength.'
**31** And the second command is: 'Love your neighbor the same way you love yourself.' No other command matters more than these two."
**32** "Well said, Teacher!" the legal expert agreed. "You're completely right that God is one and there's no other God besides him,
**33** and loving him with all your heart, mind, and strength—and loving your neighbor as yourself—means far more than every animal sacrifice and burnt offering put together."
**34** Seeing how wisely the man answered, Jesus told him, "You aren't far from God's kingdom." After that, nobody was brave enough to throw another question at him.
**35** Later, while teaching inside the temple courts, Jesus raised a question of his own: "How can the legal experts say the Messiah is just David's descendant?
**36** David himself, speaking by the Holy Spirit, wrote: 'The Lord said to my Lord, "Sit at my right side until I put all your enemies under your feet."'
**37** If David himself calls the Messiah 'my Lord,' how can he merely be David's son?" The huge crowd loved listening to him.
**38** As Jesus taught, he warned them: "Watch out for those legal experts who love strutting around in fancy robes, getting bowed to in public squares,
**39** grabbing the best front-row seats in synagogues, and taking the places of honor at dinner parties—
**40** all while swindling widows out of their homes and praying long, showy prayers! Those men are heading for much harsher judgment."
**41** Jesus sat down across from the temple offering boxes and watched people dropping money in. Plenty of wealthy folks tossed in huge amounts.
**42** Then one poor widow walked up and dropped in two tiny copper coins worth next to nothing.
**43** Jesus called his disciples over and told them, "Listen closely: this poor widow just gave more than everybody else who put money into the offering box!
**44** All those rich people gave out of what they had left over, while she, in her poverty, gave away every single cent she had to survive on."

## Crossings & Lexical Notes

- **v. 4 `ἐκεφαλίωσαν` (*ekephaliosan*):** Rare verb from *kephalē* ("head"), meaning "struck on the head / wounded in the head"; `Treg` and `RP` spell *ekephalaiōsan* ("summarized / struck on the head").
- **v. 14 `κῆνσον` (*kēnson*):** Latin loanword (*census*), the imperial capitation or poll tax imposed on Judea by Rome—paying it signaled submission, refusing it risked rebellion.
- **v. 17 `ἀπόδοτε` (*apodote*):** Compound verb *apodidōmi* means "give back / render what is due," contrasted with merely "giving" (*dōmen* in v. 14), hinting that Caesar's coin belongs to Caesar while humanity bearing God's image (*eikōn*) belongs to God.
- **v. 26 `ἐπὶ τοῦ βάτου` (*epi tou batou*):** "At the bush"—the standard ancient Jewish/Greek citation shorthand referring to the Exodus 3 burning-bush section before chapter-and-verse numbers existed.
- **v. 34 `νουνεχῶς` (*nounechōs*):** From *nous* ("mind") + *echō* ("having"), meaning "sensibly / thoughtfully / discerningly"; occurs nowhere else in the New Testament.
- **v. 42 `λεπτὰ δύο, ὅ ἐστιν κοδράντης` (*lepta dyo, ho estin kodrantēs*):** Mark translates the small Judean bronze *lepton* into the Roman *quadrans* (a quarter of an *as*, roughly 1/64 of a day's wage) for readers in a Roman context.
