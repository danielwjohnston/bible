## Clear & Faithful

**1** Once again Jesus began teaching beside the sea. Such an enormous crowd gathered around him that he stepped into a boat and sat on the water, while the entire crowd stood on the shore facing the sea.
**2** He taught them many things in parables, and in his teaching he told them:
**3** "Listen! A sower went out to sow.
**4** As he scattered it, some seeds dropped beside the path, and birds flew down and ate them up.
**5** Other seed fell on rocky ground where it did not have much soil; it sprouted right away because the soil had no depth,
**6** yet once the sun rose high, it was scorched and shriveled up because it lacked any root.
**7** Still other seed fell among thorns, and the thorns grew up and choked it, so it yielded no grain.
**8** And other seeds fell into good soil, and produced a crop growing up and increasing—yielding thirtyfold, sixtyfold, and a hundredfold."
**9** Then he added, "Whoever has ears to hear, let them hear."
**10** When he was alone, those around him together with the Twelve questioned him about the parables.
**11** He told them, "To you has been granted the secret of the kingdom of God; but to those outside, everything comes in parables,
**12** so that 'looking they may look and never perceive, and listening they may listen and never understand, lest they turn back and be forgiven.'"
**13** Then he asked them, "Do you not grasp this parable? How then will you understand any of the parables?
**14** The sower sows the word.
**15** The ones along the path are those where the word is sown: as soon as they hear it, Satan immediately comes and carries off the word sown in them.
**16** Similarly, those sown on rocky ground are people who, the moment they hear the word, welcome it with joy,
**17** yet they have no root within themselves and last only a short while; then when trouble or persecution arises on account of the word, they immediately fall away.
**18** Still others represent those sown among thorns: these are the listeners who have heard the word,
**19** yet the worries of this age, the illusion of wealth, and desires for everything else creep in and stifle the word, rendering it unfruitful.
**20** And those sown on the good soil are the ones who hear the word, embrace it, and bear a harvest—one thirtyfold, one sixtyfold, and one a hundredfold."
**21** He also told them, "Does a lamp get brought in to be set under a measuring basket or under a bed? Is it not meant to be placed on a lampstand?
**22** Nothing is hidden except to be brought to light, nor has anything been kept secret except to come out into the open.
**23** Anyone who possesses ears to listen, let him pay heed."
**24** And he told them, "Pay close attention to what you hear. By the measure you measure out, it will be measured back to you, and even more will be added to you.
**25** For whoever has will be given more, and whoever lacks, even what they have will be taken from them."
**26** He went on to say, "The kingdom of God is like this: a person scatters seed on the earth,
**27** sleeps by night and rises by day, and meanwhile the seed sprouts and grows without the farmer knowing how.
**28** All by itself the earth yields a crop: first the blade, then the ear, then the full kernel inside the ear.
**29** And the moment the crop is ripe, he puts in the sickle, because harvest time has arrived."
**30** He asked further, "With what shall we compare the kingdom of God, or what parable can we use to picture it?
**31** It is like a mustard seed, which, when planted in the ground, is the smallest of all seeds on earth;
**32** yet once planted, it shoots up and grows taller than all the garden plants, putting out large branches so that the birds of the sky can nest beneath its shade."
**33** With many parables of this sort he kept speaking the word to them, as much as they were able to take in.
**34** He never spoke to them without a parable, though privately he explained everything to his own disciples.
**35** That same day, when evening fell, he said to them, "Let us cross over to the opposite shore."
**36** Leaving the crowd behind, they took him along just as he was in the boat, and other boats accompanied him.
**37** A violent windstorm blew up, and waves kept crashing into the boat until it was already filling with water.
**38** Jesus himself was in the stern, asleep on the cushion. They woke him and cried out, "Teacher, do you not care that we are perishing?"
**39** Waking up, he rebuked the wind and commanded the sea, "Hush! Be still!" The wind died down, and a profound calm settled over the water.
**40** Then he asked them, "Why are you so terrified? Have you still no faith?"
**41** Overcome with deep awe, they kept saying to one another, "Who can this man be, that even the wind and the sea obey him?"

## Everyday English

**1** Jesus started teaching beside the lake again. The crowd pressing around him grew so huge that he climbed into a boat and sat out on the water, while everyone stood packed along the shoreline.
**2** He used lots of story-pictures to teach them, saying:
**3** "Listen closely! A farmer went out to scatter seed.
**4** As he tossed it, some seeds landed along the footpath, and birds swooped down and gobbled them up.
**5** Other seeds landed on thin rocky ground where there wasn't much dirt. They popped up fast because the soil was so shallow,
**6** but once the hot sun came up, they got scorched and dried out since they had no roots.
**7** Still other seeds fell right among thornbushes, and the thorns shot up, crowded them out, and kept them from producing any grain.
**8** Finally, some seeds landed in rich soil. They sprouted, grew tall, and produced a great harvest—some thirty times as much, some sixty times, and some a hundred times!"
**9** Then Jesus added, "If you've got ears to listen, pay attention!"
**10** Later, when he was away from the crowd, his inner circle and the Twelve asked him what those stories meant.
**11** Jesus told them, "You've been let in on the hidden truth about God's kingdom. For outsiders, though, everything stays wrapped in riddles,
**12** so that 'they keep staring without ever really seeing, and keep listening without ever catching on—otherwise they'd turn back to God and be forgiven!'"
**13** Then he asked them, "Don't you understand this story? If you miss this one, how are you going to make sense of any of the others?
**14** What the farmer scatters is God's message.
**15** Some people are like the beaten path where the message lands: the second they hear it, Satan shows up and snatches away the message planted in them.
**16** Other people are like the rocky patches: they hear the message and immediately welcome it with excitement,
**17** but they don't let it put down deep roots. They only stick with it for a little while; as soon as hardship or opposition hits because of the message, they bail out right away.
**18** Still others are like seeds scattered among thorns: they hear the message,
**19** but everyday worries, the false promise of money, and craving all kinds of other things crowd in, choke the message out, and keep it from bearing fruit.
**20** Then there are the ones planted in rich soil: they hear the message, take it to heart, and produce a rich harvest—thirty, sixty, even a hundred times over!"
**21** Jesus also asked them, "You don't bring in an oil lamp just to stick it under a grain bucket or shove it under a bed, do you? No, you set it up on a lampstand!
**22** Whatever is tucked out of sight is meant to be uncovered, and whatever is kept secret is meant to come into full view.
**23** If you've got ears to listen, take it to heart!"
**24** He continued, "Be careful how closely you listen. Whatever standard you use for others will be used for you—and God will pour out even more on top of it.
**25** Anyone who already holds onto what they hear will be given more; anyone who doesn't will lose even the little they have."
**26** Jesus said, "God's kingdom works like this: a farmer scatters seed in his field.
**27** Night after night he goes to sleep, day after day he gets up, and all the while the seed sprouts and grows tall, though he has no idea how it happens.
**28** All on its own, the soil brings forth the crop—first a green shoot, then a stalk with a head, then full kernels inside the head.
**29** As soon as the grain ripens, the farmer grabs his sickle because harvest time has come."
**30** He asked again, "What can we compare God's kingdom to? What picture fits it best?
**31** It's like a tiny mustard seed. When it's dropped into the ground, it's smaller than any other seed around.
**32** Yet once it's planted, it grows up taller than every other garden plant and spreads out wide branches, big enough for wild birds to build nests in its shade."
**33** Jesus kept sharing God's message with them through lots of stories like these, giving them as much as they could handle.
**34** He wouldn't teach the crowds without using parables, though when he was alone with his own followers he explained everything clearly.
**35** Later that evening, Jesus told his followers, "Let's sail over to the far shore."
**36** Leaving the crowd on the beach, they took Jesus just as he was in the boat, with several other boats sailing alongside.
**37** Suddenly a fierce windstorm tore down on the lake. Waves slammed into the boat until it began to swamp.
**38** Jesus was in the back of the boat, fast asleep on a cushion. The disciples shook him awake, shouting, "Teacher, don't you care that we're about to drown?"
**39** Jesus stood up, scolded the wind, and told the lake, "Quiet down! Be muzzled!" Instantly the wind stopped, and the lake turned completely flat.
**40** Then he asked them, "Why are you so terrified? Don't you trust God yet?"
**41** They were struck with sheer awe and whispered to each other, "Who on earth is this man? Even the wind and the waves do whatever he commands!"

## Crossings & Lexical Notes

- **v. 8 `ἓν τριάκοντα … ἓν ἑξήκοντα … ἓν ἑκατόν` (*hen triakonta … hen hexēkonta … hen hekaton*):** Literally "one thirty, one sixty, one a hundred" (SBLGNT / `NA28`), contrasting with the preposition `εἰς` ("up to") in `WH`/`Treg` and `ἐν` ("in") in `RP` (repeated in v. 20).
- **v. 11 `τὸ μυστήριον` (*to mystērion*):** Not an unsolvable puzzle, but God's hidden royal purpose now disclosed to insiders through Jesus' proclamation.
- **v. 17 `πρόσκαιροι … σκανδαλίζονται` (*proskairoi … skandalizontai*):** *Proskairos* means "for a season / temporary"; *skandalizō* (from *skandalon*, a trap-stick or stumbling block) means "to be tripped up" or "fall away."
- **v. 21 `τὸν μόδιον` (*ton modion*):** A Roman dry-grain measure (*modius*, roughly a peck / 8.75 liters) found in ordinary households—inverting it over a clay oil lamp extinguished or hid the light.
- **v. 28 `αὐτομάτη` (*automatē*):** "Of its own accord / spontaneously," highlighting that the kingdom's growth depends on God's hidden vitality rather than anxious human forcing.
- **v. 29 `παραδοῖ` (*paradoi*):** From *paradidōmi* ("hands over / yields itself"), used here idiomatically of grain offering itself up when ripe.
- **v. 39 `πεφίμωσο` (*pephimōso*):** Perfect passive imperative of *phimoō* ("be muzzled and stay muzzled"), the exact verb Jesus used to silence the unclean spirit in Mark 1:25.

## Supplemental Texts & Allusions

- **Isaiah 6:9–10** (quoted in Mark 4:12): *"Keep listening, yet never understand; keep looking, yet never perceive; make the heart of this people dull... lest they turn back and be healed."*
- **Joel 3:13** (alluded to in Mark 4:29): *"Put the sickle to work, because the grain harvest has matured."*
- **Ezekiel 17:23 & Daniel 4:12, 21** (echoed in Mark 4:32): God's mighty tree spreads branches so that every bird of the sky nests in its shade—here reimagined as an ordinary garden mustard shrub.
- **Psalm 107:23–30 & Jonah 1:4–15** (echoed in Mark 4:37–41): Sailors in a raging storm cry out while Jonah sleeps below, and the LORD stills the roaring waves to a whisper.

**1** Again he began to teach by the seaside. A great multitude was gathered to him, so that he entered into a boat in the sea and sat down. All the multitude were on the land by the sea.

**3** “Listen! Behold, the farmer went out to sow.
**4** As he sowed, some seed fell by the road, and the birds came and devoured it.
**5** Others fell on the rocky ground, where it had little soil, and immediately it sprang up, because it had no depth of soil.
**6** When the sun had risen, it was scorched; and because it had no root, it withered away.
**7** Others fell among the thorns, and the thorns grew up and choked it, and it yielded no fruit.
**8** Others fell into the good ground and yielded fruit, growing up and increasing. Some produced thirty times, some sixty times, and some one hundred times as much.”

**10** When he was alone, those who were around him with the twelve asked him about the parables.
**11** He said to them, “To you is given the mystery of God’s Kingdom, but to those who are outside, all things are done in parables,
**12** that ‘seeing they may see and not perceive, and hearing they may hear and not understand, lest perhaps they should turn again, and their sins should be forgiven them.’”
**13** He said to them, “Don’t you understand this parable? How will you understand all of the parables?
**14** The farmer sows the word.
**15** The ones by the road are the ones where the word is sown; and when they have heard, immediately Satan comes and takes away the word which has been sown in them.
**16** These in the same way are those who are sown on the rocky places, who, when they have heard the word, immediately receive it with joy.
