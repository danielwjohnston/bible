## Clear & Faithful

**1** At that hour the disciples came to Jesus and asked, "Who then is greatest in the kingdom of heaven?"
**2** Calling a young child over, he stood the child in the middle of them
**3** and said, "Truly I tell you, unless you turn around and become like young children, you will never enter the kingdom of heaven.
**4** Therefore whoever humbles himself like this young child is the greatest in the kingdom of heaven.
**5** And whoever welcomes one such young child in my name welcomes me.
**6** Yet if anyone leads one of these little ones who trust in me into sin, it would be far better for that person to have a heavy millstone fastened around his neck and be plunged into the open depths of the sea.
**7** Woe to the world because of stumbling blocks! Temptations to fall are bound to come, yet woe to the person through whom the stumbling block arrives!
**8** If your hand or your foot causes you to sin, cut it off and throw it away from you; it is better for you to enter life crippled or lame than to have two hands or two feet and be thrown into the eternal fire.
**9** And if your eye causes you to sin, tear it out and throw it away from you; it is better for you to enter life with one eye than to have two eyes and be thrown into the fire of Gehenna.
**10** See that you do not look down on one of these little ones; for I tell you that in heaven their angels continually see the face of my Father in heaven.
**12** What do you think? If a man owns a hundred sheep and one of them strays away, will he not leave the ninety-nine on the hills and go search for the one that strayed?
**13** And if he happens to find it, truly I tell you, he rejoices over it more than over the ninety-nine that never went astray.
**14** Just so, it is not the desire of your Father in heaven that even one of these little ones should be lost.
**15** Now if your brother sins against you, go and show him his fault in private, just between the two of you. If he listens to you, you have won back your brother.
**16** But if he does not listen, take one or two others along with you, so that every word may be confirmed by the testimony of two or three witnesses.
**17** If he refuses to listen to them, tell the congregation; and if he refuses to listen even to the congregation, treat him as you would a Gentile and a tax collector.
**18** Truly I tell you, whatever you bind on earth will already be bound in heaven, and whatever you loose on earth will already be loosed in heaven.
**19** Again, truly I assure you that if two of you on earth agree on anything they ask for, it will be done for them by my Father in heaven.
**20** For where two or three are gathered in my name, there am I right among them."
**21** Then Peter approached him and asked, "Lord, how often will my brother sin against me and I must forgive him? Up to seven times?"
**22** Jesus told him, "I do not say to you up to seven times, but up to seventy-seven times.
**23** For this reason, the kingdom of heaven may be compared to a human king who decided to settle accounts with his servants.
**24** As he began the accounting, one debtor was brought before him who owed ten thousand talents.
**25** Since he had no way to repay, his master ordered that he be sold—along with his wife, his children, and everything he owned—so that repayment could be made.
**26** So the servant fell down, prostrated himself before him, and begged, 'Be patient with me, and I will pay you back everything!'
**27** Moved with deep compassion, that servant's master released him and canceled the entire debt.
**28** Yet when that servant went out, he encountered one of his fellow servants who owed him a hundred denarii. He grabbed him, began choking him, and demanded, 'Pay back whatever you owe!'
**29** So his fellow servant fell down and pleaded with him, 'Be patient with me, and I will pay you back!'
**30** But he refused; instead, he went and had him thrown into prison until he repaid what was owed.
**31** When his fellow servants saw what had taken place, they were deeply distressed and went and reported everything that had happened to their master.
**32** Then his master summoned him and said, 'You wicked servant! I canceled all that debt for you because you pleaded with me.
**33** Should you not also have shown mercy to your fellow servant, just as I showed mercy to you?'
**34** In his anger, his master handed him over to the jailers to be tortured until he repaid everything he owed.
**35** That is how my heavenly Father will treat each of you unless you forgive your brother from your heart."

## Everyday English

**1** About that time, the disciples came up to Jesus and asked, "So who ranks highest in heaven's kingdom?"
**2** Jesus called a little child over, stood the child right in the middle of the group,
**3** and said, "Listen to me: unless you change direction and become like little children, you won't even enter heaven's kingdom.
**4** Whoever makes themselves humble like this child is the one who ranks highest in God's kingdom.
**5** And anyone who welcomes a child like this because of me is welcoming me.
**6** On the other hand, if anyone trips up one of these humble believers who trusts me, they'd be better off having a giant millstone tied around their neck and being dropped into the deepest part of the sea!
**7** How tragic it is for the world because of all the traps that make people fall! Traps are guaranteed to show up, yet how terrible it will be for the person who sets them!
**8** If your hand or your foot keeps dragging you into sin, chop it off and throw it away. It's far better to enter eternal life disabled or limping than to keep both hands and both feet and end up thrown into the fire that never dies.
**9** And if your eye keeps dragging you into sin, gouge it out and toss it aside. It's better to enter life with one eye than to keep both eyes and be thrown into the fires of Gehenna.
**10** Be careful never to look down on even one of these little ones. I'm telling you, their angels in heaven always stand directly before my heavenly Father.
**12** Think about it this way: if someone owns a hundred sheep and one wanders off, won't they leave the ninety-nine up in the hills and go look for the missing one?
**13** And if they manage to find it, believe me, they're happier over that single sheep than over the ninety-nine that never wandered off.
**14** In the exact same way, your Father in heaven never wants even one of these little ones to be lost.
**15** Now, if a fellow believer wrongs you, go speak to them privately, just the two of you, and point out the fault. If they listen, you've repaired the relationship.
**16** If they won't listen, bring one or two others along with you so that every claim can be backed up by two or three witnesses.
**17** If they refuse to listen to them too, bring it before the whole congregation. And if they won't listen even to the congregation, treat them as an outsider—like a Gentile or a tax collector.
**18** I promise you, whatever you prohibit on earth stands confirmed in heaven, and whatever you permit on earth stands confirmed in heaven.
**19** What's more, I assure you that if two of you on earth come together in agreement about anything you pray for, my Father in heaven will grant it to you.
**20** Because wherever two or three people gather together in my name, I'm right there with them."
**21** Then Peter walked up and asked him, "Lord, how many times do I have to forgive my brother when he keeps wronging me? Seven times?"
**22** "Not just seven times," Jesus answered, "but seventy-seven times!
**23** Let me show you what heaven's kingdom is like: a king decided to audit the accounts of his officials.
**24** As soon as he started reviewing the books, someone brought in a man who owed him ten thousand talents—an impossible fortune.
**25** Since the man couldn't pay a cent, the king ordered that he, his wife, his children, and everything he owned be sold off to settle the debt.
**26** The servant dropped to his knees, bowed low, and begged, 'Please give me more time, and I'll pay back every penny!'
**27** The king felt deep pity for him, let him go free, and wiped out the entire debt.
**28** Not long after, that very servant walked out and ran into a coworker who owed him a mere hundred silver coins. He grabbed him by the throat and started choking him, yelling, 'Pay me back what you owe!'
**29** His coworker dropped to his knees and pleaded, 'Please give me more time, and I'll pay you back!'
**30** But he wouldn't hear of it—he went straight off and threw the man in debtors' prison until every coin was paid.
**31** When the other servants saw what happened, they were sickened and went straight to the king to report the whole affair.
**32** The king called the man back in and said, 'You cruel servant! I canceled your massive debt simply because you begged me!
**33** Shouldn't you have shown the same mercy to your coworker that I showed to you?'
**34** Furious, the king turned him over to the prison torturers until he paid back every last coin he owed.
**35** That's exactly how my Father in heaven will deal with you if each of you refuses to genuinely forgive your brother or sister from the heart."

## Crossings & Lexical Notes

- **v. 6 `μύλος ὀνικός` (*mylos onikos*):** Literally "donkey millstone"—the heavy upper stone turned by a donkey, far larger than a household hand-mill.
- **v. 9 `γέενναν` (*geennan*):** Greek transliteration of Hebrew *Ge-Hinnom* ("Valley of Hinnom" south of Jerusalem), associated with judgment in prophetic literature and later Jewish apocalyptic imagery of final punishment.
- **v. 11 `Ἦλθεν γὰρ ὁ υἱὸς τοῦ ἀνθρώπου σῶσαι τὸ ἀπολωλός` (*Ēlthen gar ho huios tou anthrōpou sōsai to apolōlos*):** Omitted in SBLGNT and Nestle 1904 (following WH, Treg, NA28; absent from `א`, `B`, `L*`, and early versions); RP inserts this line ("For the Son of Man came to save what was lost"), assimilated from Luke 19:10.
- **v. 15 `ἔλεγξον` (*elenxon*):** From *elenchō*, "to expose, bring to light, convict, or reprove" privately so as to restore rather than shame.
- **v. 22 `ἑβδομηκοντάκις ἑπτά` (*hebdomēkontakis hepta*):** Ambiguous Greek idiom meaning either "seventy-seven times" (cf. Gen 4:24 LXX) or "seventy times seven" (490), in either case signifying boundless forgiveness.
- **v. 24 `μυρίων ταλάντων` (*myriōn talantōn*):** Ten thousand talents—a talent equaled roughly 6,000 denarii (a laborer's daily wage), making 10,000 talents (60 million denarii) astronomically beyond the 100 denarii in v. 28.

## Supplemental Texts & Allusions

- **Deuteronomy 19:15** (quoted in v. 16): *"A single witness shall not suffice against a person for any crime... only on the testimony of two or three witnesses shall a charge be established."*
- **Genesis 4:24** (echoed and reversed in v. 22): Lamech boasts of seventy-sevenfold vengeance (*hebdomēkontakis hepta* in LXX), which Jesus transforms into seventy-sevenfold forgiveness.
- **Ezekiel 34:11–16** (echoed in vv. 12–14): God as the shepherd who leaves the flock to seek out the straying and lost sheep.
- **Sirach 28:2–4** (conceptual parallel to vv. 23–35): *"Forgive your neighbor the wrong done to you, and then your sins will be pardoned when you pray."*
