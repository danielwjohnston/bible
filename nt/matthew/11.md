## Clear & Faithful

**1** When Jesus had finished instructing his twelve disciples, he moved on from there to teach and proclaim the message in their towns.
**2** Now John, hearing in prison about the deeds of the Messiah, sent word by his disciples
**3** and asked him, "Are you the Coming One, or should we look for someone else?"
**4** Jesus answered them, "Go and report to John what you hear and see:
**5** those who were blind see again, the lame walk, lepers are made clean, the deaf hear, the dead are raised to life, and the poor have the good news proclaimed to them.
**6** And blessed is anyone who does not stumble on account of me."
**7** As those messengers were leaving, Jesus began to speak to the crowds about John: "What did you go out into the wilderness to gaze at? A reed shaken by the wind?
**8** Then what did you go out to see? Someone dressed in fine robes? Look—those who wear fine clothing live inside royal palaces!
**9** Why then did you go out—to see a prophet? Yes, I tell you, and far more than a prophet.
**10** This is the very one about whom Scripture says: 'Look, I am sending my messenger ahead of you, who will make ready your road before you.'
**11** Truly I tell you, among those born of women no one greater than John the Baptist has arisen; yet the least in the kingdom of heaven is greater than he.
**12** From the days of John the Baptist until now, the kingdom of heaven has been advancing with force, and forceful people are laying hold of it.
**13** Because all the Prophets and the Law prophesied up until John;
**14** and if you are willing to accept it, he is Elijah who was destined to come.
**15** Whoever has ears, let him hear.
**16** Yet what can I compare this generation to? They are like children seated in the open squares who call out to their playmates,
**17** saying, 'We played the flute for you, and you did not dance; we sang a funeral dirge, and you did not mourn.'
**18** Because John arrived neither eating nor drinking, and people say, 'He has a demon.'
**19** The Son of Man came eating and drinking, and they say, 'Look, a glutton and a drunkard, a friend of tax collectors and sinners!' Yet wisdom is vindicated by her deeds."
**20** Then Jesus began to denounce the towns in which most of his mighty works had been performed, because they had not repented:
**21** "Woe to you, Chorazin! Woe to you, Bethsaida! Because if the mighty deeds performed among you had happened in Tyre and Sidon, they would have repented long ago in sackcloth and ashes.
**22** Even so, I tell you that on judgment day it will go easier for Tyre and Sidon than for you.
**23** And you, Capernaum, will you be exalted up to heaven? Down to Hades you shall descend! For if the mighty works done in you had been done in Sodom, it would have remained standing until this very day.
**24** Even so, I tell you that on judgment day it will go easier for Sodom's land than for you."
**25** At that time Jesus declared, "I praise you, Father, Lord of heaven and earth, because you have hidden these things from the wise and learned and revealed them to little children;
**26** yes, Father, for such was your gracious will.
**27** All things have been handed over to me by my Father, and no one fully knows the Son except the Father, nor does anyone fully know the Father except the Son and anyone to whom the Son chooses to reveal him.
**28** Come to me, all who are weary and weighed down by burdens, and I will refresh you.
**29** Shoulder my yoke and learn from me, because I am gentle and lowly in heart, and you will discover true rest for your lives.
**30** For my yoke is kindly and my burden is light."

## Everyday English

**1** After Jesus finished giving orders to his twelve disciples, he left that area to teach and preach in the surrounding towns.
**2** Meanwhile, John—who was locked up in prison—heard what the Messiah was doing and sent his followers to ask him,
**3** "Are you the One we've been waiting for, or should we expect someone else?"
**4** Jesus answered them, "Go back and tell John everything you're hearing and seeing:
**5** blind people can see again, lame people are walking, people with skin diseases are healed, deaf people can hear, dead people are brought back to life, and poor people are hearing good news!
**6** And God blesses anyone who doesn't lose faith in me."
**7** As John's followers walked away, Jesus started talking to the crowds about John: "What did you hike out into the desert to look at? Tall grass swaying in the breeze?
**8** Really, what did you go out to see? Somebody dressed in fancy silk clothes? People who wear fine outfits live in royal palaces!
**9** Then what did you go out for—to see a prophet? Yes, I tell you, and someone even greater than a prophet!
**10** John is the one Scripture talked about when it wrote: 'Look, I am sending my messenger right ahead of you to clear the road in front of you.'
**11** I assure you, out of everyone ever born, nobody stands higher than John the Baptist—yet even the smallest person in heaven's kingdom ranks higher than he does.
**12** Ever since John the Baptist began his work right up to today, heaven's kingdom has been breaking forward with power, and determined people are grabbing hold of it.
**13** All the Prophets and the Law pointed forward until John arrived.
**14** And if you're willing to accept the truth, John is the Elijah who was promised to come!
**15** Anyone who has ears should really listen.
**16** What can I compare this generation to? They act like kids sitting in the town square, shouting at their playmates:
**17** 'We played happy wedding music for you, and you wouldn't dance! We sang a sad funeral song, and you wouldn't cry!'
**18** When John came along fasting from food and wine, people said, 'He's got a demon!'
**19** Then the Son of Man came enjoying meals and wine with people, and they say, 'Look at him—a glutton and a heavy drinker, hanging out with tax collectors and outcasts!' Still, wisdom proves herself right through what she actually does."
**20** Then Jesus began calling out the towns where he had performed most of his miracles, because their people still refused to turn from sin:
**21** "How terrible for you, Chorazin! How terrible for you, Bethsaida! If the miracles performed in your streets had happened in Tyre and Sidon, those cities would have repented ages ago in sackcloth and ashes.
**22** Listen to me: on judgment day, Tyre and Sidon will get off lighter than you!
**23** And what about you, Capernaum? Do you think you'll be lifted all the way up to heaven? No—you'll drop straight down to the realm of the dead! If the miracles performed in your town had happened in Sodom, Sodom would still be standing today.
**24** Believe me, on judgment day the ruins of Sodom will get off lighter than you!"
**25** Right then Jesus prayed aloud: "I thank you, Father, Lord of heaven and earth, that you hid these truths from clever scholars and intellectuals and revealed them to simple children.
**26** Yes, Father, that's exactly what pleased you to do!
**27** My Father has placed everything into my hands. Nobody truly knows the Son except the Father, and nobody truly knows the Father except the Son—and anyone the Son chooses to show him to.
**28** Come over to me, all of you who are worn out and weighed down by heavy loads, and I will give you rest.
**29** Put my yoke across your shoulders and learn from me, because I am gentle and humble at heart, and you will discover real rest for your souls.
**30** My yoke fits well and feels kind, and the load I give you is light."

## Crossings & Lexical Notes

- **v. 2 `διὰ τῶν μαθητῶν` (*dia tōn mathētōn*):** SBLGNT, `WH`, `Treg`, and `NA28` read "through his disciples"; `RP` reads *dyo tōn mathētōn* ("two of his disciples," matching Luke 7:19).
- **v. 12 `βιάζεται` (*biazetai*) & `βιασταί` (*biastai*):** Famously ambiguous Greek clause: *biazetai* can be middle ("forcefully presses forward") or passive ("suffers violence/opposition"), and *biastai* ("forceful/violent people") can be positive (eager disciples pressing in) or negative (hostile opponents trying to snatch it away).
- **v. 19 `ἀπὸ τῶν ἔργων αὐτῆς` (*apo tōn ergōn autēs*):** SBLGNT, `WH`, `Treg`, and `NA28` read "by her works/deeds" (linking back to v. 2 *ta erga tou christou*), whereas `RP` reads *tōn teknōn autēs* ("her children," harmonizing with Luke 7:35).
- **v. 23 `μὴ ἕως οὐρανοῦ ὑψωθήσῃ` (*mē heōs ouranou hypsōthēsē*):** SBLGNT, `WH`, `Treg`, and `NA28` frame this as a rhetorical question expecting a "no" (*mē*); `RP` reads the participial phrase *hē heōs tou ouranou hypsōtheisa* ("you who were exalted to heaven").
- **v. 25 `ἐξομολογοῦμαί` (*exomologoumai*):** Here used in its Septuagintal liturgical sense ("I openly praise/give thanks," translating Hebrew *hodah*), addressing the Father as covenant sovereign.
- **v. 30 `χρηστός` (*chrēstos*):** Meaning "well-fitting," "kindly," "good," or "gracious," standing in deliberate contrast to the crushing burdens (*phortia*, cf. 23:4) imposed by rigorous legalism.

## Supplemental Texts & Allusions

- **Isaiah 35:5–6; 29:18–19; 61:1** (echoed in v. 5): Messianic signs of blind eyes opening, the lame leaping, the deaf hearing, and good news proclaimed to the poor.
- **Malachi 3:1 & 4:5 (3:23 MT)** (quoted/alluded to in vv. 10, 14): God sends his messenger ahead to prepare his way, explicitly identified with the coming Elijah.
- **Isaiah 14:13–15** (echoed in v. 23): Pronouncement against Babylon's pride—*"I will ascend to heaven... yet you are brought down to Sheol/Hades."*
- **Jeremiah 6:16 & Sirach 51:23–27** (echoed in vv. 28–30): Invitation to put one's neck under wisdom's yoke and discover *"refreshment for your weary souls."*
