## Clear & Faithful

**1** "For the kingdom of heaven is like a landowner who went out at dawn to hire laborers for his vineyard.
**2** Having settled with the workers on one denarius per day, he sent them out into his vineyard.
**3** Going out around the third hour, he saw others standing idle in the marketplace,
**4** and told them, 'You also go into the vineyard, and I will pay you whatever is fair.'
**5** So they went. Going out again around the sixth and the ninth hour, he did the same thing.
**6** Around the eleventh hour he went out and found still others standing there, and he asked them, 'Why have you been standing here idle all day?'
**7** They answered him, 'Because nobody hired us.' He told them, 'You also go into the vineyard.'
**8** When evening fell, the vineyard owner instructed his foreman, 'Summon the workers and pay them their wages, beginning with the last hired and ending with the first.'
**9** Those hired around the eleventh hour came and received a denarius each.
**10** When the first ones came, they assumed they would receive more, yet each of them also received a single denarius.
**11** Upon receiving it, they began grumbling against the landowner,
**12** saying, 'These last workers put in only one hour, yet you have made them equal to us who carried the heavy burden of the day and the scorching heat!'
**13** He answered one of them, 'Friend, I am not treating you unfairly. Did you not agree with me for a denarius?
**14** Take what belongs to you and go. I choose to give to this last worker the same as I give to you.
**15** Do I not have the right to do what I want with my own money? Or is your eye envious because I am generous?'
**16** So the last will be first, and the first will be last."
**17** While Jesus was traveling up toward Jerusalem, he drew the twelve disciples aside privately and told them along the road,
**18** "Listen, we are heading up to Jerusalem, where the Son of Man will be betrayed to the chief priests and scribes; they will sentence him to death
**19** and hand him over to the Gentiles to be mocked, flogged, and crucified; and on the third day he will be raised."
**20** At that moment the mother of Zebedee's sons approached him alongside her sons, bowing low to make a request of him.
**21** He asked her, "What do you want?" She told him, "Command that these two sons of mine may sit one at your right hand and one at your left in your kingdom."
**22** Jesus answered, "You do not realize what you are asking for. Are you able to drink the cup that I am about to drink?" They replied to him, "We are able."
**23** He said to them, "You will indeed drink my cup, yet to sit at my right and at my left is not mine to grant, but is for those for whom it has been prepared by my Father."
**24** Upon hearing this, the other ten became angry with the two brothers.
**25** Jesus summoned them and told them, "You know how Gentile rulers dominate their subjects, and how their high officials exercise control over them.
**26** It shall not be so among you. Instead, whoever wants to become great among you must be your servant,
**27** and whoever wants to be first among you must be your slave—
**28** just as the Son of Man arrived not to be waited upon, but to serve others and to offer his life as a ransom on behalf of many."
**29** As they were leaving Jericho, a huge crowd followed him.
**30** Suddenly two blind men sitting beside the road heard that Jesus was passing by and shouted out, "Lord, have mercy on us, Son of David!"
**31** The crowd sternly told them to be quiet, yet they shouted all the louder, "Lord, have mercy on us, Son of David!"
**32** Jesus stopped, called out to them, and asked, "What do you want me to do for you?"
**33** They answered him, "Lord, let our eyes be opened!"
**34** Moved with compassion, Jesus touched their eyes; immediately they regained their sight and followed him.

## Everyday English

**1** "Here's another picture of heaven's kingdom: a vineyard owner went out early at sunrise to hire day laborers.
**2** He agreed to pay them a standard day's wage—one silver coin—and sent them to work in his vineyard.
**3** Around nine in the morning he went back out, spotted other workers standing around the town square with nothing to do,
**4** and told them, 'Go work in my vineyard too, and I'll pay you whatever's fair.'
**5** So off they went. He went out again at noon and at three in the afternoon and did the exact same thing.
**6** Even at five in the afternoon he went back out, found still more men standing around, and asked them, 'Why have you stood here doing nothing all day long?'
**7** 'Nobody hired us,' they replied. 'Go on into my vineyard too,' he told them.
**8** When evening fell, the vineyard owner told his foreman, 'Call the workers together and hand out their pay, starting with the ones hired last and finishing with the first.'
**9** The men hired at five o'clock stepped up, and every one of them received a full day's coin.
**10** When the workers hired at dawn walked up, they expected a bigger payout—yet each of them got the exact same silver coin.
**11** As soon as they took their pay, they started complaining to the owner:
**12** 'Those guys who came last only worked a single hour, and you paid them just as much as us after we hauled the load all day in the blazing heat!'
**13** The owner answered one of them, 'My friend, I haven't cheated you. Didn't you agree with me on one silver coin for the day?
**14** Take your pay and head home. It's my choice to give the last worker the same wage I gave you.
**15** Don't I have the right to use my own money however I please? Or are you glaring with jealousy just because I'm generous?'
**16** That's how it will be: the ones in last place will be first, and the ones in first place will end up last."
**17** On the road up to Jerusalem, Jesus pulled the twelve disciples aside privately and told them,
**18** "Listen: we're heading up to Jerusalem right now, and the Son of Man will be betrayed to the chief priests and legal experts. They'll sentence him to death
**19** and hand him over to the Gentiles to be mocked, whipped, and crucified—and on the third day he'll rise again."
**20** Just then, the mother of Zebedee's two sons walked up with her boys, knelt before Jesus, and asked for a favor.
**21** "What would you like?" he asked her. She said, "Promise that when your kingdom comes, these two sons of mine can sit in the places of honor—one at your right side and one at your left."
**22** Jesus answered, "You don't understand what you're asking for. Can you two drink the bitter cup I'm about to drink?" "Yes, we can," they told him.
**23** Jesus replied, "You certainly will drink from my cup. Still, deciding who sits on my right or left isn't mine to hand out—those places belong to the ones my Father has prepared them for."
**24** When the other ten disciples heard about this, they were furious with the two brothers.
**25** Jesus gathered all of them together and said, "You know how Gentile rulers throw their weight around, and how powerful leaders dominate people.
**26** Things must never work that way among you! Instead, whoever wants to be great among you has to serve everyone else,
**27** and whoever wants to be at the top has to act like everyone's slave—
**28** just like the Son of Man didn't come to be waited on, but to serve others and give up his own life as a ransom to free many people."
**29** As they walked out of Jericho, a massive crowd trailed behind him.
**30** Two blind men sitting along the roadside heard that Jesus was passing by and started shouting, "Lord, Son of David, take pity on us!"
**31** The crowd snapped at them to keep quiet, so they only yelled louder, "Lord, Son of David, take pity on us!"
**32** Jesus stopped right where he was, called them over, and asked, "What do you want me to do for you?"
**33** "Lord," they answered, "we want our eyes opened!"
**34** Deeply moved with compassion, Jesus touched their eyes. Instantly they could see, and they started following him.

## Crossings & Lexical Notes

- **v. 2 `δηναρίου` (*dēnariou*):** Roman silver coin (*denarius*), the standard daily wage for an agricultural laborer.
- **v. 15 `ὁ ὀφθαλμός σου πονηρός ἐστιν` (*ho ophthalmos sou ponēros estin*):** Literally "is your eye evil?"—a Semitic idiom (cf. Deut 15:9; Prov 23:6) meaning "stingy, grudging, or envious," contrasted directly with `ἀγαθός` (*agathos*, "good/generous").
- **v. 16 `ἔσχατοι` (*eschatos*):** WH, NA28, and SBLGNT end v. 16 here; Treg and RP add `πολλοὶ γάρ εἰσιν κλητοί, ὀλίγοι δὲ ἐκλεκτοί` ("for many are called, few are chosen"), harmonized from Matt 22:14.
- **v. 22 `τὸ ποτήριον` (*to potērion*):** "The cup" (metaphor for suffering/destiny, cf. Isa 51:17; Matt 26:39). RP adds the parallel clause about baptism from Mark 10:38–39 in both v. 22 and v. 23, absent in WH, Treg, NA28, and SBLGNT.
- **v. 28 `λύτρον ἀντὶ πολλῶν` (*lytron anti pollōn*):** *Lytron* denotes a ransom price paid to release captives or slaves, paired with the preposition *anti* ("in place of / in exchange for").

## Supplemental Texts & Allusions

- **Deuteronomy 15:9** (idiom echoed in v. 15): Warns against letting an "evil eye" (grudging stinginess) harden one's heart toward a needy neighbor.
- **Psalm 75:8; Isaiah 51:17, 22; Jeremiah 25:15** (imagery in vv. 22–23): The "cup" of divine wrath or appointed suffering given to drink.
- **Isaiah 53:10–12** (echoed in v. 28): The Servant of the Lord pours out his soul/life as a guilt offering on behalf of "many."
- **Isaiah 35:5** (echoed in vv. 30–34): Opening the eyes of the blind as a sign of the Messiah's arrival.
