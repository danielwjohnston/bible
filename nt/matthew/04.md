## Clear & Faithful

**1** Then Jesus was led up into the wilderness by the Spirit to be tested by the devil.
**2** And after fasting forty days and forty nights, he became hungry.
**3** The tempter approached him and said, "If you are the Son of God, command these stones to become loaves of bread."
**4** But he answered, "It is written: 'Man shall not live by bread alone, but by every word that comes forth from the mouth of God.'"
**5** Then the devil brought him into the holy city and stood him on the parapet of the temple,
**6** saying to him, "If you are God's Son, hurl yourself down; for Scripture states: 'He will instruct his angels about you,' and 'They will lift you up in their hands so that you do not strike your foot on a stone.'"
**7** Jesus replied to him, "Again it is written: 'You shall not put the Lord your God to the test.'"
**8** Once more the devil took him to a very high mountain and showed him all the kingdoms of the world and their splendor,
**9** and he said to him, "All these things I will give to you, if you fall down and worship me."
**10** Then Jesus said to him, "Be gone, Satan! For it is written: 'You shall worship the Lord your God, and him alone shall you serve.'"
**11** At that point the devil left him, and suddenly angels arrived and began attending to him.
**12** Now when Jesus heard that John had been arrested, he withdrew into Galilee.
**13** Leaving Nazareth, he went and made his home in Capernaum by the sea, in the territory of Zebulun and Naphtali,
**14** so that what had been spoken through the prophet Isaiah might be fulfilled:
**15** "Land of Zebulun and land of Naphtali, along the road to the sea, across the Jordan, Galilee of the Gentiles—
**16** the people sitting in darkness have seen a great light, and upon those sitting in the region and shadow of death, light has dawned."
**17** From that time on Jesus began to proclaim, "Repent, for the kingdom of heaven has drawn near."
**18** As he was walking beside the Sea of Galilee, he saw two brothers, Simon who is called Peter and his brother Andrew, casting a circular net into the sea, for they were fishermen.
**19** He said to them, "Follow me, and I will make you fishers of people."
**20** At once they left their nets behind and followed him.
**21** Moving a little farther along from there, he spotted another pair of brothers, James the son of Zebedee and his brother John, sitting in the boat alongside their father Zebedee repairing their nets; and he summoned them.
**22** At once they left behind both the boat and their father and followed him.
**23** Jesus traveled all around Galilee, teaching inside their synagogues, announcing the good news of the kingdom, and curing every disease and every kind of illness among the people.
**24** News about him spread throughout all Syria, and people brought to him all who were ill—those afflicted with various diseases and severe pains, the demon-possessed, those suffering seizures, and the paralyzed—and he healed them.
**25** Large crowds followed him from Galilee, the Decapolis, Jerusalem, Judea, and from beyond the Jordan.

## Everyday English

**1** Afterward, the Spirit guided Jesus out into the desert to face temptation from the devil.
**2** He went without food for forty days and forty nights, and by the end he was starving.
**3** The tempter came up to him and challenged him, "If you're really God's Son, tell these rocks to turn into loaves of bread."
**4** Jesus answered, "Scripture says, 'People don't survive on bread alone, but on every word that comes straight from God's mouth.'"
**5** Next, the devil carried him to the holy city and set him high up on the edge of the temple roof.
**6** "If you're really God's Son, jump off!" he urged. "After all, Scripture says, 'He'll put his angels in charge of you,' and 'They'll catch you in their hands so you won't even stub your toe on a rock.'"
**7** Jesus shot back, "Scripture also says, 'Never put the Lord your God to the test.'"
**8** A third time, the devil took him up onto an enormously tall mountain and displayed every empire on earth in all its glory.
**9** "I'll hand all of this over to you," he offered, "if you'll just drop to your knees and worship me."
**10** "Get out of here, Satan!" Jesus told him. "Scripture declares, 'Worship the Lord your God, and serve him only.'"
**11** At that, the devil left him alone, and angels came right in to take care of him.
**12** When Jesus got word that John had been locked up in prison, he pulled back into Galilee.
**13** Moving away from Nazareth, he settled in the lakeside town of Capernaum, inside the old regions of Zebulun and Naphtali.
**14** This fulfilled what God had spoken through the prophet Isaiah:
**15** "Territory of Zebulun and Naphtali, along the coastal highway across the Jordan—Galilee where the nations mix—
**16** people living in deep darkness have witnessed a brilliant light, and light has risen over everyone sitting in death's dark shadow."
**17** From that moment forward, Jesus started announcing publicly, "Turn your lives around, because heaven's kingdom has arrived near you!"
**18** One day as he walked along the shore of Lake Galilee, he noticed two brothers—Simon (nicknamed Peter) and Andrew—tossing a fishing net into the water, since they made their living as fishermen.
**19** "Come follow me," Jesus called out to them, "and I'll teach you how to catch people instead of fish."
**20** Right away they dropped their nets and went with him.
**21** Further down the beach, he spotted two more brothers, James and John, the sons of Zebedee. They were sitting in a boat with their father, repairing their nets, and Jesus invited them too.
**22** Right away they left both the boat and their father behind and followed him.
**23** Jesus traveled all across Galilee, teaching inside local synagogues, announcing the good news of God's kingdom, and curing every kind of illness and weakness among the people.
**24** Word about him traveled as far as Syria, and people brought him everyone who was suffering—those racked by all sorts of diseases and intense pain, people tormented by demons, epileptics, and those who were paralyzed—and he cured every one of them.
**25** Huge crowds trailed behind him from Galilee, the Ten Towns, Jerusalem, Judea, and across the Jordan River.

## Crossings & Lexical Notes

- **v. 1 `πειρασθῆναι` (*peirasthēnai*):** Aorist passive infinitive of *peirazō*, a verb spanning both "to test/prove" (loyalty) and "to tempt/entice" (to sin); compare v. 3 *ho peirazōn* ("the tester/tempter").
- **v. 5 `πτερύγιον` (*pterygion*):** Diminutive of *pteryx* ("wing"), denoting a projecting parapet, edge, or wing-like turret high above the temple courts.
- **v. 10 `Ὕπαγε, Σατανᾶ` (*Hypage, Satana*):** SBLGNT, `WH`, `Treg`, and `NA28` read simply "Go away, Satan!"; `RP` adds *opisō mou* ("behind me"), harmonizing with Matt 16:23.
- **v. 10 `λατρεύσεις` (*latreuseis*):** From *latreuō* ("to render cultic/sacred service" or "worship"), distinct from *proskyneō* ("bow down/do homage"); both are reserved exclusively for God.
- **v. 23 `εὐαγγέλιον` (*euangelion*):** "Good news" or royal "gospel proclamation," used in the Greco-Roman world for announcements of victory or a king's accession and here for the arrival of God's reign.
- **v. 24 `σεληνιαζομένους` (*selēniazomenous*):** Literally "moon-struck" (from *selēnē*, "moon"), ancient medical terminology for epileptic or convulsive seizures once linked with lunar cycles.

## Supplemental Texts & Allusions

- **Deuteronomy 8:3; 6:16; 6:13** (quoted in vv. 4, 7, 10): Jesus answers each test from the wilderness-testing chapters of Deuteronomy, echoing Israel's 40 years in the desert.
- **Psalm 91:11–12** (quoted in v. 6): *"He will command his angels concerning you... on their hands they will lift you up so your foot does not strike a stone."*
- **Isaiah 9:1–2 (8:23–9:1 MT)** (quoted in vv. 15–16): Promises that light will dawn on the darkened northern tribes of Zebulun and Naphtali along the Sea road and Galilee of the nations.
