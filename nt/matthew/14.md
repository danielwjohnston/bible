## Clear & Faithful

**1** At that time Herod the tetrarch heard the reports about Jesus
**2** and said to his attendants, "This is John the Baptist; he has been raised from the dead, and that is why miraculous powers are at work in him."
**3** For Herod had arrested John, bound him, and put him away in prison on account of Herodias, the wife of his brother Philip,
**4** because John had been telling him, "It is not lawful for you to have her."
**5** Although Herod wanted to put him to death, he feared the crowd, because they regarded John as a prophet.
**6** Yet when Herod's birthday celebration arrived, the daughter of Herodias danced in the middle of the gathering and pleased Herod,
**7** so that he promised with an oath to give her whatever she might ask.
**8** Prompted beforehand by her mother, she said, "Give me right here on a platter the head of John the Baptist."
**9** Although the king was distressed, because of his oaths and his dinner guests he commanded it to be granted,
**10** and sent word to have John beheaded in the prison.
**11** His head was brought in on a platter and given to the girl, and she carried it to her mother.
**12** Then his disciples came, took away the body, buried him, and went and informed Jesus.
**13** When Jesus heard this, he withdrew from there by boat to a deserted place by himself; yet when the crowds heard of it, they followed him on foot from the towns.
**14** Stepping ashore, he saw a large crowd, was moved with compassion for them, and healed their sick.
**15** When evening arrived, the disciples approached him and said, "This place is desolate and the hour is already late; dismiss the crowds so they may go into the villages and buy food for themselves."
**16** Yet Jesus said to them, "They have no need to go away; you give them something to eat."
**17** They said to him, "We have nothing here except five loaves and two fish."
**18** He told them, "Bring those over here to me."
**19** Then he directed the crowds to sit down on the grass. Taking the five loaves and the two fish, he looked up to heaven, blessed them, broke the loaves, and gave them to the disciples, and the disciples gave them to the crowds.
**20** Everyone ate and was satisfied, and they picked up twelve baskets full of the leftover broken pieces.
**21** Those who ate numbered about five thousand men, besides women and children.
**22** At once Jesus urged his disciples to board the boat and cross ahead of him to the far shore while he sent the crowds away.
**23** After dismissing the crowds, he went up onto the mountain by himself to pray. When evening came, he was there alone.
**24** Meanwhile, the boat was already many stadia from shore, battered by the waves, because the wind was against them.
**25** Around the fourth watch of the night he approached them, walking right across the sea.
**26** When the disciples saw him walking on the sea, they were terrified, crying out, "It is a ghost!"—and out of fear they screamed.
**27** Immediately Jesus spoke to them, saying, "Take courage! It is I; do not be afraid."
**28** Peter called back to him, "Lord, if it really is you, order me to walk out to you across the water."
**29** He said, "Come." Stepping down out of the boat, Peter walked on the water and came toward Jesus.
**30** Yet seeing the fierce wind, he grew afraid, and beginning to sink he cried out, "Lord, save me!"
**31** Instantly Jesus reached out his hand, caught hold of him, and said to him, "O you of little faith, why did you doubt?"
**32** When they climbed into the boat, the wind died down.
**33** And those in the boat bowed down before him, saying, "Truly you are the Son of God!"
**34** After crossing over, they landed at Gennesaret.
**35** When the men of that place recognized him, they sent word throughout that whole surrounding district and brought to him all who were sick,
**36** begging him that they might just touch the tassel of his cloak; and everyone who touched it was completely healed.

## Everyday English

**1** Around that time, King Herod heard the stories spreading about Jesus
**2** and told his court officials, "This must be John the Baptist! He's come back from the dead—that's why miracle-working power is operating through him."
**3** Earlier, Herod had ordered John arrested, chained up, and locked in prison because of Herodias, the wife of Herod's brother Philip.
**4** John had kept confronting him: "It breaks God's law for you to take her as your wife!"
**5** Herod wanted John executed, yet he was afraid of how the public would react, since everyone considered John a prophet.
**6** Then Herod's birthday party came around. Herodias's daughter performed a dance in front of all the guests and thrilled Herod so much
**7** that he swore an oath to hand over anything she requested.
**8** Coached by her mother beforehand, the girl said, "Bring me John the Baptist's head right here on a serving tray!"
**9** The king felt sick about it, yet because of the oath he had sworn in front of his dinner guests, he ordered it done.
**10** He sent an executioner to behead John inside the prison.
**11** John's head was carried out on a tray and handed to the girl, who took it straight to her mother.
**12** Afterward, John's followers came for his body, buried him, and went to break the news to Jesus.
**13** As soon as Jesus heard what had happened, he slipped away by boat to a quiet, deserted spot to be alone. Still, crowds found out and hiked along the shore from the towns to meet him.
**14** When Jesus stepped out and saw the massive crowd, his heart went out to them, and he cured everyone among them who was sick.
**15** Toward evening, his disciples walked over and told him, "We're out in the middle of nowhere and it's getting late. Send the crowds away so they can head into nearby villages and buy themselves dinner."
**16** "They don't need to leave," Jesus replied. "You feed them."
**17** "All we have here," they said, "is five loaves of bread and two small fish!"
**18** "Bring them over to me," Jesus told them.
**19** He had the whole crowd sit down on the grass. Picking up the five loaves and two fish, he looked toward heaven, gave thanks, broke the bread, and handed the pieces to his disciples, who passed them out to the people.
**20** Everyone ate until they were completely full, and afterward the disciples gathered twelve baskets packed with leftovers.
**21** Around five thousand men ate that meal, not even counting all the women and children!
**22** Right after that, Jesus urged his disciples to climb back into the boat and sail ahead of him to the far shore while he sent the crowds home.
**23** Once everyone had left, he hiked up into the hills by himself to pray, and stayed there alone into the night.
**24** Meanwhile, the boat was already miles offshore, getting hammered by high waves because a strong headwind was blowing.
**25** Around three in the morning, Jesus walked out toward them right on top of the water.
**26** When the disciples spotted him striding across the lake, they panicked. "It's a ghost!" they yelled, screaming in terror.
**27** Straightaway Jesus called out to them, "Don't lose heart! It's me—don't be afraid!"
**28** Peter shouted back, "Lord, if it's really you, tell me to walk out to you on the water!"
**29** "Come on," Jesus said. Peter climbed over the side of the boat, walked on top of the lake, and headed toward Jesus.
**30** But when he noticed how fiercely the wind was blowing, terror gripped him. As he started sinking, he yelled, "Lord, rescue me!"
**31** Instantly Jesus shot out his hand, grabbed hold of him, and said, "You have such tiny faith—why did you waver?"
**32** The moment they stepped into the boat, the wind dropped dead calm.
**33** Everyone inside the boat knelt before him and declared, "You really are the Son of God!"
**34** Once they finished crossing, they came ashore at Gennesaret.
**35** Word spread fast as soon as the local people recognized Jesus. They sent runners through the entire countryside and brought him everyone who was sick,
**36** begging him just to let them touch the edge of his coat—and every single person who touched it was completely healed.

## Crossings & Lexical Notes

- **v. 1 `τετραάρχης` (*tetraarchēs*):** Literally "ruler of a fourth part," the official Roman title for Herod Antipas, who governed Galilee and Perea after Herod the Great's kingdom was divided.
- **v. 12 `πτῶμα` (*ptōma*):** "Fallen body/corpse" (`WH`, `Treg`, `NA28`), whereas `RP` reads *sōma* ("body") and shifts the pronoun *auton* ("him") to *auto* ("it").
- **v. 24 `σταδίους πολλοὺς ἀπὸ τῆς γῆς ἀπεῖχεν` (*stadious pollous apo tēs gēs apeichen*):** SBLGNT, `WH`, `Treg`, and `NA28` describe the boat as "many stadia distant from the land" (1 *stadion* ≈ 185 meters), while `RP` reads *meson tēs thalassēs ēn* ("was in the middle of the sea").
- **v. 25 `τετάρτῃ φυλακῇ` (*tetartē phylakē*):** Under the Roman four-watch night division, the fourth watch ran roughly from 3:00 a.m. to 6:00 a.m.
- **v. 27 `ἐγώ εἰμι` (*egō eimi*):** Literally "I am," functioning both as ordinary self-identification ("It is I") and as an echo of God's self-revelation in Exod 3:14 and Isa 43:10–11.
- **v. 30 `ἰσχυρόν` (*ischyron*):** "Strong/fierce" (`Treg`, `NA28`, `RP`), omitted in `WH` and a few early Alexandrian witnesses.
- **v. 31 `ἐδίστασας` (*edistasas*):** Aorist of *distazō* ("to hesitate," "waver," or "be divided in two minds," from *dis*, "twice"), used in the NT only here and in Matt 28:17.

## Supplemental Texts & Allusions

- **2 Kings 4:42–44** (parallel motif in vv. 16–21): Elisha feeds a hundred men with twenty barley loaves and has food left over, prefiguring Jesus's abundant wilderness banquet.
- **Job 9:8 & Psalm 77:19 (76:20 LXX)** (echoed in vv. 25–27): God alone *"strides across the crests of the sea"* and walks a path through mighty waters.
- **Isaiah 41:10, 13; 43:2** (echoed in vv. 27, 31): *"Do not fear, for I am with you... I am the LORD your God who grasps your right hand."*
