## Clear & Faithful

**1** At that point Pharisees and scribes arrived from Jerusalem and approached Jesus, asking,
**2** "Why do your disciples violate the tradition passed down by the elders? They do not wash their hands before eating a meal."
**3** He answered them, "And why do you break the commandment of God for the sake of your tradition?
**4** For God said, 'Honor your father and mother,' and, 'Whoever speaks evil of father or mother must surely be put to death.'
**5** But you say, 'Whoever tells his father or his mother, "Whatever benefit you might have received from me is a dedicated gift,"
**6** need not honor his father.' So you have nullified the word of God for the sake of your tradition.
**7** Hypocrites! Isaiah prophesied rightly about you when he said:
**8** 'This people honors me with their lips, yet their heart stays far away from me;
**9** in vain they worship me, teaching human decrees as doctrines.'"
**10** Then he called the crowd to himself and said to them, "Listen and understand:
**11** it is not what goes into the mouth that defiles a person, but what comes out of the mouth—this is what defiles a person."
**12** Then his disciples approached and said to him, "Do you realize that the Pharisees were offended when they heard that statement?"
**13** He replied, "Every plant that my heavenly Father did not plant will be pulled up by the roots.
**14** Leave them alone. They are blind guides of the blind; and if a blind person leads a blind person, both will fall into a pit."
**15** Peter responded to him, "Explain this parable to us."
**16** Jesus said, "Are even you still without understanding?
**17** Do you not recognize that everything entering the mouth passes into the stomach and is expelled into the latrine?
**18** But the things coming out of the mouth proceed from the heart, and those defile a person.
**19** For out of the heart come evil schemes, murders, adulteries, sexual immorality, thefts, false testimonies, and slanders.
**20** These are the things that defile a person; eating with unwashed hands does not defile a person."
**21** Leaving that place, Jesus withdrew toward the district of Tyre and Sidon.
**22** Suddenly a Canaanite woman from that region came out crying, "Have mercy on me, Lord, Son of David! My daughter is severely tormented by a demon."
**23** Yet he did not answer her a word. His disciples came and urged him, "Send her away, because she keeps shouting after us."
**24** He answered, "I was sent only to the lost sheep of the house of Israel."
**25** Still she came and knelt before him, pleading, "Lord, help me!"
**26** He replied, "It is not right to take the children's bread and throw it to the house dogs."
**27** She said, "Yes, Lord, yet even the house dogs eat the crumbs that fall from their masters' table."
**28** Then Jesus answered her, "Woman, your faith is great! Let it happen for you just as you desire." And her daughter was healed from that very hour.
**29** Moving on from there, Jesus arrived beside the Sea of Galilee, went up onto the mountain, and sat down there.
**30** Large crowds came to him, bringing with them the deaf and mute, the blind, the lame, the crippled, and many others. They laid them at his feet, and he healed them,
**31** so that the crowd marveled as they saw the mute speaking, the crippled restored to health, the lame walking, and the blind seeing; and they praised the God of Israel.
**32** Then Jesus summoned his disciples and said, "My heart goes out to this crowd, because they have already stayed with me three days and have nothing to eat. I do not want to send them away hungry, lest they collapse along the road."
**33** His disciples asked him, "Where in this desolate place could we get enough loaves to feed such a large crowd?"
**34** Jesus asked them, "How many loaves do you have?" They answered, "Seven, and a few small fish."
**35** Directing the crowd to sit down on the ground,
**36** he took the seven loaves and the fish, gave thanks, broke them, and kept giving them to the disciples, and the disciples to the crowds.
**37** Everyone ate and was satisfied, and they picked up seven large baskets full of the leftover fragments.
**38** Those who ate numbered four thousand men, besides women and children.
**39** After sending the crowds away, he boarded the boat and went to the region of Magadan.

## Everyday English

**1** At that time, some Pharisees and teachers of the Law arrived from Jerusalem and confronted Jesus:
**2** "Why are your followers ignoring the traditions passed down by our elders? They don't even wash their hands before eating!"
**3** Jesus shot back, "And why do you ignore God's own command just to protect your traditions?
**4** God commanded, 'Respect your father and mother,' and 'Anyone who curses their father or mother must die.'
**5** But you tell people they can say to their parents, 'Sorry, whatever help I might have given you is already dedicated to God,'
**6** and then they don't have to support their father at all! You've canceled out God's message to keep your own rules going.
**7** What a show of hypocrisy! Isaiah nailed it when he prophesied about people like you:
**8** 'These people pay me lip service, while their hearts couldn't be farther from me.
**9** Their worship is empty because they pass off human rules as God's teaching.'"
**10** Then Jesus called the crowd closer and told them, "Listen closely and get this straight:
**11** what goes into your mouth doesn't make you spiritually unclean—it's what comes out of your mouth that makes you unclean."
**12** Later, his disciples pulled him aside and asked, "Did you notice how offended the Pharisees were by what you said?"
**13** Jesus answered, "Every plant my Father in heaven didn't plant himself will be ripped up by the roots.
**14** Forget about them. They're blind guides leading blind followers—and when someone who can't see leads another person who can't see, both end up in a ditch."
**15** Peter spoke up, "Explain that saying to us."
**16** "Are you still not getting it?" Jesus asked.
**17** "Don't you see that anything you swallow goes straight to your stomach and eventually leaves your body?
**18** What comes out of your mouth, though, starts in your heart—and that's what truly contaminates a person.
**19** Wicked thoughts, murder, adultery, sexual sin, theft, lying in court, and slander all pour out of the human heart.
**20** Those are the things that make someone unclean, not eating without a ceremonial handwashing."
**21** After leaving that area, Jesus traveled toward the region around Tyre and Sidon.
**22** A local Canaanite woman came out to meet him, shouting, "Show me mercy, Lord, Son of David! My daughter is horribly tormented by an evil spirit."
**23** Jesus didn't say a word in reply. His disciples stepped in and begged him, "Tell her to leave—she's following us and yelling nonstop!"
**24** Jesus responded, "My mission is only to the lost sheep among Israel's people."
**25** Even so, the woman came right up, knelt at his feet, and pleaded, "Lord, please help me!"
**26** Jesus told her, "It isn't right to take food meant for the children and toss it to the household dogs."
**27** "True, Lord," she replied, "but even the puppies get to eat the scraps that drop off their owners' table."
**28** Jesus looked at her and said, "Woman, your trust is remarkable! You'll have exactly what you're asking for." At that very moment, her daughter was healed.
**29** Leaving there, Jesus walked back along the Sea of Galilee, climbed a hillside, and sat down.
**30** Huge crowds gathered around him, bringing people who couldn't speak or hear, people who were blind, lame, or crippled, along with many others. They placed them right at his feet, and he healed every one of them.
**31** The crowd couldn't believe their eyes—people who couldn't speak were talking, crippled limbs were whole again, those who couldn't walk were striding around, and the blind had sight! And everyone praised the God of Israel.
**32** Jesus called his disciples over and said, "I deeply feel for this crowd. They've stayed with me three whole days now and have run out of food. I don't want to send them home hungry, or they'll collapse on the way back."
**33** His disciples replied, "Where on earth could we find enough bread out here in the middle of nowhere to feed a crowd this size?"
**34** "How many loaves do you have?" Jesus asked. "Seven," they said, "plus a few small fish."
**35** Jesus had everyone sit down on the ground.
**36** He took the seven loaves and the fish, thanked God for them, broke them into pieces, and kept handing them to the disciples to distribute to the crowd.
**37** Everyone ate until they were completely full, and afterward they collected seven large baskets packed with leftovers.
**38** Four thousand men ate that day, not counting all the women and children.
**39** Once Jesus sent the crowds on their way, he got into the boat and sailed across to the territory of Magadan.

## Crossings & Lexical Notes

- **v. 5 `Δῶρον` (*dōron*):** Literally "gift" or "offering," corresponding to Aramaic/Hebrew *qorban* (cf. Mark 7:11), a vow dedicating property to temple use so that it could be withheld from parental support.
- **v. 6 `τὸν λόγον` (*ton logon*):** "The word" of God (supported by WH, Treg, NA28); RP reads `τὴν ἐντολὴν` ("the commandment"), harmonizing with v. 3.
- **v. 11 `κοινοῖ` (*koinoi*):** From *koinoō*, "to make common, profane, or ritually impure," contrasting ceremonial handwashing (v. 2) with moral defilement from the heart (vv. 18–19).
- **v. 26 `κυναρίοις` (*kynariois*):** Diminutive of *kyōn* ("dog"), denoting household pets/puppies rather than wild street dogs (*kynes*), softening the metaphor while preserving the salvation-historical priority of Israel (v. 24).
- **v. 30 `κωφούς, τυφλούς, χωλούς, κυλλούς` (*kōphous, typhlous, chōlous, kyllous*):** "Deaf/mute, blind, lame, crippled"; *kōphos* spans both "deaf" and "mute" (here clearly paired with "speaking" in v. 31). Manuscript traditions permute the order of these four adjectives, and WH omits `κυλλοὺς ὑγιεῖς` ("crippled [made] whole") in v. 31.
- **v. 37 `σπυρίδας` (*spyridas*):** Large woven hampers/baskets (used in the feeding of the 4,000), distinct from the twelve smaller wicker baskets (*kophinoi*) in the feeding of the 5,000 (Matt 14:20; cf. Matt 16:9–10).
- **v. 39 `Μαγαδάν` (*Magadan*):** Read by WH, Treg, and NA28; later Byzantine/RP manuscripts substitute the more familiar `Μαγδαλά` (*Magdala*).

## Supplemental Texts & Allusions

- **Exodus 20:12; 21:17** (quoted in v. 4): *"Give due respect to your father and mother"* and *"Anyone who reviles father or mother must surely face death."*
- **Isaiah 29:13** (quoted from LXX in vv. 8–9): *"This people draws near with their mouth and honors me with their lips, while their heart stays far from me; their reverence toward me has become a human rule learned by rote."*
- **Isaiah 35:5–6** (echoed in vv. 30–31): Promises that in God's restorative reign the eyes of the blind will open, the deaf will hear, the lame will leap, and the mute tongue will shout for joy.
- **2 Kings 4:42–44** (echoed in vv. 32–38): Elisha's miraculous multiplication of barley loaves so that a hundred men eat and leave leftovers behind.
