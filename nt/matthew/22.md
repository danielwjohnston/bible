## Clear & Faithful

**1** Jesus answered them again in parables, saying:
**2** "The kingdom of heaven may be compared to a king who prepared a wedding banquet for his son.
**3** He sent his servants to summon those who had been invited to the wedding banquet, yet they refused to come.
**4** Again he sent other servants, instructing them, 'Tell those who are invited, "Look, I have prepared my feast; my oxen and fattened calves have been slaughtered, and everything is ready. Come to the wedding banquet."'
**5** Yet they paid no attention and went away—one to his own farm, another to his business—
**6** while the rest seized his servants, mistreated them, and killed them.
**7** Then the king grew furious; he sent his troops, destroyed those murderers, and burned down their city.
**8** At that point he told his servants, 'The marriage feast stands ready, yet those who were invited proved unworthy.
**9** Go therefore to the main crossroads of the highways, and invite everyone you find to the wedding banquet.'
**10** Those servants went out onto the roads and gathered together everyone they found, both bad and good; and the wedding hall was filled with reclining guests.
**11** Yet when the king came in to look over the guests, he saw a man there who was not wearing a wedding garment.
**12** He said to him, 'Friend, how did you get in here without a wedding garment?' And the man was speechless.
**13** Then the king commanded the attendants, 'Bind him hand and foot and throw him out into the outer darkness, where there will be weeping and gnashing of teeth.'
**14** For many are called, yet few are chosen."
**15** Then the Pharisees went out and plotted how they might trap him in his own words.
**16** So they dispatched their own disciples along with the Herodians to tell him, "Teacher, we recognize that you are truthful, that you genuinely teach God's way, and that you curry favor with no one, since you pay no regard to human status.
**17** Tell us then what you think: is it lawful to pay the poll tax to Caesar, or not?"
**18** Perceiving their malice, Jesus said, "Why are you testing me, you hypocrites?
**19** Show me the coin used for the tax." So they brought him a denarius.
**20** He asked them, "Whose image is this, and whose inscription?"
**21** They answered him, "Caesar's." Then he told them, "Give back to Caesar the things that belong to Caesar, and to God the things that belong to God."
**22** When they heard this, they were amazed, and leaving him, they went away.
**23** That same day, Sadducees—who say there is no resurrection—came to him and questioned him:
**24** "Teacher, Moses said that if a man dies without having children, his brother must marry the widow and raise up offspring for his brother.
**25** Among us there once were seven brothers. The oldest took a wife and passed away, and leaving no descendants, he left his wife behind for his brother.
**26** The same happened to the second and the third, right down to the seventh.
**27** Last of all, the woman died.
**28** In the resurrection, then, whose wife of the seven will she be? For all of them had her."
**29** Jesus answered them, "You are mistaken because you know neither the Scriptures nor the power of God.
**30** Because in the resurrection people neither marry nor are given in marriage; instead, they are just like God's angels in heaven.
**31** And as for the resurrection of the dead, have you not read what was spoken to you by God, saying:
**32** 'I am Abraham's God, Isaac's God, and Jacob's God'? God is not God over the dead, rather over the living."
**33** When the crowds heard this, they were astonished at his teaching.
**34** Now when the Pharisees heard that he had silenced the Sadducees, they gathered together in one group.
**35** One of them, an expert in the law, asked him a question to test him:
**36** "Teacher, which commandment in the law is greatest?"
**37** Jesus answered him, "'Love the Lord your God with your whole heart, your whole soul, and your whole mind.'
**38** This is the greatest and foremost commandment.
**39** And a second stands alongside it: 'Love your neighbor just as yourself.'
**40** On these two commandments hang the entire law and the prophets."
**41** While the Pharisees were still gathered together, Jesus questioned them,
**42** asking, "What do you think about the Messiah? Whose son is he?" They answered him, "David's."
**43** He asked them, "How then does David, inspired by the Spirit, call him 'Lord,' saying:
**44** 'The Lord said to my Lord, "Sit at my right hand until I place your enemies beneath your feet"'?
**45** If David therefore calls him 'Lord,' how is he his son?"
**46** No one could answer him a single word, nor did anyone dare from that day on to ask him any more questions.

## Everyday English

**1** Once again, Jesus used stories to answer them:
**2** "Here's what heaven's kingdom is like: a king threw a grand wedding feast for his son.
**3** He sent his servants out to tell the invited guests it was time to come, yet they refused to show up.
**4** So he sent a second wave of servants with a message: 'Tell everyone on the guest list, "Look, the banquet is ready! My bulls and prize cattle have been butchered, and everything is set. Come to the wedding feast!"'
**5** Still, they brushed it off and went about their lives—one headed out to his farm, another to his shop—
**6** while the rest grabbed the king's servants, beat them brutally, and murdered them.
**7** The king was outraged. He sent out his soldiers, wiped out those murderers, and burned their city to the ground.
**8** Then he told his servants, 'The wedding feast is ready, though the people I originally invited proved unworthy of it.
**9** Head out to the busy street corners and invite everyone you meet to the wedding!'
**10** The servants walked out into the streets and brought in everybody they ran across—both bad people and good people—until the banquet hall was packed with guests.
**11** When the king stepped in to greet the guests, he noticed a man who wasn't dressed in wedding clothes.
**12** 'My friend,' the king asked him, 'how did you walk in here without proper wedding attire?' The man didn't have a word to say.
**13** So the king instructed his attendants, 'Tie his hands and feet and throw him outside into the pitch darkness, where people weep and grind their teeth.'
**14** Remember: many receive the invitation, yet only a few are chosen."
**15** After that, the Pharisees walked off and huddled to scheme how they might trap Jesus with his own words.
**16** They sent a group of their disciples along with some Herodians to flatter him: "Teacher, we know you're an honest man who teaches God's way truthfully. You don't bend for anyone or play favorites based on status.
**17** So tell us your opinion: is it right under God's Law to pay imperial taxes to Caesar, or not?"
**18** Seeing right through their malice, Jesus said, "You hypocrites! Why are you trying to bait me?
**19** Hand me the coin used to pay the tax." They passed him a Roman denarius.
**20** "Whose portrait is stamped on this, and whose title?" Jesus asked.
**21** "Caesar's," they answered. "Well then," Jesus said, "pay back to Caesar what belongs to Caesar, and give to God what belongs to God!"
**22** His answer left them stunned; they walked away and left him alone.
**23** That very same day, some Sadducees—who deny there is any resurrection—came to challenge Jesus with a puzzle:
**24** "Teacher, Moses wrote that if a married man dies without leaving children, his brother is obligated to marry his widow and carry on his family line.
**25** Suppose there were seven brothers right here among us. The oldest got married and died with no children, leaving his wife to the next brother.
**26** The same thing happened with the second brother, and the third, all the way through all seven!
**27** Finally, the woman herself died too.
**28** So in the resurrection, which of the seven brothers gets her as his wife, since every single one of them was married to her?"
**29** Jesus replied, "You're completely off track because you neither understand the Scriptures nor grasp God's power!
**30** Once the dead are raised, people don't get married or give each other in marriage; instead, they're like God's angels in heaven.
**31** As for whether the dead rise again, haven't you read what God himself told you in Scripture?
**32** 'I am the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob.' He isn't the God of dead corpses—he's the God of the living!"
**33** The crowds listening to this were amazed by his teaching.
**34** When the Pharisees heard how Jesus had shut the Sadducees down, they grouped together again.
**35** One of them, a legal scholar, tried to trip him up with another question:
**36** "Teacher, which command in the Law is the most important of all?"
**37** Jesus answered him, "'Love the Lord your God with every bit of your heart, your soul, and your mind.'
**38** That's the greatest and number-one commandment.
**39** And the second most important is right alongside it: 'Love your neighbor the same way you love yourself.'
**40** Every single command in the Law and the Prophets hangs on those two!"
**41** While the Pharisees were still gathered around, Jesus turned the tables and asked them:
**42** "What do you think about the Messiah? Whose descendant is he?" "David's," they replied.
**43** "Then how can David, speaking under the Spirit's inspiration, call the Messiah 'Lord'?" Jesus asked. "Remember what David wrote:
**44** 'The Lord said to my Lord: Sit at my right side until I put all your enemies beneath your feet.'
**45** If David himself calls him 'Lord,' how could he simply be David's son?"
**46** Nobody could think of a single word to reply, and from that day forward, nobody dared try to quiz him again.

## Crossings & Lexical Notes

- **v. 12 `ἐφιμώθη` (*ephimōthē*):** Literally "was muzzled" (aorist passive of *phimoō*, repeated in v. 34 where Jesus "muzzled" the Sadducees), evoking complete, stunned silence.
- **v. 14 `κλητοὶ … ἐκλεκτοί` (*klētoi … eklektoi*):** Wordplay between *klētoi* ("invited/called," picking up *keklēmenoi* in vv. 3–4, 8) and *eklektoi* ("chosen/selected").
- **v. 17 `κῆνσον` (*kēnson*):** Latin loanword (*census*), denoting the Roman provincial poll-tax paid in silver denarii (`δηνάριον`, v. 19) bearing the emperor's portrait (`εἰκών`) and title (`ἐπιγραφή`).
- **v. 24 `ἐπιγαμβρεύσει` (*epigambreusei*):** Technical Septuagint verb for fulfilling the levirate marriage duty (Deut 25:5), whereby a brother marries his deceased brother's childless widow to preserve his line.
- **v. 30 `ἄγγελοι θεοῦ` (*angeloi theou*):** SBLGNT prints `ἄγγελοι θεοῦ` ("angels of God"), following Holmes/Alexandrian evidence; RP reads `ἄγγελοι τοῦ θεοῦ`, whereas WH, Treg, and NA28 read simply `ἄγγελοι` ("angels").
- **v. 37 `διανοίᾳ` (*dianoia*):** "Mind, understanding, faculty of thought"—here Matthew expands Deut 6:5's triad (heart, soul, strength) using *dianoia* alongside *kardia* (heart) and *psychē* (soul).

## Supplemental Texts & Allusions

- **Deuteronomy 25:5–6** (paraphrased in v. 24): Requires a surviving brother to marry his childless brother's widow to raise up an heir in the deceased brother's name.
- **Exodus 3:6** (quoted in v. 32): *"I am the God who watched over your ancestor—Abraham's God, Isaac's God, and Jacob's God."*
- **Deuteronomy 6:5; Leviticus 19:18** (quoted in vv. 37, 39): *"Love the Lord your God with every part of your heart and soul..."* paired with *"Show love to your neighbor as you do to yourself."*
- **Psalm 110:1** (quoted in v. 44): *"The Lord declared to my Lord, 'Take your seat at my right side until I place your foes beneath your feet.'"*
