## Clear & Faithful

**1** "At that time the kingdom of heaven will be like ten bridesmaids who took their lamps and went out to meet the bridegroom.
**2** Five among them were senseless, and five were discerning.
**3** When the foolish ones took their lamps, they did not take extra oil with them;
**4** yet the wise ones took oil in flasks along with their lamps.
**5** As the bridegroom was delayed, all of them grew drowsy and fell asleep.
**6** At midnight a shout rang out: 'Look, the bridegroom! Come out to meet him!'
**7** Then all those bridesmaids woke up and trimmed their lamps.
**8** The foolish ones said to the wise ones, 'Give us some of your oil, because our lamps are going out.'
**9** Yet the wise ones answered, 'No, lest there not be enough for both us and you; go instead to the merchants and buy some for yourselves.'
**10** While they were off making their purchase, the bridegroom arrived; those who were prepared entered with him into the wedding feast, and the door was locked.
**11** Later the rest of the bridesmaids also arrived, pleading, 'Lord, lord, open the door for us!'
**12** Yet he answered, 'Truly I tell you, I do not know you.'
**13** Stay alert, therefore, because you know neither the day nor the hour.
**14** "For it is just like a man about to travel abroad who summoned his own servants and entrusted his property to them.
**15** To one he gave five talents, to another two, and to another one—to each according to his own ability—and then he went abroad. At once
**16** the one who had received the five talents went, put them to work, and earned five more.
**17** Likewise, the one with the two talents earned two more.
**18** Yet the one who had received the single talent went off, dug a hole in the ground, and hid his master's silver.
**19** After a long time, the master of those servants returned and settled accounts with them.
**20** The one who had received five talents approached and presented another five talents, saying, 'Master, you entrusted five talents to me; look, I have earned five more talents.'
**21** His master told him, 'Well done, good and faithful servant! You were faithful over a little; I will put you in charge of much. Enter into the joy of your master.'
**22** Next, the one with the two talents approached and said, 'Master, you entrusted two talents to me; look, I have earned two more talents.'
**23** His master told him, 'Well done, good and faithful servant! You were faithful over a little; I will put you in charge of much. Enter into the joy of your master.'
**24** Then the one who had received the single talent also approached and said, 'Master, I knew you to be a hard man, reaping where you did not sow and gathering where you did not scatter seed;
**25** so I was afraid, went off, and hid your talent in the ground. Look, here is what belongs to you.'
**26** His master answered him, 'You wicked and lazy servant! So you knew that I reap where I did not sow and gather where I did not scatter?
**27** Then you ought to have deposited my money with the bankers, so that when I returned I would have received back my own with interest.
**28** Take the talent away from him, therefore, and give it to the one who has the ten talents.
**29** Because to everyone who already has, even more will be granted so that they have abundance; yet from anyone lacking, even what they possess will be stripped away.
**30** Throw that worthless servant out into the outer darkness, where there will be weeping and gnashing of teeth.'
**31** "When the Son of Man comes in his glory, and all the angels with him, then he will sit upon his glorious throne.
**32** Before him every nation will assemble, and he will sort them out from one another, just as a shepherd divides sheep from goats;
**33** he will place the sheep at his right hand and the goats at his left.
**34** At that point the King will address those at his right hand, 'Come, you whom my Father has blessed—take possession of the kingdom prepared for you ever since the world's creation.
**35** For I was hungry and you gave me food; I was thirsty and you gave me something to drink; I was a stranger and you welcomed me in;
**36** I was naked and you clothed me; I was sick and you cared for me; I was in prison and you visited me.'
**37** Then the righteous will reply to him, 'Lord, when did we ever notice you starving and feed you, or parched and give you a drink?
**38** When did we ever notice you as a foreigner and take you in, or unclothed and dress you?
**39** When did we ever notice you ill or imprisoned and come to see you?'
**40** And the King will answer them, 'Truly I tell you, whatever you did for one of the least of these brothers of mine, you did for me.'
**41** Then he will also say to those at his left hand, 'Depart from me, you accursed ones, into the eternal fire prepared for the devil and his angels.
**42** For I was hungry and you gave me nothing to eat; I was thirsty and you gave me nothing to drink;
**43** I was a foreigner and you failed to welcome me; unclothed and you failed to dress me; ill and imprisoned and you failed to look after me.'
**44** Then they also will reply, 'Lord, when did we ever notice you starving or parched, a foreigner, unclothed, ill, or imprisoned, and fail to care for you?'
**45** Then he will answer them, 'Truly I tell you, whatever you failed to do for one of the least of these, you failed to do for me.'
**46** And these will depart into eternal punishment, whereas the righteous will enter eternal life."

## Everyday English

**1** "Here's what heaven's kingdom will be like on that day: ten young women grabbed their oil lamps and went out to welcome the groom.
**2** Five of them were careless, and five thought ahead.
**3** The careless ones carried their lamps without packing any extra oil,
**4** whereas the ones who thought ahead packed jars of oil alongside their lamps.
**5** Because the groom took a long time arriving, all ten got sleepy and dozed off.
**6** Then right in the middle of the night someone shouted, 'Wake up! The groom is here! Come out to meet him!'
**7** All the young women jumped up and got their lamps ready.
**8** The careless ones turned to the wise ones and begged, 'Share some of your oil with us—our lamps are flickering out!'
**9** 'We can't,' the wise ones replied, 'or there won't be enough for either you or us. Hurry over to the shops and buy some for yourselves!'
**10** While they were out shopping, the groom arrived. Those who were ready walked straight in with him to the wedding celebration, and the door was locked behind them.
**11** Afterward, the other young women showed up knocking: 'Sir, sir, let us in!'
**12** But he answered from inside, 'Believe me, I don't even know who you are.'
**13** So keep your eyes open, because you don't know the day or the hour!
**14** "It's also like a wealthy man heading out on a trip who called his servants together and put them in charge of his estate.
**15** He handed five bags of silver to one, two bags to another, and one bag to a third—matching each person's ability—and then left on his journey. Right away,
**16** the servant given five bags put the money to work and doubled it, earning five more.
**17** The servant given two bags did the same and earned two more.
**18** But the servant given just one bag walked out, dug a pit in the dirt, and buried his master's money.
**19** A long time later, the master returned from his trip and asked those servants for a full accounting.
**20** The servant who'd received five bags stepped forward holding five extra bags and said, 'Master, you trusted me with five bags of silver—look, I made five more!'
**21** His master smiled and said, 'Awesome job, you good and trustworthy servant! Since you proved faithful with a smaller amount, I'm putting you in charge of much bigger things. Come share your master's celebration!'
**22** Next, the servant who'd received two bags stepped up and said, 'Master, you trusted me with two bags—look, I made two more!'
**23** His master said, 'Awesome job, you good and trustworthy servant! Since you proved faithful with a smaller amount, I'm putting you in charge of much bigger things. Come share your master's celebration!'
**24** Finally, the servant who'd received one bag walked up and said, 'Master, I knew you're a tough businessman who harvests crops you never planted and collects grain where you never tossed seed.
**25** Frankly, I was scared, so I went and buried your silver underground. Here—take back what's yours.'
**26** His master shot back, 'You wicked, lazy servant! So you knew I harvest where I didn't plant and collect where I didn't sow, did you?
**27** Then the least you could've done was put my money in the bank, so when I got back I could've collected it with interest!
**28** Take that bag of silver away from him and hand it to the servant who has ten!
**29** Everyone who makes use of what they're given will be given even more until they overflow—but whoever does nothing will have even what little they hold stripped away.
**30** Now throw that good-for-nothing servant outside into the dark, where people weep and grind their teeth.'
**31** "When the Son of Man arrives in blazing splendor with all his angels, he'll take his seat on his glorious royal throne.
**32** Every nation on earth will be gathered in front of him, and he'll sort the people into two groups, just like a shepherd separates sheep from goats—
**33** putting the sheep on his right side and the goats on his left.
**34** Then the King will tell the people on his right, 'Come! You're the ones my Father has blessed! Step into the kingdom prepared for you ever since the world began!
**35** When I was starving, you fed me. When I was parched, you gave me water. When I was a stranger far from home, you invited me in.
**36** When I had no clothes, you dressed me. When I was sick, you looked after me. When I was locked in prison, you came to visit me.'
**37** Puzzled, the righteous people will ask him, 'Lord, when did we ever see you starving and feed you, or thirsty and give you something to drink?
**38** When did we ever see you as a stranger and invite you in, or without clothes and dress you?
**39** When did we ever see you sick or in jail and come visit you?'
**40** And the King will tell them, 'Believe me: every time you did it for even the most overlooked person in my family, you did it for me.'
**41** Then he'll turn to the people on his left and say, 'Get away from me, you cursed people, into the eternal fire prepared for the devil and his angels!
**42** When I was starving, you didn't give me a bite to eat. When I was parched, you didn't offer me a sip of water.
**43** When I was a stranger, you shut your doors on me. When I needed clothes, you gave me nothing. When I was sick and in prison, you never visited me.'
**44** They'll answer right back, 'Lord, when did we ever see you starving or thirsty or a stranger or needing clothes or sick or in jail and refuse to help you?'
**45** He'll answer them, 'Believe me: every time you refused to help even the most overlooked person here, you refused to help me.'
**46** So those people will walk away into eternal punishment, while the righteous step into eternal life."

## Crossings & Lexical Notes

- **v. 1 `λαμπάδας` (*lampadas*):** Oil-fed torches or lamps with small oil reservoirs that required frequent refilling from extra flasks (`ἀγγείοις`, v. 4) and trimming (`ἐκόσμησαν`, v. 7).
- **v. 13 `ὥραν` (*hōran*):** SBLGNT, WH, Treg, and NA28 end the verse at `ὥραν`; RP appends `ἐν ᾗ ὁ υἱὸς τοῦ ἀνθρώπου ἔρχεται` ("in which the Son of Man comes").
- **v. 15 `τάλαντα` (*talanta*):** A talent was a weight of silver (roughly 6,000 denarii—nearly twenty years' wages for a day laborer), underscoring the enormous trust placed in each servant.
- **v. 27 `τραπεζίταις … σὺν τόκῳ` (*trapezitais … syn tokō*):** Literally "table-keepers" (money-changers/bankers who sat at tables, *trapezai*, in the market) and "with interest/yield" (*tokos*).
- **v. 46 `κόλασιν αἰώνιον … ζωὴν αἰώνιον` (*kolasin aiōnion … zōēn aiōnion*):** Both destinies share the exact same adjective `αἰώνιον` (*aiōnion*, "eternal/age-long"); *kolasis* originally meant "pruning" or "corrective punishment," later becoming the standard term for judicial penalty.

## Supplemental Texts & Allusions

- **Amos 5:18–20; Zephaniah 1:14–16** (background to vv. 1–13): Prophetic warnings about the sudden arrival of the Day of the Lord and the contrast between darkness and light.
- **Ezekiel 34:17–22** (echoed in vv. 31–33): *"Look, I myself will judge between one sheep and another, and between rams and male goats."*
- **Isaiah 58:6–7; Job 22:6–7** (echoed in vv. 35–36): Covenant care shown by sharing bread with the hungry, welcoming the homeless, and clothing the naked.
- **Daniel 12:2** (echoed in v. 46): *"Multitudes sleeping in the dusty earth will awaken—some to eternal life, others to disgrace and eternal abhorrence."*
