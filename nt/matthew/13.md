## Clear & Faithful

**1** On that very day Jesus stepped out of the house and sat down along the seashore.
**2** Such great crowds gathered around him that he stepped into a boat and sat down, while the whole crowd stood on the shore.
**3** And he told them many things in parables, saying: "Picture a farmer who went out to sow.
**4** As he sowed, some seeds fell along the path, and birds came and devoured them.
**5** Other seeds dropped onto rocky ground where there was little earth; they sprouted quickly because the soil lacked depth,
**6** yet when the sun rose they were scorched, and because they had no root they withered away.
**7** Still other seeds fell among thorns, and the thorns shot up and suffocated them.
**8** Still other seeds fell on good soil and kept yielding fruit—some a hundredfold, some sixty, and some thirty.
**9** Whoever has ears, let him hear."
**10** Then the disciples approached and asked him, "Why do you speak to them in parables?"
**11** He answered them, "To you it has been granted to know the mysteries of the kingdom of heaven, yet to them it has not been granted.
**12** For whoever has, more will be given to him, and he will have an abundance; yet whoever does not have, even what he has will be taken away from him.
**13** For this reason I speak to them in parables, because seeing they do not see, and hearing they do not hear, nor do they understand.
**14** And in their case the prophecy of Isaiah is completely fulfilled, which says: 'You will indeed hear yet never understand, and you will indeed see yet never perceive.
**15** For the heart of this people has grown dull, their ears scarcely hear, and they have shut their eyes—lest they should see with their eyes, hear with their ears, understand with their heart, and turn back, and I would heal them.'
**16** Yet how favored are your eyes because they perceive, and your ears because they listen!
**17** For truly I tell you that many prophets and righteous people longed to see what you see and did not see it, and to hear what you hear and did not hear it.
**18** Hear then the parable of the sower.
**19** Whenever anyone hears the message of the kingdom without grasping it, the evil one comes and steals away what was planted in his heart; that is the seed sown beside the road.
**20** And what was sown on rocky ground—this is the one who hears the word and immediately receives it with joy,
**21** yet he has no root in himself and endures only for a season; when affliction or persecution arises because of the word, immediately he falls away.
**22** And what was sown among thorns—this is the one who hears the word, yet the anxiety of the age and the deceitfulness of wealth choke the word, and it becomes unfruitful.
**23** Yet what was sown on good soil—this is the one who hears the word and understands it; he indeed bears fruit and produces—one a hundredfold, another sixty, and another thirty."
**24** He put another parable before them, saying, "The kingdom of heaven may be compared to a man who sowed good seed in his field.
**25** While everyone was sleeping, his enemy came, sowed darnel weeds amidst the wheat, and went away.
**26** When the grain sprouted and formed heads, then the darnel weeds appeared as well.
**27** So the master's slaves approached and said to him, 'Sir, did you not sow good seed in your field? Where then did the darnel come from?'
**28** He said to them, 'An enemy has done this.' The slaves asked him, 'Do you want us to go and pull them up?'
**29** But he said, 'No, lest in pulling up the darnel you uproot the wheat along with it.
**30** Let both grow together until the harvest; and at harvest time I will tell the reapers, "First gather the darnel and tie it in bundles to be burned, then gather the wheat into my barn."'"
**31** He set another parable before them: "The kingdom of heaven resembles a mustard seed that a farmer took and planted in his field.
**32** Though it is smaller than all the other seeds, when it has grown it is larger than the garden plants and becomes a tree, so that the birds of the sky come and nest in its branches."
**33** He spoke another parable to them: "The kingdom of heaven is like yeast that a woman took and mixed into three measures of flour until all of it was leavened."
**34** Jesus spoke all these things to the crowds in parables, and he did not say anything to them without a parable,
**35** so that what had been spoken through the prophet might be fulfilled: "I will open my mouth in parables; I will proclaim things kept secret from the foundation."
**36** Then he left the crowds and went inside the house. His disciples came to him and said, "Explain to us the parable of the darnel in the field."
**37** He answered, "The one who sows the good seed is the Son of Man;
**38** the field represents the world; the good seed represents the people of the kingdom; the darnel weeds represent the people of the evil one;
**39** the enemy who planted them is the devil; the harvest represents the culmination of the age; and the reapers are the angels.
**40** Therefore, just as the darnel is gathered and burned with fire, so will it be at the close of the age.
**41** The Son of Man will dispatch his angels to collect out of his kingdom every snare of sin and all who practice lawlessness,
**42** and throw them into the fiery furnace; there will be weeping and gnashing of teeth.
**43** Then the righteous will blaze forth like the sun inside their Father's kingdom. Anyone who has ears, let him hear.
**44** The kingdom of heaven is like a treasure hidden in a field, which a man found and hid again; out of his joy he goes and sells everything he owns and buys that field.
**45** Again, the kingdom of heaven is like a merchant searching for fine pearls;
**46** having found one pearl of great value, he went and sold everything he possessed and bought it.
**47** Again, the kingdom of heaven is like a dragnet cast into the sea that gathered fish of every kind.
**48** When it was full, they hauled it ashore, sat down, and sorted the good into baskets, while throwing the rotten ones away.
**49** So will it be at the close of the age: the angels will go out and separate the wicked from among the righteous,
**50** and throw them into the fiery furnace; there will be weeping and gnashing of teeth.
**51** Have you understood all these things?" They answered him, "Yes."
**52** Then he said to them, "Therefore every scribe who has been made a disciple of the kingdom of heaven is like a householder who brings out of his storeroom things both new and old."
**53** Once Jesus had concluded all these parables, he moved on from that place.
**54** Arriving in his hometown, he taught the people inside their synagogue, leaving them amazed and asking, "Where did this man acquire such wisdom and these miraculous powers?
**55** Is he not the builder's son? Is his mother not named Mary, and are his brothers not James, Joseph, Simon, and Judas?
**56** And do all his sisters not live right here among us? Where then did he obtain all these things?"
**57** And they stumbled in offense at him. Yet Jesus told them, "A prophet never lacks honor except in his own hometown and inside his own household."
**58** Because of their lack of faith, he performed very few mighty deeds there.

## Everyday English

**1** Later that same day, Jesus stepped outside the house and sat down by the lakeshore.
**2** So many people crowded around him that he climbed into a boat and sat there while everyone stood listening along the beach.
**3** He taught them many lessons using parables: "Picture a farmer walking out to plant seed.
**4** As he scattered the seed, some landed on the packed footpath, and birds swooped down and gobbled it up.
**5** Other seed fell on thin soil covering bedrock. It sprouted right away because the dirt was shallow,
**6** but as soon as the sun climbed high, the young shoots scorched and dried up because they had no roots.
**7** Other seed landed in a patch of thornbushes, and the thorns shot up and choked out the grain.
**8** Still other seed fell into rich, deep soil and produced a bumper crop—some a hundred times what was planted, some sixty, and some thirty!
**9** If you've got ears, listen closely."
**10** His disciples walked up and asked him, "Why do you always use parables when you talk to the crowds?"
**11** Jesus answered, "You have been given the privilege of knowing the secrets of heaven's kingdom, while they haven't.
**12** Whoever already holds truth will be given even more until they overflow, while anyone who lacks it will lose even the little they think they have.
**13** That's why I teach them in stories: they look right at things without seeing, and listen without hearing or grasping what it means.
**14** In fact, Isaiah's prophecy is coming true right in front of them: 'You'll hear the words over and over without ever understanding; you'll stare right at it without ever seeing!
**15** This nation's heart has grown calloused; their ears barely work, and they've squeezed their eyes shut—otherwise they might see with their eyes, hear with their ears, understand with their hearts, turn back to me, and let me heal them!'
**16** How blessed are your eyes because they actually see, and your ears because they actually hear!
**17** Believe me, generations of prophets and godly people longed to see what you're seeing and never saw it, and to hear what you're hearing and never heard it.
**18** So listen to what the story of the farmer means:
**19** Whenever someone hears the message about God's kingdom and doesn't grasp it, the evil one swoops in and steals away what was planted in their heart. That's the seed along the footpath.
**20** The seed planted on rocky soil represents someone who hears the message and immediately celebrates it with excitement—
**21** yet it puts down no deep roots, so it only lasts briefly. As soon as trouble or persecution hits because of that message, they trip and quit.
**22** The seed scattered among thornbushes represents someone who hears the message, yet everyday worries and the false promises of wealth crowd it out so nothing grows.
**23** Finally, the seed dropped into rich soil is the person who hears the message and truly grasps it—that's the one who bears a huge harvest, yielding a hundred, sixty, or thirty times over!"
**24** Jesus shared another story with them: "Heaven's kingdom is like a farmer who planted top-grade wheat in his field.
**25** One night while everyone was asleep, his enemy snuck in, scattered weed seeds right through the wheat, and slipped away.
**26** When the wheat sprouted and started forming grain heads, the weeds showed up too.
**27** The landowner's workers ran to him and asked, 'Boss, didn't you plant good seed in your field? Where on earth did all these weeds come from?'
**28** 'An enemy did this,' he told them. 'Should we go out and pull up the weeds right now?' the workers asked.
**29** 'No,' he said, 'if you yank out the weeds now, you'll pull the young wheat up by the roots along with them.
**30** Let both grow side by side until harvest. When harvest arrives, I'll instruct the reapers: "Pull up the weeds first and bundle them together for burning, then haul the wheat safely into my barn."'"
**31** He offered another story: "Heaven's kingdom is like a tiny mustard seed that someone planted in his garden.
**32** It starts out smaller than any other garden seed, yet once it grows up, it towers over every garden plant and becomes a tree where wild birds come and build nests in its branches."
**33** He gave them another picture: "Heaven's kingdom is like a pinch of yeast that a woman kneaded into sixty pounds of flour until the whole batch of dough rose."
**34** Jesus always taught the crowds using stories like these—in fact, he never spoke to them without a parable—
**35** fulfilling what the prophet had declared: "I will open my mouth in parables; I will bring out truths hidden since the beginning."
**36** Then he dismissed the crowds and went back inside the house. His disciples gathered around and asked, "Explain the story of the weeds in the field to us."
**37** Jesus answered, "The farmer planting good seed is the Son of Man.
**38** The field is the entire world. The good seed represents the people who belong to God's kingdom, while the weeds represent those who belong to the evil one.
**39** The enemy who planted the weeds is the devil. The harvest stands for the end of this age, and the reapers are angels.
**40** Just as weeds get gathered up and burned in a bonfire, that's how things will go at the end of the age:
**41** the Son of Man will send out his angels to clear out of his kingdom everyone who traps others in sin and everyone who lives lawlessly,
**42** and toss them into the blazing furnace, where people weep and grind their teeth.
**43** Then godly people will shine bright as the sun inside their Father's kingdom! Anyone with ears should really listen.
**44** Heaven's kingdom is like hidden treasure buried in a field. A man stumbled across it, buried it right back, and in sheer joy ran off, sold everything he owned, and bought that field!
**45** Again, heaven's kingdom is like a pearl dealer hunting for top-quality pearls.
**46** Once he spotted one priceless pearl, he went straight out, liquidated everything he owned, and bought it.
**47** Again, heaven's kingdom is like a wide fishing net cast into the sea that scooped up every kind of creature.
**48** When the net was packed full, the fishermen dragged it up onto the beach, sat down, and sorted the good fish into baskets while tossing the spoiled ones away.
**49** That's how it will be at the end of the age: angels will come out, separate wicked people from the righteous,
**50** and throw them into the blazing furnace, where there will be weeping and grinding of teeth.
**51** Do all of these make sense to you?" "Yes," they answered.
**52** Then Jesus told them, "That means every scripture teacher who becomes a student of heaven's kingdom is like a homeowner pulling both brand-new treasures and treasured heirlooms out of his storeroom."
**53** When Jesus wrapped up these parables, he left that region.
**54** Returning to his hometown, he started teaching in the local synagogue, leaving everyone stunned. "Where did this guy get such wisdom and miracle-working power?" they asked.
**55** "Isn't he just the carpenter's son? Isn't his mother named Mary, and aren't his brothers James, Joseph, Simon, and Judas?
**56** Aren't all his sisters living right here among us? Where did he pick up all of this?"
**57** They took deep offense at him. Jesus told them, "A prophet gets respect everywhere except in his own hometown and inside his own family."
**58** And he didn't perform many miracles there because they refused to believe.

## Crossings & Lexical Notes

- **v. 3 `παραβολαῖς` (*parabolais*):** From *paraballō* ("to cast/place alongside"), spanning brief similes, proverbs, riddles, and extended narrative analogies comparing earthly life with God's reign.
- **v. 25 `ζιζάνια` (*zizania*):** Bearded darnel (*Lolium temulentum*), a weed whose young shoots closely resemble wheat until grain heads form and whose roots tangle around wheat roots underground.
- **v. 33 `σάτα τρία` (*sata tria*):** Three *seahs* (approx. 39 liters or 50+ pounds of flour), an astonishing banquet-sized quantity capable of feeding well over a hundred people.
- **v. 35 `ἀπὸ καταβολῆς` (*apo katabolēs*):** SBLGNT, `WH`, and `Treg` end with *apo katabolēs* ("from [the] foundation"), whereas `NA28` and `RP` add *kosmou* ("of the world").
- **v. 47 `σαγήνῃ` (*sagēnē*):** A large weighted dragnet or seine hauled between boats or onto shore, sweeping up every species in its path.
- **v. 51 `Συνήκατε ταῦτα πάντα;` (*Synēkate tauta panta?*):** SBLGNT, `WH`, `Treg`, and `NA28` open directly with the question "Have you understood all these things?"; `RP` adds the introductory *Legei autois ho Iēsous* ("Jesus says to them").
- **v. 55 `τέκτονος` (*tektonos*):** Genitive of *tektōn* ("builder," "woodworker," or "artisan"), denoting a skilled craftsman working in wood and stone.

**28** “He said to them, ‘An enemy has done this.’ “The servants asked him, ‘Do you want us to go and gather them up?’
**29** “But he said, ‘No, lest perhaps while you gather up the darnel weeds, you root up the wheat with them.
**30** Let both grow together until the harvest, and in the harvest time I will tell the reapers, “First, gather up the darnel weeds, and bind them in bundles to burn them; but gather the wheat into my barn.”’”
**31** He set another parable before them, saying, “The Kingdom of Heaven is like a grain of mustard seed which a man took, and sowed in his field,
**32** which indeed is smaller than all seeds. But when it is grown, it is greater than the herbs and becomes a tree, so that the birds of the air come and lodge in its branches.”
**33** He spoke another parable to them. “The Kingdom of Heaven is like yeast which a woman took and hid in three measures of meal, until it was all leavened.”
**34** Jesus spoke all these things in parables to the multitudes; and without a parable, he didn’t speak to them,
**35** that it might be fulfilled which was spoken through the prophet, saying, “I will open my mouth in parables; I will utter things hidden from the foundation of the world.”
**36** Then Jesus sent the multitudes away, and went into the house. His disciples came to him, saying, “Explain to us the parable of the darnel weeds of the field.”
**37** He answered them, “He who sows the good seed is the Son of Man,
**38** the field is the world, the good seeds are the children of the Kingdom, and the darnel weeds are the children of the evil one.
**39** The enemy who sowed them is the devil. The harvest is the end of the age, and the reapers are angels.
**40** As therefore the darnel weeds are gathered up and burned with fire; so will it be at the end of this age.
**41** The Son of Man will send out his angels, and they will gather out of his Kingdom all things that cause stumbling and those who do iniquity,
**42** and will cast them into the furnace of fire. There will be weeping and gnashing of teeth.
**43** Then the righteous will shine like the sun in the Kingdom of their Father. He who has ears to hear, let him hear.
**44** “Again, the Kingdom of Heaven is like treasure hidden in the field, which a man found and hid. In his joy, he goes and sells all that he has and buys that field.
**45** “Again, the Kingdom of Heaven is like a man who is a merchant seeking fine pearls,
**46** who having found one pearl of great price, he went and sold all that he had and bought it.
**47** “Again, the Kingdom of Heaven is like a dragnet that was cast into the sea and gathered some fish of every kind,
**48** which, when it was filled, fishermen drew up on the beach. They sat down and gathered the good into containers, but the bad they threw away.
**49** So it will be in the end of the world. The angels will come and separate the wicked from among the righteous,
**50** and will cast them into the furnace of fire. There will be weeping and gnashing of teeth.”
**51** Jesus said to them, “Have you understood all these things?” They answered him, “Yes, Lord.”
**52** He said to them, “Therefore every scribe who has been made a disciple in the Kingdom of Heaven is like a man who is a householder, who brings out of his treasure new and old things.”
**53** When Jesus had finished these parables, he departed from there.
**54** Coming into his own country, he taught them in their synagogue, so that they were astonished and said, “Where did this man get this wisdom and these mighty works?
**55** Isn’t this the carpenter’s son? Isn’t his mother called Mary, and his brothers James, Joses, Simon, and Judas?
**56** Aren’t all of his sisters with us? Where then did this man get all of these things?”
**57** They were offended by him. But Jesus said to them, “A prophet is not without honor, except in his own country and in his own house.”
**58** He didn’t do many mighty works there because of their unbelief.
