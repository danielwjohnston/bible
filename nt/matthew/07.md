## Clear & Faithful

**1** "Do not pass judgment on others, so that you will not be judged.
**2** For with the judgment you pronounce you will be judged, and with the measure you use it will be measured to you.
**3** Why do you look at the speck in your brother's eye, yet fail to notice the beam in your own eye?
**4** Or how can you tell your brother, 'Let me pull that speck out of your eye,' while a wooden beam is sitting right in your own eye?
**5** You hypocrite! Pull the beam out of your own eye first, and then you will have clear sight to take the speck out of your brother's eye.
**6** Do not give what is holy to dogs, and do not throw your pearls before pigs, lest they trample them underfoot and turn to attack and tear you apart.
**7** Keep asking, and it will be given to you; keep seeking, and you will find; keep knocking, and the door will be opened for you.
**8** For everyone who asks receives, and the one who seeks finds, and to the one who knocks it will be opened.
**9** Or what person among you, if his son asks him for bread, will hand him a stone?
**10** Or if he also asks for a fish, will he hand him a snake?
**11** If you, flawed as you are, still know how to give good gifts to your children, how much more surely will your Father in heaven give good things to those who ask him!
**12** Therefore, whatever you want people to do to you, do the exact same to them; for this sums up the Law and the Prophets.
**13** Enter through the narrow gate. For wide is the gate and broad is the road that leads to destruction, and many are those who enter through it.
**14** Because narrow is the gate and hard-pressed is the road that leads to life, and few are those who find it.
**15** Watch out for false prophets, who come to you in sheep's clothing yet inwardly are ravenous wolves.
**16** By their fruit you will recognize them. Are grapes ever gathered from thornbushes, or figs from thistles?
**17** So every healthy tree bears good fruit, whereas a diseased tree bears bad fruit.
**18** A healthy tree cannot bear bad fruit, nor can a diseased tree bear good fruit.
**19** Any tree that fails to produce wholesome fruit is chopped down and cast into the fire.
**20** Thus, by their fruit you will recognize them.
**21** Not everyone who calls out to me, 'Lord, Lord,' will enter heaven's kingdom, but only the person who carries out the will of my Father in heaven.
**22** On that day many will say to me, 'Lord, Lord, did we not prophesy in your name, and cast out demons in your name, and perform many mighty works in your name?'
**23** And then I will declare openly to them, 'I never knew you; depart from me, you workers of lawlessness.'
**24** Therefore, everyone who hears these words of mine and puts them into practice will be like a wise builder who constructed his house upon solid rock.
**25** The rain poured down, the flash floods surged, and the winds blew and beat against that house; yet it did not collapse, because its foundation had been laid on the rock.
**26** Yet everyone who hears these words of mine and does not put them into practice will be like a foolish builder who constructed his house upon the sand.
**27** The rain poured down, the flash floods surged, and the winds blew and slammed against that house; and it collapsed, and its ruin was total."
**28** When Jesus finished these sayings, the crowds were astonished at his teaching,
**29** for he was teaching them as one possessing real authority, and not like their scribes.

## Everyday English

**1** "Stop passing judgment on others, or you'll be judged right back!
**2** The same standard you use to judge others is the standard that will be used on you, and the same scoop you measure out will be measured back to you.
**3** How can you stare at a tiny splinter in your brother's eye while ignoring the huge plank stuck in your own eye?
**4** How dare you say to your brother, 'Hold still, let me pull that speck out of your eye,' when there's a wooden beam sticking right out of yours!
**5** You phony! Pull the beam out of your own eye first—then you'll actually see clearly enough to help get the speck out of your brother's eye.
**6** Don't hand sacred things over to stray dogs, and don't toss your pearls in front of pigs. They'll only stomp them into the mud, then whip around and rip you to pieces.
**7** Keep asking, and you'll receive; keep searching, and you'll find; keep knocking, and the door will swing open for you.
**8** Because everyone who asks gets an answer, anyone who searches discovers what they're seeking, and whoever knocks has the door opened.
**9** Think about it: if your own kid asks you for a loaf of bread, would any parent hand them a rock?
**10** Or if they ask for a fish, would you hand them a snake?
**11** Even though you're flawed, you still know how to give your children good gifts! How much more will your Father in heaven give good things to everyone who asks him!
**12** So in everything, treat other people the exact way you'd like them to treat you—that's the entire core of the Law and the Prophets.
**13** Walk through the narrow gate! The wide gate and broad highway lead straight to ruin, and crowds of people take that route.
**14** But the gate is tight and the trail is tough that leads to real life, and only a few people ever find it.
**15** Stay on guard against false prophets. They walk up to you dressed like harmless sheep, while inside they're vicious wolves looking for prey.
**16** You can spot them by what their lives produce. Nobody picks grapes from thornbushes or figs from weeds, right?
**17** In the same way, a healthy tree produces good fruit, while a rotting tree produces bad fruit.
**18** A healthy tree can't grow rotten fruit, and a rotting tree can't grow good fruit.
**19** Any tree that fails to produce good fruit gets chopped down and tossed into the fire.
**20** So look at the fruit they produce—that's how you'll know who they really are.
**21** Calling me 'Lord, Lord' won't get everyone into heaven's kingdom—only those who actually carry out the will of my Father in heaven will enter.
**22** On judgment day, plenty of people will protest to me, 'Lord, Lord, didn't we speak prophecies in your name, drive out demons in your name, and perform amazing miracles in your name?'
**23** Then I'll tell them straight to their face, 'I never knew you at all. Get away from me, you people who live outside God's law!'
**24** So everyone who listens to my words and actually lives by them is like a smart person who built their house on solid bedrock.
**25** When the rainstorms poured down, rivers overflowed, and gale winds slammed against that house, it never collapsed, because it was anchored deep into the rock.
**26** But anyone who listens to my words and ignores them is like a fool who built their house on loose sand.
**27** When the rainstorms poured down, rivers overflowed, and gale winds hammered against that house, it caved in with a devastating crash."
**28** When Jesus wrapped up this whole sermon, the crowds were stunned by his teaching,
**29** because he taught with genuine personal authority—unlike their legal scholars.

## Crossings & Lexical Notes

- **v. 3 `κάρφος` (*karphos*) & `δοκός` (*dokos*):** *Karphos* denotes a dry bit of straw, splinter, or speck, contrasting comically with *dokos*, a heavy structural roof beam.
- **v. 14 `ὅτι` (*hoti*):** SBLGNT and `WH` read *hoti* ("because/for"), explaining why believers must take the narrow gate; `Treg`, `NA28`, and `RP` read the exclamatory *ti* ("how narrow is the gate!").
- **v. 14 `τεθλιμμένη` (*tethlimmenē*):** Perfect passive participle of *thlibō* ("pressed tight," "hemmed in," or "constricted"), contrasting with *eurychōros* ("broad/roomy") in v. 13.
- **v. 17 `σαπρόν` (*sapron*):** From *sapros* ("decayed," "rotten," "putrid"), describing an orchard tree ruined by rot and incapable of wholesome yield.
- **v. 23 `ὁμολογήσω` (*homologēsō*):** Literally "I will declare/confess publicly," an official judicial pronouncement exposing empty claims of ministry without obedience (*anomia*, "lawlessness").
- **v. 24 `φρονίμῳ` (*phronimō*) & v. 26 `μωρῷ` (*mōrō*):** Contrasts the *phronimos* (practically wise, discerning) builder with the *mōros* (foolish, shortsighted) builder, tying back to the wisdom/folly motif across the Sermon on the Mount.

## Supplemental Texts & Allusions

- **Psalm 6:8 (6:9 LXX)** (echoed in v. 23): *"Turn away from me, all of you who practice evil!"*
- **Jeremiah 7:4 & Proverbs 10:25** (background to vv. 21, 24–27): Warns against relying on hollow religious chants (*"The LORD's sanctuary!"*) and contrasts the wicked swept away by the storm with the righteous standing on an enduring foundation.
- **Ezekiel 13:10–15** (echoed in vv. 24–27): False prophets whitewash a flimsy wall that collapses completely when torrential rain and wind strike it.
