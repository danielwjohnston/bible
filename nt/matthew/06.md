## Clear & Faithful

**1** "Be careful not to practice your righteousness in front of people in order to be seen by them; otherwise you have no reward with your Father who is in heaven.
**2** Therefore, when you give to the needy, do not blow a trumpet ahead of you as the hypocrites do in the synagogues and on the streets, so that they may be praised by people. Truly I say to you, they have received their reward in full.
**3** Instead, when you give to the needy, do not let your left hand know what your right hand is doing,
**4** so that your giving may be in secret; and your Father, who sees in secret, will reward you.
**5** And when you pray, you shall not be like the hypocrites; for they love to stand and pray in the synagogues and on the street corners so that they may be visible to people. Truly I say to you, they have received their reward in full.
**6** Instead, whenever you pray, step into your private room, close the door behind you, and pray to your unseen Father; and your Father, who sees what happens in secret, will repay you.
**7** And when praying, do not heap up empty phrases like the Gentiles, for they suppose they will be heard because of their many words.
**8** Do not imitate them, because your Father already knows everything you need before you even ask him.
**9** Pray then in this manner: 'Our Father in heaven, may your name be kept holy;
**10** let your kingdom arrive; let your will be carried out on earth just as it is in heaven.
**11** Grant us today the bread we need for the day;
**12** and forgive us our debts, just as we also have forgiven our debtors;
**13** and do not lead us into temptation, but deliver us from the evil one.'
**14** Because if you pardon others for their wrongdoing, your heavenly Father will pardon you as well;
**15** yet if you do not forgive people, neither will your Father forgive your offenses.
**16** Whenever you fast, do not put on a gloomy expression like the hypocrites, for they disfigure their faces to show people that they are fasting. Truly I say to you, they have received their reward in full.
**17** Instead, whenever you fast, put oil on your head and wash your face,
**18** so that your fasting may not be obvious to people, but to your Father who is in the unseen place; and your Father, who sees in secret, will reward you.
**19** Do not store up for yourselves treasures on earth, where moth and decay destroy and where thieves break in and steal;
**20** rather, store up for yourselves treasures in heaven, where neither moth nor decay destroys and where thieves neither break in nor steal.
**21** Because wherever your stored wealth lies, that is where your heart will be too.
**22** The eye is the lamp of the body. If then your eye is healthy and generous, your whole body will be full of light;
**23** yet if your eye is diseased and stingy, your whole body will be full of darkness. If therefore the light within you is darkness, how deep is that darkness!
**24** Nobody can serve as a slave to two masters: either he will despise the one and love the other, or he will stay loyal to the first and look down on the second. You cannot serve both God and wealth.
**25** For this reason I tell you, do not be anxious about your life, what you will eat, nor about your body, what you will wear. Is not life more than food, and the body more than clothing?
**26** Look at the birds of the sky: they neither sow nor reap nor gather into barns, yet your heavenly Father feeds them. Are you not worth far more than they?
**27** Which of you by worrying can add a single cubit to his lifespan?
**28** And why are you anxious about clothing? Observe how the lilies of the field grow: they neither labor nor spin yarn,
**29** yet I tell you that not even Solomon in all his splendor was clothed like one of these.
**30** If God so clothes the grass of the field, which is alive today and tomorrow is thrown into the oven, will he not much more clothe you, O people of little faith?
**31** So do not be anxious, saying, 'What shall we eat?' or 'What shall we drink?' or 'What shall we wear?'
**32** For the Gentiles eagerly pursue all these things, and your heavenly Father knows that you need them all.
**33** Instead, make his kingdom and his righteousness your first pursuit, and every one of these things will be provided for you as well.
**34** Therefore do not worry about tomorrow, for tomorrow will worry about itself; each day has enough trouble of its own."

## Everyday English

**1** "Watch out that you don't put your good deeds on display just to impress an audience. If you do that, you won't get any reward from your Father in heaven.
**2** So when you help people in need, don't announce it with trumpets like the phonies do in the synagogues and on the streets to earn everyone's applause. Believe me, that applause is the only paycheck they'll ever get.
**3** Instead, when you help someone out, don't even let your left hand know what your right hand is doing.
**4** Keep your generosity quiet, and your Father—who sees everything done behind closed doors—will reward you.
**5** When you pray, don't act like the phonies. They love standing on street corners and inside synagogues to pray where everyone will notice them. Trust me, being noticed is all the reward they'll ever get.
**6** Instead, when you pray, slip into your private room, shut the door, and talk to your Father in secret. Your Father, who sees what happens in private, will reward you.
**7** Also, when you pray, don't rattle on with endless chatter like people who don't know God—they assume they'll be heard if they just use enough words.
**8** Don't copy them, because your Father already knows what you need even before you ask him.
**9** Here's how you should pray: 'Our Father in heaven, may your name be honored as holy.
**10** May your kingdom come soon, and may your will happen here on earth just as it does in heaven.
**11** Give us today the food we need for today.
**12** Forgive us the wrongs we owe you, just as we've forgiven those who wronged us.
**13** Don't lead us into hard testing, but rescue us from the evil one.'
**14** Remember, if you forgive other people when they sin against you, your heavenly Father will forgive you too.
**15** But if you refuse to forgive others, your Father won't forgive your sins either.
**16** When you go without food to fast, don't walk around looking miserable like the phonies do—they mess up their appearance just so everyone will know they're fasting. I'm telling you, they've already collected all the reward they're getting.
**17** Instead, when you fast, comb your hair with oil and wash your face,
**18** so nobody notices you're fasting except your Father who sees what's hidden. And your Father, who sees everything in secret, will reward you.
**19** Stop piling up valuables here on earth, where moths eat clothes, rust ruins metal, and burglars dig through walls and rob you.
**20** Instead, store up your treasures in heaven, where no moth or rust can ruin anything, and no burglar can break in and steal.
**21** Because wherever you keep your treasure, that's where your heart will stay anchored.
**22** Your eye works like a lamp for your whole body. If your eyes are clear and generous, your whole life stays bright.
**23** But if your eyes are clouded with greed, your whole life fills with shadow. And if the one light inside you is actually darkness, how dark must that darkness be!
**24** Nobody can work as a slave for two different bosses at once—you'll end up resenting one and staying loyal to the other, or clinging to one and looking down on the other. You can't serve both God and money.
**25** That's why I'm telling you: quit worrying about your life—what you'll have to eat—or your body—what you'll have to wear. Isn't life worth far more than groceries, and your body worth more than outfits?
**26** Look up at the wild birds: they never plant crops, harvest grain, or pack food into barns, yet your heavenly Father keeps them fed. Aren't you way more valuable to him than birds?
**27** Can any amount of worrying add even a single hour to your lifespan?
**28** And why stress out over clothes? Take a good look at the wildflowers growing in the fields—they don't work jobs or weave fabric,
**29** yet even King Solomon at the height of his wealth wasn't dressed as beautifully as a single flower!
**30** If God dresses up wild grass like that—here today and tossed into a cooking fire tomorrow—won't he take even better care of you, you people of little faith?
**31** So stop stressing and asking, 'What are we going to eat?' or 'What will we drink?' or 'What will we wear?'
**32** People who don't know God chase after those things all the time, while your heavenly Father already knows you need every one of them.
**33** Put his kingdom and his right way of living first, and everything else you need will be handed to you too.
**34** So don't worry about tomorrow—tomorrow can handle its own worries. Today already has enough trouble of its own."

## Crossings & Lexical Notes

- **v. 1 `δικαιοσύνην` (*dikaiosynēn*):** SBLGNT, `WH`, `Treg`, and `NA28` read "righteousness" as the umbrella term introducing almsgiving (vv. 2–4), prayer (vv. 5–15), and fasting (vv. 16–18); `RP` reads *eleēmosynēn* ("charitable giving"). Notice also `WH`/`NA28` include *de* ("now"), omitted in `Treg` and `RP`.
- **v. 2 `ὑποκριταί` (*hypokritai*):** Originally stage actors wearing masks; in the Gospels, performers putting on religious devotion for human applause, who "have received in full" (*apechousin*, a commercial receipt term) their entire wage.
- **v. 11 `ἐπιούσιον` (*epiousion*):** A rare adjective occurring only in the Lord's Prayer (here and Luke 11:3), variously understood as "daily," "necessary for existence," or "for the coming day."
- **v. 13 `πονηροῦ` (*ponērou*):** Either "the evil one" (masculine) or "evil" (neuter). Crucially, the closing doxology (*Hoti sou estin hē basileia kai hē dynamis kai hē doxa eis tous aiōnas. Amēn*, "For yours is the kingdom and the power and the glory forever. Amen") found in `RP` and `TR` is **omitted** in SBLGNT, `NA28`, Nestle 1904, `WH`, and `Treg` (absent from the earliest Greek manuscripts such as Sinaiticus and Vaticanus, and likely arose from early liturgical usage echoing 1 Chr 29:11).
- **v. 22 `ἁπλοῦς` (*haplous*):** Literally "single/undivided/healthy," contrasted with `πονηρός` (*ponēros*, "bad/envious/stingy") in v. 23, an idiom for generosity ("good eye") versus greed ("evil eye").
- **v. 24 `μαμωνᾷ` (*mamōna*):** Aramaic *mamona* ("wealth/property/possessions"), here personified as a rival master competing with God.
- **v. 25 `τί φάγητε` (*ti phagēte*):** SBLGNT (with `Holmes` and `WHmarg`) reads "what you may eat," whereas `WH`, `Treg`, and `NA28` add *ē ti piēte* ("or what you may drink") and `RP` adds *kai ti piēte*.
- **v. 33 `βασιλείαν` (*basileian*):** SBLGNT and `WH` read "his kingdom and righteousness," while `Treg`, `NA28`, and `RP` read "the kingdom of God (*tou theou*) and his righteousness."

## Supplemental Texts & Allusions

- **Psalm 37:25 & Psalm 145:15–16** (background to vv. 11, 26): God faithfully provides daily bread and feeds every living creature in its season.
- **Proverbs 22:9 & 28:22** (background to vv. 22–23): Hebrew idioms contrasting the generous "good eye" with the stingy "evil eye."
- **1 Chronicles 29:11** (liturgical background to v. 13 `RP`/`TR` doxology): *"Yours, O LORD, is the greatness, the power, the glory, the victory, and the majesty... yours is the kingdom, O LORD."*
