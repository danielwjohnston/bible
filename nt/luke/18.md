## Clear & Faithful

**1** Jesus told them a parable about their need to pray continually and never lose heart.
**2** He said, "In a certain city there was a judge who neither feared God nor respected any human being.
**3** Now there was a widow in that same city who kept coming to him, pleading, 'Grant me justice against my legal opponent!'
**4** For a long while he refused, but afterward he said to himself, 'Even though I do not fear God or respect anyone,
**5** yet because this widow keeps bothering me, I will grant her justice, so that she does not wear me out by continually coming.'"
**6** Then the Lord declared, "Pay attention to what that dishonest judge says!
**7** Will not God bring about justice for his chosen ones who cry out to him day and night, even while he bears patiently with them?
**8** I assure you, he will execute justice for them promptly. Even so, when the Son of Man arrives, will he discover faith upon the earth?"
**9** To some who trusted in themselves that they were righteous and looked down on everyone else, Jesus also told this parable:
**10** "Two men climbed up to the temple courts to pray—one of them a Pharisee, the other a tax collector.
**11** The Pharisee stood by himself and prayed like this: 'God, I thank you that I am not like the rest of humanity—swindlers, evildoers, adulterers—or even like this tax collector here.
**12** I fast twice every week, and I give a tenth of everything I acquire.'
**13** Meanwhile, the tax collector stood at a distance and would not even lift his eyes toward heaven, but kept beating his chest and saying, 'God, be merciful to me, a sinner!'
**14** I assure you, that tax collector returned down to his home declared righteous rather than the other man; for anyone who elevates himself will be brought low, whereas anyone who lowers himself will be raised up."
**15** People were also bringing infants to Jesus so that he might touch them; when the disciples saw this, they rebuked them.
**16** But Jesus called the children to him and said, "Let the little children come to me, and do not stop them, for the kingdom of God belongs to people like these.
**17** Truly I tell you, whoever does not receive the kingdom of God like a little child will never enter it."
**18** A certain official questioned him, "Good Teacher, what action must I take to inherit eternal life?"
**19** Jesus replied to him, "Why do you address me as good? Nobody is good except God alone.
**20** You are familiar with the commandments: 'Do not commit adultery, do not murder, do not steal, do not testify falsely, honor your father and mother.'"
**21** The man replied, "All of these I have kept since my youth."
**22** Hearing this, Jesus told him, "One thing you still lack: sell everything you own and distribute the proceeds to the poor, and you will have treasure in heaven; then come, follow me."
**23** When he heard these words, he became deeply saddened, because he was extraordinarily wealthy.
**24** Looking at him, Jesus said, "How hard it is for those who possess wealth to enter the kingdom of God!
**25** Indeed, it is easier for a camel to go through the eye of a needle than for a rich person to enter the kingdom of God."
**26** The listeners exclaimed, "Who, then, can possibly be saved?"
**27** Jesus replied, "What is impossible with human beings is possible with God."
**28** Peter spoke up, "Look, we have left our own homes and followed you."
**29** Jesus answered them, "Truly I assure you, nobody who has given up home, wife, brothers, parents, or children for the sake of God's kingdom
**30** will fail to receive manifold more during this present time, and in the coming age, life eternal."
**31** Taking the twelve aside, he told them, "Look, we are going up to Jerusalem, and everything written by the prophets about the Son of Man will be accomplished:
**32** he will be handed over to the Gentiles, mocked, mistreated, and spat upon;
**33** after flogging him, they will kill him, and on the third day he will rise again."
**34** Yet they understood none of these things; this message remained hidden from them, and they did not grasp what was being said.
**35** As Jesus approached Jericho, a certain blind man was sitting beside the road, begging.
**36** Hearing a crowd passing by, he asked what was happening.
**37** The bystanders informed him, "Jesus the Nazarene is walking past."
**38** Immediately he shouted out, "Jesus, Son of David, have mercy on me!"
**39** Those walking ahead scolded him to keep quiet, yet he kept shouting all the louder, "Son of David, have mercy on me!"
**40** Jesus stopped and ordered the man to be brought over to him. When he came near, Jesus asked him,
**41** "What do you want me to do for you?" He answered, "Lord, let me regain my sight!"
**42** Jesus answered him, "Regain your sight! Your trust has healed you."
**43** At once his sight was restored, and he began following Jesus, glorifying God; and all the people who witnessed it gave praise to God.

## Everyday English

**1** Jesus shared a story with his disciples to show them they should always keep praying and never give up:
**2** "In a certain town there was a judge who didn't fear God and didn't care what anybody thought.
**3** A widow lived in that same town, and she kept coming to his court demanding, 'Give me legal protection against the man cheating me!'
**4** For a long time the judge brushed her off, but eventually he thought to himself, 'Sure, I don't fear God and I don't care about people,
**5** yet this widow is driving me crazy! I'll settle her case in her favor before she wears me out with her nonstop visits.'"
**6** Then the Lord said, "Pay attention to what that crooked judge decided!
**7** Don't you think God will bring justice for his own chosen people who cry out to him day and night? Will he keep putting them off?
**8** I assure you, he'll deliver justice for them quickly! Still, when the Son of Man returns, will he actually find real faith here on earth?"
**9** Jesus also told this story to people who were convinced of their own goodness and looked down on everybody else:
**10** "Two men made their way up to the temple to pray—one was a Pharisee, and the other collected taxes.
**11** The Pharisee stood up tall by himself and prayed, 'God, thank you that I'm not like other people—crooks, cheats, adulterers—or especially like that tax collector over there!
**12** I fast two days every week and give a tenth of every penny I earn.'
**13** Meanwhile, the tax collector stood way off in the back. He wouldn't even look up at the sky, but pounded his chest in grief and prayed, 'God, please have mercy on me—I'm a sinner!'
**14** Let me tell you, that tax collector walked back home forgiven and right with God, while the Pharisee didn't! Anyone who puffs himself up will be knocked down, and anyone who humbles himself will be lifted up."
**15** People were also bringing babies to Jesus so he could lay his hands on them, and when the disciples saw it, they scolded the parents.
**16** Jesus called the children right over and said, "Let the little kids come to me, and don't try to block them! God's kingdom belongs to people just like them.
**17** I'm telling you the truth: unless you welcome God's kingdom with the trust of a little child, you'll never get inside."
**18** One day a religious leader asked him, "Good Teacher, what do I have to do to inherit eternal life?"
**19** "Why are you calling me good?" Jesus asked. "Nobody is truly good except God alone.
**20** You know God's rules by heart: 'Don't commit adultery, don't kill, don't steal, don't lie as a witness, and honor your mom and dad.'"
**21** "I've obeyed every single one of those since I was a boy," the man replied.
**22** When Jesus heard that, he told him, "You're still missing one thing: sell everything you own, give the money to the poor, and you'll have real treasure waiting in heaven. Then come follow me."
**23** The man's face fell when he heard that, because he was insanely wealthy.
**24** Looking at him, Jesus remarked, "How tough it is for rich people to step into God's kingdom!
**25** Honestly, it's easier for a camel to squeeze through the eye of a sewing needle than for a wealthy person to enter God's kingdom."
**26** Everyone listening gasped, "Then who on earth can be saved?"
**27** Jesus answered, "What's impossible for human beings is completely possible for God."
**28** Peter spoke up, "Look at us—we walked away from everything we owned to follow you!"
**29** Jesus assured them, "I promise you, nobody who has given up a home, a wife, brothers, parents, or kids for God's kingdom
**30** will fail to receive far more back in this life—and eternal life in the world to come."
**31** Pulling the twelve apostles aside, Jesus told them, "Listen—we're walking up to Jerusalem, and everything the prophets wrote about the Son of Man is about to come true.
**32** He'll be handed over to foreigners, laughed at, insulted, and spat on.
**33** They'll whip him and execute him—and three days later, he'll rise back to life."
**34** Yet they didn't understand a single word of it; the whole idea was completely hidden from them, and they couldn't figure out what he meant.
**35** As Jesus got close to Jericho, a blind man was sitting by the side of the road begging for money.
**36** When he heard a big crowd walking past, he asked what was going on.
**37** People told him, "Jesus from Nazareth is walking by!"
**38** Right away he shouted, "Jesus, Son of David, have pity on me!"
**39** The people leading the crowd told him to shut up, yet he only yelled louder: "Son of David, have pity on me!"
**40** Jesus stopped in his tracks and told them to bring the man over. When the man reached him, Jesus asked,
**41** "What do you want me to do for you?" "Lord, I want to see again!" he answered.
**42** Jesus told him, "Look up and see! Your faith has healed you."
**43** Instantly his eyes opened and he could see! He followed right behind Jesus, praising God—and the whole crowd joined in thanking God too.

## Crossings & Lexical Notes

- **v. 5 `ὑπωπιάζῃ` (*hypōpiazē*):** Boxing metaphor literally meaning "give me a black eye / strike under the eye" (from *hypōpion*), used idiomatically for wearing someone down relentlessly or ruining their reputation.
- **v. 7 `μακροθυμεῖ` (*makrothymei*):** Present indicative (`WH`, `Treg`, `NA28`) rather than participle *makrothymōn* (`RP`); literally "is long-suffering / exercises patience," raising the question of whether God delays or bears patiently while hearing his elect's cries.
- **v. 11 `πρὸς ἑαυτὸν` (*pros heauton*):** Can modify either *statheis* ("standing by himself," separated from others) or *prosēucheto* ("was praying to/about himself," i.e., self-directed prayer). Notice word order varies between `NA28`/`RP` (*pros heauton tauta*) and `WH`/`Treg` (*tauta pros heauton*).
- **v. 13 `ἱλάσθητί μοι` (*hilasthēti moi*):** Aorist passive imperative of *hilaskomai* ("be propitiated / show mercy / atone toward me"), the Septuagintal term tied to the mercy seat (*hilastērion*, Exod 25:17; Ps 51:1), grounding the tax collector's plea in atoning mercy rather than self-merit.
- **v. 14 `παρʼ ἐκεῖνον` (*par' ekeinon*):** Comparative construction ("justified rather than / alongside and surpassing that one"), printed by `WH`, `Treg`, and `NA28`, whereas `RP` reads *ē gar ekeinos* ("than that man").
- **v. 24 `Ἰησοῦς` (*Iēsous*):** `WH` reads simply "seeing him, Jesus said," whereas `Treg`, `NA28`, and `RP` add *perilypon genomenon* ("seeing that he had become very sorrowful").
- **v. 25 `τρήματος βελόνης` (*trēmatos belonēs*):** Medical/surgical term *belonē* ("needle," unique to Luke in the NT, vs. *rhaphis* in `RP` and Matt 19:24 / Mark 10:25), paired with *trēma* ("perforation/eye").
- **v. 41 `ἀναβλέψω` (*anablepsō*):** From *anablepō*, which means both "look up" and "recover sight / see again."

## Supplemental Texts & Allusions

- **Sirach 35:14–18** (thematic parallel to vv. 1–8): The cry of the widow and orphan pierces the clouds, and the Lord will not delay until he executes justice for the humble.
- **Exodus 20:12–16 & Deuteronomy 5:16–20** (quoted in v. 20): Jesus recites the second-table Commandments governing neighborly fidelity, life, property, truth, and family honor.
- **Isaiah 50:6; 53:3–8 & Psalm 22:6–8** (background to vv. 31–33): Prophetic portraits of the suffering servant mocked, spat upon, scourged, and delivered to death.
- **2 Samuel 7:12–16 & Isaiah 35:5** (echoed in vv. 38–43): Calling Jesus "Son of David" links royal messianic expectation with the prophetic promise that the eyes of the blind will be opened.
