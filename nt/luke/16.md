## Clear & Faithful

**1** Jesus also told his disciples: "There was a certain rich man who had a steward, and charges were brought to him that this steward was squandering his property.
**2** Summoning him, the master asked, 'What is this I hear about you? Turn in the ledger of your management, for you can no longer serve as steward.'
**3** The steward thought to himself, 'What am I going to do now that my master is taking the stewardship away from me? I am not strong enough to dig, and I am too ashamed to beg.
**4** Ah, I know what I will do, so that when I am dismissed from the stewardship people will welcome me into their homes.'
**5** Calling in each of his master's debtors one by one, he asked the first, 'How much do you owe my master?'
**6** The man answered, 'A hundred measures of olive oil.' The steward told him, 'Take back your promissory note, sit down quickly, and write fifty.'
**7** Next he asked another, 'And how much do you owe?' He answered, 'A hundred sacks of wheat.' The steward told him, 'Take your promissory note and write eighty.'
**8** The master commended the dishonest steward because he had acted shrewdly; for the children of this age are more shrewd in dealing with their own generation than are the children of light.
**9** And I tell you: make friends for yourselves using worldly wealth, so that when it gives out, they may welcome you into eternal dwellings.
**10** Whoever is faithful in a very small matter is faithful also in much, and whoever is dishonest in a very small matter is dishonest also in much.
**11** If, then, you have not proved trustworthy with unrighteous wealth, who will entrust you with true riches?
**12** And if you have not proved trustworthy with what belongs to someone else, who will give you what is your own?
**13** No household servant can serve two masters; either he will hate the one and love the other, or he will be devoted to one and despise the other. You cannot serve both God and wealth."
**14** The Pharisees, who loved money, were listening to all these things and sneered at him.
**15** He said to them, "You are the ones who justify yourselves in the sight of people, yet God knows your hearts; for what is prized highly among human beings is detestable in God's sight.
**16** The Law and the Prophets were proclaimed until John; since then the good news of God's kingdom is being preached, and everyone is pressing forcefully into it.
**17** Yet sooner will sky and earth vanish than even a single pen-stroke of the Law fall away.
**18** Any man who sends away his wife to marry another commits adultery, and anyone who marries a woman sent away from her husband commits adultery.
**19** There was a certain rich man who dressed in purple robes and fine linen, living in luxury and feasting every single day.
**20** At his gate lay a poor beggar named Lazarus, covered with open sores,
**21** longing to satisfy his hunger with scraps falling from the rich man's table; even the dogs would come and lick his sores.
**22** In time the poor man died and was carried by the angels to Abraham's side; the rich man also died and was buried.
**23** In Hades, being in torment, he lifted up his eyes and saw Abraham far away with Lazarus by his side.
**24** He shouted out, 'Father Abraham, show mercy to me and dispatch Lazarus to dip his fingertip in water and cool my tongue, for I am tormented inside this blaze!'
**25** But Abraham answered, 'Child, remember that during your lifetime you received your good things, while Lazarus likewise received bad things; now he is comforted here, and you are in agony.
**26** Besides all this, a vast chasm has been permanently set between us and you, so that those who wish to cross from here to you cannot, nor can anyone cross from there to us.'
**27** Then he said, 'I beg you, father, send him to my father's house—
**28** for I have five brothers—so that he may warn them solemnly, lest they also come into this place of torment.'
**29** Abraham answered, 'They possess Moses and the prophets—let them pay heed to them.'
**30** He pleaded, 'No, father Abraham! But if someone from the dead goes to them, they will repent.'
**31** Abraham answered him, 'If they refuse to heed Moses and the prophets, neither will they be persuaded even though someone rises from the dead.'"

## Everyday English

**1** Jesus also shared this story with his disciples: "A wealthy businessman employed an estate manager, and reports reached the boss that this manager was wasting his assets.
**2** So the boss called him in and said, 'What's this I'm hearing about you? Hand over the account books—you're fired as my manager.'
**3** The manager thought to himself, 'What am I going to do now that my boss is taking my job away? I'm not tough enough for manual labor, and I'm too proud to beg on the street.
**4** Wait—I've got a plan! That way, once I'm out of a job, people will welcome me into their homes.'
**5** So he called in each of his boss's clients who owed money and asked the first one, 'How much do you owe my boss?'
**6** 'Eight hundred gallons of olive oil,' the man replied. The manager told him, 'Here's your bill—sit down quick and change it to four hundred.'
**7** Then he asked the next debtor, 'How much do you owe?' 'A thousand bushels of wheat,' he answered. 'Here's your bill,' said the manager. 'Change it to eight hundred.'
**8** Surprisingly, the boss praised that crooked manager for acting so cleverly. You see, people who belong to this world are sharper at dealing with their own kind than people of the light are!
**9** Here's my advice to you: use worldly money to build real friendships, so that when money runs out, those friends will welcome you into eternal homes.
**10** Anyone you can trust with tiny details can be trusted with big responsibilities; anyone who cheats on tiny details will cheat on big ones too.
**11** So if you haven't been trustworthy with shady earthly wealth, who's going to trust you with true spiritual treasure?
**12** And if you couldn't be trusted handling someone else's property, why would anyone hand you an inheritance of your own?
**13** No worker can serve two bosses at the same time. You'll end up resenting one and loving the other, or sticking by one and looking down on the other. You can't serve both God and money."
**14** The Pharisees, who were crazy about money, overheard all of this and scoffed at him.
**15** Jesus told them straight, "You're experts at making yourselves look righteous in front of people, but God sees straight into your hearts. The things human society applauds are sickening to God!
**16** The Law of Moses and the Prophets shaped the story right up until John arrived. Ever since then, the good news of God's kingdom is being announced, and everyone is rushing to break into it.
**17** Still, it's easier for sky and earth to disappear than for the tiniest pen stroke of the Law to fail.
**18** Any man who divorces his wife and marries someone else commits adultery—and any man who marries a divorced woman commits adultery too.
**19** Once there was a rich man who wore royal purple robes and expensive linen, throwing lavish parties every single day.
**20** Right outside his front gate lay a destitute beggar named Lazarus, whose skin was covered in painful sores.
**21** He was desperate just to eat the scraps brushed off the rich man's dining table, while stray dogs wandered up to lick his sores.
**22** Eventually the poor man died, and angels carried him away to rest in honor beside Abraham. The rich man died too, and had a proper burial.
**23** From the realm of the dead, while suffering terrible pain, he looked up and spotted Abraham far off with Lazarus resting right beside him.
**24** He shouted out, 'Father Abraham, take pity on me! Send Lazarus to dip just the tip of his finger in cool water and touch my tongue, because I'm agonizing in these flames!'
**25** Abraham answered, 'Son, remember that during your life on earth you enjoyed all your comforts, while Lazarus suffered misery. Now he's being comforted here, and you're the one in agony.
**26** On top of that, a massive canyon has been permanently fixed between us and you, so nobody can cross over from here to help you, and nobody can cross from your side to ours.'
**27** The man begged, 'Then please, father, send him to my dad's house—
**28** I still have five brothers! Let him warn them seriously so they don't wind up in this place of torment too.'
**29** 'They already have Moses and the Prophets,' Abraham replied. 'Let them listen to Scripture.'
**30** 'No, Father Abraham!' he insisted. 'If someone comes back from the dead to warn them, they'll change their ways!'
**31** Abraham replied, 'If they won't listen to Moses and the prophets, not even someone coming back from the dead will convince them.'"

## Crossings & Lexical Notes

- **v. 1 `οἰκονόμον` (*oikonomon*):** From *oikos* ("house") + *nemō* ("manage"), denoting an estate steward or chief business manager entrusted with broad authority over contracts and accounts.
- **v. 6 `βάτους` (*batous*) & v. 7 `κόρους` (*korous*):** Large Semitic dry/liquid measures transliterated into Greek (*batos* from Hebrew *bat*, ~30–40 liters of oil, so 100 baths is roughly 800–900 gallons; *koros* from Hebrew *kor*, ~350–400 liters of grain, so 100 cors is roughly 1,000 bushels).
- **v. 9 `μαμωνᾶ` (*mamōna*):** Transliteration of Aramaic *māmōnā* ("wealth, property, that in which one trusts"), personified in v. 13 as a rival master competing with God for allegiance. `WH`, `Treg`, and `NA28` read *eklipē* ("when it fails/runs out"), whereas `RP` reads *eklipēte* ("when you fail/die").
- **v. 14 `ἐξεμυκτήριζον` (*exemyktērizon*):** Literally "turned up their noses at / mocked derisively" (from *myktēr*, "nostril"), a vivid compound occurring only here and in Luke 23:35 in the NT.
- **v. 16 `βιάζεται` (*biazetai*):** Ambiguous voice (middle or passive): either "everyone forces their way / presses eagerly into it" (middle) or "it is forcefully urged / suffers opposition" (passive).
- **v. 17 `κεραίαν` (*keraian*):** Literally a "little horn," referring to the tiny ornamental serif or projecting stroke distinguishing similar Hebrew letters (e.g., *dalet* vs. *resh*, or *bet* vs. *kaf*).
- **v. 22 `κόλπον Ἀβραάμ` (*kolpon Abraam*):** "Bosom/lap of Abraham," evoking both intimate embrace and the honored reclining position next to the host at the messianic banquet (cf. Luke 13:29; John 13:23).
- **v. 23 `ᾅδῃ` (*hadē*):** Greek *Hades*, used in the Septuagint to translate Hebrew *Sheol*—here depicted as the intermediate realm of the dead prior to the final resurrection.

## Supplemental Texts & Allusions

- **Proverbs 16:5** (echoed in v. 15): *"Every arrogant heart is detestable to the Lord"*—underlies Jesus's rebuke that what human society exalts is detestable before God.
- **Malachi 4:4–5 & Isaiah 40:3** (background to v. 16): John marks the covenantal hinge between the era of Law and Prophets and the arrival of the kingdom's good news.
- **Genesis 15:2** (onomastic echo in v. 20): *Lazarus* is the Greek form of Hebrew *Elazar*/*Eleazar* ("God helps"), recalling Abraham's faithful servant Eliezer of Damascus—here the helpless beggar is carried to Abraham's side.
- **Isaiah 14:9–15 & Ezekiel 32:21** (imagery in vv. 23–26): Prophetic portrayals of *Sheol*/*Hades* as a realm where the dead speak across cosmic boundaries.
