## Clear & Faithful

**1** On a Sabbath while he was passing through grainfields, his disciples were plucking heads of grain and eating them, rubbing them in their hands.
**2** Some of the Pharisees asked, "Why are you doing what is not lawful on the Sabbath?"
**3** Jesus answered them, "Have you not even read what David did when he and those who were with him were hungry—
**4** how he entered the house of God, took the consecrated bread, ate it, and gave it to those with him, which is not lawful for anyone to eat except the priests alone?"
**5** And he told them, "The Son of Man holds lordship over the Sabbath."
**6** On another Sabbath he entered the synagogue and taught, and a man was there whose right hand was withered.
**7** The scribes and the Pharisees were watching him closely to see whether he would heal on the Sabbath, so that they might find grounds to accuse him.
**8** Knowing their unspoken thoughts, Jesus told the man with the shriveled hand, "Rise and step out into the center." So he rose and stood there.
**9** Then Jesus asked them, "Tell me, what is permitted on the Sabbath—doing good or doing evil, rescuing a life or ruining it?"
**10** Looking around at all of them, he said to him, "Stretch out your hand." He did so, and his hand was restored.
**11** But they were filled with fury and began discussing with one another what they might do to Jesus.
**12** During those days he climbed up the mountain to pray, and he passed the entire night in prayer to God.
**13** When day came, he called his disciples to him and chose twelve of them, whom he also named apostles:
**14** Simon, whom he named Peter as well; his brother Andrew; James along with John; Philip along with Bartholomew;
**15** Matthew and Thomas; James, son of Alphaeus, and Simon, known as the Zealot;
**16** Judas, son of James, along with Judas Iscariot, the one who turned into a betrayer.
**17** Descending with them, he stopped on a flat stretch of ground alongside a large crowd of his disciples and a vast gathering of people from every part of Judea, Jerusalem, and the seacoast of Tyre and Sidon,
**18** who had come to hear him and to be healed of their diseases; and those troubled by unclean spirits were cured.
**19** Everyone in the crowd was trying to touch him, because power was going out from him and healing them all.
**20** Lifting up his eyes toward his disciples, he began to say: "Blessed are you who are poor, for yours is the kingdom of God.
**21** Blessed are those of you who go hungry now, because you will be filled to satisfaction. Blessed are those of you who weep now, because you will laugh.
**22** Blessed are you when people hate you, when they exclude you and insult you and cast out your name as evil on account of the Son of Man.
**23** Be glad on that day and leap for joy, because your reward in heaven is abundant; their ancestors treated the prophets in that exact same manner.
**24** But woe to you who are rich, for you have already received your comfort in full.
**25** Woe to those of you who are stuffed now, because you will suffer hunger. Woe to you who laugh now, because you will grieve and weep.
**26** Woe to you when all people speak well of you, for in this very way their ancestors treated the false prophets.
**27** But to you who are listening I say: love your enemies, do good to those who hate you,
**28** speak blessing over those who curse you, and pray on behalf of those who mistreat you.
**29** To the one who strikes you on the cheek, offer the other as well; and from the one who takes your cloak, do not withhold your tunic either.
**30** Give to everyone who asks you, and from the one who takes what is yours, do not demand it back.
**31** And just as you wish people to do to you, do the same to them.
**32** If you show love only to people who love you, what credit belongs to you? Even sinners care for those who care for them.
**33** And if you do favors only for those who do favors for you, what credit is that to you? Even sinners act that way.
**34** And if you lend only to those from whom you count on repayment, what credit belongs to you? Even sinners lend to fellow sinners expecting to collect the full amount back.
**35** Rather, love your enemies, do good, and lend without giving up hope; then your reward will be rich, and you will be children of the Most High, since he is kind toward the ungrateful and the wicked.
**36** Be compassionate, just as your Father is compassionate.
**37** Do not judge, and you will not be judged; do not condemn, and you will not be condemned. Forgive, and you will be forgiven.
**38** Give, and gifts will be returned to you: a generous measure—packed down, shaken tight, and spilling over—will be poured into the fold of your robe. For the very standard you measure with will be used to measure back to you."
**39** He also told them a parable: "Can a blind person guide a blind person? Will not both fall into a pit?
**40** No student stands above his teacher; yet everyone who completes his training will become like his teacher.
**41** Why do you look at the speck in your brother's eye, yet fail to notice the beam in your own eye?
**42** How can you say to your brother, 'Brother, let me take out the speck that is in your eye,' when you yourself do not see the beam in your own eye? Hypocrite! First take the beam out of your own eye, and then you will see clearly to take out the speck that is in your brother's eye.
**43** No healthy tree yields spoiled fruit, nor conversely does a spoiled tree yield healthy fruit.
**44** Every tree is recognized by its own harvest: people do not pick figs off thorns, nor do they gather grapes from a bramble bush.
**45** A good person brings forth good out of the good treasure of his heart, and an evil person brings forth evil out of the evil treasure; for out of the abundance of the heart his mouth speaks.
**46** Why do you call me, 'Lord, Lord,' and not do what I say?
**47** As for everyone who comes to me, listens to my teachings, and puts them into practice—let me show you whom that person resembles:
**48** he is like a man building a house, who dug deep and laid a foundation on bedrock. When a flood came, the torrent struck against that house and could not shake it, because it had been well built.
**49** But the one who hears and does not act is like a man who built a house on the ground without a foundation. The torrent struck against it, and immediately it collapsed, and the ruin of that house was complete."

## Everyday English

**1** One Sabbath, Jesus was walking along paths through grainfields, and his disciples were picking heads of grain, rubbing off the husks in their hands, and eating them.
**2** A few Pharisees confronted them: "Why are you breaking the law by doing that on the Sabbath?"
**3** Jesus answered them, "Haven't you ever read what David did when he and his companions were starving?
**4** He walked right into God's house, took the sacred bread of the Presence, ate it himself, and shared it with his men—even though the law only allowed priests to eat it!"
**5** Then he told them, "The Son of Man has authority over the Sabbath."
**6** On a different Sabbath, Jesus went into the synagogue and started teaching. A man was sitting there whose right hand was shriveled and useless.
**7** The legal experts and Pharisees kept their eyes locked on Jesus to see if he'd heal on the Sabbath, hoping to catch him in a charge they could bring against him.
**8** Knowing exactly what they were scheming, Jesus told the man with the crippled hand, "Stand up and step out into the middle." The man stood up and stepped forward.
**9** Then Jesus put them on the spot: "Answer me this—on the Sabbath, is it lawful to help someone or hurt them, to rescue a life or destroy it?"
**10** He looked around at every face in the room, then told the man, "Hold out your hand." The man stretched it out, and his hand was completely healed.
**11** That drove his opponents into a blind fury, and they started plotting together what they could do to Jesus.
**12** Around that time, Jesus climbed up a mountainside to pray, and he stayed there all night long in prayer to God.
**13** At dawn, he called his followers together and picked twelve of them, whom he also named apostles:
**14** Simon (whom he renamed Peter), his brother Andrew, James, John, Philip, Bartholomew,
**15** Matthew, Thomas, James (Alphaeus's son), Simon (known as the Zealot),
**16** Judas (James's son), and Judas Iscariot, who later betrayed him.
**17** Jesus walked down the mountain with them and stopped on a flat stretch of ground. A huge crowd of his followers was there, along with massive numbers of people from all over Judea, Jerusalem, and the coastal towns of Tyre and Sidon,
**18** who had traveled to hear him teach and be cured of their sicknesses. Even people tormented by evil spirits were set free.
**19** Everybody in the crowd tried to touch him, because healing power was pouring out of him and curing them all.
**20** Looking directly at his disciples, Jesus began to speak: "God blesses you who are poor, because God's kingdom belongs to you!
**21** God blesses you who are hungry right now, because you'll be filled! God blesses you who are crying right now, because you'll laugh!
**22** God blesses you whenever people hate you, shut you out, insult you, and smear your reputation as wicked because you belong to the Son of Man!
**23** Celebrate on that day and jump for joy—your reward in heaven is huge! Their ancestors treated the prophets the exact same way.
**24** But watch out, you who are rich—you've already cashed in all your comfort!
**25** Watch out, you who have stuffed yourselves now—you're going to go hungry! Watch out, you who are laughing now—you'll weep and grieve!
**26** Watch out when everybody praises you—their ancestors flattered the false prophets the exact same way!
**27** Now listen closely, all of you who are paying attention: love your enemies! Do good to people who hate you!
**28** Bless people who curse you, and pray for those who treat you cruelly!
**29** If someone slaps you on one cheek, turn the other cheek to them too. If someone grabs your coat, don't stop them from taking your shirt as well.
**30** Give to anyone who begs from you, and if someone takes what belongs to you, don't demand it back.
**31** Treat other people the exact way you want them to treat you.
**32** If you only love people who love you back, what's special about that? Even godless people love their friends!
**33** And if you're only kind to people who are kind to you, what's remarkable about that? Even godless people do that much!
**34** And if you only lend money to folks you expect to pay you back, what credit do you get? Even godless people lend to each other expecting full repayment!
**35** Instead, love your enemies, do good to them, and lend without expecting anything in return. Then your reward will be enormous, and you'll truly act like children of the Most High, because he shows kindness even to ungrateful and wicked people.
**36** Show mercy and compassion, just like your Father shows compassion!
**37** Stop judging others, and you won't be judged. Stop condemning others, and you won't be condemned. Forgive others, and you'll be forgiven.
**38** Give generously, and generosity will come back to you—a full basket, packed down, shaken to make room, and spilling over into your lap! Whatever standard you use for others will be used right back on you."
**39** Jesus also told them a story: "Can one blind person lead another blind person? Won't both of them tumble straight into a ditch?
**40** A student doesn't outrank their teacher, though once fully trained, every student becomes like their teacher.
**41** Why do you stare at the tiny splinter in your brother's eye while ignoring the huge wooden plank stuck in your own eye?
**42** How can you say to your brother, 'Hey brother, let me pull that speck out of your eye,' when you can't even see the log in your own? You hypocrite! Pull the log out of your own eye first—then you'll see clearly enough to remove the speck from your brother's eye.
**43** A healthy tree doesn't grow rotten fruit, and a diseased tree doesn't grow healthy fruit.
**44** Every tree is recognized by the fruit it produces. People don't pick figs off thornbushes or harvest grapes from brambles!
**45** A good person brings good things out of the good stored inside their heart, while an evil person brings evil out of the evil stored inside—because your mouth speaks whatever overflows from your heart.
**46** Why do you keep calling me, 'Lord, Lord,' when you refuse to do what I tell you?
**47** Let me show you what anyone is like who comes to me, listens to my words, and puts them into practice:
**48** they're like a builder who dug deep into the ground and set the house's foundation on solid rock. When a flash flood hit, the raging river slammed against that house, yet couldn't budge it because it was solidly built.
**49** But anyone who listens to my words and ignores them is like someone who built a house straight on bare dirt with no foundation. The moment the river slammed into it, it caved in completely—and the wreckage was total."

## Crossings & Lexical Notes

- **v. 1 `ἐν σαββάτῳ` (*en sabbatō*):** "On a Sabbath" (`WH`, `Treg`, `NA28`), whereas `RP` adds the rare adjective `δευτεροπρώτῳ` (*deuteroprōtō*, "second-first"), likely an early marginal gloss harmonizing Sabbath chronology.
- **v. 4 `ὡς` (*hōs*):** "How" (`WH`, `Treg`, `RP`, `NA28`), omitted by a minor textual tradition (`om` in `Luke 6:4` apparatus). Here `τοὺς ἄρτους τῆς προθέσεως` (*tous artous tēs protheseōs*, "loaves of the presentation") translates the Hebrew *lechem hapanim* ("bread of the Presence").
- **v. 12 `ἐν τῇ προσευχῇ τοῦ θεοῦ` (*en tē proseuchē tou theou*):** Literally "in the prayer of God" (objective genitive, "prayer to God").
- **v. 17 `τόπου πεδινοῦ` (*topou pedinou*):** "A level place" or "plain" (contrast Matthew's "mountain" in Matt 5:1), giving Luke's discourse its traditional name, the "Sermon on the Plain."
- **v. 24 `ἀπέχετε` (*apechete*):** Commercial receipt verb ("you have received in full," leaving no balance due).
- **v. 35 `μηδὲν ἀπελπίζοντες` (*mēden apelpizontes*):** "Nothing despairing" or "expecting nothing in return" (the latter sense is standard in Hellenistic medical and commercial prose for *apelpizō* when paired with lending in v. 34).
- **v. 38 `εἰς τὸν κόλπον ὑμῶν` (*eis ton kolpon hymōn*):** *Kolpos* ("bosom/fold") refers to the pouch formed by a belted outer garment above the waist, used to carry measured grain home from market.
- **v. 48 `διὰ τὸ καλῶς οἰκοδομῆσθαι αὐτήν` (*dia to kalōs oikodomēsthai autēn*):** "Because it had been well built" (`WH`, `Treg`, `NA28`); `RP` reads `τεθεμελίωτο γὰρ ἐπὶ τὴν πέτραν` ("for it had been founded on the rock"), harmonized from Matt 7:25.

## Supplemental Texts & Allusions

- **1 Samuel 21:1–6 & Leviticus 24:5–9** (cited in vv. 3–4): David and his hungry companions eating the consecrated bread of the Presence reserved for the priests.
- **Isaiah 61:1–3** (echoed in vv. 20–21): Good news to the poor and comfort for those who mourn, linking Luke 6's Beatitudes back to Jesus's programmatic Nazareth reading in Luke 4:18.
- **Leviticus 19:18 & Proverbs 25:21–22** (expanded in vv. 27–36): Extending covenant neighbor-love to enemies in imitation of God's own mercy (*oiktirmones*, cf. Exod 34:6).

**43** “For there is no good tree that produces rotten fruit, nor again a rotten tree that produces good fruit.
**44** For each tree is known by its own fruit. For people don’t gather figs from thorns, nor do they gather grapes from a bramble bush.
**45** The good man out of the good treasure of his heart brings out that which is good, and the evil man out of the evil treasure of his heart brings out that which is evil, for out of the abundance of the heart, his mouth speaks.
**46** “Why do you call me, ‘Lord, Lord,’ and don’t do the things which I say?
**47** Everyone who comes to me, and hears my words and does them, I will show you who he is like.
**48** He is like a man building a house, who dug and went deep and laid a foundation on the rock. When a flood arose, the stream broke against that house, and could not shake it, because it was founded on the rock.
**49** But he who hears and doesn’t do, is like a man who built a house on the earth without a foundation, against which the stream broke, and immediately it fell; and the ruin of that house was great.”
