## Everyday English

**1** It was the fifteenth year of Emperor Tiberius Caesar's rule. Pontius Pilate was governor of Judea, Herod ruled Galilee, his brother Philip ruled the region of Iturea and Trachonitis, and Lysanias ruled Abilene.
**2** Annas and Caiaphas were serving as high priests when God's message came to Zechariah's son John out in the desert.
**3** John traveled all through the Jordan valley, announcing a baptism of turning away from sin so people could be forgiven.
**4** This matched what was written in the prophet Isaiah's scroll: "A voice shouts in the desert: 'Clear the road for the Lord! Make his paths straight!
**5** Every deep valley will be filled in, every mountain and hill leveled down. Winding trails will straighten out, and rocky paths will become smooth highways.
**6** Everyone on earth will witness God's rescue!'"
**7** When crowds walked out to be baptized by him, John warned them, "You nest of snakes! Who tipped you off to run from the judgment that's coming?
**8** Show by how you live that you've genuinely repented! Don't hide behind the excuse, 'Abraham is our ancestor!' Let me tell you, God can turn these stones right here into children for Abraham!
**9** The axe is already resting at the base of the trees. Every tree that doesn't grow good fruit is going to be chopped down and tossed into the fire."
**10** "Well then, what are we supposed to do?" the crowds asked him.
**11** "If you've got two shirts, share one with someone who has none," John replied, "and if you've got food, share it too."
**12** Even tax collectors showed up to be baptized and asked, "Teacher, what should we do?"
**13** "Don't collect a single coin more than the law requires," he told them.
**14** Some soldiers on active duty also asked, "And what about us?" John answered, "Stop shaking people down with threats or framing them with false charges, and be satisfied with your regular pay."
**15** Everyone was buzzing with anticipation, quietly wondering whether John himself might turn out to be the Messiah.
**16** So John spoke up to all of them: "I'm baptizing you with water, yet someone far stronger than I am is on his way—I'm not even good enough to untie his sandal straps. He'll baptize you with the Holy Spirit and with fire!
**17** His winnowing shovel is ready in his hand to clean up his threshing floor—gathering the wheat safely into his barn while burning up the husks in a fire that never goes out."
**18** With many other warnings and encouragement like this, John kept preaching the good news to the people.
**19** But Ruler Herod—whom John had confronted for taking his brother's wife Herodias and for all his other corrupt deeds—
**20** topped off all his crimes by throwing John in prison.
**21** Now while all the people were getting baptized, Jesus was baptized too. As he stood praying, the sky opened up,
**22** and the Holy Spirit came down on him in physical form like a dove. Then a voice spoke from heaven: "You are my dearly loved Son; you bring me great joy."
**23** Jesus was around thirty years old when he started his public work. People assumed he was the son of Joseph, whose father was Eli,
**24** and before him Matthat, Levi, Melchi, Jannai, and Joseph,
**25** going back through Mattathias, Amos, Nahum, Esli, and Naggai,
**26** descended from Maath, Mattathias, Semein, Josech, and Joda,
**27** tracing back through Joanan, Rhesa, Zerubbabel, Shealtiel, and Neri,
**28** in the line of Melchi, Addi, Cosam, Elmadam, and Er,
**29** whose forebears were Joshua, Eliezer, Jorim, Matthat, and Levi,
**30** and earlier Simeon, Judah, Joseph, Jonam, and Eliakim,
**31** going back through Melea, Menna, Mattatha, Nathan, and David,
**32** descended from Jesse, Obed, Boaz, Sala, and Nahshon,
**33** from the line of Amminadab, Admin, Arni, Hezron, Perez, and Judah,
**34** whose forebears were Jacob, Isaac, Abraham, Terah, and Nahor,
**35** and before them Serug, Reu, Peleg, Eber, and Shelah,
**36** tracing back through Cainan, Arphaxad, Shem, Noah, and Lamech,
**37** born to Methuselah, Enoch, Jared, Mahalaleel, and Cainan,
**38** going all the way back to Enosh, Seth, and Adam, who came from God.

## Crossings & Lexical Notes

- **v. 1 `τετρααρχοῦντος` (*tetraarchountos*):** "Serving as tetrarch," originally ruler of a fourth part of a territory, used for Roman client rulers such as Herod Antipas (Galilee) and Philip (Iturea and Trachonitis).
- **v. 3 `μετανοίας` (*metanoias*):** "Repentance," a decisive change of mind and moral direction, paired in v. 8 with "fruits worthy of repentance."
- **v. 14 `διασείσητε` (*diaseisēte*) & `συκοφαντήσητε` (*sykophantēsēte*):** *Diaseiō* ("shake violently") denotes extortion by intimidation; *sykophanteō* denotes blackmail or false accusation for profit; *opsōnia* refers to military rations or wages.
- **v. 17 `πτύον` (*ptyon*):** A long-handled wooden winnowing shovel used to toss threshed grain into the breeze so wind carries off the lighter chaff (*achyron*) while heavy wheat falls back onto the threshing floor.
- **v. 23 `ἀρχόμενος` (*archomenos*):** "Beginning [his public ministry]" at roughly thirty years of age (comparable to Levitical service age in Num 4:3, 47); `ὡς ἐνομίζετο` (*hōs enomizeto*, "as was supposed/legally reckoned") preserves Joseph's legal paternity while respecting the virgin conception in Luke 1:34–35.
- **v. 32–33 `Σαλὰ` (*Sala*) & `Ἀμιναδὰβ τοῦ Ἀδμὶν τοῦ Ἀρνὶ` (*Aminadab tou Admin tou Arni*):** In v. 32, `WH` and `NA28` read `Σαλὰ` (*Sala*), whereas `Treg` and `RP` read `Σαλμών` (*Salmōn*). In v. 33, `NA28` reads `Ἀμιναδὰβ τοῦ Ἀδμὶν τοῦ Ἀρνὶ`; `WH` omits `Ἀμιναδὰβ τοῦ`, while `Treg` and `RP` read `Ἀμιναδὰβ τοῦ Ἀράμ` (*Aram*).
- **v. 36 `τοῦ Καϊνὰμ` (*tou Kainam*):** Follows the Greek Septuagint genealogy of Gen 10:24; 11:12–13 LXX, which includes a second Cainan between Arphaxad and Shelah.

## Supplemental Texts & Allusions

- **Isaiah 40:3–5 (LXX)** (quoted in vv. 4–6): Luke extends the quotation farther than Matthew and Mark to reach "all flesh shall see the salvation (*to sōtērion*) of God."
- **Psalm 2:7 & Isaiah 42:1** (echoed in v. 22): The heavenly voice combines the royal coronation declaration ("You are my Son") with the Servant's commissioning ("in whom my soul delights").
- **Genesis 5:1–32; 11:10–26; Ruth 4:18–22; 1 Chronicles 1:1–2:15** (underlying vv. 23–38): Traces Jesus's line backward from Nathan son of David all the way to Adam and God, highlighting universal humanity.
