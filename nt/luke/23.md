**1** Then the whole assembly rose up and brought Jesus before Pilate.
**2** They began bringing charges against him, saying, "We found this man misleading our nation, opposing the payment of taxes to Caesar, and claiming that he himself is Christ, a king."
**3** Pilate questioned him, "Are you the Judean King?" Jesus replied to him, "Those are your words."
**4** Pilate declared to the chief priests and the assembled crowds, "I discover no grounds for a charge against this man."
**5** Yet they kept insisting, "He stirs up the people by teaching throughout all Judea, starting from Galilee all the way to here."
**6** Hearing this, Pilate asked whether the man was a Galilean,
**7** and upon learning that he fell under Herod's jurisdiction, he sent him over to Herod, who himself was in Jerusalem during those days.
**8** When Herod saw Jesus, he was delighted, for he had been wanting to see him for a long time because of what he had heard about him, and he was hoping to watch him perform some miracle.
**9** He questioned him at considerable length, yet Jesus gave him no answer at all.
**10** Meanwhile, the chief priests and scribes stood nearby, pressing fierce accusations against him.
**11** Then Herod, together with his soldiers, treated him with contempt and mocked him; dressing him in an ornate robe, he sent him back to Pilate.
**12** That very day Herod and Pilate became friends with each other, whereas previously they had been in hostility toward one another.
**13** Pilate then summoned the chief priests, the rulers, and the people,
**14** and said to them, "You brought this man before me as one inciting the people to rebellion; yet look, after examining him in your presence, I have found nothing in this man to support the charges you bring against him.
**15** Neither has Herod, for he sent him back to us. Look, nothing deserving death has been done by him.
**16** Therefore, after having him flogged, I will release him."
**18** Yet they all shouted out together, "Away with this man! Release Barabbas for us!"—
**19** a man who had been thrown into prison for an insurrection that occurred in the city and for murder.
**20** Wanting to release Jesus, Pilate addressed them again,
**21** but they kept shouting back, "Crucify, crucify him!"
**22** A third time he asked them, "Why? What crime has this man committed? I have found no ground for death in him; therefore I will punish him and release him."
**23** Yet they pressed hard with loud cries, demanding that he be crucified, and their voices prevailed.
**24** So Pilate pronounced sentence that their demand be granted:
**25** he released the man thrown into prison for insurrection and murder whom they were demanding, and handed Jesus over to their will.
**26** As they led him away, they seized a certain Simon of Cyrene who was coming in from the countryside, and laid the cross on him to carry behind Jesus.
**27** A great crowd of people followed him, including women who were mourning and wailing over him.
**28** Turning toward them, Jesus spoke: "Daughters of Jerusalem, stop weeping over me; weep instead over yourselves and over your children.
**29** Truly, days are arriving when people will exclaim, 'Blessed are the childless women, the wombs that never conceived, and the breasts that never gave milk!'
**30** At that hour people will cry out to the mountains, 'Collapse upon us!' and plead with the hills, 'Bury us from sight!'
**31** For if they do these things when the wood is green, what will happen when it is dry?"
**32** Two others, both criminals, were also led out with him to be executed.
**33** When they reached the place called The Skull, there they crucified him along with the criminals, one on his right and one on his left.
**34** And Jesus was saying, "Father, forgive them, for they do not know what they are doing." Then they divided his garments by casting lots.
**35** The crowd stood gazing on, while the leaders sneered at him, saying, "He rescued others—let him rescue himself if this man truly is God's Messiah, the Chosen One!"
**36** The soldiers also came up and mocked him, offering him sour wine
**37** mocking him: "If you really are the Jewish King, rescue yourself!"
**38** There was also an inscription above him: "This is the King of the Jews."
**39** One of the criminals hanging there kept hurling insults at him, saying, "Are you not the Christ? Save yourself and us!"
**40** But the other rebuked him, saying, "Do you not even fear God, since you are under the very same sentence?
**41** We indeed justly so, for we are receiving what our deeds deserve; yet this man has done nothing wrong."
**42** Then he appealed, "Jesus, keep me in mind when you enter into your royal kingdom."
**43** Jesus assured him, "In truth I promise you, today you will stand with me inside Paradise."

## Crossings & Lexical Notes

- **v. 11 `ἐσθῆτα λαμπράν` (*esthēta lampran*):** Literally "shining/radiant attire," a white or dazzling royal garment draped on Jesus in mockery (`NA28` includes *kai* before *ho Hērōdēs*; `WH` and `NA28` omit *auton* after *peribalōn*, which `Treg` and `RP` retain).
- **v. 16 `παιδεύσας` (*paideusas*):** From *paideuō* ("educate/chastise/scourge"), used here in Roman judicial practice for a sharp disciplinary beating intended as a warning prior to release.
- **v. 17 `Ἀνάγκην δὲ εἶχεν ἀπολύειν αὐτοῖς κατὰ ἑορτὴν ἕνα` (*Anankēn de eichen apolyein autois kata heortēn hena*):** Omitted in SBLGNT (`WH`, `Treg`, `NA28`) and Nestle 1904; `RP` and `TR` add this verse ("Now he had necessity to release one to them at each festival"), likely harmonized from Matt 27:15 and Mark 15:6.
- **v. 34 `ὁ δὲ Ἰησοῦς ἔλεγεν· Πάτερ, ἄφες αὐτοῖς, οὐ γὰρ οἴδασιν τί ποιοῦσιν` (*ho de Iēsous elegen: Pater, aphes autois, ou gar oidasin ti poiousin*):** Retained by `Treg`, `NA28`, `RP`, and Nestle 1904, whereas `⟦WH⟧` double-brackets this prayer because it is omitted in several early witnesses (including P75, Vaticanus, and Codex Bezae) while strongly attested in Sinaiticus, early patristic writers, and Western/Byzantine streams.
- **v. 43 `παραδείσῳ` (*paradeisō*):** Persian loanword (*pairidaēza*, "walled garden/park") adopted in the Septuagint for the Garden of Eden (Gen 2:8), signifying immediate conscious fellowship with Jesus after death.
- **v. 45 `τοῦ ἡλίου ἐκλιπόντος` (*tou hēliou eklipontos*):** Genitive absolute from *ekleipō* ("the sun failing/darkening," the technical term for a solar eclipse, though impossible astronomically at Passover full moon—hence `WH` and `NA28` preserve this vivid phrasing, whereas `Treg` and `RP` read *kai eskotisthē ho hēlios*, "and the sun was darkened").
- **v. 47 `δίκαιος` (*dikaios*):** Means both "righteous/just" and legally "innocent" (echoing Pilate's repeated verdict "no basis for guilt," vv. 4, 14, 22, and the penitent criminal's "nothing wrong," v. 41).
- **v. 53 `αὐτὸν` (*auton*):** SBLGNT, `WH`, `Treg`, and `NA28` read masculine *auton* ("laid *him*"), personalizing Jesus even in death, whereas `RP` reads neuter *auto* ("it," referring to *to sōma*, "the body").

## Supplemental Texts & Allusions

- **Hosea 10:8** (quoted in v. 30): *"People will cry to the mountains, 'Hide us,' and to the hills, 'Collapse over us'"*—prophetic cry of terror amid impending judgment on Jerusalem.
- **Psalm 22:18 (21:19 LXX)** (enacted in v. 34): *"They split my clothes among themselves and threw dice for my robe."*
- **Psalm 69:21 (68:22 LXX)** (echoed in v. 36): *"In my thirst they offered me vinegar (oxos) to drink."*
- **Psalm 31:5 (30:6 LXX)** (quoted in v. 46): *"Into your care I place my spirit"*—Jesus dies with Israel's evening trust prayer on his lips, adding "Father."
- **Exodus 20:8–11** (followed in v. 56): The women observe the fourth commandment's Sabbath rest even amid their grief and burial preparations.

**50** Behold, there was a man named Joseph, who was a member of the council, a good and righteous man
**51** (he had not consented to their counsel and deed), from Arimathaea, a city of the Jews, who was also waiting for God’s Kingdom.
**52** This man went to Pilate, and asked for Jesus’ body.
**53** He took it down and wrapped it in a linen cloth, and laid him in a tomb that was cut in stone, where no one had ever been laid.
**54** It was the day of the Preparation, and the Sabbath was drawing near.
**55** The women who had come with him out of Galilee followed after, and saw the tomb and how his body was laid.
**56** They returned and prepared spices and ointments. On the Sabbath they rested according to the commandment.
