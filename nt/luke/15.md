## Clear & Faithful

**1** All the tax collectors and sinners were gathering close to listen to Jesus.
**2** Both the Pharisees and the scribes kept muttering in protest, "This man welcomes sinners and eats meals with them!"
**3** He therefore addressed this parable to them:
**4** "Which man among you, having a hundred sheep and losing one of them, does not leave the ninety-nine behind in the open pasture and go after the lost one until he finds it?
**5** And when he finds it, he joyfully lifts it onto his shoulders,
**6** goes home, and calls his friends and neighbors together, saying to them, 'Celebrate with me, because I have found my sheep that was lost!'
**7** I tell you, just like that, greater joy will fill heaven over a single sinner who turns in repentance than over ninety-nine upright people who require no repentance.
**8** Or what woman having ten silver coins, if she loses one coin, does not light a lamp, sweep the house, and search diligently until she finds it?
**9** When she finds it, she calls her women friends and neighbors together and says, 'Celebrate with me, because I have found the coin that I lost!'
**10** In the same way, I tell you, there is joy in the presence of God's angels over one sinner who repents."
**11** He went on, "There was a man who had two sons.
**12** The younger son said to his father, 'Father, hand over the portion of the estate coming to me.' So the father split his assets between the two of them.
**13** Not many days later, the younger son gathered everything together, traveled away to a distant country, and squandered his wealth there in reckless living.
**14** When he had spent everything, a severe famine struck throughout that region, and he began to go hungry.
**15** So he went off and hired himself out to a citizen of that region, who dispatched him to his fields to tend hogs.
**16** He longed to fill his stomach with the carob pods the pigs were eating, and no one gave him anything.
**17** Coming to his senses, he said, 'How many of my father's hired workers have more than enough bread, while I am dying of hunger right here!
**18** I will rise, return to my father, and tell him, "Father, I have sinned against heaven and in your presence.
**19** I am no longer worthy to be called your son; treat me like one of your hired workers."'
**20** So he got up and set out for his father. While he was still a long way off, his father saw him and was deeply moved with compassion; he ran, threw his arms around his neck, and kissed him warmly.
**21** The son began, 'Father, I have sinned against heaven and in your presence; I am no longer fit to be called your son.'
**22** The father, however, directed his servants, 'Hurry, bring out the best robe and dress him in it! Place a ring on his hand and sandals on his feet.
**23** Fetch the grain-fed calf and butcher it; let us feast and rejoice!
**24** For this son of mine was dead and has come back to life; he was lost and has been found.' And they began to celebrate.
**25** Meanwhile, his older son was out in the field. As he came back and approached the house, he heard music and dancing.
**26** Calling one of the young servants over, he asked what all this meant.
**27** The servant told him, 'Your brother has come home, and your father has slaughtered the fattened calf because he got him back safe and sound.'
**28** The older brother grew angry and refused to go inside. His father came out and kept pleading with him.
**29** Answering his father, he said, 'Look! All these years I have slaved for you and never disobeyed a single command of yours, yet you never even gave me a young goat so that I could celebrate with my friends.
**30** But the moment this son of yours arrives—after devouring your livelihood with prostitutes—you slaughter the fattened calf for him!'
**31** The father answered him, 'My child, you are always with me, and everything I have is yours.
**32** Yet we had to celebrate and rejoice, because this brother of yours was dead and is alive again; he was lost and has been found.'"

## Everyday English

**1** Crowds of tax collectors and people with bad reputations kept coming up close to listen to Jesus.
**2** The Pharisees and religious teachers grumbled constantly about it: "Look at him—welcoming sinners and even sharing meals with them!"
**3** So Jesus shared this story with them:
**4** "Suppose one of you owns a hundred sheep and loses a single one. Won't you leave the other ninety-nine out on the range and go search for the missing sheep until you track it down?
**5** When you finally find it, you hoist it happily onto your shoulders,
**6** carry it home, and call your friends and neighbors over: 'Come party with me—I found my lost sheep!'
**7** Believe me, heaven throws an even bigger celebration over one sinner who turns back to God than over ninety-nine good people who don't think they need to change.
**8** Or think about a woman who owns ten silver coins and loses one. Doesn't she light a lamp, sweep every corner of the house, and hunt carefully until she spots it?
**9** Once she finds it, she gathers her girlfriends and neighbors: 'Come celebrate with me! I found the silver coin I lost!'
**10** I'm telling you, God's angels celebrate with that exact same joy over a single sinner who turns back to God."
**11** Jesus continued with another story: "A man had two sons.
**12** One day the younger son told his father, 'Dad, give me my share of the inheritance right now.' So the father split up his estate between his two boys.
**13** A few days later, the younger son packed up everything he owned, moved to a faraway country, and blew all his money on wild living.
**14** Right after he'd spent every last cent, a terrible famine hit that entire region, and he started starving.
**15** Desperate, he hired himself out to a local citizen, who put him to work out in the fields slopping pigs.
**16** He was so starved he wished he could stuff himself on the pig pods, and nobody gave him a bite.
**17** Finally he woke up to reality and thought, 'Look how many hired hands on my dad's farm have all the food they can eat—plus leftovers—while I'm starving to death out here!
**18** I'm going to get up, head back home to my dad, and tell him, "Dad, I've sinned against God and against you.
**19** I don't deserve to be called your son anymore; just take me on as one of your hired hands."'
**20** So he got up and started the long walk home to his father. While he was still far down the road, his father spotted him. His heart broke with love—he sprinted out, threw his arms around his boy's neck, and kissed him over and over.
**21** The son started in: 'Dad, I've sinned against God and against you. I don't deserve to be called your son anymore—'
**22** But the father called out to his servants, 'Hurry! Bring out the best robe and dress him in it! Put a signet ring on his finger and sandals on his feet!
**23** Bring out the prize fattened calf and butcher it—we're throwing a feast to celebrate!
**24** This son of mine was as good as dead, and now he's alive again! He was lost, and now he's home!' And the party got underway.
**25** Meanwhile, the older son had been working out in the fields. As he walked back toward the house, he heard live music and dancing.
**26** He flagged down one of the servant boys and asked what was going on.
**27** The boy answered, 'Your brother's back! Your dad butchered the prize fattened calf because he's home safe and healthy.'
**28** That made the older brother furious, and he refused to step inside. So his father came out and begged him to join them.
**29** But he snapped back at his father, 'Look here! I've slaved for you all these years and never disobeyed a single order of yours—and you never even gave me a young goat so I could throw a party with my friends!
**30** Yet the second this son of yours shows up after burning through your estate on prostitutes, you butcher the fattened calf for him!'
**31** His father said gently, 'Son, you're always right here with me, and everything I own belongs to you.
**32** But we simply had to throw a feast and be glad—because this brother of yours was dead and came back to life; he was lost and has been found!'"

## Crossings & Lexical Notes

- **v. 2 `διεγόγγυζον` (*diegongyzon*):** Imperfect of *diagongyzō* ("kept murmuring/grumbling throughout"), an onomatopoetic verb echoing Israel's wilderness grumbling in the Septuagint (Exod 16:2; Num 14:2).
- **v. 8 `δραχμάς` (*drachmas*):** Silver Greek *drachma* coins, roughly equivalent to a Roman *denarius* (a day's wage for a laborer); losing one out of ten represents a painful loss of household savings.
- **v. 12 `οὐσίας` (*ousias*) & `βίον` (*bion*):** *Ousia* means "property/estate," while *bios* means "livelihood/means of life," underscoring that the father's estate is his very living divided prematurely.
- **v. 16 `γεμίσαι τὴν κοιλίαν αὐτοῦ ἀπὸ` (*gemisai tēn koilian autou apo*):** Printed by SBLGNT, `Treg`, and `RP` ("fill his belly from"); `WH` and `NA28` read *chortasthēnai ek* ("be satisfied/filled from"). *Keratia* are the sweetish brown pods of the carob tree (*Ceratonia siliqua*), used as cheap animal fodder and famine food.
- **v. 20 `ἐσπλαγχνίσθη` (*esplanchnisthē*):** From *splanchnizomai* ("be moved in one's inward parts/viscera"), expressing visceral, gut-deep parental compassion, matched by *katephilēsen* ("kissed repeatedly/fervently").
- **v. 21 `υἱός σου` (*huios sou*):** SBLGNT, `Treg`, `NA28`, and `RP` end the son's speech here (the father interrupts before the prepared "hired-worker" plea of v. 19 is repeated), whereas `WH` retains *poiēson me hōs hena tōn misthiōn sou* ("make me as one of your hired workers").
- **v. 25 `συμφωνίας` (*symphōnias*):** Literally "sounding together" (musical ensemble or bagpipe-like instrument accompanying festive *choroi*, "circle dances").
- **v. 32 `ἔζησεν` (*ezēsen*):** SBLGNT, `WH`, `Treg`, and `NA28` read simple aorist *ezēsen* ("lived / came to life"), whereas `RP` harmonizes to *anezēsen* ("came to life again"), matching v. 24.

## Supplemental Texts & Allusions

- **Ezekiel 34:11–16** (echoed in vv. 4–6): The Lord promises as Israel's true shepherd to seek out the scattered sheep, search for the lost, and bring them back safely.
- **Deuteronomy 21:18–21** (contrasted in vv. 13, 20–24): Instead of the Mosaic penalty of public shaming and stoning for a rebellious, gluttonous son, the father runs out in mercy and restores him with robe, ring, and feast.
- **Genesis 33:4 & 45:14–15** (echoed in v. 20): Falling on the neck and weeping/kissing recalls Jacob and Esau's reconciliation and Joseph embracing his brothers in Egypt.
- **Hosea 6:2 & Ezekiel 37:1–14** (echoed in vv. 24, 32): Returning from exile and spiritual ruin is framed as rising from death to life.
