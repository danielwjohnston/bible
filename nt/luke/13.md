## Clear & Faithful

**1** At that very time, some people were present who told Jesus about the Galileans whose blood Pilate had mingled with their sacrifices.
**2** Responding to them, he asked, "Do you suppose those Galileans were greater sinners than every other Galilean because they suffered such a fate?
**3** No, I tell you; unless you repent, you will all perish just as they did.
**4** Or take those eighteen people killed when the Siloam tower collapsed upon them—do you imagine they stood under greater moral debt than everyone else inhabiting Jerusalem?
**5** Not at all, I assure you; rather, if you fail to repent, all of you will perish in that very same manner."
**6** He went on to share this parable: "A man owned a fig tree planted inside his vineyard, and he arrived searching for fruit on it yet found none.
**7** So he instructed the vineyard keeper, 'Look here—for three years straight I have come searching for figs on this tree and found nothing. Chop it down! Why let it go on wasting the soil?'
**8** But the vinedresser replied to him, 'Sir, leave it alone for this year as well, until I dig around it and put manure on it.
**9** If it bears fruit in the coming year, fine; if not, then you can cut it down.'"
**10** One Sabbath Jesus was teaching in one of the synagogues,
**11** and there was a woman who for eighteen years had been crippled by a spirit of infirmity; she was bent double and unable to straighten up at all.
**12** Spotting her, Jesus summoned her over and announced, "Woman, you stand released from your ailment."
**13** He laid his hands on her, and at once she stood straight and began glorifying God.
**14** But the synagogue ruler, indignant because Jesus had healed on the Sabbath, kept saying to the crowd, "There are six days on which work ought to be done; come on those days to be healed, and not on the Sabbath day."
**15** The Lord answered him, "You hypocrites! Does not every single one of you untie his ox or his donkey from the feeding trough on the Sabbath and lead it out to water?
**16** And should not this daughter of Abraham, whom Satan held in chains for fully eighteen years, be untied from her bond on the Sabbath day?"
**17** As he said these things, all his opponents were put to shame, while the entire crowd rejoiced over all the splendid works he was doing.
**18** Therefore he asked, "What is the kingdom of God like, and to what shall I compare it?
**19** It resembles a mustard seed that someone took and planted in his garden; it sprouted, grew into a tree, and birds of the sky built nests within its branches."
**20** Again he asked, "To what shall I compare the kingdom of God?
**21** It resembles yeast that a woman took and kneaded into three measures of flour until the entire batch fermented."
**22** He kept traveling through towns and villages, teaching as he made his way toward Jerusalem.
**23** Someone asked him, "Lord, are only a few going to be saved?" He said to them,
**24** "Struggle to enter through the narrow door, because many, I tell you, will try to enter and will not be able.
**25** Once the master of the house gets up and locks the door, and you begin standing outside and knocking on the door, pleading, 'Lord, open up for us,' he will answer you, 'I do not know where you come from.'
**26** At that point you will begin protesting, 'We shared meals and drinks in your company, and you taught along our city streets!'
**27** Yet he will reply to you, 'I do not know where you are from. Get away from me, all you workers of unrighteousness!'
**28** There will be bitter weeping and grinding of teeth when you catch sight of Abraham, Isaac, Jacob, and every prophet inside God's kingdom, while you yourselves are cast out.
**29** People will arrive from east and west, and from north and south, and take their places at the banquet in the kingdom of God.
**30** Indeed, some who are last will be first, and some who are first will be last."
**31** At that very hour some Pharisees approached and told him, "Leave and get away from here, because Herod wants to kill you."
**32** He replied to them, "Go and tell that fox, 'Look, I am driving out demons and performing healings today and tomorrow, and on the third day I reach my goal.'
**33** Still, I must keep moving today, tomorrow, and the day after, because it cannot be that a prophet perishes outside Jerusalem.
**34** O Jerusalem, Jerusalem, who kills the prophets and stones those sent to her—how often I longed to gather your children together as a hen gathers her brood under her wings, yet you refused!
**35** Look, your house is left to you. And I tell you, you will certainly not see me until the time comes when you say, 'Blessed is the one who comes in the name of the Lord.'"

## Everyday English

**1** Just then some people in the crowd brought up the Galileans whose blood Pilate had spilled right alongside their temple sacrifices.
**2** Jesus answered them, "Do you imagine those Galileans were bigger sinners than anyone else in Galilee just because they died that way?
**3** Not at all! Unless you turn back to God, every one of you will meet ruin too.
**4** Think about those eighteen people who were crushed when the tower in Siloam collapsed. Were they somehow guiltier than everybody else living in Jerusalem?
**5** Not a chance! If you don't change your ways, you'll all end up destroyed the exact same way."
**6** Then Jesus shared this story: "A man planted a fig tree in his vineyard and came looking for figs on it, but he couldn't find a single one.
**7** So he told the gardener, 'Look here—for three years straight I've come checking this tree for fruit, and it hasn't produced anything. Chop it down! Why let it keep draining the soil?'
**8** The gardener answered, 'Sir, give it one more year. Let me dig around the roots and work in some fertilizer.
**9** If it bears fruit next season, great; if it still doesn't, you can chop it down.'"
**10** One Sabbath day, Jesus was teaching in a local synagogue.
**11** A woman was there who'd been bent over by a disabling spirit for eighteen years—she couldn't straighten her back at all.
**12** When Jesus noticed her, he called her over and said, "Ma'am, you're free from your illness."
**13** He placed his hands on her, and instantly she stood up tall and started praising God.
**14** The synagogue leader was furious that Jesus had healed someone on the Sabbath, so he lectured the crowd: "There are six regular days to get things done! Come get healed on those days, not on the Sabbath!"
**15** "You phonies!" the Lord shot back at him. "Doesn't every single one of you untie your ox or donkey from its stall on the Sabbath and walk it out for a drink?
**16** Here's a daughter of Abraham whom Satan has kept tied up for eighteen long years—shouldn't she be set free from her chains on the Sabbath?"
**17** That silenced and embarrassed all of Jesus's critics, while everyone else in the crowd celebrated the amazing things he was doing.
**18** So Jesus asked, "What does God's kingdom look like? How can I picture it for you?
**19** It's like a tiny mustard seed a man dropped into his garden. It sprouted and grew into a full-sized tree, and wild birds built nests in its branches."
**20** He tried another picture: "What else can I compare God's kingdom to?
**21** It's like a pinch of yeast a woman kneaded into three big measures of flour until the whole batch of dough rose."
**22** Jesus kept making his way from town to town and village to village, teaching as he headed toward Jerusalem.
**23** Along the road someone asked him, "Lord, will only a small handful of people be saved?" Jesus told the crowd,
**24** "Give everything you've got to enter through the narrow doorway! Let me tell you, plenty of people will try to get in later and won't be able to.
**25** Once the owner of the house gets up and bolts the door, you'll be left standing outside pounding on it and begging, 'Sir, open up for us!' He'll call back, 'I don't recognize where you're from.'
**26** Then you'll argue, 'Hey, we shared meals with you! You taught right in our own streets!'
**27** But he'll tell you straight, 'I don't know where you belong. Get out of my sight, all of you who make a habit of wrongdoing!'
**28** You'll cry and grind your teeth in despair when you see Abraham, Isaac, Jacob, and all the prophets sitting inside God's kingdom while you're locked out.
**29** Guests will pour in from east and west, north and south, to take their seats at God's royal feast.
**30** Watch carefully: some people at the back of the line right now will end up first, and plenty of those in front will wind up last."
**31** Right around that same hour, a few Pharisees came up and warned him, "Get out of here and move on! Herod wants you dead."
**32** Jesus told them, "Go tell that sly fox, 'Listen—I'm driving out demons and healing sick people today and tomorrow, and on the third day I'll finish what I came to do.'
**33** Even so, I have to keep walking today, tomorrow, and the day after, because it's unthinkable for a prophet to die anywhere except Jerusalem.
**34** O Jerusalem, Jerusalem! You're the city that murders the prophets and stones the messengers God sends to you. How many times I've wanted to pull your children close, the way a mother hen tucks her chicks under her wings—but you wouldn't let me!
**35** Now your house is left empty to you. Mark my words: you won't lay eyes on me again until the day you finally say, 'God bless the one who comes in the Lord's name!'"

## Crossings & Lexical Notes

- **v. 3 `μετανοῆτε` (*metanoēte*):** Present subjunctive from *metanoeō* ("repent, change one's mind and direction"), contrasted with the aorist subjunctive *metanoēsēte* read by `WH` and `Treg` in v. 5 (`NA28` and `RP` retain present *metanoēte* in both v. 3 and v. 5, stressing ongoing turning).
- **v. 4 `ὀφειλέται` (*opheiletai*):** Literally "debtors," used in Aramaic-inflected Greek idiom for "moral offenders" or "guilty ones" who owe a debt of justice to God, paralleling *hamartōloi* ("sinners") in v. 2.
- **v. 7 `καταργεῖ` (*katargei*):** From *katargeō* ("render idle, make unproductive, drain/exhaust"), depicting the barren fig tree not merely taking up space but depleting the vineyard soil; `NA28` adds the inferential particle *oun* ("therefore, cut it down"), omitted in `WH`, `Treg`, and `RP`.
- **v. 11 `πνεῦμα ... ἀσθενείας` (*pneuma ... astheneias*):** Literally "a spirit of weakness/infirmity," linking physical curvature (*synkyptousa*, "bent double") to spiritual bondage, which Jesus explicitly attributes to Satan in v. 16.
- **v. 21 `ἀλεύρου σάτα τρία` (*aleurou sata tria*):** Three *sata* (from Hebrew *se'ah*, roughly 12–13 liters each, totaling nearly 40 liters of wheat flour), echoing Sarah's lavish hospitality in Gen 18:6—an enormous batch of dough silently permeated by a small portion of leaven (*zymē*).
- **v. 24 `ἀγωνίζεσθε` (*agōnizesthe*):** Present imperative from *agōnizomai* ("strive earnestly, contend like an athlete in the *agōn*"), urging decisive effort before the householder shuts the door.
- **v. 32 `τελειοῦμαι` (*teleioumai*):** Present passive (or middle) of *teleioō* ("be brought to completion, reach one's appointed goal"), foreshadowing Jesus's passion and resurrection in Jerusalem ("today and tomorrow, and on the third day").
- **v. 35 `ὁ οἶκος ὑμῶν` (*ho oikos hymōn*):** SBLGNT, `WH`, `Treg`, and `NA28` read simply "your house" without *erēmos* ("desolate"), whereas `RP` adds *erēmos* (likely harmonized from Matt 23:38). For the final clause, `Treg` and `NA28` print *hēxei hote* ("will come when"), while `WH` omits *hēxei hote* and `RP` reads *an hēxei hote*.

## Supplemental Texts & Allusions

- **Genesis 18:6 (LXX)** (alluded to in v. 21): *Three measures of fine flour* echoes Abraham and Sarah's sudden feast for the Lord's visitors, reimagined here as the quiet, unstoppable spread of God's reign.
- **Psalm 6:9 [6:8 ET] (LXX)** (quoted in v. 27): *"Leave my presence, all you practitioners of iniquity"*—Jesus applies the psalmist's dismissal of evildoers to presumptuous insiders locked outside the banquet.
- **Psalm 107:3 & Isaiah 43:5–6** (echoed in v. 29): The prophetic hope that redeemed exiles will stream in from east, west, north, and south to feast in God's presence.
- **Psalm 118:26 (117:26 LXX)** (quoted in v. 35): *"Blessed is he who arrives in the Lord's name,"* the pilgrim acclamation that anticipates Jesus's royal entry into Jerusalem (Luke 19:38).
