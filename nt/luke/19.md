## Clear & Faithful

**1** Jesus entered Jericho and was passing through it.
**2** A man named Zacchaeus lived there; he served as a chief tax collector and possessed great wealth.
**3** He was trying to see who Jesus was, yet he could not because of the crowd, since he was short in stature.
**4** So he ran on ahead and climbed up into a sycamore-fig tree to see him, because Jesus was about to pass that way.
**5** Reaching that spot, Jesus glanced up and spoke to him: "Zacchaeus, make haste and climb down, because I need to lodge at your house today."
**6** He hurried down and welcomed him with joy.
**7** Seeing this, everyone began to grumble, "He has gone in to be the guest of a sinful man!"
**8** But Zacchaeus stood up and said to the Lord, "Look, Lord, half of my possessions I am giving to the poor, and if I have extorted anything from anyone, I am paying it back four times over."
**9** Jesus declared to him, "Today deliverance has arrived at this household, since this man too is Abraham's son;
**10** indeed, the Son of Man arrived to search out and rescue the lost."
**11** As they were listening to these things, he went on to tell a parable, because he was near Jerusalem and they supposed that the kingdom of God was about to appear immediately.
**12** So he said, "A certain nobleman traveled to a distant country to receive a kingdom for himself and then return.
**13** Calling ten of his servants, he gave them ten minas and told them, 'Do business with these while I am away.'
**14** But his citizens hated him and sent a delegation after him to declare, 'We do not want this man to rule over us.'
**15** When he returned after receiving the kingdom, he ordered those servants to whom he had given the money to be summoned before him, so he could learn what each had gained by trading.
**16** The first arrived and said, 'Lord, your mina has earned ten more minas.'
**17** He told him, 'Well done, good servant! Because you proved faithful in a very small matter, take charge over ten cities.'
**18** The second servant stepped forward and reported, 'Sir, your mina earned five additional minas.'
**19** To him too he said, 'You also take charge over five cities.'
**20** Then another came and said, 'Lord, here is your mina, which I kept tucked away in a cloth;
**21** for I was afraid of you, because you are an exacting man—you withdraw what you did not deposit and harvest what you did not sow.'
**22** The king told him, 'Out of your own mouth I pass judgment on you, wicked servant! You knew I am a stern master, collecting what I never deposited and reaping crops I never planted?
**23** Then why did you not put my money in the bank, so that when I returned I could have collected it with interest?'
**24** And to those standing by he ordered, 'Take the mina away from him and give it to the one who has ten minas.'
**25** They protested to him, 'Master, he already holds ten minas!'
**26** 'I tell you that to everyone who has, more will be given; yet from the one who has nothing, even what he has will be taken away.
**27** Meanwhile, bring here those adversaries of mine who refused to have me rule over them, and put them to the sword right before my eyes!'"
**28** After saying these things, Jesus went on ahead, going up toward Jerusalem.
**29** Drawing near to Bethphage and Bethany beside the hill named the Mount of Olives, Jesus dispatched two of his disciples ahead,
**30** telling them, "Go into the village just opposite you. As you enter it, you will find a colt tied up that no one has ever ridden; untie it and bring it here.
**31** Should anyone question you, 'Why are you untying it?' answer this: 'The Lord has need of it.'"
**32** Those dispatched went ahead and discovered everything precisely as Jesus had told them.
**33** While they were untying the colt, its owners challenged them, "Why are you untying that colt?"
**34** They answered, "The Lord has need of it."
**35** They brought the colt to Jesus, threw their cloaks over it, and set Jesus on it.
**36** As he rode along, people kept spreading their cloaks on the road.
**37** By the time he came near the descent from the Mount of Olives, the entire crowd of disciples began joyfully praising God with loud voices for all the mighty deeds they had seen,
**38** proclaiming aloud, "Blessed is the King arriving in the Lord's name! Peace in heaven, and glory in the highest heights!"
**39** A few Pharisees within the crowd called to him, "Teacher, silence your disciples!"
**40** He answered, "I tell you, if these stay silent, the very stones will cry out!"
**41** As he drew near and saw the city, he wept over it,
**42** saying, "If only you had known, even you, on this very day, what makes for peace! Yet now it is hidden from your eyes.
**43** Indeed, days will overtake you when your foes build a siege wall around you, encircle you, and hem you in from every direction.
**44** They will smash you to the ground—you and your children within your walls—and will not leave one stone standing upon another inside you, because you did not recognize the time of your visitation."
**45** Entering the temple courts, he began driving out those who were selling there,
**46** declaring to them, "It is written: 'And my house shall be a house of prayer,' yet you have turned it into a den of robbers!"
**47** Day after day he was teaching in the temple. Meanwhile, the chief priests, the scribes, and the leaders of the people were looking for a way to destroy him,
**48** yet they could not find anything they could do, because all the people hung intently on every word he spoke.

## Everyday English

**1** Jesus walked into Jericho and was making his way through town.
**2** A man named Zacchaeus lived there—he was the head tax collector and very rich.
**3** He badly wanted to get a look at who Jesus was, but he couldn't see over the crowd because he was short.
**4** So he sprinted ahead along the route and climbed up into a sycamore-fig tree to get a view, since Jesus was about to walk right past it.
**5** When Jesus reached that spot, he glanced up and said, "Zacchaeus, hurry down right now! I have to stay at your house today."
**6** Zacchaeus scrambled down as fast as he could and welcomed Jesus with huge joy.
**7** Everyone watching started grumbling, "He actually went to stay as a guest at a notorious sinner's house!"
**8** But Zacchaeus stood his ground and told the Lord, "Look, Lord—right now I'm giving half of everything I own to the poor, and if I've cheated anybody out of a single coin, I'll pay them back four times over!"
**9** Jesus announced to him, "Today salvation has arrived in this home, because this man is truly a son of Abraham too!
**10** After all, the Son of Man came to hunt down and rescue people who are lost."
**11** Since everyone was listening closely and assumed God's kingdom was going to show up any second as they neared Jerusalem, Jesus added a story:
**12** "A high-ranking nobleman traveled to a faraway country to be crowned king and then return home.
**13** Before leaving, he called ten of his servants, handed each of them a gold coin worth months of wages, and instructed them, 'Invest this and do business until I get back.'
**14** Now his subjects hated him and sent an official delegation after him to say, 'We refuse to have this man rule as our king!'
**15** Eventually he got the kingship and returned home. Then he summoned the servants he'd entrusted with the money so he could find out what profits they'd made.
**16** The first servant stepped up and reported, 'Sir, your single coin earned ten more!'
**17** 'Fantastic job, good servant!' he replied. 'Since you proved faithful with a small sum, I'm putting you in charge of ten cities.'
**18** The second servant came up and said, 'Sir, your coin made five more!'
**19** He told him, 'You take charge of five cities.'
**20** Then another servant walked up and said, 'Sir, here's your coin back—I kept it wrapped up safely in a handkerchief.
**21** Honestly, I was terrified of you because you're a tough, demanding boss! You take money you never deposited and harvest crops you never planted.'
**22** The king snapped back, 'You wicked servant, I'll convict you with your own words! So you knew I'm a tough, demanding boss who takes what I didn't deposit and harvests what I didn't plant?
**23** Then why didn't you at least put my money in the bank, so when I got back I could collect it with interest?'
**24** Turning to the attendants standing nearby, he ordered, 'Take his coin away and hand it to the servant who already has ten!'
**25** 'Wait, sir,' they protested, 'he already has ten coins!'
**26** 'Listen to me: whoever produces results will be given even more, while whoever produces nothing will lose even the little they have.
**27** As for those enemies of mine who didn't want me ruling over them—drag them right here and execute them in front of me!'"
**28** After telling that story, Jesus walked out ahead of everyone, climbing the road up toward Jerusalem.
**29** As Jesus got close to Bethphage and Bethany near the ridge called the Mount of Olives, he sent two disciples on ahead,
**30** telling them, "Walk into the village right across from you. As soon as you enter, you'll spot a young donkey tied up that nobody has ever ridden. Untie it and bring it back.
**31** If anyone asks why you're untying it, just tell them, 'The Lord needs it.'"
**32** The two disciples went ahead and found everything exactly as Jesus had described.
**33** While they were untying the colt, its owners walked out and asked, "Why are you untying our colt?"
**34** "The Lord needs it," they replied.
**35** So they brought the colt back to Jesus, draped their coats across its back, and helped Jesus climb on.
**36** As he rode forward, people kept spreading their cloaks across the road like a royal carpet.
**37** Right as he reached the downhill slope of the Mount of Olives, the whole crowd of disciples erupted in joyful shouts, praising God at full volume for all the miracles they'd witnessed:
**38** "God bless the King who comes in the Lord's name! Peace in heaven, and glory in the highest skies!"
**39** Some Pharisees mixed into the crowd called out, "Teacher, tell your followers to be quiet!"
**40** Jesus answered them, "I'm telling you, if they stay silent, the rocks along this road will burst out shouting!"
**41** As he came closer and the city came into view, Jesus broke down weeping over Jerusalem:
**42** "Oh, if only you had recognized today—even you—what truly brings peace! But right now it's hidden from your sight.
**43** Days are coming when your enemies will build siege ramps around you, trap you inside a ring of soldiers, and crush you from every direction.
**44** They'll smash you and your children inside your walls flat to the ground, not leaving one stone stacked on top of another—all because you didn't recognize the moment when God came to rescue you."
**45** When Jesus walked into the temple courtyard, he immediately started chasing out the merchants,
**46** declaring, "Scripture says, 'My house is going to be a house of prayer,' yet you've turned it into a hideout for bandits!"
**47** Every single day he taught inside the temple. Meanwhile, the chief priests, religious scholars, and city leaders kept plotting how to kill him,
**48** but they couldn't figure out any way to pull it off, because the entire crowd hung on his every word.

## Crossings & Lexical Notes

- **v. 2 `ἀρχιτελώνης` (*architelōnēs*):** Compound occurring only here in the NT ("chief tax-collector"), denoting a senior regional contractor overseeing local customs collectors along the lucrative Jericho trade route. Note `WH`, `Treg`, `NA28`, and `RP` retain *kaloumenos* ("by name called Zacchaeus").
- **v. 8 `ἐσυκοφάντησα` (*esykophantēsa*):** Literally "acted as a sycophant" (originally exposing fig-smugglers, later meaning "extorted by false accusation or blackmail"); Zacchaeus pledges fourfold (*tetraploun*) restitution, exceeding the Torah penalty for voluntary confession (Lev 6:5) and matching the penalty for stolen sheep (Exod 22:1).
- **v. 13 `μνᾶς` (*mnas*):** Ten Greek *minas* (each roughly 100 drachmas, or about three to four months' wages for a common laborer), far smaller than the talents in Matt 25:14–30—fitting the contrast "faithful in a very small matter" (v. 17).
- **v. 20 `σουδαρίῳ` (*soudariō*):** Latin loanword *sudarium* ("sweat-cloth / face-napkin"), highlighting negligent hoarding rather than safe banking (*epì trapezan*, v. 23, literally "upon a money-changer's table/bank").
- **v. 38 `ὁ ἐρχόμενος βασιλεὺς` (*ho erchomenos basileus*):** Luke inserts the royal title *basileus* ("King") into Ps 118:26 (`Treg` and `RP` omit the second article *ho* before *basileus*, while `WH` and `NA28` read *ho basileus*), and shifts the angelic song of Luke 2:14 ("peace on earth") to "peace in heaven" (*en ouranō eirēnē*).
- **v. 43 `χάρακα` (*charaka*):** Technical military term for a wooden palisade or siege-rampart thrown up around a besieged city, vividly anticipating the Roman siege of Jerusalem in 70 CE.
- **v. 44 `ἐπισκοπῆς` (*episkopēs*):** From *episkeptomai* ("visit/inspect," cf. Luke 1:68; 7:16), denoting God's decisive coming to inspect and rescue his people.
- **v. 48 `ἐξεκρέματο` (*exekremato*):** Imperfect of *ekkremannymi* ("hung from / clung to"), picturing the crowds figuratively suspended on Jesus's lips as they listened.

## Supplemental Texts & Allusions

- **Exodus 22:1 [21:37 MT] & Leviticus 6:1–5** (background to v. 8): Torah restitution law distinguishes voluntary confession plus twenty percent from fourfold/fivefold restitution for theft—Zacchaeus voluntarily imposes the highest restitution upon himself.
- **Zechariah 9:9** (echoed in vv. 30–38): The humble king of Zion arrives riding on a donkey's colt, bringing peace and salvation.
- **Psalm 118:26 (117:26 LXX)** (quoted in v. 38): *"Blessed is the king arriving in the Lord's name,"* now explicitly acclaimed with the title "King."
- **Isaiah 56:7 & Jeremiah 7:11** (combined in v. 46): *"My house will be named a place of prayer for every nation"* paired with Jeremiah's indictment against turning the temple into a *"a hideout for bandits."*
