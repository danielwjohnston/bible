## Clear & Faithful

**1** One Sabbath Jesus went into the house of one of the leading Pharisees to share a meal, and they were watching him closely.
**2** Right in front of him stood a man suffering from dropsy.
**3** Jesus spoke up and asked the legal scholars and Pharisees, "Is it lawful to heal on the Sabbath, or not?"
**4** They remained silent. So Jesus took hold of the man, healed him, and sent him on his way.
**5** Then he asked them, "If your son or ox tumbles into a cistern, which one of you would hesitate to haul him straight out on a Sabbath day?"
**6** And they had no ability to answer back against this.
**7** Observing how the invited guests were picking out the places of honor at the table, Jesus told them a parable:
**8** "When someone invites you to a wedding banquet, do not take the seat of honor, in case someone more distinguished than you has been invited by your host,
**9** and the host who invited both of you comes and tells you, 'Give this person your seat.' Then, humiliated, you will have to take the lowest place.
**10** Instead, when you are invited, go and sit in the lowest seat, so that when your host comes he will say to you, 'Friend, move up to a better place.' Then you will be honored in front of all your fellow guests.
**11** For everyone who lifts himself up will be brought low, whereas the one who humbles himself will be lifted high."
**12** He also said to the man who had invited him, "When you host a luncheon or a dinner, do not invite your friends, your brothers, your relatives, or your wealthy neighbors, lest they invite you back in return and you receive your repayment.
**13** Rather, when you throw a banquet, invite the poor, the crippled, the lame, and the blind;
**14** and you will be blessed, because they have no way to repay you—for repayment will be given to you at the resurrection of the righteous."
**15** Hearing this, one of the guests reclining at table said to him, "Blessed is anyone who will eat bread in the kingdom of God!"
**16** Jesus replied to him, "A certain man was preparing a grand banquet and invited many guests.
**17** When the hour for the dinner arrived, he sent his servant to tell those who were invited, 'Come, for everything is now ready.'
**18** Yet one and all, they began making excuses. The first told him, 'I have bought a field and must go out to inspect it; please consider me excused.'
**19** Another said, 'I have bought five pairs of oxen and am on my way to test them out; please consider me excused.'
**20** Still another said, 'I just got married, and for that reason I cannot come.'
**21** Returning, the servant reported these replies to his master. Then the homeowner grew angry and told his servant, 'Go out quickly into the streets and alleys of the city, and bring in here the poor, the crippled, the blind, and the lame.'
**22** The servant reported, 'Master, what you commanded has been done, and there is still room.'
**23** The master told the servant, 'Go out along the highways and hedgerows and urge people to come in, so that my house may be filled.
**24** For I tell you that not one of those men who were invited will taste my banquet.'"
**25** Large crowds were traveling along with Jesus, and he turned and spoke to them:
**26** "If anyone comes to me and does not hate his father and mother, his wife and children, his brothers and sisters—yes, even his own life—he cannot be my disciple.
**27** Anyone who refuses to shoulder his own cross and walk behind me is unable to be my disciple.
**28** Suppose one of you wants to build a watchtower: does he not first sit down and calculate the cost to see whether he has enough to finish it?
**29** Otherwise, after he lays the foundation and cannot complete it, everyone watching will begin to ridicule him,
**30** saying, 'This fellow started building and was unable to finish!'
**31** Or what king marching out to engage another king in war does not first sit down and deliberate whether with ten thousand troops he can confront the one advancing against him with twenty thousand?
**32** If he cannot, while the other is still far away he sends an embassy to ask for terms of peace.
**33** In the same way, then, none of you who fails to renounce all his own possessions can be my disciple.
**34** Salt is good; yet if even the salt loses its taste, with what can it be seasoned again?
**35** It serves neither the soil nor the compost heap—people simply throw it away. Let anyone with ears to hear listen closely."

## Everyday English

**1** One Sabbath, Jesus went to eat dinner at the home of an influential Pharisee, and the guests were keeping a sharp eye on him.
**2** Right there in front of him was a man whose body was badly swollen with fluid.
**3** Jesus looked at the experts in Moses's law and the Pharisees and asked, "Does the law allow healing on the Sabbath or not?"
**4** Nobody said a word. So Jesus took hold of the man, healed him completely, and sent him home.
**5** Then he asked them, "If your child or your ox falls into a deep well on the Sabbath, won't every single one of you pull them straight back out right away?"
**6** They couldn't come up with a single reply to that.
**7** Noticing how the dinner guests kept scrambling for the best seats, Jesus gave them a story:
**8** "When somebody invites you to a wedding reception, don't grab the head seat right away. What if the host invited someone more important than you?
**9** The host will walk over and tell you, 'You'll need to give this guest your seat.' Then you'll feel humiliated as you have to walk all the way down to the worst seat left.
**10** Instead, when you're invited, go sit in the lowest spot from the start. That way, when your host comes by, he'll say, 'My friend, come move up closer to the head table!' Then everyone sitting with you will see you honored.
**11** That's how it works: anyone who puts himself up high gets brought low, and anyone who takes the humble spot gets raised up."
**12** Next Jesus turned to his host: "When you throw a lunch or dinner party, don't just invite your friends, your brothers, your relatives, or your rich neighbors. If you do, they'll pay you back by inviting you to their place, and you're even.
**13** Instead, when you host a feast, invite people who are poor, disabled, lame, and blind.
**14** You'll be truly blessed, because they can't pay you back—God himself will repay you when good people rise from the dead."
**15** One of the guests heard this and remarked, "How fortunate anyone will be who sits down to feast in God's kingdom!"
**16** Jesus answered him with a story: "A man was hosting a huge dinner party and sent invitations to plenty of people.
**17** When dinner was ready, he sent his servant out to tell the invited guests, 'Come on over! Everything's ready now.'
**18** But one after another, they all started making excuses. The first one said, 'I just bought a piece of land and I really have to go inspect it. Sorry, please excuse me.'
**19** Another said, 'I just bought five teams of oxen and I'm heading out to try them out. Please excuse me.'
**20** Another said, 'I just got married, so obviously I can't make it.'
**21** The servant went back and reported all of this to his master. The host was furious and ordered his servant, 'Hurry out into the main streets and back alleys of the city and bring in the poor, the disabled, the blind, and the lame!'
**22** Before long the servant reported, 'Sir, I did everything you ordered, and there's still plenty of open seats.'
**23** So the master said, 'Go out along the country roads and behind the hedges and press people to come in—I want my house packed!
**24** Let me tell you, not one of those original guests will get a single bite of my dinner.'"
**25** Huge crowds were walking alongside Jesus, so he turned around and spoke plainly to them:
**26** "If anyone wants to follow me, their love for me has to outrank their father, mother, spouse, children, brothers, sisters—even their own life—otherwise they can't be my disciple.
**27** Unless you shoulder your own cross and walk behind me, you can't be my follower.
**28** Think about it: if one of you plans to build a tower, don't you sit down first and figure out the budget to make sure you have enough cash to finish?
**29** Otherwise, if you pour the foundation and run out of money halfway through, everybody passing by will laugh at you:
**30** 'Look at that guy—started building and couldn't even finish!'
**31** Or take a king marching out to fight another king. Doesn't he sit down with his advisers first to calculate if ten thousand soldiers can hold off an enemy coming at him with twenty thousand?
**32** If the odds are hopeless, he sends diplomats while the enemy is still miles away to negotiate peace.
**33** It's the exact same with you: unless you let go of your hold on everything you own, you can't be my disciple.
**34** Salt is great—but if even the salt goes flat and loses its punch, how do you make it salty again?
**35** It's useless for the garden and useless even for the compost heap; people just toss it out. If you've got ears, really listen to this!"

## Crossings & Lexical Notes

- **v. 2 `ὑδρωπικός` (*hydrōpikos*):** Medical term (only here in the NT) for someone suffering from *hydrōps* ("dropsy" or edema, abnormal accumulation of fluid in body tissues), a condition often viewed with social stigma.
- **v. 5 `υἱὸς ἢ βοῦς` (*huios ē bous*):** SBLGNT, `WH`, `Treg`, and `NA28` read "son or ox" (echoing the assonance of *huios* / *bous* and elevating human value alongside livestock); some later manuscripts substitute *onos* ("donkey") for *huios* ("son"), likely assimilated to Luke 13:15.
- **v. 7 `πρωτοκλισίας` (*prōtoklisias*):** Literally "first reclining-places," referring to the seats of highest social honor next to the host on Roman/Hellenistic dining couches (*triclinia*).
- **v. 15 `ἄρτον` (*arton*):** Literally "bread" (`WH`, `Treg`, `NA28`), an idiom for sharing a full covenant meal; `RP` reads *ariston* ("breakfast/noon banquet").
- **v. 18 `ἀπὸ μιᾶς` (*apo mias*):** Elliptical Greek idiom (supplying *gnōmēs* or *phōnēs*, "with one mind / as one"), meaning "all alike" or "one and all."
- **v. 24 `δείπνου` (*deipnou*):** SBLGNT, `WH`, `Treg`, and `NA28` end v. 24 here; `RP` appends the proverb "For many are called, but few are chosen" (parallel to Matt 22:14).
- **v. 26 `μισεῖ` (*misei*):** Semitic comparative idiom for "loves less / sets second to" (cf. Gen 29:30–31; Matt 10:37), demanding undivided covenant loyalty above family ties and self-preservation (*psychē*).
- **v. 34 `μωρανθῇ` (*mōranthē*):** Literally "becomes foolish/insipid" (from *mōrainō*, used of salt losing its tang or savor), creating a wordplay between foolishness and flavorlessness.

## Supplemental Texts & Allusions

- **Proverbs 25:6–7** (echoed in vv. 8–11): *"Do not exalt yourself in the king's presence or claim a place among the great; it is better to be told, 'Come up here,' than to be put down before a noble."*
- **Deuteronomy 14:29; 16:11–14** (background to vv. 13, 21): Covenant commands to invite the vulnerable, landless, and needy to sacred festive meals before God.
- **Isaiah 25:6–8** (echoed in v. 15): The prophetic promise that the Lord of hosts will spread a rich banquet of aged wine and choice meats for all peoples on his mountain.
- **Deuteronomy 33:9** (background to v. 26): Commends Levi's radical covenant faithfulness that placed God's command ahead of parents, brothers, and children.
