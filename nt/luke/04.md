## Clear & Faithful

**1** Jesus, overflowing with the Holy Spirit, turned back from the Jordan and was led along by the Spirit in the desert
**2** for forty days, being tempted by the devil. During those days he ate nothing, and when they were over, he was hungry.
**3** The devil challenged him, "Since you are God's Son, tell this stone to turn into a loaf of bread."
**4** Jesus answered him, "It is written, 'Man shall not live on bread alone.'"
**5** Then the devil led him up and showed him all the kingdoms of the world in a single instant.
**6** The devil offered him, "I will grant you all this dominion and its splendor, because it has been entrusted to me, and I bestow it on anyone I please.
**7** So if you bow in worship before me, the whole of it will belong to you."
**8** Jesus answered him, "It is written, 'You shall worship the Lord your God, and him alone shall you serve.'"
**9** Next he led him into Jerusalem, placed him on the highest edge of the temple, and told him, "If you are God's Son, throw yourself down from up here;
**10** for it is written, 'He will command his angels concerning you to guard you carefully,'
**11** and, 'They will hold you up in their hands so that you never dash your foot against a rock.'"
**12** Jesus answered him, "It is said, 'You shall not put the Lord your God to the test.'"
**13** Having exhausted every test, the devil withdrew from him until a later opportune moment.
**14** Jesus returned to Galilee in the Spirit's power, and reports of him traveled throughout the entire surrounding district.
**15** He taught in their synagogues and was praised by everyone.
**16** He arrived in Nazareth, where he had been raised, and following his usual habit, he went into the synagogue on the Sabbath and rose to read.
**17** The scroll of the prophet Isaiah was handed to him. Unrolling the scroll, he found the place where it was written:
**18** "The Spirit of the Lord is upon me, because he has anointed me to proclaim good news to the poor. He has sent me to announce release to the captives and recovery of sight to the blind, to set free those who are oppressed,
**19** to proclaim the year of the Lord's favor."
**20** Then he rolled up the scroll, gave it back to the attendant, and sat down; and the eyes of everyone in the synagogue were fixed intently on him.
**21** He began addressing them: "Today, right in your ears, this passage of Scripture stands fulfilled."
**22** All spoke well of him and marveled at the gracious words coming from his mouth, and they asked, "Is not this Joseph's son?"
**23** He said to them, "No doubt you will quote this proverb to me: 'Physician, heal yourself! Do here in your hometown all the things we heard were done in Capernaum.'"
**24** And he said, "Truly I tell you, no prophet is accepted in his own hometown.
**25** Truly I tell you, plenty of widows lived in Israel during Elijah's time, when the heavens remained closed for three and a half years and a terrible famine swept across the entire region;
**26** yet Elijah was sent to none of them except to a widow at Zarephath in the region of Sidon.
**27** Likewise, many lepers lived in Israel during the prophet Elisha's era, yet not one of them was healed except Naaman from Syria."
**28** All those in the synagogue were filled with fury when they heard these things.
**29** They rose up, drove him out of the town, and brought him to the brow of the hill on which their town was built, in order to hurl him down headlong.
**30** But passing right through the midst of them, he went on his way.
**31** So he traveled down to Capernaum, a Galilean town, where he taught the people on the Sabbath days.
**32** They were astonished at his teaching, because his message carried authority.
**33** Inside the synagogue sat a man under the grip of an unclean demonic spirit, and he shouted at the top of his voice,
**34** "Aha! What business do you have with us, Jesus of Nazareth? Have you arrived to ruin us? I recognize who you are—God's Holy One!"
**35** Jesus rebuked it, saying, "Be silent and come out of him!" And when the demon had thrown him down in their midst, it came out of him without harming him at all.
**36** Astonishment gripped everyone, and they began discussing among themselves, "What kind of message is this? With sheer authority and power he gives orders to unclean spirits, and out they go!"
**37** Soon word about him echoed through every corner of the surrounding countryside.
**38** Leaving the synagogue, he entered Simon's house. Now Simon's mother-in-law was suffering with a high fever, and they appealed to him on her behalf.
**39** Standing over her, he rebuked the fever, and it left her; immediately she got up and began serving them.
**40** When the sun went down, everyone who had loved ones suffering from all sorts of illnesses brought them to Jesus; placing his hands on each individual, he cured them.
**41** Demons also came out of many, shouting, "You are the Son of God!" Yet he rebuked them and would not allow them to speak, because they knew he was the Messiah.
**42** At daybreak he departed and went out to a solitary place. The crowds searched for him, came to him, and tried to keep him from leaving them.
**43** But he said to them, "I must proclaim the good news of the kingdom of God to the other towns as well, because that is why I was sent."
**44** And he kept preaching in the synagogues of Judea.

**2** For forty days he faced testing from the devil. He ate nothing at all during those weeks, and by the time they ended, he was starving.

**7** Just bow down and worship me, and every bit of it will belong to you."
**8** Jesus answered him, "Scripture says, 'Worship the Lord your God, and serve only him.'"

## Crossings & Lexical Notes

- **v. 4 `ὁ ἄνθρωπος` (*ho anthrōpos*):** SBLGNT, `WH`, and `NA28` end the quotation from Deut 8:3 here; `Treg` and `RP` add `ἀλλʼ ἐπὶ παντὶ ῥήματι θεοῦ` ("but by every word of God"), harmonized from Matt 4:4.
- **v. 5 `ἀναγαγὼν αὐτὸν` (*anagagōn auton*):** `WH`, `Treg`, and `NA28` read simply "having led him up"; `RP` adds `ὁ διάβολος εἰς ὄρος ὑψηλὸν` ("the devil onto a high mountain"), harmonized from Matt 4:8.
- **v. 8 `Κύριον τὸν θεόν σου προσκυνήσεις` (*Kyrion ton theon sou proskynēseis*):** `WH`, `Treg`, and `NA28` omit the command "Get behind me, Satan" (`Ὕπαγε ὀπίσω μου, Σατανᾶ`), which `RP` supplies at the start of the reply from Matt 4:10.
- **v. 13 `ἄχρι καιροῦ` (*achri kairou*):** "Until an opportune time" or "appointed season," signaling that the conflict will resume (cf. Luke 22:3, 53).
- **v. 17 `ἀναπτύξας` (*anaptyxas*):** "Having unrolled" a scroll (`NA28`, `RP`), paired with `πτύξας` ("having rolled up") in v. 20; `WH` and `Treg` read `ἀνοίξας` ("having opened").
- **v. 18 `ἄφεσιν` (*aphesin*):** "Release," "pardon," or "liberty," echoing the Jubilee proclamation of Lev 25:10 LXX and the forgiveness of sins in Luke 1:77; 3:3. `RP` inserts `ἰάσασθαι τοὺς συντετριμμένους τὴν καρδίαν` ("to heal the brokenhearted") from Isa 61:1 LXX.
- **v. 44 `τῆς Ἰουδαίας` (*tēs Ioudaias*):** Earliest Alexandrian witnesses (`WH`, `NA28`) read "of Judea" (used here broadly for the land of the Jews, or anticipating ministry southward), whereas `Treg` and `RP` harmonize to `Γαλιλαίας` ("of Galilee") matching v. 14 and Mark 1:39.

## Supplemental Texts & Allusions

- **Deuteronomy 8:3; 6:13; 6:16** (quoted in vv. 4, 8, 12): Jesus answers each test from the wilderness chapters of Deuteronomy where Israel failed, while the devil quotes **Psalm 91:11–12** in vv. 10–11.
- **Isaiah 61:1–2 & Isaiah 58:6 (LXX)** (quoted in vv. 18–19): Luke combines Isa 61:1–2a with Isa 58:6 (setting crushed people free) and stops short of the day of God's vengeance, framing Jesus's mission around Jubilee release.
- **1 Kings 17:8–16 & 2 Kings 5:1–14** (recalled in vv. 25–27): Elijah's aid to the Sidonian widow of Zarephath and Elisha's healing of Naaman the Syrian foreshadow God's mercy reaching outside Israel's borders.
