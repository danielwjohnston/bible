## Clear & Faithful

**1** One day, as Jesus was teaching the people in the temple courts and proclaiming the good news, the chief priests and the scribes came up together with the elders
**2** and spoke to him, saying, "Tell us by what authority you are doing these things, or who it is that gave you this authority."
**3** He replied to them, "Let me put a question to you as well; answer me:
**4** Was John's baptism from heaven, or from human origin?"
**5** They discussed this among themselves, saying, "If we say, 'From heaven,' he will ask, 'Why did you not believe him?'
**6** Yet if we say, 'From human origin,' the entire crowd will stone us, because they are convinced that John was a prophet."
**7** They therefore replied that they had no idea where it originated.
**8** Jesus then told them, "Nor am I telling you by whose authority I carry out these deeds."
**9** Next he began relating this parable to the people: "A man planted a vineyard, rented it out to vine-growers, and left on an extended journey.
**10** At harvest time he sent a servant to the farmers so that they might give him part of the vineyard's fruit; but the farmers beat him and sent him away empty-handed.
**11** So he sent yet another servant; him too they beat, treated shamefully, and sent away empty-handed.
**12** Again he sent a third; this one also they wounded and threw out.
**13** The vineyard's owner asked himself, 'What ought I to do? I will send my beloved son; surely they will show respect to him.'
**14** On catching sight of him, however, the farmers plotted together, saying, 'Here comes the heir! Let us murder him so the estate becomes ours.'
**15** Throwing him outside the vineyard, they murdered him. What, then, will the owner of the vineyard do to them?
**16** He will come and destroy those farmers and give the vineyard to others." Hearing this, the people exclaimed, "May it never be!"
**17** Looking straight at them, Jesus said, "What then does this Scripture mean: 'The stone that the builders rejected has become the cornerstone'?
**18** Everyone who falls on that stone will be broken to pieces, and anyone on whom it falls will be crushed to dust."
**19** At that very hour the scribes and the chief priests looked for a way to lay hands on him, yet they were afraid of the people—for they knew he had told this parable against them.
**20** Keeping close watch on him, they sent spies pretending to be upright people so they might catch him in something he said, in order to hand him over to the jurisdiction and authority of the governor.
**21** They questioned him, "Teacher, we know that you speak and teach rightly and show partiality to no one, but truthfully teach the way of God:
**22** does our law permit us to pay tribute to Caesar, or not?"
**23** Seeing right through their craftiness, he said to them,
**24** "Display a denarius coin for me. Whose likeness and legend appear on it?" They replied, "Caesar's."
**25** Jesus instructed them, "Render to Caesar, then, whatever belongs to Caesar, and to God whatever belongs to God."
**26** They could not trap him by what he said in front of the people, and marveling at his answer, they fell silent.
**27** Some of the Sadducees—who argue that there is no resurrection—came up and asked him,
**28** "Teacher, Moses wrote for us that if a man's brother dies leaving a wife but no children, his brother should take the widow and raise up offspring for his brother.
**29** Now there were seven brothers. The first married a wife and died childless.
**30** Afterward the second,
**31** and the third married her, and likewise all seven died leaving no children.
**32** Finally the woman died as well.
**33** In the resurrection, therefore, whose wife will the woman be? For all seven had her as a wife."
**34** Jesus replied to them, "People belonging to this present age wed and are given in marriage,
**35** whereas those counted worthy to attain that coming age and rise from the dead neither marry nor are given away in marriage;
**36** indeed, they can no longer die, for they are like angels, and they are God's children, being children of the resurrection.
**37** Even Moses made clear in the passage of the bush that the dead are raised, when he speaks of the Lord as 'Abraham's God, Isaac's God, and Jacob's God.'
**38** He is God not of corpses, however, but of living souls, since in his presence every person lives."
**39** Some of the scribes responded, "Teacher, you have spoken well."
**40** For they no longer dared to ask him anything at all.
**41** Then Jesus asked them, "How can people say that the Christ is David's son?
**42** Indeed, David himself declares in the scroll of Psalms: 'The Lord spoke to my Lord, "Be seated at my right side
**43** until I place your foes as a footstool beneath your feet."'
**44** David thus addresses him as 'Lord'; how, then, can he be David's son?"
**45** While all the people were listening, he told his disciples,
**46** "Watch out for the scribes, who love to parade around in flowing robes, crave greetings in the marketplaces, and seek the front seats in the synagogues and the places of honor at banquets;
**47** they devour widows' houses and offer long prayers for show. These men will receive a far harsher judgment."

## Everyday English

**1** One day while Jesus was in the temple courtyard teaching the crowd and sharing the good news, the chief priests, religious scholars, and elders walked up to confront him.
**2** "Tell us," they demanded, "by what right are you doing all this? Who gave you this kind of authority?"
**3** Jesus answered them, "Let me ask you one question first—answer me this:
**4** Did John's baptism come from God in heaven, or was it just human?"
**5** They huddled up to talk it over: "If we say, 'From heaven,' he'll ask, 'Then why didn't you believe him?'
**6** But if we say, 'Just human,' this whole crowd will stone us to death, because everybody is convinced John was a real prophet!"
**7** So they gave up and said, "We don't know where it came from."
**8** "Then I won't tell you where my authority comes from either," Jesus replied.
**9** Next Jesus turned to the people and told them this story: "A landowner planted a vineyard, leased it to tenant farmers, and left on a long trip.
**10** When harvest time came, he dispatched a slave to the farmers to receive his portion of the crop. Instead, the farmers beat him up and sent him packing with nothing.
**11** So he sent a second servant, and they beat him up too, humiliated him, and sent him away with nothing.
**12** He even sent a third servant—and they wounded him and threw him out as well.
**13** Finally the vineyard owner thought, 'What should I do now? I'll send my own dearly loved son! Surely they'll respect him.'
**14** But the moment the farmers spotted the son, they plotted together: 'Look, here's the heir! Let's murder him, and the whole estate will be ours!'
**15** So they dragged him outside the vineyard and killed him. Now tell me, what will the owner of that vineyard do to them?
**16** He'll come back, wipe out those farmers, and hand the vineyard over to others!" When the crowd heard that, they gasped, "God forbid!"
**17** Jesus looked straight into their eyes and asked, "Then what does this Scripture mean: 'The very stone the builders tossed aside has become the main cornerstone'?
**18** Anyone who trips over that stone will be smashed to pieces, and anyone it lands on will be ground to powder!"
**19** The religious teachers and chief priests wanted to arrest him right then and there, because they realized he'd aimed that story straight at them—but they were terrified of the crowd.
**20** So they kept him under close surveillance and hired undercover agents pretending to be sincere seekers, hoping to twist something Jesus said so they could turn him over to the Roman governor's court.
**21** These spies asked him, "Teacher, we know you speak and teach the truth honestly and don't play favorites with anyone—you genuinely teach God's way.
**22** So tell us: is it right for us to pay taxes to Caesar or not?"
**23** Seeing straight through their trap, Jesus told them,
**24** "Show me a silver denarius coin. Whose face and name are stamped on it?" "Caesar's," they answered.
**25** "Well then," Jesus said, "pay back to Caesar whatever belongs to Caesar, and give back to God whatever belongs to God!"
**26** Right in front of the crowd, they couldn't catch him in a single mistake. Stunned by his reply, they kept their mouths shut.
**27** Later, a group of Sadducees—who deny that anyone rises from the dead—came up and tested him with a question:
**28** "Teacher, Moses wrote a law for us: if a married man dies without leaving any children, his brother must marry the widow and raise children to carry on his brother's name.
**29** Suppose there were seven brothers. The oldest got married and died with no kids.
**30** Then the second brother married her and died childless too,
**31** and the third married her—in fact, all seven married her and died without leaving any children.
**32** Finally, the woman passed away too.
**33** Now, in the resurrection, whose wife will she be, since all seven brothers were married to her?"
**34** Jesus answered them, "People in this present world get married and give each other in marriage.
**35** But those whom God counts worthy to enter the coming age and rise from the dead won't get married anymore—
**36** in fact, they can never die again! They'll be like angels, and as people of the resurrection, they are God's own children.
**37** Even Moses proved in the burning-bush passage that dead people rise again, when he calls the Lord 'Abraham's God, Isaac's God, and Jacob's God.'
**38** He isn't the God of dead corpses—he's the God of the living, because to him everybody is alive!"
**39** A few of the Torah scholars spoke up, "Teacher, that was a brilliant answer!"
**40** After that, nobody dared ask him another trap question.
**41** Then Jesus turned the tables and asked them, "How can people claim the Messiah is merely David's son?
**42** David himself wrote in the book of Psalms: 'God the Lord said to my Lord, "Take your seat at my right hand
**43** until I put all your enemies under your feet like a footstool."'
**44** Look—David himself calls him 'Lord'! So how can he also be David's son?"
**45** With the entire crowd listening in, Jesus told his disciples,
**46** "Stay away from those religious scholars who love parading around in fancy robes, craving respectful greetings in the marketplace, grabbing the front seats in synagogues and the seats of honor at dinner parties!
**47** They swallow up widows' homes and put on a show with long-winded prayers. Men like that are going to face the strictest judgment of all."

## Crossings & Lexical Notes

- **v. 9 `Ἄνθρωπος` (*Anthrōpos*):** SBLGNT, `WH`, `Treg`, and `RP` read simply "a man," whereas `NA28` adds the indefinite pronoun *tis* ("a certain man").
- **v. 18 `λικμήσει` (*likmēsei*):** Future of *likmaō* ("winnow, scatter like chaff, crush to powder"), merging the stone imagery of Ps 118:22 and Isa 8:14 with the crushing stone of Dan 2:34–45.
- **v. 20 `ἐγκαθέτους` (*enkathetous*):** Literally "ones set in / planted" (suborned agents or spies hired to entrap someone in speech), appearing only here in the NT.
- **v. 25 `ἀπόδοτε` (*apodote*):** From *apodidōmi* ("give back / repay what is owed"), contrasting the debt due to Caesar (whose *eikōn*, "image," is stamped on the coin) with the whole life owed to God (whose image humanity bears).
- **v. 36 `ἰσάγγελοι` (*isangeloi*):** Compound hapax legomenon in the NT ("equal to / like angels"), explaining why marriage—instituted to preserve lineage against mortality—is transcended when death itself ends.
- **v. 37 `ἐπὶ τῆς βάτου` (*epi tēs batou*):** "At the [passage about the] bush," an ancient rabbinic/Greek method of citing Scripture by its notable episode before chapter-and-verse divisions existed (Exod 3:6).
- **v. 45 `μαθηταῖς` (*mathētais*):** Printed by SBLGNT, `WH`, and `Treg` ("to the disciples"); `NA28` and `RP` add *autou* ("his disciples").
- **v. 46 `στολαῖς` (*stolais*):** Long, flowing outer robes signaling scholarly status and public prestige; paired with *prōtokathedrias* ("front benches" facing the congregation in the synagogue) and *prōtoklisias* ("first couches" at banquets).

## Supplemental Texts & Allusions

- **Isaiah 5:1–7** (echoed in v. 9): The Song of the Vineyard depicts Israel as the Lord's carefully planted vineyard from which he seeks rightful fruit.
- **Psalm 118:22–23 (117:22–23 LXX)** (quoted in v. 17): *"The very stone cast aside by the builders has become the chief cornerstone"*—applied to the vindication of the rejected beloved son.
- **Isaiah 8:14–15 & Daniel 2:34–45** (alluded to in v. 18): The sanctuary stone over which many stumble and the kingdom-stone that crushes rival empires to dust.
- **Deuteronomy 25:5–6** (cited in v. 28): Levirate marriage law requiring a surviving brother to marry a childless widow to preserve his deceased brother's name and inheritance.
- **Exodus 3:6** (quoted in v. 37): God reveals himself to Moses at the burning bush as *"Abraham's God, Isaac's God, and Jacob's God."*
- **Psalm 110:1 (109:1 LXX)** (quoted in vv. 42–43): *"The Lord declared to my Lord, 'Take your seat at my right side until I set your foes beneath your feet as a footstool.'"*
