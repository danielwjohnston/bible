## Clear & Faithful

**1** Summoning the twelve together, Jesus conferred upon them power and authority over every demon as well as to heal illnesses,
**2** and he sent them out to proclaim the kingdom of God and to heal the sick.
**3** He said to them, "Take nothing for your journey—no staff, no traveler's bag, no bread, no silver, and do not have two tunics apiece.
**4** Whatever house you enter, stay there, and from there depart.
**5** And wherever they do not welcome you, as you leave that town, shake the dust off your feet as a testimony against them."
**6** Departing from there, they traveled from village to village, announcing the good news and healing people in every place.
**7** Now Herod the tetrarch heard about all that was happening, and he was perplexed, because some were saying that John had been raised from the dead,
**8** others that Elijah had appeared, and others that one of the ancient prophets had arisen.
**9** Herod said, "John I beheaded; who then is this man about whom I hear such things?" And he kept trying to see him.
**10** When the apostles returned, they told him everything they had done. Taking them with him, he withdrew privately to a town called Bethsaida.
**11** Yet the crowds learned of it and followed him; and welcoming them, he spoke to them about the kingdom of God and healed those who needed curing.
**12** As the day began to draw to a close, the twelve came and said to him, "Send the crowd away so they may go into the surrounding villages and farms to find lodging and provisions, because we are here in a desolate place."
**13** He told them, "You yourselves provide them something to eat." They protested, "All we possess is five loaves of bread and two fish—unless we are supposed to go buy provisions for this entire crowd!"
**14** (There were roughly five thousand men present.) Jesus directed his disciples, "Seat them in dining groups of around fifty each."
**15** They did so and had everyone sit down.
**16** Picking up the five loaves and two fish, he gazed up into heaven, spoke a blessing over them, broke them apart, and kept handing them to the disciples to distribute to the crowd.
**17** Everyone ate and was satisfied, and what was left over by them was gathered up—twelve baskets of broken pieces.
**18** Now it happened while he was praying alone that the disciples were with him, and he asked them, "Who do the crowds say that I am?"
**19** They replied, "John the Baptist; some say Elijah; still others say one of the ancient prophets has come back to life."
**20** He asked them, "And you—who do you declare me to be?" Peter replied, "God's Anointed One!"
**21** Strictly warning them, he commanded them to tell this to no one,
**22** telling them, "The Son of Man has to endure much suffering, face rejection from the elders, chief priests, and scribes, be put to death, and on the third day be raised to life."
**23** He addressed everyone: "Whoever wishes to walk after me must renounce himself, take up his cross day by day, and follow me.
**24** For whoever aims to preserve his life will lose it, whereas whoever gives up his life on my account will preserve it.
**25** What does it profit a person to gain the whole world and lose or forfeit himself?
**26** Whoever is ashamed of me and of my teachings, of that person the Son of Man will be ashamed when he arrives in his own glory and that of the Father and the holy angels.
**27** Yet I tell you truly, there are some standing here who will certainly not taste death until they see the kingdom of God."
**28** About eight days after these words, taking Peter, John, and James with him, he went up onto the mountain to pray.
**29** And as he was praying, the appearance of his face changed, and his clothing became dazzling white.
**30** Suddenly two men were conversing with him—they were Moses and Elijah—
**31** who appeared in glory and were speaking of his departure, which he was about to bring to fulfillment in Jerusalem.
**32** Now Peter and those with him were heavy with sleep; yet staying fully awake, they saw his glory and the two men standing with him.
**33** Just as those two were parting from him, Peter addressed Jesus: "Master, how good it is for us to be right here! Let us set up three shelters—one for you, one for Moses, and one for Elijah"—not realizing what he was saying.
**34** Even as he spoke those words, a cloud formed and cast its shadow over them, and terror seized them as they stepped inside the cloud.
**35** Then a voice came out of the cloud, saying, "This is my Son, my Chosen One; listen to him!"
**36** When the voice had spoken, Jesus was found alone. They kept silent and in those days told no one anything of what they had seen.
**37** The following day, after they descended from the mountain, a huge crowd came out to meet him.
**38** Suddenly a man from the crowd cried out, "Teacher, I beg you to look upon my son, for he is my only child!
**39** A spirit seizes him, and he suddenly screams; it throws him into convulsions with foaming at the mouth, and scarcely leaves him, bruising him severely.
**40** I pleaded with your disciples to drive it out, but they proved unable."
**41** Jesus responded, "O unbelieving and twisted generation! How much longer must I remain among you and endure you? Bring your boy over here."
**42** Even while the boy was coming forward, the demon threw him down and shook him in convulsions. Yet Jesus sternly rebuked the unclean spirit, cured the boy, and restored him to his father.
**43** And all were astounded at the majesty of God. While everyone was marveling at everything he was doing, he said to his disciples,
**44** "Let these words sink into your ears: the Son of Man is about to be handed over into human hands."
**45** Yet they failed to grasp this statement; it remained veiled from them so they could not comprehend it, and they hesitated to question him about what he meant.
**46** An argument arose among them as to which of them might be the greatest.
**47** Knowing the reasoning of their hearts, Jesus took a little child and stood him beside himself,
**48** and said to them, "Whoever welcomes this little child in my name welcomes me, and whoever welcomes me welcomes the one who sent me; for the one who is least among all of you—this one is great."
**49** John spoke up: "Master, we spotted someone expelling demons using your name, and we tried to stop him because he does not travel with our company."
**50** Jesus said to him, "Do not stop him; for whoever is not against you is for you."
**51** Now when the days were approaching for his being taken up, he resolutely set his face to go to Jerusalem,
**52** and he sent messengers ahead of him. Going on their way, they entered a village of Samaritans to make preparations for him;
**53** yet they did not welcome him, because his face was set toward Jerusalem.
**54** Witnessing this, his disciples James and John asked, "Lord, do you want us to call down fire from heaven to burn them up?"
**55** But he turned and rebuked them.
**56** And they went on to another village.
**57** As they were traveling along the road, someone said to him, "I will follow you wherever you go."
**58** Jesus replied to him, "Foxes possess dens and wild birds have nests, yet the Son of Man lacks anywhere to rest his head."
**59** To another he said, "Follow me." Yet he replied, "Lord, permit me first to go and bury my father."
**60** Jesus told him, "Leave the dead to bury their own dead; as for yourself, go out and herald the kingdom of God far and wide."
**61** Another also said, "I will follow you, Lord; first let me say farewell to those at my home."

## Crossings & Lexical Notes

- **v. 14 `κλισίας` (*klisias*):** "Dining-groups" or "reclining companies" (from *klinō*, to recline), arranged in fifties like Israel's wilderness camp divisions (Exod 18:21) and Qumran messianic meal ranks.
- **v. 23 `καθʼ ἡμέραν` (*kath' hēmeran*):** "Daily" (`WH`, `Treg`, `NA28`), Luke's distinctive addition to the cross-bearing saying; omitted by `RP` (matching Matt 16:24 and Mark 8:34).
- **v. 31 `ἔξοδον` (*exodon*):** "Departure" (literally "exodus"), encompassing Jesus's death, resurrection, and ascension in Jerusalem as a new liberating exodus.
- **v. 35 `ὁ ἐκλελεγμένος` (*ho eklelegmenos*):** "The Chosen One" (`WH`, `Treg`, `NA28`), echoing Isa 42:1 (the chosen Servant); `RP` harmonizes to `ἀγαπητός` ("beloved") from Matt 17:5 and Mark 9:7.
- **v. 51 `ἀναλήμψεως` (*analēmpseōs*) & `τὸ πρόσωπον ἐστήρισεν` (*to prosōpon estērisen*):** *Analēmpsis* ("taking up / ascension") introduces Luke's long Jerusalem travel narrative (9:51–19:27); "set his face firmly" echoes Isa 50:7 LXX and Ezekiel's prophetic resolve.
- **v. 55–56 `ἐπετίμησεν αὐτοῖς` (*epetimēsen autois*):** `WH`, `Treg`, and `NA28` end v. 55 cleanly without the later scribal expansions found in `RP` ("and said, 'You do not know what spirit you are of; for the Son of Man did not come to destroy human lives but to save them'").
- **v. 62 `ὁ Ἰησοῦς` (*ho Iēsous*):** SBLGNT omits `πρὸς αὐτόν` ("to him") here; `WH` and `NA28` place `πρὸς αὐτὸν` before `ὁ Ἰησοῦς`, while `Treg` and `RP` place `πρὸς αὐτὸν` after it. `Εὔθετος` (*euthetos*) means "well-placed," "usable," or "fit."

## Supplemental Texts & Allusions

- **2 Kings 4:42–44 & Exodus 16:4–35** (background to vv. 12–17): Elisha multiplying loaves for a hundred men with leftovers, and God feeding Israel with manna in the wilderness.
- **Exodus 24:15–18 & 34:29–35** (echoed in vv. 28–36): Moses ascending the mountain with three companions, a radiant face, the overshadowing cloud of glory, and God's voice speaking from the cloud.
- **Deuteronomy 18:15 & Isaiah 42:1; Psalm 2:7** (blended in v. 35): "This is my Son, my Chosen One; listen to him!" unites the royal Messiah, the Servant, and the promised prophet like Moses.
- **2 Kings 1:9–12 & 1 Kings 19:19–21** (contrasted in vv. 54, 61–62): James and John ask to call down fire as Elijah did, while the third would-be disciple echoes Elisha's request to kiss his parents goodbye before leaving his plow.

**11** But the multitudes, perceiving it, followed him. He welcomed them, spoke to them of God’s Kingdom, and he cured those who needed healing.
**12** The day began to wear away; and the twelve came and said to him, “Send the multitude away, that they may go into the surrounding villages and farms and lodge and get food, for we are here in a deserted place.”
**13** But he said to them, “You give them something to eat.” They said, “We have no more than five loaves and two fish, unless we should go and buy food for all these people.”
**14** For they were about five thousand men. He said to his disciples, “Make them sit down in groups of about fifty each.”
**15** They did so, and made them all sit down.
**16** He took the five loaves and the two fish, and looking up to the sky, he blessed them, broke them, and gave them to the disciples to set before the multitude.
**17** They ate and were all filled. They gathered up twelve baskets of broken pieces that were left over.
**18** As he was praying alone, the disciples were near him, and he asked them, “Who do the multitudes say that I am?”
**19** They answered, “‘John the Baptizer,’ but others say, ‘Elijah,’ and others, that one of the old prophets has risen again.”
**20** He said to them, “But who do you say that I am?” Peter answered, “The Christ of God.”
**21** But he warned them and commanded them to tell this to no one,
**22** saying, “The Son of Man must suffer many things, and be rejected by the elders, chief priests, and scribes, and be killed, and the third day be raised up.”
**23** He said to all, “If anyone desires to come after me, let him deny himself, take up his cross, and follow me.
**24** For whoever desires to save his life will lose it, but whoever will lose his life for my sake will save it.
**25** For what does it profit a man if he gains the whole world, and loses or forfeits his own self?
**26** For whoever will be ashamed of me and of my words, of him will the Son of Man be ashamed when he comes in his glory, and the glory of the Father, and of the holy angels.
**27** But I tell you the truth: There are some of those who stand here who will in no way taste of death until they see God’s Kingdom.”
**28** About eight days after these sayings, he took with him Peter, John, and James, and went up onto the mountain to pray.
**29** As he was praying, the appearance of his face was altered, and his clothing became white and dazzling.
**30** Behold, two men were talking with him, who were Moses and Elijah,
**31** who appeared in glory and spoke of his departure, which he was about to accomplish at Jerusalem.
**32** Now Peter and those who were with him were heavy with sleep, but when they were fully awake, they saw his glory, and the two men who stood with him.
**33** As they were parting from him, Peter said to Jesus, “Master, it is good for us to be here. Let’s make three tents: one for you, one for Moses, and one for Elijah,” not knowing what he said.
**34** While he said these things, a cloud came and overshadowed them, and they were afraid as they entered into the cloud.
**35** A voice came out of the cloud, saying, “This is my beloved Son. Listen to him!”
**36** When the voice came, Jesus was found alone. They were silent, and told no one in those days any of the things which they had seen.
**37** On the next day, when they had come down from the mountain, a great multitude met him.
**38** Behold, a man from the crowd called out, saying, “Teacher, I beg you to look at my son, for he is my only born child.
**39** Behold, a spirit takes him, he suddenly cries out, and it convulses him so that he foams; and it hardly departs from him, bruising him severely.
**40** I begged your disciples to cast it out, and they couldn’t.”
**41** Jesus answered, “Faithless and perverse generation, how long shall I be with you and bear with you? Bring your son here.”
**42** While he was still coming, the demon threw him down and convulsed him violently. But Jesus rebuked the unclean spirit, healed the boy, and gave him back to his father.
**43** They were all astonished at the majesty of God. But while all were marveling at all the things which Jesus did, he said to his disciples,
**44** “Let these words sink into your ears, for the Son of Man will be delivered up into the hands of men.”
**45** But they didn’t understand this saying. It was concealed from them, that they should not perceive it, and they were afraid to ask him about this saying.
**46** An argument arose among them about which of them was the greatest.
**47** Jesus, perceiving the reasoning of their hearts, took a little child, and set him by his side,
**48** and said to them, “Whoever receives this little child in my name receives me. Whoever receives me receives him who sent me. For whoever is least among you all, this one will be great.”
**49** John answered, “Master, we saw someone casting out demons in your name, and we forbade him, because he doesn’t follow with us.”
**50** Jesus said to him, “Don’t forbid him, for he who is not against us is for us.”
**51** It came to pass, when the days were near that he should be taken up, he intently set his face to go to Jerusalem
**52** and sent messengers before his face. They went and entered into a village of the Samaritans, so as to prepare for him.
**53** They didn’t receive him, because he was traveling with his face set toward Jerusalem.
**54** When his disciples, James and John, saw this, they said, “Lord, do you want us to command fire to come down from the sky and destroy them, just as Elijah did?”
**55** But he turned and rebuked them, “You don’t know of what kind of spirit you are.
**56** For the Son of Man didn’t come to destroy men’s lives, but to save them.” They went to another village.
**57** As they went on the way, a certain man said to him, “I want to follow you wherever you go, Lord.”
**58** Jesus said to him, “The foxes have holes and the birds of the sky have nests, but the Son of Man has no place to lay his head.”
**59** He said to another, “Follow me!” But he said, “Lord, allow me first to go and bury my father.”
**60** But Jesus said to him, “Leave the dead to bury their own dead, but you go and announce God’s Kingdom.”
**61** Another also said, “I want to follow you, Lord, but first allow me to say good-bye to those who are at my house.”
**62** But Jesus said to him, “No one, having put his hand to the plow and looking back, is fit for God’s Kingdom.”
