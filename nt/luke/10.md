## Clear & Faithful

**1** After this, the Lord designated seventy-two additional followers and dispatched them in pairs ahead of himself into every town and district he planned to visit.
**2** He instructed them, "The harvest is abundant, yet the workers are scarce; pray, therefore, to the Lord of the harvest to thrust laborers out into his harvest.
**3** Go now; look—I am sending you forth like lambs into a pack of wolves.
**4** Carry no money bag, no traveler's sack, no sandals, and greet no one along the road.
**5** Whenever you step inside any household, first declare, 'May peace rest on this home!'
**6** If a person of peace lives there, your blessing of peace will settle on him; otherwise, it will come back to you.
**7** Remain inside that single home, eating and drinking whatever they set before you, since a worker deserves his pay. Stop moving from one house to another.
**8** Whatever town you enter and they welcome you, eat what is set before you;
**9** cure those who are ill there, and announce to them, 'The kingdom of God has drawn near upon you.'
**10** Whenever you enter a town, however, and people refuse to welcome you, walk out into its broad streets and declare,
**11** 'Even the dust from your town clinging to our feet we wipe off against you! Yet know this for certain: God's kingdom has arrived near.'
**12** I tell you that on that day it will be more bearable for Sodom than for that town.
**13** Woe to you, Chorazin! Woe to you, Bethsaida! Had the mighty deeds performed in you taken place in Tyre and Sidon, they would have repented ages ago, seated in sackcloth and ashes.
**14** Yet in the judgment it will be more bearable for Tyre and Sidon than for you.
**15** And you, Capernaum—will you be exalted to heaven? Down to Hades you shall be brought!
**16** Anyone who listens to you listens to me, and anyone who spurns you spurns me; moreover, whoever spurns me spurns the One who sent me."
**17** Later the seventy-two came back brimming with joy and reported, "Lord, even the demons submit to us when we speak your name!"
**18** He said to them, "I was watching Satan fall like lightning from heaven.
**19** Look—I have conferred upon you authority to step over snakes and scorpions, and over all the enemy's power, and nothing whatsoever will harm you.
**20** Even so, do not celebrate merely because the spirits obey you; celebrate rather because your names stand inscribed in heaven."
**21** In that very hour he rejoiced greatly in the Holy Spirit and said, "I praise you, Father, Lord of heaven and earth, because you have hidden these things from the wise and intelligent and revealed them to little children; yes, Father, for such was your gracious will.
**22** All things have been handed over to me by my Father, and no one knows who the Son is except the Father, or who the Father is except the Son and anyone to whom the Son chooses to reveal him."
**23** Turning to the disciples privately, he said, "Blessed are the eyes that see what you see!
**24** For I tell you that many prophets and kings longed to see what you see and did not see it, and to hear what you hear and did not hear it."
**25** Just then a legal scholar stood up to test him, saying, "Teacher, what must I do to inherit eternal life?"
**26** Jesus asked him, "What stands written in the Law? How do you interpret it?"
**27** He answered, "'Love the Lord your God out of your entire heart, within your entire soul, within your entire strength, and within your entire mind,' and 'your neighbor as yourself.'"
**28** Jesus told him, "You answered accurately; practice this, and you will live."
**29** Yet wanting to justify himself, he said to Jesus, "And who is my neighbor?"
**30** Taking up the question, Jesus replied, "A certain man was going down from Jerusalem to Jericho and fell among robbers, who stripped him, beat him, and went away leaving him half dead.
**31** As it happened, a priest was traveling down that same road, and upon spotting the man, he skirted past on the opposite side.
**32** Likewise a Levite also came to the place, saw him, and passed by on the opposite side.
**33** Yet a Samaritan who was traveling came upon him, and when he saw him, he was moved with compassion.
**34** Stepping up to him, he dressed his wounds, pouring olive oil and wine on them; then he lifted him onto his own pack animal, brought him to an inn, and cared for him.
**35** The next day he took out two denarii, gave them to the innkeeper, and said, 'Take care of him, and whatever more you spend, I will repay you when I come back.'
**36** In your judgment, which of those three men acted as a true neighbor to the traveler who fell prey to bandits?"
**37** The expert replied, "The one who practiced mercy toward him." Jesus told him, "Go and do the same yourself."
**38** Now as they went on their way, he entered a certain village, and a woman named Martha welcomed him.
**39** She had a sister called Mary, who sat down at Jesus's feet and was listening to his word.
**40** Martha, however, was distracted by all her preparations; coming up to him, she said, "Lord, do you not care that my sister has left me to serve alone? Tell her then to help me."
**41** The Lord replied to her, "Martha, Martha, you are fretting and flustered over so many details,
**42** yet few things are needed—or indeed only one. For Mary has chosen the good portion, which will not be taken away from her."

## Everyday English

**1** After this, the Lord chose seventy-two more followers and sent them out in pairs ahead of him into every town and village he planned to visit.
**2** He told them, "There's a huge harvest waiting, yet very few workers to bring it in. So pray to the Lord of the harvest to send more workers out into his fields!
**3** Now get going! Watch out—I'm sending you out like lambs straight into a pack of wolves.
**4** Don't pack a wallet, a travel bag, or extra sandals, and don't stop along the road for long greetings.
**5** Whenever you step into a home, start by saying, 'Peace to this house!'
**6** If someone there welcomes peace, your blessing of peace will stay with them; if not, it will come right back to you.
**7** Stay in that one home, eating and drinking whatever they serve you—after all, a worker deserves their pay. Don't keep hopping from house to house.
**8** Whenever you enter a town and the people welcome you, eat whatever food they put in front of you,
**9** heal anyone there who is sick, and tell them, 'God's kingdom has come right up to your doorstep!'
**10** But whenever you enter a town and they refuse to welcome you, walk out into the public square and announce,
**11** 'Even the dust from your streets stuck to our feet we're wiping off against you! Still, remember this: God's kingdom has come near!'
**12** I promise you, on judgment day it will be easier for Sodom than for that town!
**13** How terrible for you, Chorazin! How terrible for you, Bethsaida! If the miracles performed in your streets had happened in Tyre and Sidon, they would have repented ages ago, sitting in rough sackcloth and ashes.
**14** At the judgment, it will go easier for Tyre and Sidon than for you!
**15** And what about you, Capernaum? Do you think you'll be lifted all the way up to heaven? No—you'll be thrown straight down to Hades!

**26** Jesus answered, "What's written in the law? How do you read it?"

**28** "You nailed the answer!" Jesus said. "Do that, and you'll live."
**29** Wanting to make himself look good, the legal expert asked Jesus, "And just who counts as my neighbor?"
**30** Jesus answered with a story: "A man was traveling down the steep road from Jerusalem to Jericho when muggers jumped him, stripped off his clothes, beat him up, and left him half-dead in the ditch.
**31** By chance, a priest happened to be walking down that same road; when he spotted the man, he crossed over to the far side and kept going.
**32** A Levite came along next, saw him lying there, and also crossed over to the other side and walked past.
**33** Then a Samaritan traveling down the road came right up to him, saw him, and felt deep pity.
**34** He knelt beside him, poured olive oil and wine on his wounds to soothe and clean them, and bandaged them up. Then he lifted the man onto his own donkey, took him to an inn, and stayed to nurse him.
**35** The next morning, he pulled out two silver coins, handed them to the innkeeper, and said, 'Look after him, and if it costs anything extra, I'll pay you back when I come through again.'
**36** Now, which of those three men do you think acted like a real neighbor to the man who was mugged?"

**41** The Lord answered her gently, "Martha, Martha, you're worried and upset over so many things—

## Crossings & Lexical Notes

- **v. 1 `ἑβδομήκοντα δύο` (*hebdomēkonta dyo*):** "Seventy-two" (`WH`, `NA28`, supported by `P75` and `B`, also in v. 17), whereas `Treg` and `RP` read `ἑβδομήκοντα` ("seventy," echoing the seventy elders of Num 11:16–25 or seventy nations of Gen 10 MT vs seventy-two in Gen 10 LXX).
- **v. 21 `ἐξομολογοῦμαί` (*exomologoumai*):** In Septuagint and New Testament Greek, *exomologeomai* with the dative means not admitting guilt, but openly praising and acknowledging God's sovereign wisdom.
- **v. 34 `πανδοχεῖον` (*pandocheion*):** Literally "all-receiving place," a commercial roadside inn with a paid innkeeper (*pandocheus*, v. 35), distinct from the family/public *katalyma* ("guest room") of Luke 2:7.
- **v. 35 `δύο δηνάρια` (*dyo dēnaria*):** Two silver denarii—roughly two days' wages for an ordinary laborer, sufficient for several days' lodging and care at a Judean inn.
- **v. 40 `περισπᾶτο` (*periespato*):** Imperfect passive of *perispaō* ("to be pulled in different directions / distracted").
- **v. 42 `ὀλίγων δέ ἐστιν χρεία ἢ ἑνός` (*oligōn de estin chreia ē henos*):** Literally "there is need of few things or of one" (`WH`, supported by `P75`, `ℵ`, `B`, `C²`, `L`), playfully shifting from Martha's multi-course preparations ("few dishes, or just one") to spiritual priority; `Treg`, `RP`, and `NA28` read the shorter `ἑνὸς δέ ἐστιν χρεία` ("only one thing is needed").

## Supplemental Texts & Allusions

- **Numbers 11:16–25 & Genesis 10 (LXX)** (background to vv. 1, 17): Appointing seventy/seventy-two helpers recalls Moses's seventy elders sharing the Spirit and the seventy-two nations of the table of nations in the Greek Pentateuch.
- **Isaiah 14:13–15** (echoed in v. 15): The taunt against Babylon's boast ("You said in your heart, 'I will ascend to heaven'... yet you shall be brought down to Sheol/Hades") is applied to Capernaum.
- **Deuteronomy 6:5 & Leviticus 19:18** (quoted in v. 27): The two great commandments—wholehearted love of God and love of neighbor—paired directly before the parable of the Samaritan.
- **2 Chronicles 28:8–15** (background to vv. 30–35): Samaritans clothing, anointing, feeding, and mounting wounded Judean captives on donkeys to return them to Jericho.
