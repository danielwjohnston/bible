## Clear & Faithful

**1** When he had finished all his words in the hearing of the people, he entered Capernaum.
**2** Now a centurion's servant, who was highly valued by him, was sick and at the point of death.
**3** Having heard reports about Jesus, he dispatched Jewish elders to him, pleading for him to come and rescue his servant's life.
**4** When they came to Jesus, they pleaded with him earnestly, saying, "He is worthy for you to grant him this,
**5** since he genuinely loves our people and even funded the construction of our synagogue."
**6** So Jesus set out with them. When he was already not far from the estate, the centurion sent friends out to tell him, "Lord, do not bother yourself further, because I am not worthy to welcome you beneath my roof;
**7** that is why I did not even consider myself worthy to approach you in person. Just speak the command, and my servant will be cured.
**8** Even I am a man stationed under authority, with soldiers serving beneath me; when I order one, 'Go,' off he goes, and another, 'Come,' in he comes, and to my servant, 'Carry this out,' he carries it out."
**9** Hearing these words, Jesus marveled at him, and turning to the crowd following him, he said, "I tell you, not even in Israel have I found such great faith."
**10** And when those who had been sent returned to the house, they found the servant well.
**11** Soon afterward, he went to a town called Nain, and his disciples and a large crowd traveled with him.
**12** As he neared the city gate, a dead man was being carried out in a funeral procession—the only son of his mother, who was a widow, accompanied by a large crowd from the city.
**13** When the Lord saw her, he was moved with compassion for her and said to her, "Do not weep."
**14** Then he came up and touched the bier, and the bearers stood still. He said, "Young man, I say to you, get up!"
**15** The dead man sat up and began to speak, and Jesus gave him back to his mother.
**16** Reverent awe gripped everyone, and they praised God, proclaiming, "A mighty prophet has risen up in our midst!" and, "God has come to the aid of his people!"
**17** And this report about him went out through all Judea and all the surrounding district.
**18** John's disciples reported to him about all these things. Calling two of his disciples,
**19** John sent them to the Lord to ask, "Are you the one who is to come, or should we look for another?"
**20** Arriving before him, the men announced, "John the Baptist sent us to ask you, 'Are you the coming one, or should we watch for someone else?'"
**21** At that very moment he healed many people from sicknesses, plagues, and evil spirits, and restored sight to many who were blind.
**22** In reply he told them, "Go back and report to John what you have witnessed and heard: blind people regain sight, the lame walk, lepers are purified, the deaf hear, the dead are raised, and the poor receive good news.
**23** And blessed is anyone who is not offended by me."
**24** Once John's messengers departed, Jesus began addressing the crowds concerning John: "What did you travel out into the wilderness to gaze at? A reed swayed by the wind?
**25** What, then, did you go out to see? A man clothed in luxurious garments? Look—those dressed in splendid clothing and living in indulgence reside in royal courts.
**26** What, then, did you go out to see? A prophet? Yes, I assure you, and someone far greater than a prophet!
**27** This is the very one about whom Scripture says: 'Look, I dispatch my messenger ahead of you, who will make ready your road in front of you.'
**28** I tell you, among those born of women there is no one greater than John; yet the least in the kingdom of God is greater than he."
**29** (All the people who heard this, including the tax collectors, acknowledged God's justice, having been baptized with John's baptism;
**30** but the Pharisees and the lawyers rejected God's purpose for themselves, not having been baptized by him.)
**31** "With what, then, can I compare the people of this generation, and whom do they resemble?
**32** They are like children sitting in the marketplace and calling out to one another: 'We played the flute for you, and you did not dance; we sang a mourning song, and you did not weep.'
**33** John the Baptist arrived abstaining from bread and wine, yet you say, 'He is possessed by a demon!'
**34** The Son of Man arrived eating and drinking, and you say, 'Look at that glutton and wine-drinker, a companion of tax collectors and sinners!'
**35** Yet wisdom is vindicated by all her children."
**36** One of the Pharisees asked Jesus to dine with him; entering the Pharisee's home, he took his place at the table.
**37** There happened to be a woman in that city known as a sinner; learning that he was dining inside the Pharisee's house, she brought an alabaster flask of fragrant oil.
**38** Standing behind him near his feet, crying, she started bathing his feet with her tears and drying them with the hair of her head, repeatedly kissing his feet and anointing them with the fragrant oil.
**39** Seeing this, the Pharisee who had invited him thought to himself, "If this fellow were genuinely a prophet, he would recognize who and what sort of woman is touching him—that she is a sinner."
**40** Jesus answered him, "Simon, I have something to say to you." And he replied, "Say it, Teacher."
**41** "A certain creditor held two debtors: one owed five hundred silver denarii, while the other owed fifty.
**42** When they could not pay, he graciously canceled the debt for both. Which of them, then, will love him more?"
**43** Simon answered, "I suppose the one for whom he canceled more." And Jesus said to him, "You have judged rightly."
**44** Turning toward the woman, he addressed Simon: "Do you see this woman? When I stepped into your home, you offered me no water for my feet, whereas she bathed my feet with tears and dried them with her hair.
**45** You gave me no kiss, yet from the time I came in she has not stopped kissing my feet.
**46** You poured no olive oil over my head, yet she anointed my feet with costly perfume.
**47** For this reason I tell you, her many sins stand forgiven, because she has shown great love; yet the one forgiven little shows little love."
**48** Then he assured her, "Your sins stand forgiven."
**49** At this, the fellow dinner guests began murmuring among themselves, "Who is this man who presumes even to forgive sins?"
**50** Jesus told the woman, "Your trust has delivered you; go forward in peace."

## Everyday English

**1** Once Jesus had finished speaking all these words to the listening crowd, he walked into Capernaum.
**2** There, a Roman army officer had a servant he cared about deeply who was critically ill and about to die.
**3** When the officer heard about Jesus, he sent some respected Jewish elders to beg Jesus to come and save his servant's life.
**4** They hurried to Jesus and pleaded earnestly with him: "This man really deserves your help,
**5** because he loves our people and even paid to build our synagogue."
**6** So Jesus started walking with them. Before he got near the house, the officer sent some friends out with a message: "Lord, don't bother coming all the way here—I'm not worthy to have you step under my roof.
**7** That's why I didn't even feel worthy to come meet you in person. Just give the command, and my servant will be healed!
**8** I understand how authority works—I answer to commanders above me, and I have soldiers under me. When I tell one soldier, 'Go,' he goes; when I tell another, 'Come,' he comes; and when I tell my servant, 'Do this,' he does it."
**9** Jesus was amazed when he heard that. Turning back to the crowd following behind him, he declared, "I'm telling you, I haven't found faith like this anywhere in Israel!"
**10** And when the officer's friends got back to the house, they found the servant completely healthy.
**11** Not long after that, Jesus traveled to a town called Nain, accompanied by his disciples and a large crowd.
**12** Just as he approached the town gate, a funeral procession was coming out—a widow's only son had died, and a big crowd from the town was walking with her.
**13** When the Lord saw her, his heart broke with compassion. "Don't cry," he told her.
**14** Stepping up, he touched the open coffin, and the pallbearers stopped in their tracks. Jesus spoke: "Young man, I'm telling you, get up!"
**15** The dead man sat straight up and started talking, and Jesus handed him back to his mother.
**16** Everyone was gripped with awe and praised God: "A mighty prophet has appeared among us!" and, "God has stepped in to help his people!"
**17** Word about what he'd done spread across all of Judea and every surrounding town.
**18** John's followers told him all about these events. Calling two of his disciples over,
**19** John sent them to ask the Lord, "Are you the Messiah who's supposed to come, or should we keep waiting for someone else?"
**20** When the men reached Jesus, they asked, "John the Baptist sent us to ask you: 'Are you the one who is coming, or should we look for someone else?'"
**21** Right then and there, Jesus cured many people of sicknesses, painful diseases, and evil spirits, and gave sight to many who were blind.
**22** Then he answered the messengers: "Go back and tell John what you've seen and heard with your own eyes: blind people are seeing again, crippled people are walking, people with skin diseases are made clean, deaf people are hearing, the dead are being raised back to life, and good news is being preached to the poor.
**23** And blessed is anyone who doesn't trip over me and fall away."
**24** Once John's messengers left, Jesus turned to the crowds and spoke about John: "What did you hike out into the desert to see? Tall grass blowing in the wind?
**25** Seriously, what did you go out looking for? A man dressed in luxury silk? Look, people wearing fancy robes and living in pampered luxury live in royal palaces!
**26** Then what did you go out to see? A prophet? Yes—I'm telling you, someone far greater than an ordinary prophet!
**27** He's the very one Scripture wrote about: 'Look, I'm sending my messenger ahead of you to clear your path before you.'
**28** Let me tell you: out of everyone ever born, nobody is greater than John—yet even the smallest person in God's kingdom is greater than he is."
**29** (Everyone listening—even the tax collectors—agreed that God's way was right, because they'd already been baptized by John.
**30** But the Pharisees and legal experts rejected God's plan for themselves by refusing John's baptism.)
**31** "How can I describe the people of this generation? What are they like?
**32** They're like kids sitting in the town square shouting at one another: 'We played happy wedding music on the flute, and you wouldn't dance! We sang a sad funeral song, and you wouldn't cry!'
**33** Look—John the Baptist showed up fasting from bread and wine, and you claim, 'He's possessed by a demon!'
**34** Now the Son of Man comes eating and drinking like everyone else, and you say, 'Look at that glutton and heavy drinker—hanging out with tax collectors and sinners!'
**35** Still, God's wisdom is proven right by everyone who accepts it."
**36** One day, a Pharisee invited Jesus over for a meal, so Jesus went into his home and took his place at the table.
**37** A woman in that town who had a reputation as a sinner found out Jesus was eating at the Pharisee's house, so she brought along an alabaster bottle of expensive perfume.
**38** Standing behind him near his feet, she was crying so hard her tears began soaking his feet. She wiped them dry with her long hair, kissed his feet over and over, and poured the perfume over them.
**39** When the Pharisee who'd invited Jesus saw what was happening, he thought to himself, "If this man really were a prophet, he'd know what kind of woman is touching him—she's a notorious sinner!"
**40** Jesus spoke up to him: "Simon, I have something I want to tell you." "Go ahead, Teacher," Simon answered.
**41** "Two people owed money to the same lender. One owed five hundred silver coins, and the other owed fifty.
**42** Since neither of them had the money to pay him back, he generously forgave both of their debts. Now tell me—which of those two will love him more?"
**43** "I'd guess the one who had the bigger debt wiped out," Simon replied. "You're completely right," Jesus said.
**44** Then, turning toward the woman while still speaking to Simon, he continued: "Do you see this woman? When I walked into your house, you didn't even offer me water to wash my feet—yet she washed my feet with her tears and dried them with her hair!
**45** You didn't greet me with a welcoming kiss—yet from the moment I walked in, she hasn't stopped kissing my feet!
**46** You didn't put even ordinary olive oil on my head—yet she poured costly perfume on my feet!
**47** So I'm telling you, her many sins are forgiven—that's why she's shown so much love. Whoever has only a little forgiven shows only a little love."
**48** Then Jesus told the woman, "Your sins are forgiven."
**49** The other dinner guests began whispering to each other, "Who does this man think he is, forgiving sins?"
**50** Jesus told the woman, "Your faith has rescued you. Go in peace."

## Crossings & Lexical Notes

- **v. 6 `μὴ σκύλλου` (*mē skyllou*):** Present imperative passive/middle of *skyllō* ("do not trouble/bother yourself"; originally "flay/harass"), a polite colloquialism also used in Luke 8:49.
- **v. 7 `ἰαθήτω` (*iathētō*):** Aorist passive imperative ("let him be healed," `WH`, `Treg`, `NA28`), emphasizing the centurion's trust in an authoritative command; `RP` reads future indicative `ἰαθήσεται` ("will be healed").
- **v. 12 `μονογενὴς` (*monogenēs*):** "Only-begotten" or "only" child (used by Luke in 7:12; 8:42; 9:38 for an only son or daughter), sharpening the widow's plight without an heir.
- **v. 14 `σοροῦ` (*sorou*):** An open funeral bier or plank litter (rather than a sealed modern coffin), explaining how the young man sat up immediately when spoken to.
- **v. 35 `ἐδικαιώθη` (*edikaiōthē*):** "Was vindicated / shown to be right" (`dikaioō`, matching v. 29 where the people "justified" God by acknowledging his righteousness).
- **v. 38 `κατεφίλει` (*katephilei*):** Imperfect of the intensive compound *kataphileō* ("was kissing warmly/repeatedly"), contrasting with the simple *philēma* ("a greeting kiss") Simon omitted in v. 45.
- **v. 47 `ὅτι ἠγάπησεν πολύ` (*hoti ēgapēsen poly*):** As the preceding parable of the canceled debts (vv. 41–43) demonstrates, her abundant love is the *evidence* (not the prior price) of her abundant forgiveness—"for [as is evident because] she loved much."

## Supplemental Texts & Allusions

- **1 Kings 17:17–24 & 2 Kings 4:32–37** (echoed in vv. 11–16): Raising the widow's son at Nain recalls Elijah restoring the Zarephath widow's son ("and placed him back in his mother's care," 1 Kgs 17:23 LXX) and evokes the crowd's acclaim, "A mighty prophet has risen up in our midst!"
- **Isaiah 29:18–19; 35:5–6; 61:1** (echoed in v. 22): Jesus points John's disciples to Isaiah's messianic signs—sight for the blind, hearing for the deaf, cleansing, resurrection, and good news to the poor.
- **Malachi 3:1 & Exodus 23:20** (quoted in v. 27): Conflates the Lord's messenger sent before Israel's face (Exod 23:20) with the messenger who prepares the Lord's way (Mal 3:1).

**43** Simon answered, “He, I suppose, to whom he forgave the most.” He said to him, “You have judged correctly.”
**44** Turning to the woman, he said to Simon, “Do you see this woman? I entered into your house, and you gave me no water for my feet, but she has wet my feet with her tears, and wiped them with the hair of her head.
**45** You gave me no kiss, but she, since the time I came in, has not ceased to kiss my feet.
**46** You didn’t anoint my head with oil, but she has anointed my feet with ointment.
**47** Therefore I tell you, her sins, which are many, are forgiven, for she loved much. But one to whom little is forgiven, loves little.”
**48** He said to her, “Your sins are forgiven.”
**49** Those who sat at the table with him began to say to themselves, “Who is this who even forgives sins?”
**50** He said to the woman, “Your faith has saved you. Go in peace.”
