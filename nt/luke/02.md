## Clear & Faithful

**1** Around that time a decree was issued by Caesar Augustus ordering a census of the entire inhabited world.
**2** This was the first registration, taken while Quirinius was governing Syria.
**3** Everyone traveled to be registered, each to his own ancestral town.
**4** So Joseph traveled up from the Galilean town of Nazareth to Judea, to David's city known as Bethlehem, since he belonged to David's household and ancestral line,
**5** to register with Mary, his betrothed, who was pregnant.
**6** While they remained there, the time arrived for her delivery.
**7** She bore her firstborn son, bound him in swaddling cloths, and placed him in a manger, as there was no place for them inside the guest quarters.
**8** Now there were shepherds in that same region living out in the fields and keeping watch over their flock by night.
**9** An angel of the Lord suddenly stood beside them, the Lord's radiance blazed around them, and they were overwhelmed with fear.
**10** The angel assured them, "Do not be frightened, for look—I am announcing to you good tidings of immense joy intended for the entire people:
**11** today in the city of David a Savior has been born to you, who is Messiah the Lord.
**12** This will be the sign for you: you will find a baby wrapped in bands of cloth and lying in a manger."
**13** All at once a vast company of heaven's angelic host joined that angel, giving praise to God and declaring,
**14** "Glory to God in the highest, and on earth peace among people on whom his favor rests."
**15** Once the angels had left them to return into heaven, the shepherds urged one another, "Let us go straight to Bethlehem and witness this event that has occurred, which the Lord has revealed to us."
**16** They went in haste and found Mary and Joseph, and the baby lying in the manger.
**17** When they saw him, they made known what had been told them concerning this child.
**18** All who heard it marveled at what the shepherds told them.
**19** But Mary treasured all these words, pondering them in her heart.
**20** Then the shepherds went back, giving glory and praise to God over everything they had heard and witnessed, just as it had been spoken to them.
**21** When eight days were completed for his circumcision, he was named Jesus, the name given by the angel before he was conceived in the womb.
**22** Once the days of their ceremonial purification under Moses's law were finished, they carried him up to Jerusalem to dedicate him to the Lord—
**23** just as the Lord's law prescribes, "Every firstborn male who opens the womb must be consecrated as holy to the Lord"—
**24** and to present the offering specified by the Lord's law: either a pair of turtledoves or two young doves.
**25** At that time a righteous and God-fearing man named Simeon lived in Jerusalem, eagerly awaiting Israel's consolation, and the Holy Spirit rested on him.
**26** The Holy Spirit had revealed to him that he would not experience death before seeing the Lord's Messiah.
**27** Led by the Spirit, he came into the temple courts; and when the parents brought in the child Jesus to carry out for him the custom of the law,
**28** Simeon took him into his arms, blessed God, and said:
**29** "Now, Sovereign Lord, you are releasing your servant in peace according to your word,
**30** for my own eyes have beheld your deliverance,
**31** that you have prepared in the presence of all peoples—
**32** a light bringing revelation to the nations and splendor to your people Israel."
**33** His father and mother were amazed at what was being said about him.
**34** Simeon pronounced a blessing on them and told his mother Mary, "Understand this: this child is destined to bring about the downfall and rising of many in Israel, and to stand as a sign that people will oppose—
**35** and a sword will pierce your own soul as well—so that the inner thoughts of many hearts may be laid bare."
**36** There was also a prophetess, Anna, daughter of Phanuel, of the tribe of Asher. She was very old, having lived with her husband seven years after her marriage,
**37** and then as a widow until the age of eighty-four. She never left the temple, worshiping night and day with fasting and prayer.
**38** Coming up at that very hour, she began giving thanks to God and speaking about him to all who were waiting for the redemption of Jerusalem.
**39** When they had fulfilled everything required by the law of the Lord, they returned to Galilee, to their own town of Nazareth.
**40** The child grew and became strong, filled with wisdom, and the grace of God was upon him.
**41** Now his parents went to Jerusalem every year at the Feast of the Passover.
**42** When Jesus reached twelve years of age, they traveled up for the feast in keeping with annual custom.
**43** After the days of the feast were finished, as they were returning, the boy Jesus stayed behind in Jerusalem, and his parents did not know it.
**44** Supposing him to be in the traveling caravan, they went a day's journey and then began searching for him among their relatives and acquaintances.
**45** Failing to locate him, they turned back to Jerusalem to look for him.
**46** Three days later they discovered him inside the temple courts, seated in the midst of the teachers, listening to them and posing questions.
**47** Everyone who listened to him was amazed by his discernment and his replies.
**48** His parents were astounded when they saw him, and his mother asked him, "Child, why have you done this to us? Look—your father and I have been searching for you in great distress."
**49** He replied to them, "Why were you looking for me? Did you not realize I had to be in my Father's house?"
**50** Yet they did not understand what he was saying to them.
**51** Then he went down with them, came to Nazareth, and remained obedient to them. His mother kept all these things treasured in her heart.
**52** Meanwhile, Jesus kept advancing in wisdom and stature, and in favor before God and humanity.

## Everyday English

**1** Around that time, Emperor Augustus issued an order for a census across the entire Roman world.
**2** This was the very first census, conducted when Quirinius was governor of Syria.
**3** So everybody had to travel back to their family hometown to register.
**4** That meant Joseph left Nazareth in Galilee and went up to Judea, to David's hometown of Bethlehem, since he belonged to King David's family line.
**5** He went there to register alongside Mary, who was engaged to him and expecting a baby.
**6** While they were in Bethlehem, Mary went into labor,
**7** and she gave birth to her first son. She wrapped him snugly in strips of cloth and laid him in an animal feeding trough, since there wasn't any space for them in the guest room.
**8** Out in the nearby countryside, some shepherds were camping in the fields, taking turns guarding their sheep through the night.
**9** All at once, an angel of the Lord stood right in front of them, and the Lord's dazzling glory lit up the night around them. They were terrified.
**10** "Don't be afraid!" the angel told them. "Look, I'm bringing you wonderful news that will fill all the people with joy:
**11** Today, right in David's town, a Savior has been born for you—he is the Messiah, the Lord!
**12** Here's how you'll recognize him: you'll find a newborn wrapped in strips of cloth, lying in a feeding trough."
**13** Instantly, a huge company of heaven's angels joined that angel, praising God and singing:
**14** "Glory to God in the highest heavens, and peace on earth to the people he delights in!"
**15** Once the angels left them and went back into heaven, the shepherds said to each other, "Come on, let's head straight into Bethlehem and see what happened—what the Lord just told us about!"
**16** They hurried off and found Mary, Joseph, and the baby lying right there in the feeding trough.
**17** After seeing him, they told everyone what the angel had said about this little boy.
**18** Everyone who heard the shepherds' story was amazed.
**19** Mary, though, held onto every word and kept turning it all over in her heart.
**20** Then the shepherds headed back to their flocks, praising and thanking God for everything they'd seen and heard—it matched every detail they'd been told.
**21** Eight days later, when it was time to circumcise the baby, they named him Jesus, the name the angel had given before Mary even became pregnant.
**22** When the waiting period for their ritual purification under Moses' law was over, Mary and Joseph brought Jesus up to Jerusalem to dedicate him to the Lord.
**23** The Lord's law says, "Every firstborn boy must be set apart as holy to the Lord."
**24** They also brought the sacrifice the law required—either two turtledoves or two young pigeons.
**25** Now there was a man living in Jerusalem named Simeon, a good and deeply devout man who was waiting eagerly for God to comfort Israel. The Holy Spirit rested on him
**26** and had promised him he wouldn't die until he saw the Lord's Messiah with his own eyes.
**27** Guided by the Spirit, Simeon walked into the temple courtyard just as Jesus' parents were carrying the baby in to fulfill the ceremony required by the law.
**28** Simeon took the child into his arms and praised God:
**29** "Sovereign Master, now you can let your servant depart in peace, just as you promised!
**30** My own eyes have seen the salvation
**31** you prepared in full view of every nation—
**32** a light that reveals you to the Gentiles and brings honor to your people Israel."
**33** Jesus' father and mother stood amazed at what Simeon was saying about him.
**34** Simeon blessed them and then told Mary, the baby's mother, "Listen—this child is set to cause both the downfall and the rise of many in Israel. He'll be a sign that people argue against—
**35** and sorrow will cut right through your own heart like a sword—so that what people really think deep inside will come out into the open."
**36** There was also an elderly prophetess named Anna, Phanuel's daughter from the tribe of Asher. She had been married for only seven years as a young woman
**37** and had lived as a widow until she was eighty-four. She never left the temple grounds, worshiping God day and night through fasting and prayer.
**38** Stepping up at that exact moment, she began thanking God and telling everyone who was longing for Jerusalem's rescue about this child.
**39** Once Mary and Joseph had completed everything the Lord's law required, they went back home to Nazareth in Galilee.
**40** There the boy grew up strong and full of wisdom, and God's favor rested on him.
**41** Every year, Jesus' parents made the trip to Jerusalem for the Passover festival.
**42** When Jesus turned twelve, they traveled up for the festival just like always.
**43** After the week of celebrations ended and they started heading home, young Jesus stayed behind in Jerusalem without his parents realizing it.
**44** Assuming he was walking somewhere in the big travel group, they traveled a full day before checking for him among their family and friends.
**45** When they couldn't find him anywhere, they turned right back around to Jerusalem to look for him.
**46** Three days later, they finally found him inside the temple courtyard, sitting among the religious teachers, listening closely and asking them questions.
**47** Everyone listening to him was blown away by his insight and his answers.
**48** His parents were stunned when they saw him. "Son, why did you do this to us?" his mother asked. "Your father and I have been frantic looking for you!"
**49** "Why were you looking for me?" Jesus asked them. "Didn't you realize I had to be in my Father's house?"
**50** They didn't grasp what he meant by that.
**51** So he walked back home to Nazareth with them and obeyed them as their son, while his mother kept every single memory tucked safely in her heart.
**52** And Jesus kept growing in wisdom, in height, and in favor with both God and everyone around him.

## Crossings & Lexical Notes

- **v. 1 `οἰκουμένην` (*oikoumenēn*):** Literally "the inhabited [earth]," used in Hellenistic and Roman administrative Greek for the Roman Empire.
- **v. 7 `καταλύματι` (*katalymati*):** "Guest room," "upper room," or "lodging place" (the same noun Luke uses for the upper guest room in 22:11, distinct from *pandocheion*, "commercial inn," in 10:34), likely referring to a crowded family guest chamber rather than a commercial hotel.
- **v. 14 `εὐδοκίας` (*eudokias*):** Genitive ("of good will / favor," supported by `WH`, `Treg`, `NA28`), meaning people upon whom God's gracious favor rests; `RP` reads nominative `εὐδοκία` ("good will among people").
- **v. 29 `δέσποτα` (*despota*) & `ἀπολύεις` (*apolyeis*):** Simeon addresses God as *despotēs* ("Sovereign Master") and uses *apolyō* ("release/dismiss" a slave or sentinel from duty), framing his death as peaceful discharge after seeing the Messiah.
- **v. 35 `ῥομφαία` (*rhomphaia*):** A large broadsword (echoing the sword of judgment/testing in Ezek 14:17 LXX), signifying Mary's piercing anguish as Jesus faces rejection.
- **v. 37 `ἕως ἐτῶν ὀγδοήκοντα τεσσάρων` (*heōs etōn ogdoēkonta tessarōn*):** Can denote either Anna's total age ("until eighty-four years [of age]") or the duration of her widowhood ("for eighty-four years" after seven years of marriage).
- **v. 49 `ἐν τοῖς τοῦ πατρός μου` (*en tois tou patros mou*):** Neuter plural *en tois* with a genitive can mean "in my Father's house/courts" or "about my Father's business/affairs"; the temple setting favors "house" while leaving room for his mission.
- **v. 52 `σοφίᾳ καὶ ἡλικίᾳ` (*sophia kai hēlikia*):** *Hēlikia* encompasses both physical stature and maturity/age. Manuscripts vary slightly in word order and article/preposition usage across `WH`, `Treg`, `NA28`, and `RP`.

## Supplemental Texts & Allusions

- **Micah 5:2 (5:1 MT) & 2 Samuel 7:12–16** (echoed in vv. 4, 11): Bethlehem ("city of David") as the birthplace of Israel's promised Davidic ruler.
- **Exodus 13:2, 12 & Leviticus 12:2–8** (cited in vv. 22–24): Consecration of every firstborn male to the Lord and the purification offering of two turtledoves or two young pigeons for households unable to afford a lamb.
- **Isaiah 40:1; 49:6; 52:10** (echoed in vv. 25, 30–32): Israel's consolation (*paraklēsis*), God's salvation displayed before all peoples, and a light illuminating the nations.
