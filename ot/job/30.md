## Clear & Faithful

**1** "But now those younger than I am laugh at me—men whose fathers I would have disdained to set alongside the dogs of my flock.
**2** Even the strength of their hands—what use was it to me? Their full vigor had perished from them.
**3** Gaunt with want and hunger, they gnawed the arid wilderness, in the gloom of waste and desolation,
**4** plucking saltwort leaves among the scrub, with broom-tree roots as their food.
**5** They were driven out from human company; people shouted after them as after a thief,
**6** forcing them to dwell in gloomy ravines, in holes of earth and jagged rock.
**7** Among the bushes they brayed like wild asses, huddled together beneath the thornbushes—
**8** a senseless rabble, a nameless brood, whipped out of the land.
**9** Yet now I have become the subject of their mocking song; I am a byword on their lips.
**10** They loathe me and keep their distance from me, nor do they hesitate to spit before my face.
**11** Because God has unstrung my bow and humbled me, they have thrown off all restraint in my presence.
**12** At my right hand this rabble rises up; they trip my feet and build their siege-ramps of ruin against me.
**13** They tear up my path and hasten my downfall, with none to hold them back.
**14** As through a wide breach in a wall they burst in; amid the crashing ruin they roll forward.
**15** Terrors have turned against me; my honor is chased away like the wind, and my deliverance vanishes like a passing cloud.
**16** And now my soul pours itself out within me; days of affliction have gripped me.
**17** Night pierces my bones from within, and my gnawing pains take no rest.
**18** With overwhelming force my garment is twisted out of shape; it chokes me like the collar of my tunic.
**19** God has flung me into the mire, and I have come to resemble dust and ashes.
**20** I cry out to you for help, yet you do not answer me; I stand before you, and you merely stare at me.
**21** You have turned cruel toward me; with the sheer strength of your hand you assail me.
**22** You lift me up into the wind and make me ride upon it, then dissolve me in the roaring tempest.
**23** Indeed, I am certain you are leading me back to death, to the meeting-house set aside for every living creature.
**24** Surely a sinking man stretches out his hand in a heap of ruins—in his calamity he cries out for rescue!
**25** Did I not weep for those whose days were harsh? Was my soul not grieved for the needy?
**26** Yet when I looked for good, calamity arrived; when I waited for light, deep darkness came instead.
**27** My inward parts churn without ceasing; days of misery confront me.
**28** I go about darkened, without the sun; I stand in the public assembly and cry out for help.
**29** I have grown to be kin to desert jackals and a comrade to the ostriches.
**30** My skin peels black from off me, and my bones burn with feverish heat.
**31** So my lyre is tuned to mourning, and my pipe to the sound of those who weep."

## Everyday English

**1** "Look at me now—kids half my age are laughing in my face, kids whose fathers weren't even good enough to watch my sheepdogs!
**2** What good would their muscle have done me anyway? Their energy was already completely spent.
**3** Starving and destitute, they chewed on dry desert scrub out in barren wastelands after dark,
**4** picking salty weeds off desert shrubs and eating tough broom roots just to survive.
**5** Society chased them out; people yelled at them as if they were common thieves,
**6** so they lived in jagged gullies, dirt caves, and rocky crevices.
**7** They howled in the brush and crowded together under thorny weeds—
**8** foolish nobodies with no reputation, driven out of the country.
**9** Now those very people turn my misery into a joke—I'm the punchline of their songs!
**10** They're disgusted by me and stay far away, yet they don't hold back from spitting right at me.
**11** Since God snapped my bowstring and brought me low, they've dropped every ounce of restraint around me.
**12** A hostile mob swarms up on my right side; they knock my feet out from under me and lay down tracks to destroy me.
**13** They rip up the road ahead of me and celebrate my collapse, and nobody steps in to stop them.
**14** They pour in like a flood through a broken city wall, tumbling over each other in the wreckage.
**15** Panic has cornered me; my dignity gets blown away in the wind, and any hope of rescue drifts off like mist.
**16** Now my spirit is draining right out of me; agonizing days have locked me in their grip.
**17** Every night pain drills straight into my bones, and the ache never lets up for a second.
**18** My clothes hang twisted on my body like a straitjacket, strangling me around the neck like a tight shirt collar.
**19** God threw me straight into the mud—now I look just like dirt and ashes.
**20** I scream out to you, God, and you don't say a word! I stand right here pleading, and you just watch me suffer.
**21** You've turned ruthless against me, attacking me with all your power.
**22** You scoop me up into the storm winds, toss me around, and shred me to pieces in the gale.
**23** I know where this ends—you're sending me straight to death, the final destination waiting for everyone alive.
**24** Still, doesn't a crushed man reach out from the rubble and beg for help when disaster hits?
**25** Didn't I cry whenever someone else had a brutal day? Didn't my heart break for people in poverty?
**26** I expected blessing, and disaster showed up; I waited for sunrise, and pitch-black night rolled in.
**27** My stomach churns nonstop; days of suffering stare me in the face.
**28** I walk around sunless and dark; I stand up in court and cry out for justice.
**29** Now I'm practically family to howling jackals and screeching ostriches.
**30** My skin turns black and peels away; my bones are on fire with fever.
**31** My harp only plays funeral songs now, and my flute sounds like a crowd of mourners."

## Crossings & Lexical Notes

- **v. 11 `יִתְרִי` (*yitri*, Qere) vs. `יִתְרוֹ` (*yitro*, Ketiv):** The Qere reads "my bowstring/cord" (1cs: God loosened Job's bowstring, disarming him, reversing 29:20), whereas the Ketiv reads "his cord" (3ms: God loosened his own restraint or the rabble's cord).
- **v. 17 `עֹרְקַי` (*oreqay*):** Literally "my gnawers"—either Job's "gnawing pains" or his "sinews/arteries" throbbing relentlessly through the night.
- **v. 22 `תּוּשִׁיָּה` (*tushiyyah*, Qere) vs. `תשוה` (Ketiv):** The Qere *tushiyyah* elsewhere means "sound wisdom/success/substance" (often parsed here as Job's substance dissolving, or as a textual echo of *teshu'ah*, "roaring storm"), while the unpointed Ketiv `תשוה` can be read as a storm/crash word or verb "you make equal/level."
- **v. 24 `לָהֶן שׁוּעַ` (*lahen shua*):** A notoriously terse line; *shua* means "cry for help" (or "wealth/opulence"), yielding the sense that even amid total collapse (`בְּעִי`, "in a ruin-heap") a victim instinctively reaches out and cries for rescue.
- **v. 29 `תַּנִּים` (*tannim*) & `בְּנוֹת יַעֲנָה` (*benot ya'anah*):** Jackals and "daughters of the ostrich"—desert scavengers whose eerie nocturnal cries symbolize utter isolation and lamentation (compare Mic 1:8).

## Supplemental Texts & Allusions

- **Micah 1:8** (lament motif): Employs the same pairing of mourning like jackals and ostriches to express desolate grief.
- **Ecclesiastes 12:5–7** (mortality motif): Mirrors Job's "house appointed for all the living" (`בֵּית מוֹעֵד לְכָל־חָי`) as humanity's shared journey to the grave and return to dust.
- **Psalm 102:3–6** (affliction imagery): Parallel complaint of burning bones, blackened skin, and lonely desert birds in bodily suffering.

## Reference Text (WEB Updated, Public Domain)

**1** “But now those who are younger than I have me in derision, whose fathers I considered unworthy to put with my sheep dogs.
**2** Of what use is the strength of their hands to me, men in whom ripe age has perished?
**3** They are gaunt from lack and famine. They gnaw the dry ground, in the gloom of waste and desolation.
**4** They pluck salt herbs by the bushes. The roots of the broom tree are their food.
**5** They are driven out from among men. They cry after them as after a thief,
**6** so that they live in frightful valleys, and in holes of the earth and of the rocks.
**7** They bray among the bushes. They are gathered together under the nettles.
**8** They are children of fools, yes, children of wicked men. They were flogged out of the land.
**9** “Now I have become their song. Yes, I am a byword to them.
**10** They abhor me, they stand aloof from me, and don’t hesitate to spit in my face.
**11** For he has untied his cord, and afflicted me; and they have thrown off restraint before me.
**12** On my right hand rise the rabble. They thrust aside my feet. They cast their ways of destruction up against me.
**13** They mar my path. They promote my destruction without anyone’s help.
**14** As through a wide breach they come. They roll themselves in amid the ruin.
**15** Terrors have turned on me. They chase my honor as the wind. My welfare has passed away as a cloud.
**16** “Now my soul is poured out within me. Days of affliction have taken hold of me.
**17** In the night season my bones are pierced in me, and the pains that gnaw me take no rest.
**18** My garment is disfigured by great force. It binds me about as the collar of my tunic.
**19** He has cast me into the mire. I have become like dust and ashes.
**20** I cry to you, and you do not answer me. I stand up, and you gaze at me.
**21** You have turned to be cruel to me. With the might of your hand you persecute me.
**22** You lift me up to the wind, and drive me with it. You dissolve me in the storm.
**23** For I know that you will bring me to death, to the house appointed for all living.
**24** “However doesn’t one stretch out a hand in his fall? Or in his calamity therefore cry for help?
**25** Didn’t I weep for him who was in trouble? Wasn’t my soul grieved for the needy?
**26** When I looked for good, then evil came. When I waited for light, darkness came.
**27** My heart is troubled, and doesn’t rest. Days of affliction have come on me.
**28** I go mourning without the sun. I stand up in the assembly, and cry for help.
**29** I am a brother to jackals, and a companion to ostriches.
**30** My skin grows black and peels from me. My bones are burned with heat.
**31** Therefore my harp has turned to mourning, and my pipe into the voice of those who weep.
