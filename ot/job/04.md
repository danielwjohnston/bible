## Clear & Faithful

**1** Then Eliphaz the Temanite replied and said:
**2** "If someone ventures a word with you, will you grow impatient? Yet who can hold back from speaking?
**3** Think how many you have guided, and how many drooping hands you once strengthened;
**4** your words lifted up the stumbling, and you braced knees that were giving way.
**5** Yet now that hardship reaches you, you lose heart; it touches your own skin, and you panic.
**6** Should not your reverence for God be your confidence, and the integrity of your ways be your hope?
**7** Recall, please: whoever perished while innocent? Where were the upright ever blotted out?
**8** From all that I have observed, those who plow iniquity and sow trouble harvest the very same.
**9** By the breath of God they perish; by the blast of his anger they are swept away.
**10** The roar of the lion and the growl of the fierce beast subside, and the fangs of young lions are shattered.
**11** The strong lion dies for want of prey, and the cubs of the lioness scatter abroad.
**12** Now a word stole quietly to me, and my ear caught only a faint whisper of it.
**13** Amid troubling thoughts stirred by night visions, when deep slumber settles over mortals,
**14** terror seized me along with trembling, setting every bone in my frame shuddering.
**15** A spirit glided right across my face; the hairs on my skin bristled upright.
**16** It halted, though I could make out no features—a vague silhouette before my eyes. There was stillness, then I heard a voice:
**17** 'Can frail humanity be more righteous than God? Can any mortal stand purer than his own Maker?
**18** Look: he places no absolute trust even in his heavenly servants, and he detects folly in his angels.
**19** How much less those inhabiting houses of clay, whose foundation rests in mere dust, crushed faster than a moth!
**20** Between dawn and dusk they are shattered to pieces; with nobody taking notice, they vanish forever.
**21** Is not their tent cord yanked loose inside them? They die—and without wisdom.'"

## Everyday English

**1** Then Eliphaz from Teman spoke up:
**2** "Will you lose patience if someone tries to talk with you? Honestly, who could stay quiet after hearing what you just said?
**3** Look, you're the one who used to counsel crowds of people and put strength back into weak hands.
**4** Your advice kept stumbling people on their feet, and you steadied knees that were about to buckle.
**5** But now that trouble hits you personally, you fall apart; it touches you once, and you're panicked!
**6** Shouldn't your devotion to God give you confidence? Shouldn't your honest track record give you hope?
**7** Stop and think: when has a truly innocent person ever been wiped out? Where have decent people ever been destroyed?
**8** Every time I've watched this happen, the people who plow evil and plant misery end up harvesting that exact crop.
**9** One breath from God wipes them out; one blast of his fury finishes them off.
**10** Even when fierce lions roar and snarl, God knocks out the young lions' teeth.
**11** The mighty lion starves without prey, and the lioness's cubs get scattered everywhere.
**12** Listen—a message crept up to me in secret, and my ear picked up a bare whisper of it.
**13** During restless thoughts from nighttime visions, when heavy sleep drops over everyone,
**14** sudden fear and shivering grabbed me and rattled every bone in my body.
**15** A wind—or a spirit—brushed past my face, making the hair on my arms stand straight up.
**16** Something stopped right there, though I couldn't tell what it looked like—just a shape hanging in front of me. Total silence. Then a voice asked:
**17** 'Can a human being possibly be more in the right than God? Can anyone be cleaner than the One who made them?
**18** Look, God doesn't even trust his own heavenly staff completely—he spots flaws even in his angels!
**19** So what chance do people have, living in fragile mud-clay bodies built on dust, easier to squash than a moth?
**20** Between sunrise and sunset they get pounded to bits; nobody even notices when they're gone for good.
**21** Pull out their tent stake and they collapse right away—they die without ever figuring out wisdom.'"

## Crossings & Lexical Notes

- **v. 2 `בְּמִלִּין` (*be-millin*):** Features the Aramaizing masculine plural ending *-in* (`-ִין`) on *millah* ("word"), a hallmark of Job's elevated poetic diction (13 occurrences in the book).
- **v. 4 `יְקִימוּן` (*yeqimun*):** Imperfect verb with paragogic *nun* (`-וּן`), lending archaic poetic gravity to Eliphaz's reminder of Job's past pastoral care.
- **v. 6 `כִּסְלָתֶךָ` (*kislatekha*):** From *kesel* ("confidence/assurance" or "loin/folly"), here used positively by Eliphaz ("your confidence") in parallel with *tiqvatekha* ("your hope").
- **v. 13 `תַּרְדֵּמָה` (*tardemah*):** Deep, divinely induced sleep/trance (the same noun used for Adam in Gen 2:21 and Abram in Gen 15:12).
- **v. 17 `מֵאֱלוֹהַּ` (*me-Eloah*):** The preposition *min* can denote comparison ("more righteous than God") or standpoint ("before/in the sight of God"). Both senses underscore the creature-Creator gulf.
- **v. 18 `תָּהֳלָה` (*toholah*):** A rare *hapax legomenon* meaning "error, defect, or folly."
- **v. 21 `יִתְרָם` (*yitram*):** Literally "their tent-cord" (or "what remains / preeminence in them"); when the main tent rope snaps, the fragile dwelling of the mortal body collapses.

## Supplemental Texts & Allusions

- **Genesis 2:7; 3:19** (creation & mortality): Eliphaz's description of mortals as "dwellers in houses of clay whose foundation is in the dust" (v. 19) recalls humanity's formation from dust and clay and inevitable return to it.
- **Galatians 6:7–8** (sowing-and-reaping maxim): Echoes the wisdom principle articulated in v. 8 that what one sows—whether trouble or righteousness—is what one ultimately reaps.
- **Psalm 39:11 (MT 39:12)** (moth imagery): Compares the fragility of human splendor and vitality to something consumed or crushed like a moth.

## Reference Text (WEB Updated, Public Domain)

**1** Then Eliphaz the Temanite answered,
**2** “If someone ventures to talk with you, will you be grieved? But who can withhold himself from speaking?
**3** Behold, you have instructed many, you have strengthened the weak hands.
**4** Your words have supported him who was falling, you have made the feeble knees firm.
**5** But now it has come to you, and you faint. It touches you, and you are troubled.
**6** Isn’t your piety your confidence? Isn’t the integrity of your ways your hope?
**7** “Remember, now, who ever perished, being innocent? Or where were the upright cut off?
**8** According to what I have seen, those who plow iniquity and sow trouble, reap the same.
**9** By the breath of God they perish. By the blast of his anger are they consumed.
**10** The roaring of the lion, and the voice of the fierce lion, the teeth of the young lions, are broken.
**11** The old lion perishes for lack of prey. The cubs of the lioness are scattered abroad.
**12** “Now a thing was secretly brought to me. My ear received a whisper of it.
**13** In thoughts from the visions of the night, when deep sleep falls on men,
**14** fear came on me, and trembling, which made all my bones shake.
**15** Then a spirit passed before my face. The hair of my flesh stood up.
**16** It stood still, but I couldn’t discern its appearance. A form was before my eyes. Silence, then I heard a voice, saying,
**17** ‘Shall mortal man be more just than God? Shall a man be more pure than his Maker?
**18** Behold, he puts no trust in his servants. He charges his angels with error.
**19** How much more those who dwell in houses of clay, whose foundation is in the dust, who are crushed before the moth!
**20** Between morning and evening they are destroyed. They perish forever without any regarding it.
**21** Isn’t their tent cord plucked up within them? They die, and that without wisdom.’
