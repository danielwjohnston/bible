## Clear & Faithful

**1** "Indeed, there is a vein for silver, and a place where gold is refined.
**2** Iron is mined out of the earth, and copper is smelted from rock.
**3** Miners push back the boundary of darkness and probe to the farthest depths for ore hidden in gloom and deep shadow.
**4** They sink a shaft far from human habitation, in places forgotten by passing feet; far from other mortals they dangle and sway on ropes.
**5** Out of the earth's surface grows bread, while down beneath it is churned upside down as if by fire.
**6** Its stones hold beds of sapphire, and its dust glints with specks of gold.
**7** No hawk knows that hidden trail; no falcon's keen eye has ever spotted it.
**8** No proud beast has set foot there; no prowling lion has ever crossed it.
**9** The miner lays his hand to the flinty rock and overturns mountains from their very roots.
**10** He chisels tunnels straight through solid cliffs, and his eyes catch sight of every treasure.
**11** He dams back subterranean streams from weeping through, and brings hidden riches out into daylight.
**12** Yet where can wisdom be found? Where is the dwelling place of understanding?
**13** Mortals do not know its true worth, nor can it be discovered in the land of the living.
**14** The Ocean Deep says, 'It is not inside me,' and the Sea replies, 'It is not with me.'
**15** Solid gold cannot be traded for it, nor can silver be weighed out as its purchase price.
**16** It cannot be appraised in gold from Ophir, in precious onyx or sapphire.
**17** Neither gold nor crystal compares with it, nor can vessels of pure gold buy it in exchange.
**18** Coral and quartz are not even worth mentioning; drawing up wisdom is worth far more than pearls.
**19** Topaz from Cush cannot rival it, nor can it be bought with the purest gold.
**20** From where, then, does wisdom come? Where is the dwelling place of understanding?
**21** It stays veiled from the eyes of every living creature, hidden even from the birds soaring across the sky.
**22** Destruction and Death declare, 'We have only heard a rumor of it with our ears.'
**23** God alone understands the road that leads to it; he alone knows where it lives.
**24** For he gazes to the farthest edges of the earth and sees everything beneath the entire sky.
**25** When he assigned the wind its weight and measured out the waters by volume,
**26** when he set an ordinance for the rain and carved a track for the lightning's thunder,
**27** then he beheld wisdom and declared its worth; he set it in place and examined it thoroughly.
**28** And he said to humanity: 'Look—the fear of the Lord is wisdom, and turning away from evil is understanding.'"

## Everyday English

**1** "People know where to dig for silver and where to refine gold.
**2** They pull iron ore straight out of the dirt and melt copper right out of stone.
**3** Miners drive light into pitch-black tunnels and hunt down rocks buried in total darkness.
**4** Far away from towns where anyone walks, they cut deep vertical shafts and swing suspended on ropes far below ground.
**5** Up on top, the earth grows grain for bread—down underneath, it looks blasted apart like a furnace.
**6** Those underground rocks hold blue sapphires and dirt packed with gold dust.
**7** Not even a sharp-eyed hawk knows that trail, and no falcon has ever glimpsed it from above.
**8** Proud wild animals have never walked down there, and no lion has ever prowled through those tunnels.
**9** Humans tackle solid flint with their bare tools and blast mountains open at their roots.
**10** They carve drainage tunnels right through stone until their eyes spot every buried jewel.
**11** They seal off underground springs from leaking and haul hidden treasures up into the sunlight.
**12** Yet for all that, where on earth can wisdom be found? Where does real understanding live?
**13** Humans have no clue what it's actually worth, and you won't unearth it anywhere in the world of the living.
**14** The deepest ocean says, 'Don't look in me,' and the open sea says, 'I don't have it either.'
**15** You can't buy it with bars of solid gold or weigh out piles of silver to pay for it.
**16** Even the finest Ophir gold, rare onyx, or sapphires can't match its value.
**17** Gold and fine crystal don't come close, and priceless gold jewelry can't trade for it.
**18** Forget comparing it to coral or rock crystal—pulling in wisdom beats diving for pearls!
**19** Gems from Cush aren't in its league, and pure gold can't buy it.
**20** So where does wisdom actually come from? Where can understanding be located?
**21** It's completely out of sight to every living thing—even birds high in the sky can't spot it.
**22** Ruin and Death admit, 'All we've ever heard is a distant rumor about it.'
**23** Only God knows the path to wisdom; he's the only one who knows where it lives,
**24** because he watches all the way to the ends of the earth and sees everything under the sky.
**25** When God decided how heavy the wind should be and measured out every ocean,
**26** when he set rules for rainfall and mapped out paths for lightning and thunder,
**27** that's when he looked right at wisdom, valued it, put it to work, and tested it through and through.
**28** And here is what he told the human race: 'Respecting the Lord with deep awe—*that* is wisdom! Turning your back on evil—*that* is real understanding!'"

## Crossings & Lexical Notes

- **v. 3 `צַלְמָוֶת` (*tsalmavet*):** "Deep shadow / death-darkness" (traditionally vocalized as a compound of *tsel* "shadow" + *mavet* "death," or derived from *tsalmut* "pitch blackness"), describing deep mine shafts beyond sunlight.
- **v. 11 `מִבְּכִי נְהָרוֹת` (*mibbekhi neharot*):** Literally "from weeping of streams"—a vivid mining metaphor for water seeping through rock fractures, which miners caulk/bind up (`חִבֵּשׁ`, *chibbesh*).
- **v. 12 `חָכְמָה` (*chokhmah*) & `בִּינָה` (*binah*):** Twin refrain terms framing the poem (vv. 12, 20): *chokhmah* ("skillful wisdom") and *binah* ("discernment/understanding"). Both are personified as cosmic realities surveyed by God at creation (v. 27) yet inaccessible to human mining or commerce.
- **v. 18 `מֶשֶׁךְ חָכְמָה מִפְּנִינִים` (*meshekh chokhmah mippeninim*):** *Meshekh* from *mashakh* ("draw up / acquire," as fishing or mining) or "price/worth"; *peninim* denotes red coral or pearls/precious stones.
- **v. 28 `יִרְאַת אֲדֹנָי` (*yir'at Adonai*):** Notably the **only** occurrence of the title `אֲדֹנָי` (*Adonai*, "Lord") in the entire book of Job! After demonstrating that cosmic *chokhmah* belongs exclusively to God (`Elohim`, v. 23), v. 28 defines human wisdom practically as reverent awe of the Lord (*yir'at Adonai*) and moral turning from evil (echoing `1:1, 8; 2:3`).

## Supplemental Texts & Allusions

- **Proverbs 1:7; 9:10; Psalm 111:10** (the fear of the LORD as wisdom's anchor): Establishes the foundational Israelite wisdom motto echoed in Job 28:28, pairing reverence for God with moral discernment.
- **Proverbs 3:13–15; 8:10–11, 22–31** (wisdom beyond gold and present at creation): Parallels both halves of Job 28—wisdom's value surpassing silver, fine gold, and jewels (vv. 15–19) and wisdom's presence when God measured the waters, winds, and skies (vv. 25–27).
- **Romans 11:33–35** (unsearchable depths of divine wisdom): Reflects the awe of Job 28:12–24 in celebrating the unfathomable depth of God's wisdom and knowledge, beyond mortal discovery.

**1** “Surely there is a mine for silver, and a place for gold which they refine.
**2** Iron is taken out of the earth, and copper is smelted out of the ore.
**3** Man sets an end to darkness, and searches out, to the furthest bound, the stones of obscurity and of thick darkness.
**4** He breaks open a shaft away from where people live. They are forgotten by the foot. They hang far from men, they swing back and forth.
**5** As for the earth, out of it comes bread. Underneath it is turned up as it were by fire.
**6** Sapphires come from its rocks. It has dust of gold.
**7** That path no bird of prey knows, neither has the falcon’s eye seen it.
**8** The proud animals have not trodden it, nor has the fierce lion passed by there.
**9** He puts his hand on the flinty rock, and he overturns the mountains by the roots.
**10** He cuts out channels among the rocks. His eye sees every precious thing.
**11** He binds the streams that they don’t trickle. The thing that is hidden he brings out to light.
**12** “But where will wisdom be found? Where is the place of understanding?
**13** Man doesn’t know its price, and it isn’t found in the land of the living.
**14** The deep says, ‘It isn’t in me.’ The sea says, ‘It isn’t with me.’
**15** It can’t be gotten for gold, neither will silver be weighed for its price.
**16** It can’t be valued with the gold of Ophir, with the precious onyx, or the sapphire.
**17** Gold and glass can’t equal it, neither will it be exchanged for jewels of fine gold.
**18** No mention will be made of coral or of crystal. Yes, the price of wisdom is above rubies.
**19** The topaz of Ethiopia will not equal it. It won’t be valued with pure gold.
**20** Where then does wisdom come from? Where is the place of understanding?
**21** Seeing it is hidden from the eyes of all living, and kept close from the birds of the sky.
**22** Destruction and Death say, ‘We have heard a rumor of it with our ears.’
**23** “God understands its way, and he knows its place.
**24** For he looks to the ends of the earth, and sees under the whole sky.
**25** He establishes the force of the wind. Yes, he measures out the waters by measure.
**26** When he made a decree for the rain, and a way for the lightning of the thunder,
**27** then he saw it, and declared it. He established it, yes, and searched it out.
**28** To man he said, ‘Behold, the fear of the Lord, that is wisdom. To depart from evil is understanding.’”
