## Clear & Faithful

**1** Then Job answered and said:
**2** "How long will you torment my soul and batter me with words?
**3** Ten times over you have humiliated me; you feel no shame in treating me so harshly.
**4** Even if I truly had gone astray, my error remains my own concern.
**5** If you insist on exalting yourselves over me and using my disgrace as proof against me,
**6** understand then that it is God who has twisted my cause and thrown his siege-net around me.
**7** Look—I cry out, 'Wrong!' and receive no answer; I shout for help, and there is no justice.
**8** He has blocked off my road so I cannot pass, and blanketed my paths in darkness.
**9** He has stripped my honor off my back and lifted the crown from my head.
**10** He tears me down on every side until I am finished, and uproots my hope like a felled tree.
**11** His fury blazes against me, and he counts me among his enemies.
**12** His raiding bands march up together, throw up their siege ramp against me, and pitch camp all around my tent.
**13** He has driven my own brothers far away, and those who knew me best act like complete strangers.
**14** My kinsfolk have vanished, and my closest friends have put me out of mind.
**15** Guests in my home and my own servant women regard me as an outsider; in their eyes I have become a foreigner.
**16** I call my servant, and he refuses to answer; I have to beg him with my own lips.
**17** My breath repels my wife, and I am repulsive to my own mother's children.
**18** Even little boys look down on me; whenever I try to stand, they mock me behind my back.
**19** Every trusted confidant detests me, and those I held dear have turned against me.
**20** My bones stick straight to my skin and wasted flesh; I have barely escaped with the skin of my teeth.
**21** Have mercy on me, have mercy on me, my friends, for the hand of God has struck me!
**22** Why must you hound me just as God does, never getting your fill of tearing at my flesh?
**23** Oh, that my words could be written down! Oh, that they could be recorded in a book—
**24** engraved with an iron chisel and inlaid with lead, carved permanently into stone!
**25** For I know that my Vindicator lives, and at the last he will stand upon the dust;
**26** and after my skin has been stripped away like this, still from my flesh I shall gaze upon God—
**27** whom I myself will see on my own side; my very own eyes will behold him, and no stranger. My deepest heart faints within me!
**28** When you say, 'How shall we keep pursuing him?'—claiming the root of the trouble lies in me—
**29** tremble for yourselves before the sword, for fury brings the sword's punishments, so that you may know there is a judgment."

## Everyday English

**1** Job answered them:
**2** "How long are you going to keep torturing me and pounding me down with your speeches?
**3** You've insulted me ten times already—aren't you even ashamed of how cruelly you treat me?
**4** Even suppose I did mess up; that mistake is on me, not you!
**5** If you're determined to look down on me and throw my misery back in my face as evidence,
**6** then at least recognize this: God is the one who bent justice against me and trapped me in his net.
**7** I scream 'Injustice!' and nobody answers; I yell for help, and there's no fair trial anywhere.
**8** God built a wall across my road so I can't get through, and plunged every path I walk into darkness.
**9** He ripped away my dignity and knocked the crown right off my head.
**10** He's tearing me down piece by piece until I'm done for, yanking my hope up by the roots like a dead tree.
**11** He's furious with me and treats me like his worst enemy.
**12** His troops march in all at once, build siege ramps up against me, and camp right around my tent.
**13** He pushed my brothers far away from me; people who knew me well treat me like they've never met me.
**14** My family has disappeared, and my closest friends have forgotten I exist.
**15** Houseguests and my own servants treat me like an outsider—to them I'm a total stranger.
**16** I call my servant and he won't answer; I actually have to plead with him just to help me.
**17** My own breath disgusts my wife, and my family can't stand being near me.
**18** Even little kids mock me; as soon as I stand up, they jeer at me.
**19** My closest friends can't stand the sight of me; the people I loved most have turned on me.
**20** I'm nothing but bones clinging to skin; I've barely survived by the skin of my teeth.
**21** Please, have some pity on me, my friends—God's own hand has hit me!
**22** Why do you keep hunting me down like God does? Haven't you chewed me up enough already?
**23** How I wish my words could be written down—recorded permanently in an official book,
**24** or chiseled with an iron tool and filled with lead, carved deep into rock forever!
**25** Because I know my Defender is alive, and at the end he will take his stand upon this earth.
**26** Even after my skin has rotted off like this, right from my own body—or free of it—I will see God with my own eyes!
**27** Yes, I will see him for myself, standing on my side, not as a stranger—my heart aches inside me just waiting for that!
**28** So if you keep plotting, 'How can we pin more blame on him?' as if the fault started with me,
**29** you'd better watch out for the sword yourselves! Rage brings the sword's penalty—and you'll learn firsthand that judgment is real."

## Crossings & Lexical Notes

- **v. 20 `בְּעוֹר שִׁנָּי` (*be'or shinnay*):** Literally "by the skin of my teeth"—the famous Hebrew idiom originating right here in Job 19:20, meaning survival by the barest, almost nonexistent margin (since teeth have no skin).
- **v. 25 `גֹּאֲלִי` (*go'ali*):** From *ga'al* ("redeem, reclaim, vindicate"). In Israelite family law, the *go'el* is the next-of-kin who buys back enslaved relatives, redeems lost land, or avenges wrongful death (cf. Lev 25:25; Ruth 3–4). Here Job invokes a living heavenly *Go'el* who will stand up after him (`אַחֲרוֹן`, *acharon*, "at the last / as guarantor") over the dust (*afar*, either the earth or Job's grave).
- **v. 26 `מִבְּשָׂרִי` (*mibbessari*):** One of the most debated prepositions in Job: the prefix *min-* (`מִ-`) can be spatial/instrumental ("from within my flesh," i.e., embodied vision) or privative/separative ("apart from / without my flesh"). Either way, Job insists in v. 27 that *I myself* (`אֲנִי`) and *my own eyes* (`עֵינַי`) will see God as advocate rather than stranger.
- **v. 29 `שַׁדּוּן` (*shaddun*, Qere) vs. `שדין` (Ketiv):** Compounds the archaic/northern relative prefix `שֶׁ-` (*sha-*, "that") with `דּוּן`/`דִּין` ("judgment"): "so that you may know that [there is] a judgment" (or reading Qere *shaddun* as a plural/intensive form "judgments").

## Supplemental Texts & Allusions

- **Psalm 38:11; 88:8, 18** (social alienation in lament): Echoes Job's painful catalog in vv. 13–19 where relatives, companions, and loved ones recoil from the sufferer as a stranger.
- **Leviticus 25:25, 48–49; Isaiah 41:14** (*go'el* tradition): Grounds Job's courtroom hope in v. 25 in Israel's kinsman-redeemer institution—the relative obligated to step in and vindicate a helpless family member, later applied by Isaiah to the LORD as Israel's Redeemer.
- **1 John 3:2** (eschatological vision of God): Parallels Job's longing in vv. 26–27 to behold God face-to-face with one's own eyes when vindication finally arrives.
