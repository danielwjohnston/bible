## Clear & Faithful

**1** Then Job answered and said:
**2** "No doubt you alone are the true people, and when you die, wisdom will perish with you!
**3** Yet I have a mind just as good as yours; I am not inferior to you—and who does not know commonplace things like these?
**4** I have turned into a laughingstock to my own friend—one who called out to God and received an answer—a laughingstock, though righteous and blameless!
**5** Contempt flares like a torch in the mind of the comfortable, aimed ready for those whose feet begin to slip.
**6** Meanwhile the tents of marauders sit completely at ease, and those who provoke God enjoy total security—men who carry their god in their own fist!
**7** Just ask the wild beasts, and they will instruct you; ask the birds of the sky, and they will tell you;
**8** or speak to the earth itself, and it will teach you; let the fish of the sea recount it to you.
**9** Which among all of them fails to recognize that the hand of the LORD has brought this about—
**10** in whose hand rests the life of every living creature and the breath of all mortal flesh?
**11** Does not the ear sift words just as the palate tastes its own food?
**12** Surely wisdom belongs with the aged, and long life brings understanding—
**13** yet with him reside wisdom and power; counsel and understanding belong to him!
**14** Look: when he demolishes, nothing can be rebuilt; when he locks a person in, no one can open the door.
**15** Look: when he withholds the waters, drought dries them out; when he unleashes them, they overturn the land.
**16** With him belong strength and effective mastery; both the misled and the misleader are in his power.
**17** He marches counselors off stripped barefoot and turns judges into fools.
**18** He unbuckles the royal belt of kings and ties a captive's loincloth around their waists.
**19** He marches priests away stripped barefoot and topples entrenched dynasties.
**20** He strips eloquence from trusted advisors and takes away the judgment of elders.
**21** He pours scorn upon princes and unlooses the sash of the mighty.
**22** He exposes deep mysteries out of pitch-blackness and drags death's deepest shadow into daylight.
**23** He builds nations up high, then ruins them; he expands peoples wide, then marches them off into exile.
**24** He strips away the wits of earth's foremost leaders and leaves them wandering through a pathless wasteland.
**25** They feel their way through darkness without a spark of light, and he sets them reeling like drunkards."

## Everyday English

**1** Job fired right back:
**2** "Oh, sure—you guys must be the smartest people alive! Once you die, I guess wisdom goes straight into the coffin with you!
**3** Well, I have a brain just like you do. I'm not a bit dumber than you—honestly, everybody on earth already knows the clichés you're repeating!
**4** My own friends treat me like a joke—me, someone who used to pray to God and get answers—reduced to a laughingstock even though I'm innocent and honest!
**5** People living in comfort look down their noses at anyone whose foot starts slipping.
**6** Yet look around: violent bandits relax peacefully in their tents, and people who openly provoke God live completely safe—folks whose own fist is their god!
**7** Go ask the animals, and they'll spell it out for you; ask the birds up in the sky, and they'll tell you.
**8** Talk to the earth, and it'll teach you; let the fish in the ocean explain it to you!
**9** Doesn't every single creature know that the LORD's own hand is behind all this?
**10** He holds the life of every living thing and the breath of every human being right in his grip!
**11** Doesn't your ear test out words the same way your tongue tastes food?
**12** Yes, people say older folks have wisdom and long years bring insight—
**13** but real wisdom and raw power belong to God! He's the one who holds strategy and insight!
**14** Look at him: when he tears something down, nobody can rebuild it; when he slams a door shut on someone, nobody can pry it open.
**15** When he holds back the rain, everything dries up; when he lets the floods loose, they wash the land upside down.
**16** Power and success are his—both the person who gets fooled and the con artist who fools them answer to him!
**17** He strips political advisors bare and makes court judges look like idiots.
**18** He unlocks the chains of kings—or wraps prisoners' rags around their own waists!
**19** He marches priests out barefoot and knocks long-standing rulers off their thrones.
**20** He takes away the smooth talk of trusted speakers and strips elders of their good sense.
**21** He dumps humiliation on nobles and unbuckles the armor of warriors.
**22** He pulls hidden secrets out of the pitch-black dark and drags death's shadows straight into daylight.
**23** He makes countries huge and then wipes them out; he spreads empires across the map and then marches them away.
**24** He scrambles the brains of world leaders and leaves them wandering lost in a trackless desert.
**25** They grope around in pitch darkness with zero light, staggering back and forth like they're drunk."

## Crossings & Lexical Notes

- **v. 2 `אַתֶּם־עָם` (*attem-am*):** Literally "you [are] a people"—biting sarcasm ("you are the entire human race / the only people who matter!"), directly mocking Zophar's lecture in ch. 11.
- **v. 6 `לַאֲשֶׁר הֵבִיא אֱלוֹהַּ בְּיָדוֹ` (*la-asher hevi Eloah be-yado*):** Ambiguous syntax: either "those who carry their god (*eloah*) in their own fist" (deifying brute force, cf. Hab 1:11) or "those into whose hand God (*Eloah*) brings wealth."
- **v. 9 `יַד־יְהוָה` (*yad-YHWH*):** The **sole occurrence** of the covenant name `יְהוָה` (*YHWH*) in the entire poetic dialogue (`chs 3–37`)! Job borrows a well-known creation proverb (cf. Isa 41:20) to argue that God's sovereign hand explains *even the prospering of the wicked* (v. 6) and the shattering of rulers (vv. 14–25).
- **v. 16 `עֹז וְתוּשִׁיָּה ... שֹׁגֵג וּמַשְׁגֶּה` (*oz ve-tushiyyah ... shogeg u-mashgeh*):** Pairing *tushiyyah* ("effective wisdom/success," cf. 5:12; 11:6) with *shogeg u-mashgeh* ("the erring one and the one who causes to err"), Job pushes God's sovereignty to its unsettling edge: even human error and deception fall under divine control.
- **v. 24 `בְּתֹהוּ לֹא־דָרֶךְ` (*be-tohu lo-darekh*):** "In a wasteland without a path"—invoking primeval chaos (*tohu*, Gen 1:2) applied to disoriented political rulers (virtually verbatim in Ps 107:40).

## Supplemental Texts & Allusions

- **Psalm 107:40** (nearly verbatim parallel): *"He pours contempt on nobles and makes them wander in a pathless wasteland"* matches Job 12:21a and 12:24b nearly word-for-word.
- **Isaiah 41:20** (shared confession formula): Uses the phrase *"that the hand of the LORD has done this"* (*ki yad-YHWH asetah zot*) as a universal acknowledgment of divine sovereignty over history and nature.
- **Daniel 2:20–22** (doxology of sovereignty): Echoes Job 12:13–22 in celebrating God as the possessor of wisdom and might who removes and sets up kings and reveals deep and hidden things out of darkness.
