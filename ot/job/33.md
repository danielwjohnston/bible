## Clear & Faithful

**1** "Now therefore, Job, please listen to my words, and pay attention to everything I have to say.
**2** Look, I have opened my mouth; the tongue inside my mouth has begun to speak.
**3** My words come from an upright heart, and my lips speak knowledge sincerely.
**4** It is God's own Spirit that fashioned me, and the Almighty's living breath that sustains me.
**5** Answer me if you can; set your case in order before me and take your stand!
**6** Look, before God I stand just like you; I too was pinched out of clay.
**7** So dread of me will not overwhelm you, nor will my hand press heavily upon you.
**8** Yet you have spoken right in my hearing, and I heard the sound of your words:
**9** 'I am pure, without transgression; I am innocent, and there is no guilt in me.
**10** Look, he hunts for pretexts against me; he regards me as his enemy.
**11** He locks my feet in the stocks and keeps watch over all my paths.'
**12** Look, in this you are not in the right—let me answer you—for God is far greater than any mortal.
**13** Why do you contend against him, saying that he does not answer any of humanity's words?
**14** For God speaks in one way, and in two, though no one notices it:
**15** Through a dream or a midnight vision, as heavy slumber settles over mortals lying upon their couches,
**16** then he opens human ears and seals his warning upon them,
**17** to turn a person aside from wrongdoing and keep pride hidden away from them,
**18** to spare their soul from the pit and keep their life from perishing by the sword.
**19** Or someone is chastened with pain upon their bed, with continual ache throughout their bones,
**20** until their appetite loathes bread and their soul rejects even choice food.
**21** Their flesh wastes away out of sight, and their bones that were unseen stick out.
**22** Their soul draws near to the pit, and their life to the agents of death.
**23** Yet if there is a messenger at their side, a mediator—one out of a thousand—to declare to a person what is right for them,
**24** then he shows mercy to that person and commands, 'Rescue them from descending to the pit, for I have secured a ransom.'
**25** Then their flesh grows fresher than in youth; they return to the days of their vigor.
**26** They pray to God and he accepts them; they behold his face with shouts of joy, and he restores their righteousness to them.
**27** Then they sing before others and confess, 'I sinned and twisted what was straight, yet it was not repaid to me!
**28** He redeemed my soul from crossing into the pit, and my life shall look upon the light!'
**29** Look—God accomplishes all this time after time, twice and even three times, for a mortal,
**30** in order to draw back their soul from the abyss, that they may bask in the radiance of the living.
**31** Pay attention, Job, and listen to me; keep quiet, and let me speak.
**32** Should you possess any counterargument, reply to me; go ahead and speak, since I long to prove you right.
**33** If not, then listen to me; keep quiet, and I will teach you wisdom."

## Everyday English

**1** "So please, Job, hear me out and listen carefully to everything I'm about to say.
**2** I'm opening my mouth now—it's my turn to speak.
**3** Everything I say comes straight from an honest heart, and I'm telling you what I know with total sincerity.
**4** God's Spirit created me, and the Almighty's breath keeps me alive.
**5** If you can counter what I say, go ahead—lay out your defense and stand your ground!
**6** Look, in God's eyes you and I are equals; I was molded out of the very same clay.
**7** So you don't need to be intimidated by me—I won't crush you with heavy pressure.
**8** Now, I heard what you said with my own ears; your exact words are still ringing in my head:
**9** 'I'm completely innocent—I haven't broken any rule! I'm clean, and there's zero guilt on my record.
**10** Yet God keeps looking for excuses to pick a fight with me and treats me like his sworn enemy.
**11** He clamps my feet in wooden shackles and watches every move I make.'
**12** Let me tell you plainly: you're wrong about this, because God towers far above any human being.
**13** Why pick a fight with him by claiming he never bothers to answer anyone?
**14** God actually speaks in one way, and then another—even when people miss it completely.
**15** Sometimes he speaks through a dream or a nighttime vision, when people fall into a deep sleep on their beds.
**16** That's when he opens their ears and drives home his warning,
**17** steering them away from bad choices and stripping away their arrogance,
**18** rescuing their lives from the grave and keeping them from crossing over into death.
**19** Other times God corrects someone through agonizing pain in their sickbed—an ache deep in their bones that never quits,
**20** until their stomach turns against bread and even their favorite meal makes them sick.
**21** Their body wastes away to nothing, and bones you never noticed before start poking through.
**22** They hover right on the edge of the grave, staring death in the face.
**23** Still, if an angel—a mediator, one in a thousand—stands beside that person to show them the right path,
**24** then God shows mercy and says, 'Release them from going down to the grave; I've found a ransom price!'
**25** Suddenly their skin turns fresh and healthy like a teenager's, and they bounce back to their youthful strength!
**26** They pray to God and he welcomes them back; they see his smile with a shout of joy, and God puts them right with him again.
**27** Then they testify openly in front of everyone: 'I messed up and twisted what was right, and God didn't pay me back what I deserved!
**28** He rescued my life from going down to the grave, so I get to live on and enjoy the daylight!'
**29** See, God does things like this twice—even three times—for a human being,
**30** pulling them back from the brink of death so they can step into the light of real life.
**31** Listen closely, Job—stay quiet and hear me out.
**32** If you've got a reply, go ahead and speak; honestly, I'd love to see you cleared.
**33** Otherwise, keep listening quietly, and I'll teach you what true wisdom looks like."

## Crossings & Lexical Notes

- **v. 6 `מֵחֹמֶר קֹרַצְתִּי` (*me-chomer qoratsti*):** "From clay I was pinched/nipped off"—a potter's image (echoing Gen 2:7 and Job 10:9) by which Elihu reassures Job that he is a fellow creature, not an overwhelming divine terror (contrasting Job's wish in 9:34; 13:21).
- **v. 19 `וְרוֹב` (*ve-rov*, Qere) vs. `וְרִיב` (*ve-riv*, Ketiv):** Qere means "and multitude/continual intensity [of pain in his bones]" (from *rav*), whereas Ketiv reads "and strife/throbbing conflict [of his bones]" (from *riv*).
- **v. 21 `וְשֻׁפּוּ` (*ve-shuppu*, Qere) vs. `וְשָׁפִי` (*ve-shafi*, Ketiv):** Qere is a Pual passive verb ("and [his bones] are laid bare / stick out"); Ketiv `ושפי` reflects an unusual noun/adjective form ("and bareness").
- **v. 23 `מַלְאָךְ מֵלִיץ` (*mal'akh melits*):** A heavenly "messenger-interpreter/mediator" who advocates for the sufferer and announces a `כֹּפֶר` (*kofer*, "ransom/atonement," v. 24).
- **v. 28 `נַפְשׁוֹ` / `וְחַיָּתוֹ` (*nafsho* / *ve-chayyato*, Qere) vs. `נַפְשִׁי` / `וְחַיָּתִי` (*nafshi* / *ve-chayyati*, Ketiv):** The Qere keeps the third-person pronoun ("his soul / his life") consistent with vv. 22–26, 30, while the Ketiv shifts into first-person direct speech ("my soul / my life") inside the penitent's confession begun in v. 27 (a *tiqqun* or grammatical bridge; CF/EE renders the first-person confession naturally while noting the 3rd-person Qere).

## Supplemental Texts & Allusions

- **Genesis 2:7 & 15:12** (creation & vision motifs): Recalls both humanity shaped from clay/dust (`חֹמֶר`, v. 6) and the prophetic "deep sleep" (`תַּרְדֵּמָה`, v. 15) in which God reveals truth at night.
- **Psalm 107:17–20** (healing and deliverance): Mirrors the sequence of bodily wasting, loathing food near the gates of death, crying to God, and physical restoration.
- **Mark 10:45 / 1 Timothy 2:5–6** (mediator and ransom motif): Echoes the conceptual pairing of a singular mediator (`מֵלִיץ`) and a life-saving ransom (`כֹּפֶר`, vv. 23–24).
