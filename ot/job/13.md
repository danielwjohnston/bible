## Clear & Faithful

**1** "Look: my own eye has seen all of this, my ear has heard it and grasped it for itself.
**2** Whatever you know, I know just as well; I do not fall a bit behind you.
**3** Yet I mean to speak directly to the Almighty; I long to argue my case before God!
**4** As for you, you are all smears of whitewash covering falsehood, worthless quack doctors—every last one of you!
**5** If only you would stay utterly silent—that would count as real wisdom for you!
**6** Listen now to my counter-argument, and pay close attention to the pleading of my lips.
**7** Will you tell lies on God's behalf? Will you utter deceit to defend him?
**8** Will you show biased favoritism toward him, or step up as God's hired advocate?
**9** Would it go well with you if he cross-examined you, or can you fool him the way someone fools a mortal?
**10** He will fiercely rebuke you if you secretly play favorites in court!
**11** Does not his blazing majesty terrify you, and dread of him crash down upon you?
**12** Your prized maxims are proverbs made of ash; your bulwarks are fortifications built of clay!
**13** Keep quiet and step back from me so that I may speak—and let whatever happens overtake me!
**14** Why should I carry my own flesh between my teeth and place my very life in the palm of my hand?
**15** Look: though he slay me, yet will I hope in him; nevertheless I will defend my ways straight to his face!
**16** This very boldness will prove to be my deliverance, for no godless hypocrite dares step into his presence!
**17** Listen intently to my statement; let my declaration sink into your ears.
**18** See here: I have prepared my legal brief in order, and I know that I will be declared righteous!
**19** Who is there to prosecute a case against me? For if anyone can prove me wrong, I will fall silent right now and die!
**20** Only two conditions grant me, God, and then I will never shrink from your presence:
**21** withdraw your heavy hand far off from me, and do not let your terror paralyze me with dread;
**22** then summon me, and I will respond—or let me speak first, and you answer me back!
**23** How many offenses and sins stand against me? Show me plainly my rebellion and my sin!
**24** Why do you conceal your face and treat me as your mortal enemy?
**25** Would you terrorize a wind-tossed leaf, or chase down dried-out chaff,
**26** that you write out bitter indictments against me and saddle me with the sins of my youth,
**27** clamping my feet in wooden stocks, policing every trail I walk, and carving boundary lines around the soles of my feet—
**28** while a mortal crumbles away like dry rot, like a garment eaten up by a moth?"

## Everyday English

**1** "Look, I've seen all of this with my own eyes, heard it with my own ears, and understood it perfectly.
**2** I know everything you know—I'm not one step behind you!
**3** Still, I'm not interested in debating you; I want to talk straight to the Almighty and lay out my case before God himself!
**4** All you guys do is slap fake paint over lies—every single one of you is a fraud of a doctor!
**5** Honestly, I wish you'd just zip your mouths shut! That's the smartest thing you could possibly do!
**6** Now listen to my rebuttal and pay attention to the defense I'm making:
**7** Are you seriously going to tell lies just to protect God? Are you going to spin fake arguments in his honor?
**8** Are you trying to bribe the Judge or play defense attorney for God?
**9** What's going to happen when God puts *you* under oath? Do you think you can con him like you con a human?
**10** He'll tear right into you if he catches you quietly rigging the trial in his favor!
**11** Doesn't his sheer majesty scare you? Doesn't dread of him make your knees shake?
**12** All your fancy slogans are worthless dust, and your airtight arguments are shields made of mud!
**13** Shut up and leave me alone so I can say my piece—whatever happens to me next, let it come!
**14** Why not take my life in my teeth and put everything on the line?
**15** Look, even if he kills me, I'm going to keep hoping in him—and at the same time, I'm going to defend my record right to his face!
**16** Actually, that boldness itself will be my rescue, because no fake hypocrite would ever dare show up before God!
**17** Listen closely to every word I'm saying; let my testimony sink in:
**18** Look, I've got my legal brief lined up and ready—I know for a fact I'll be cleared!
**19** Who thinks they can beat me in court? If anyone proves me guilty, I'll shut up right now and die!
**20** God, just agree to two things, and I won't hide from you anymore:
**21** take your heavy hand off me, and stop scaring me out of my mind with your terror.
**22** Then call my name and I'll take the stand—or let me ask the questions and you answer me!
**23** How many crimes and sins do I supposedly have? Show me the exact charges!
**24** Why are you turning your back on me and treating me like your sworn enemy?
**25** Are you really going to bully a leaf blowing in the wind or hunt down a dry piece of straw?
**26** You're writing up harsh charges against me and dredging up mistakes from when I was young!
**27** You lock my ankles in wooden stocks, track every footprint I make, and draw chalk lines right around my feet—
**28** even though a human falls apart like rotten wood, or like a sweater chewed through by moths!"

## Crossings & Lexical Notes

- **v. 4 `טֹפְלֵי־שָׁקֶר` (*tofelei-shaqer*):** Literally "smearers/plasterers of falsehood" (using *tafal*, "to coat/daub over" like plastering a cracked wall, cf. 14:17), paired with `רֹפְאֵי אֱלִל` ("healers of nothingness / worthless physicians").
- **v. 8 `הֲפָנָיו תִּשָּׂאוּן` (*ha-fanav tissa'un*):** Literally "will you lift his face?"—the standard Hebrew legal idiom for judicial partiality/favoritism (cf. Lev 19:15; Deut 10:17), warning the friends against sycophantic defense of God at truth's expense. Note also the archaic paragogic *-un* endings in vv. 5, 8, 10.
- **v. 14 `אֶשָּׂא בְשָׂרִי בְשִׁנָּי` (*essa vesari ve-shinnai*):** "Take my flesh in my teeth"—an obscure, visceral idiom for risking everything in a desperate gamble, parallel to "put my life (*nafshi*) in my palm" (cf. Judg 12:3; 1 Sam 19:5).
- **v. 15 `לא` / `לוֹ` (*lo*):** The most famous Ketiv/Qere crux in Job! Ketiv reads `לֹא` ("not": *"Behold, he will slay me—I have no hope"* or *"will I not wait?"*), capturing Job's raw despair; Qere reads `לוֹ` ("in him": *"Though he slay me, yet in him will I hope/wait"*), expressing defiant trust amid death. Both readings lead into the second clause (*akh derakhai el-panav okhiach*, "yet I will argue my ways to his face").
- **v. 16 `גַּם־הוּא־לִי לִישׁוּעָה` (*gam-hu-li li-yeshu'ah*):** "This too will turn out for my deliverance/vindication"—Job's confidence that his willingness to stand trial openly before God proves he is no secret *chanef* ("godless fraud").
- **v. 28 `כְּרָקָב יִבְלֶה` (*ke-raqav yivleh*):** Shifts abruptly into third person ("while he wears away like dry-rot"), either referring back to the speaker himself ("such a fragile mortal as I") or transitioning smoothly into 14:1's meditation on all mortals (*adam*).

## Supplemental Texts & Allusions

- **Philippians 1:19** (verbatim LXX citation): Paul echoes Greek Job 13:16 word-for-word (*"for I know that this will turn out for my deliverance"*), drawing on Job's confidence that candid integrity before God brings vindication.
- **Proverbs 17:28** (silence as wisdom): Mirrors Job's sharp quip in v. 5 that if his counselors remained completely silent, it would actually count as wisdom for them.
- **Leviticus 19:15 & Deuteronomy 1:17** (impartial judgment): Forbid "lifting faces" (showing partiality) in court, grounding Job's accusation in vv. 8–10 that his friends are trying to flatter God instead of telling the truth.

## Reference Text (WEB Updated, Public Domain)

**1** “Behold, my eye has seen all this. My ear has heard and understood it.
**2** What you know, I know also. I am not inferior to you.
**3** “Surely I would speak to the Almighty. I desire to reason with God.
**4** But you are forgers of lies. You are all physicians of no value.
**5** Oh that you would be completely silent! Then you would be wise.
**6** Hear now my reasoning. Listen to the pleadings of my lips.
**7** Will you speak unrighteously for God, and talk deceitfully for him?
**8** Will you show partiality to him? Will you contend for God?
**9** Is it good that he should search you out? Or as one deceives a man, will you deceive him?
**10** He will surely reprove you if you secretly show partiality.
**11** Won’t his majesty make you afraid and his dread fall on you?
**12** Your memorable sayings are proverbs of ashes. Your defenses are defenses of clay.
**13** “Be silent! Leave me alone, that I may speak. Let come on me what will.
**14** Why should I take my flesh in my teeth, and put my life in my hand?
**15** Behold, he will kill me. I have no hope. Nevertheless, I will maintain my ways before him.
**16** This also will be my salvation, that a godless man will not come before him.
**17** Listen carefully to my speech. Let my declaration be in your ears.
**18** See now, I have set my cause in order. I know that I am righteous.
**19** Who is he who will contend with me? For then I would hold my peace and give up the spirit.
**20** “Only don’t do two things to me, then I will not hide myself from your face:
**21** withdraw your hand far from me, and don’t let your terror make me afraid.
**22** Then call, and I will answer, or let me speak, and you answer me.
**23** How many are my iniquities and sins? Make me know my disobedience and my sin.
**24** Why do you hide your face, and consider me your enemy?
**25** Will you harass a driven leaf? Will you pursue the dry stubble?
**26** For you write bitter things against me, and make me inherit the iniquities of my youth.
**27** You also put my feet in the stocks, and mark all my paths. You set a bound to the soles of my feet,
**28** though I am decaying like a rotten thing, like a garment that is moth-eaten.
