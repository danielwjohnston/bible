## Clear & Faithful

**1** "Do you know the time when the mountain goats of the crags give birth? Do you watch over the calving of the deer?
**2** Can you count the months they carry to full term, and do you know the exact hour of their delivery?
**3** They crouch down, bring forth their young, and cast off their labor pains.
**4** Their offspring grow strong and thrive in the open wild; they go out and never return to them.
**5** Who set the wild donkey free? Who loosed the bonds of the swift wild ass,
**6** whose home I made the desert steppe and whose dwelling place is the salt flats?
**7** He laughs at the clamor of the town; he never hears the shouts of a taskmaster.
**8** He roams the mountain ranges as his pasture, searching out every patch of green.
**9** Will the wild ox consent to serve you, or spend the night beside your feeding trough?
**10** Can you harness the wild ox to a furrow with a rope, or will he plow the valley behind you?
**11** Can you rely on him because his strength is vast, and entrust your heavy labor to him?
**12** Can you trust him to haul home your grain and gather it onto your threshing floor?
**13** The wings of the ostrich beat proudly—yet are her pinions and feathers like those of the stork?
**14** For she abandons her eggs upon the ground and lets them warm in the dust,
**15** forgetting that a foot might crush them or a wild beast of the field might trample them.
**16** She deals harshly with her young, as though they were not hers; her labor is in vain, unconcerned with fear—
**17** because God has withheld wisdom from her and granted her no share in discernment.
**18** Yet when she spreads herself aloft to run, she laughs at the horse and its rider!
**19** Did you bestow strength upon the horse? Did you clothe his neck with a flowing mane?
**20** Do you make him leap like a locust? His majestic snort strikes terror!
**21** He paws the valley floor and exults in his strength; he charges out to meet the clash of arms.
**22** He laughs at fear and is never dismayed; he does not shrink back from the sword.
**23** The quiver rattles against his flank, along with the flashing spear and javelin.
**24** Fierce and trembling with fury, he swallows up the ground; he cannot stand still at the sound of the trumpet.
**25** At every blast of the horn he snorts, 'Aha!' From far away he catches the scent of battle, the thunder of captains and the war cry.
**26** Is it by your insight that the hawk takes flight and spreads his wings toward the south?
**27** Is it by your word that the eagle soars aloft, setting his nest upon the lofty heights?
**28** He dwells upon the cliff and makes his nightly perch on the jagged crag and fortress peak.
**29** From there he scans for prey; his eyes spot it from far away.
**30** His fledglings gulp down blood, and wherever the slain lie, there he is."

## Everyday English

**1** "Do you even know when wild mountain goats give birth, or keep watch while wild deer go into labor?
**2** Do you count the months until they're full term and mark the exact day they deliver?
**3** They squat down, drop their fawns, and leave the birth pains behind.
**4** Their young grow tough and strong out in the open brush—then they wander off and never come back.
**5** Who turned the wild donkey loose to run free? Who untied the ropes on the swift desert donkey?
**6** I gave him the open wilderness for a home and the salty badlands to live in.
**7** He mocks the noise of crowded cities and never listens to a driver yelling orders.
**8** He scouts the mountain slopes for grazing land and hunts down every blade of green grass.
**9** Think a wild ox will agree to work for you, or sleep quietly beside your barn manger?
**10** Can you tie a rope around a wild ox and make him plow straight rows behind you down the valley?
**11** Would you trust his massive muscles enough to hand over your farm chores to him?
**12** Do you seriously count on him to haul your harvest home and pile it on your threshing floor?
**13** The ostrich flaps her wings with excitement—though her feathers are nothing like a caring stork's!
**14** She leaves her eggs sitting right on the dirt, letting the warm dust incubate them,
**15** completely forgetting that somebody's boot could smash them or a wild animal could step on them.
**16** She treats her own chicks roughly as if they belong to someone else, never worrying if all her effort goes to waste—
**17** because God didn't build maternal wisdom into her or give her that kind of common sense.
**18** Yet the instant she jumps up and hits full stride, she leaves the fastest horse and rider in the dust!
**19** Were you the one who gave the warhorse its power, or draped that rippling mane down its neck?
**20** Do you make it bound forward like a locust? One thunderous snort from its nostrils makes soldiers shudder!
**21** It paws restlessly in the valley, bursting with energy, and charges straight toward the weapons!
**22** It laughs in the face of panic, refuses to flinch, and never backs down from a drawn sword.
**23** Arrows rattle in the quiver against its side while lances and javelins gleam nearby.
**24** Shaking with battle fever, it eats up the miles; when the war horn blows, it can't hold still!
**25** Every time the trumpet sounds, it snorts, 'Let's go!' Miles away it picks up the smell of war—officers shouting and troops roaring!
**26** Does a hawk learn how to soar from your brilliance, stretching out its wings to migrate south?
**27** Does an eagle wait for your permission before climbing into the clouds to build its nest on a high ridge?
**28** It lives on sheer cliff walls, roosting overnight on sharp rocky peaks like a fortress.
**29** From way up there it scouts for food; its sharp eyes spot prey from miles away.
**30** Its chicks gulp down fresh blood, and wherever dead bodies lie, that's where you'll find it."

## Crossings & Lexical Notes

- **v. 9 `רֵים` (*re'em*):** The wild aurochs (*Bos primigenius*), an untamable, massive wild ox renowned in the ancient Near East for terrifying strength (Num 23:22; Ps 22:21).
- **v. 12 `יָשִׁיב` (*yashiv*, Qere) vs. `יָשׁוּב` (*yashuv*, Ketiv):** The Qere transitive Hiphil ("he will bring back your grain") fits the direct object `זַרְעֶךָ` ("your seed/harvest"), whereas the Ketiv reads intransitive Qal ("your seed will return").
- **v. 13 `חֲסִידָה` (*chasidah*):** The stork—whose Hebrew name derives from *chesed* ("loyal devotion/kindness") for its famous care of its young, sharply contrasting with the seemingly neglectful ostrich (`רְנָנִים`, "the loudly crying/flapping bird").
- **v. 19 `רַעְמָה` (*ra'mah*):** Hapax legomenon from *ra'am* ("thunder/quiver"), denoting either the horse's trembling, rippling mane or its "thunderous majesty."
- **v. 26 `כְּנָפָיו` (*kenafav*, Qere) vs. `כְּנָפוֹ` (*kenafo*, Ketiv):** Plural Qere ("his wings") vs. singular collective Ketiv ("his wing/pinion").
- **v. 30 `וְאֶפְרֹחָיו` (*ve-efrochav*, Qere) vs. `וְאֶפְרֹחוֹ` (*ve-efrocho*, Ketiv):** Plural Qere ("and his fledglings") agrees with the plural verb `יְעַלְעוּ` ("they gulp down"), whereas Ketiv writes singular defective `ואפרחו`.

## Supplemental Texts & Allusions

- **Psalm 104:11, 18** (wild creatures' habitats): Celebrates wild donkeys and mountain goats inhabiting rocky crags and wilderness expanses created for them by God (vv. 1–8).
- **Lamentations 4:3** (ostrich cruelty proverb): Invokes the ostrich's reputation for harshness toward its young as a proverb for unnatural neglect (vv. 14–16).
- **Matthew 24:28 / Luke 17:37** (proverbial echo): Mirrors the closing proverb of v. 30—wherever the carcass/slain lie, there the vultures/eagles gather.
