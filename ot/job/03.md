## Clear & Faithful

**1** Afterward, Job opened his mouth and cursed the day of his birth.
**2** Job spoke out and said:
**3** "May the day I was born vanish, along with the night that announced, 'A male child has been conceived!'
**4** Let that day turn to darkness; let God above take no notice of it, nor let daylight gleam upon it.
**5** Let gloom and deepest shadow reclaim it as kin; let storm clouds settle over it, and solar eclipses fill it with dread.
**6** As for that night, may thick blackness seize it; let it never join the days of the year, nor enter the count of the months.
**7** Look—let that night stay barren and solitary; let no shout of gladness ring within it.
**8** Let those who lay curses on days place a hex upon it—those ready to awaken Leviathan!
**9** May the stars of its twilight be extinguished; let it long for dawn in vain and never behold the eyelids of morning,
**10** because it failed to bar the doors of my mother's womb and conceal misery from my eyes.
**11** Why did I not perish right out of the womb, breathe my last the moment I left the belly?
**12** Why were knees there to welcome me, or breasts for me to nurse?
**13** Otherwise I would be lying down right now in quiet; I would be asleep and finally at peace—
**14** alongside kings and counselors of the earth who built grand tombs for themselves,
**15** or alongside princes who possessed gold and crammed their mansions with silver;
**16** or, like a hidden stillborn child, I would never have existed, like infants who never glimpsed the light.
**17** There the wicked stop their raging, and there the exhausted in strength find rest.
**18** Together the captives relax at ease; they hear the slave-driver's voice no more.
**19** Both insignificant and mighty are there alike, and the enslaved stands freed from his master.
**20** Why grant light to a sufferer, or life to those embittered in soul—
**21** who long for death when it refuses to come, excavating for it harder than for buried treasure,
**22** who swell with ecstatic delight and exult the moment they discover a tomb—
**23** to a man whose path lies concealed, whom God has fenced in on every side?
**24** Instead of bread, my groaning arrives; my roaring sighs spill out like rushing water.
**25** For the very terror I dreaded has overtaken me, and what I shrank back from has come upon me.
**26** I have found no ease, no quiet, no rest—only relentless turmoil has arrived."

## Everyday English

**1** Finally, Job broke the silence and cursed the day he was born.
**2** Here is what Job said:
**3** "Erase the day I came into this world, and wipe out the night someone cheered, 'It's a boy!'
**4** Turn that whole day pitch-black! May God up above ignore it completely—never let a single ray of sunlight hit it.
**5** Let pitch-darkness and death's shadow take it over; bury it under heavy storm clouds and terrifying blackouts.
**6** And that night—let thick blackness swallow it up! Kick it off the calendar so it's never counted in any month or year again.
**7** Leave that night sterile and empty, without one sound of celebration.
**8** Hire the sorcerers who curse days—the ones who can stir up the sea monster Leviathan—to put a curse on that night!
**9** Snuff out its early-morning stars; leave it waiting forever for sunrise, never catching even a blink of dawn,
**10** because it didn't slam shut the doors of my mother's womb and keep all this agony out of my sight.
**11** Why didn't I die at birth? Why didn't I take my last breath the second I left my mother's belly?
**12** Why were there knees waiting to cradle me, or breasts to feed me?
**13** If I'd died then, I'd be lying down right now in total quiet. I'd be sleeping in peace—
**14** right alongside ancient kings and royal advisors who built stone monuments for themselves,
**15** or wealthy rulers who stuffed their palaces with gold and silver.
**16** Better yet, I could've been a miscarriage buried out of sight, like babies who never opened their eyes to light.
**17** Down in the grave, violent people stop causing trouble, and worn-out workers finally get to rest.
**18** Prisoners get a break together down there; nobody's yelling at them or cracking a whip.
**19** Important people and nobodies are equal there, and slaves are finally free from their masters.
**20** Why keep handing out daylight to someone in agony, or dragging out life for people whose hearts are completely bitter?
**21** They beg for death and it won't show up—they dig for it more desperately than treasure hunters dig for gold.
**22** They're thrilled and ecstatic the day they finally find their grave!
**23** Why keep a man alive when his road is blocked off and God has trapped him behind a wall?
**24** Sighing has replaced my meals, and my cries pour out nonstop like water.
**25** Everything I ever panicked about has actually happened to me; my worst nightmare walked right through my door.
**26** I can't relax, I can't catch my breath, I get zero rest—nothing keeps coming except wave after wave of trouble."

## Crossings & Lexical Notes

- **v. 1 `וַיְקַלֵּל אֶת־יוֹמוֹ` (*va-yeqallel et-yomo*):** Unlike the Adversary's challenge to "curse God" (*barekh*), Job carefully avoids cursing God (`qillel` here targets *his birth-day*, not the Creator).
- **v. 5 `יִגְאָלֻהוּ` (*yig'aluhu*):** Literally "let darkness and deep-shadow (*tsalmavet*) act as kinsman-redeemer (*go'el*) for it"—a bitterly ironic reversal of redemption law (cf. `19:25`), inviting Chaos to reclaim his birthday as its own property.
- **v. 8 `לִוְיָתָן` (*Livyatan*):** "Leviathan," the primordial chaos sea-dragon (cf. 41:1; Ps 74:14; Isa 27:1). Job invokes mythical day-cursers who unleash cosmic uncreation to swallow the light of his birth night.
- **v. 14 `חֳרָבוֹת` (*choravot*):** Literally "desolate places / ruins," widely understood either as grand mausoleums/pyramids that eventually crumble into ruins or desolate tombs kings built for their afterlife.
- **v. 23 `וַיָּסֶךְ אֱלוֹהַּ בַּעֲדוֹ` (*va-yasekh Eloah ba'ado*):** Uses the exact verb *sakh* ("hedge in") from 1:10! What the Adversary called a protective fence of blessing, Job now feels as an inescapable prison wall.
- **v. 24 `לִפְנֵי לַחְמִי` (*lifnei lachmi*):** Either "before my bread" (sighing precedes every meal) or "in place of my bread" (sighs have become his daily sustenance).

## Supplemental Texts & Allusions

- **Genesis 1:3–5** (cosmic reversal): Job's opening cry *"Let that day be darkness"* (*yehi choshekh*) directly inverts God's creation command *"Let there be light"* (*yehi or*), wishing an unmaking of creation upon his birth.
- **Jeremiah 20:14–18** (prophetic lament parallel): Jeremiah similarly curses the day of his birth and asks why he emerged from the womb only to see toil and sorrow.
- **Isaiah 27:1; Psalm 74:14** (Leviathan imagery): Depict Leviathan as the primeval serpent/dragon of chaotic waters subdued by the LORD, illuminating Job's invocation of day-cursers arousing Leviathan in v. 8.

## Reference Text (WEB Updated, Public Domain)

**1** After this Job opened his mouth, and cursed the day of his birth.
**2** Job answered:
**3** “Let the day perish in which I was born, the night which said, ‘There is a boy conceived.’
**4** Let that day be darkness. Don’t let God from above seek for it, neither let the light shine on it.
**5** Let darkness and the shadow of death claim it for their own. Let a cloud dwell on it. Let all that makes the day black terrify it.
**6** As for that night, let thick darkness seize on it. Let it not rejoice among the days of the year. Let it not come into the number of the months.
**7** Behold, let that night be barren. Let no joyful voice come therein.
**8** Let them curse it who curse the day, who are ready to rouse up leviathan.
**9** Let the stars of its twilight be dark. Let it look for light, but have none, neither let it see the eyelids of the morning,
**10** because it didn’t shut up the doors of my mother’s womb, nor did it hide trouble from my eyes.
**11** “Why didn’t I die from the womb? Why didn’t I give up the spirit when my mother bore me?
**12** Why did the knees receive me? Or why the breast, that I should nurse?
**13** For now I should have lain down and been quiet. I should have slept, then I would have been at rest,
**14** with kings and counselors of the earth, who built up waste places for themselves;
**15** or with princes who had gold, who filled their houses with silver;
**16** or as a hidden untimely birth I had not been, as infants who never saw light.
**17** There the wicked cease from troubling. There the weary are at rest.
**18** There the prisoners are at ease together. They don’t hear the voice of the taskmaster.
**19** The small and the great are there. The servant is free from his master.
**20** “Why is light given to him who is in misery, life to the bitter in soul,
**21** who long for death, but it doesn’t come; and dig for it more than for hidden treasures,
**22** who rejoice exceedingly, and are glad, when they can find the grave?
**23** Why is light given to a man whose way is hidden, whom God has hedged in?
**24** For my sighing comes before I eat. My groanings are poured out like water.
**25** For the thing which I fear comes on me, that which I am afraid of comes to me.
**26** I am not at ease, neither am I quiet, neither do I have rest; but trouble comes.”
