## Clear & Faithful

**1** There lived a man in the land of Uz whose name was Job. That man was blameless and upright, one who revered God and shunned evil.
**2** Seven sons and three daughters were born to him.
**3** His wealth consisted of seven thousand sheep and goats, three thousand camels, five hundred teams of oxen, five hundred female donkeys, and a vast household of servants; indeed, this man was the greatest among all the peoples of the East.
**4** His sons used to go and hold a feast at each one's house on his appointed day, and they would send and invite their three sisters to eat and drink alongside them.
**5** Whenever the cycle of feasting days concluded, Job would send for them to consecrate them; rising early in the morning, he offered whole burnt offerings corresponding to the number of them all. For Job reasoned, "Perhaps my children have sinned and cursed God within their hearts." Such was Job's steady practice every day.
**6** Now there came a day when the sons of God arrived to take their stand before the LORD, and the Adversary also entered among them.
**7** The LORD asked the Adversary, "Where are you arriving from?" And the Adversary replied to the LORD, "From ranging across the earth and patrolling to and fro within it."
**8** Then the LORD asked the Adversary, "Have you fixed your attention on my servant Job? There is nobody like him on the earth—a blameless and upright man who reveres God and shuns evil."
**9** The Adversary answered the LORD, "Is it for nothing that Job reveres God?
**10** Have you not built a protective hedge around him, around his household, and around everything he possesses on every side? You have prospered the labor of his hands, and his herds have spilled across the land.
**11** Yet extend your hand now and strike all that belongs to him—surely he will curse you straight to your face!"
**12** The LORD told the Adversary, "Look, everything he owns is placed in your power; only against his person do not stretch out your hand." So the Adversary withdrew from the presence of the LORD.
**13** One day, while his sons and daughters were eating and drinking wine in the house of their eldest brother,
**14** a runner arrived at Job's place and reported, "The oxen were plowing and the donkeys were grazing beside them,
**15** when Sabean raiders swooped down and seized them, striking the attendants with the edge of the sword. I alone survived to bring you word!"
**16** While he was still speaking, another arrived and cried, "Lightning from God dropped from the sky, ignited the flocks and the attendants, and devoured them! I alone survived to bring you word!"
**17** While he was still speaking, yet another arrived and said, "Chaldean bands formed three columns, charged the camels, and carried them off, striking the attendants with the edge of the sword. I alone survived to bring you word!"
**18** While he was still speaking, another arrived and declared, "Your sons and daughters were eating and drinking wine inside their oldest brother's house,
**19** when suddenly a violent gale swept in from across the desert, slammed against the four corners of the house, and collapsed it onto the young people so that they perished. I alone survived to bring you word!"
**20** At this, Job stood up, tore his outer robe, shaved his head, threw himself to the ground, and bowed in worship.
**21** He spoke: "Bare I emerged from my mother's womb, and bare I shall return thither. The LORD gave, and the LORD has taken away; blessed be the name of the LORD."
**22** Throughout all of this, Job did not sin, nor did he charge God with any wrongdoing.

## Everyday English

**1** Out in the region of Uz, there lived a man named Job. He was a genuinely good man with complete integrity—someone who honored God and stayed far away from evil.
**2** He was the father of seven sons and three daughters.
**3** He owned huge herds: seven thousand sheep and goats, three thousand camels, five hundred pairs of oxen, and five hundred donkeys, along with a large staff of workers. Nobody in the entire East was wealthier or more respected than Job.
**4** Job's sons had a custom of taking turns hosting dinners at one another's homes, always sending word to invite their three sisters to come eat and drink with them.
**5** Once each round of celebrations wrapped up, Job would summon his children to purify themselves. Before dawn the next morning, he'd offer a burnt sacrifice for each one of them, thinking, "Just in case my kids slipped up and insulted God silently in their minds." That's how careful Job was all the time.
**6** One day, the heavenly beings came to report to the LORD, and the Accuser slipped in right along with them.
**7** "Where have you been?" the LORD asked the Accuser. "Oh, wandering around the earth, walking up and down through it," he answered.
**8** Then the LORD said to the Accuser, "Have you noticed my servant Job? Nobody else on earth compares to him. He's completely honest and decent, honors God, and refuses to have anything to do with evil."
**9** The Accuser shot back at the LORD, "Why wouldn't Job honor God when you pay him so well for it?
**10** You've fenced him in with protection—him, his family, and everything he owns! You bless every project he touches, so his livestock have multiplied until they cover the countryside.
**11** Try reaching out and taking away everything he has, and watch if he doesn't curse you right to your face!"
**12** "Fine," the LORD told the Accuser. "Everything he owns is yours to touch. Just don't lay a finger on Job himself." With that, the Accuser walked out from the LORD's presence.
**13** Not long after, on a day when Job's sons and daughters were sharing food and wine at their oldest brother's home,
**14** a breathless messenger ran up to Job. "The oxen were pulling plows and the donkeys were grazing nearby," he gasped,
**15** "when raiders from Sheba ambushed us! They stole the animals and killed the farmhands with their swords. I'm the only one who got away to tell you!"
**16** Before he even finished talking, a second messenger burst in: "A massive bolt of fire dropped out of the sky, burned up the sheep and the shepherds, and wiped them all out! I'm the only one left to tell you!"
**17** Before he stopped speaking, a third messenger ran up: "Three squads of Chaldean bandits attacked us, rustled the camels, and cut down the herders with swords! I'm the only survivor to tell you!"
**18** He wasn't even done speaking when a fourth messenger arrived: "Your sons and daughters were having dinner and drinking wine at your oldest son's house,
**19** when a fierce desert wind roared in, hit all four walls at once, and brought the roof crashing down on your children. They're all dead! I'm the only one who escaped to tell you."
**20** Job stood up, ripped his cloak in grief, shaved his head bare, dropped face-first onto the dirt, and worshiped.
**21** Then he said: "I entered this world from my mother's womb with nothing, and I'll leave it with nothing. The LORD gives, and the LORD takes away—praise the LORD's name anyway!"
**22** Through every single blow, Job didn't sin once, and he never accused God of acting unfairly.

## Crossings & Lexical Notes

- **v. 1 `תָּם וְיָשָׁר` (*tam ve-yashar*):** *Tam* denotes wholeness, soundness, and moral completeness ("blameless"), while *yashar* means "straight/level/upright." Paired with "fearing God" and "turning aside from evil," these four traits establish Job's character before any crisis hits.
- **v. 4 `אחיתיהם` / `אַחְיוֹתֵיהֶם` (*achyoteihem*):** Ketiv reads defective `אחיתיהם`, while the pointed Qere supplies `אַחְיוֹתֵיהֶם` ("their sisters"), standardizing the plural noun morphology.
- **v. 5 `וּבֵרֲכוּ` (*u-verakhu*):** Literally "and blessed," used here (and in v. 11; 2:5, 9) as a scribal euphemism (antiphrasis / *tiqqun soferim* tradition) to avoid printing the verb "curse" directly beside the divine name.
- **v. 6 `הַשָּׂטָן` (*ha-satan*):** Carries the Hebrew definite article (*ha-*), functioning not yet as a proper name but as a courtroom role or title: "the Adversary" or "the Accuser" among the *bene ha-Elohim* ("sons of God," divine council).
- **v. 10 `את` / `אַתָּה` (*attah*):** Ketiv lacks the final *he* (`את`), whereas Qere reads the full masculine singular pronoun `אַתָּה` ("you").
- **v. 21 `יצתי` / `יָצָאתִי` (*yatsati*):** Ketiv omits the quiescent *alef* (`יצתי`), while Qere restores the standard spelling `יָצָאתִי` ("I came forth"). Note also `שָׁמָה` ("thither/there"), viewing the earth itself as the return-womb (cf. Ps 139:15; Eccl 5:15).
- **v. 22 `תִּפְלָה` (*tiflah*):** Unsavory/tasteless speech, moral impropriety, or foolishness imputed to God (same root as *tafel* "unsalted/tasteless" in 6:6).

## Supplemental Texts & Allusions

- **Ezekiel 14:14, 20** (canonical reputation): Ezekiel names Job alongside Noah and Daniel as an exemplar of personal righteousness who can deliver his own life through faithfulness.
- **James 5:11** (NT reception): Points back to Job's steadfast endurance and the eventual compassion and mercy the Lord showed at the outcome of his trial.
- **Ecclesiastes 5:15 (MT 5:14)** (shared wisdom motif): Echoes Job 1:21's reflection that humans depart this life as naked and empty-handed as they emerged from the womb.

**2** There were born to him seven sons and three daughters.
**3** His possessions also were seven thousand sheep, three thousand camels, five hundred yoke of oxen, five hundred female donkeys, and a very great household; so that this man was the greatest of all the children of the east.
**4** His sons went and held a feast in the house of each one on his birthday; and they sent and called for their three sisters to eat and to drink with them.
**5** It was so, when the days of their feasting had run their course, that Job sent and sanctified them, and rose up early in the morning, and offered burnt offerings according to the number of them all. For Job said, “It may be that my sons have sinned, and renounced God in their hearts.” Job did so continually.
**6** Now on the day when God’s sons came to present themselves before the LORD, Satan also came among them.
**7** The LORD said to Satan, “Where have you come from?” Then Satan answered the LORD, and said, “From going back and forth on the earth, and from walking up and down in it.”
**8** The LORD said to Satan, “Have you considered my servant, Job? For there is no one like him on the earth, a blameless and an upright man, one who fears God, and turns away from evil.”
**9** Then Satan answered the LORD, and said, “Does Job fear God for nothing?
**10** Haven’t you made a hedge around him, and around his house, and around all that he has, on every side? You have blessed the work of his hands, and his substance is increased in the land.
**11** But stretch out your hand now, and touch all that he has, and he will renounce you to your face.”
**12** The LORD said to Satan, “Behold, all that he has is in your power. Only on himself don’t stretch out your hand.” So Satan went out from the presence of the LORD.

**14** that a messenger came to Job, and said, “The oxen were plowing, and the donkeys feeding beside them,
**15** and the Sabeans attacked, and took them away. Yes, they have killed the servants with the edge of the sword, and I alone have escaped to tell you.”

**17** While he was still speaking, another also came and said, “The Chaldeans made three bands, and swept down on the camels, and have taken them away, yes, and killed the servants with the edge of the sword; and I alone have escaped to tell you.”

**20** Then Job arose, and tore his robe, and shaved his head, and fell down on the ground, and worshiped.
**21** He said, “Naked I came out of my mother’s womb, and naked will I return there. The LORD gave, and the LORD has taken away. Blessed be the LORD’s name.”
