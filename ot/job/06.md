## Clear & Faithful

**1** Then Job answered and said:
**2** "If only my anguish could be fully weighed, and all my calamity laid onto the scales beside it!
**3** For right now it outweighs the sand of the seas—that is why my words have sounded wild.
**4** The arrows of the Almighty stick fast in me, and my spirit drinks in their venom; the terrors of God stand marshaled against me.
**5** Does a wild donkey bray when it has fresh grass, or an ox bellow over its own fodder?
**6** Can bland food be eaten without salt, or is there any flavor in mallow slime?
**7** My appetite refuses to touch them; they taste like spoiled bread that makes me sick.
**8** Oh, that my plea might be granted, that God would hand me what I long for—
**9** that God would consent to crush me, let loose his hand, and sever my thread!
**10** That alone would remain my consolation—I would even exult amid unrelenting pain—for I have never disowned the words of the Holy One.
**11** What strength do I have left to keep waiting? What future awaits me to keep stretching out my life?
**12** Is my strength made of granite, or my flesh cast from bronze?
**13** Have I any resource left inside myself, when every means of survival has been driven far from me?
**14** A person in despair deserves steadfast kindness from his friend, even if he were to abandon the fear of the Almighty.
**15** Yet my brothers have proved as treacherous as a seasonal stream, like desert wadis that rush past and vanish—
**16** choked dark with winter ice, buried beneath drifting snow,
**17** yet the moment warm weather strikes, they dry up; in the summer heat they evaporate from their beds.
**18** Caravans turn aside along their winding course; they climb out into barren wastes and perish.
**19** Trading caravans from Tema search for them; merchant convoys from Sheba pin their hopes on them.
**20** They end up humiliated for trusting; arriving on the spot, they stand dismayed.
**21** Just like that, you have become utterly useless to me: you witness horror, and panic grips you.
**22** Did I ever ask you, 'Hand something over to me,' or 'Spend your own fortune to buy me off,
**23** rescue me from an enemy's grip, or ransom me from ruthless tyrants'?
**24** Instruct me, and I will hold my tongue; point out clearly where I have gone astray!
**25** How piercing are honest words! Yet what does any lecturing from you actually prove?
**26** Do you intend to put mere words on trial, treating a desperate man's speeches as empty wind?
**27** You would even gamble away an orphan and bargain over your own companion!
**28** Come now, consent to look straight at me—surely I would not lie to your faces!
**29** Think again, please—let there be no injustice; yes, reconsider, for my integrity is still intact!
**30** Is there deceit upon my tongue, or cannot my own palate recognize sheer ruin?"

## Everyday English

**1** Job spoke back:
**2** "I wish someone could put my grief on a scale and pile all my disasters onto the other side!
**3** Honestly, my pain weighs more than every grain of sand on every beach—no wonder my words came out reckless!
**4** The Almighty has shot poisoned arrows straight into me, and my spirit is soaking up the toxin. God's nightmares have me surrounded.
**5** Does a wild donkey bray when it has plenty of grass? Does a cow complain when its feed trough is full?
**6** Who wants to swallow bland food without salt, or gulp down tasteless egg white?
**7** My stomach turns just looking at your advice—it makes me sick like rotten bread.
**8** I wish God would grant my one request and hand me what I'm praying for:
**9** I wish God would go ahead and crush me—reach down and cut my life short!
**10** Knowing I'd die soon would actually comfort me; I'd jump for joy through ruthless pain, because I've never hidden or denied the Holy One's words.
**11** Where am I supposed to get the energy to hang on? What am I waiting around for?
**12** Am I made out of rock? Is my skin solid bronze?
**13** I have zero strength left in me, and every last option has been knocked out of my reach.
**14** When someone is ready to give up, a loyal friend owes them kindness—even if that person starts letting go of reverence for the Almighty.
**15** Instead, you brothers have let me down like a seasonal creek bed, like desert streams that flood and disappear!
**16** They look full when they're choked with dark ice and packed snow,
**17** but as soon as warm sun hits them, they dry right up and vanish in the heat.
**18** Travelers detour off the trail looking for those streams, wander out into empty desert, and die.
**19** Merchants from Tema scan the horizon for water; trade caravans from Sheba count on finding it.
**20** Their confidence turns into shock; when they reach the dry riverbed, they're crushed.
**21** That's exactly how useless you've been to me! You saw how terrifying my suffering is, and you got scared.
**22** Did I ever beg you, 'Give me money'? Did I ask you to dip into your savings to bribe someone for me,
**23** or buy me out of an enemy's hands, or pay my ransom to thugs?
**24** Teach me, and I'll shut right up—show me where I messed up!
**25** Truthful words hit hard, sure—but what are your lectures actually proving?
**26** Are you really going to pick apart my grammar and treat a hopeless man's cries like hot air?
**27** At this rate, you'd throw dice over an orphan's clothes and auction off your own friend!
**28** Please, just look me in the eyes—would I lie straight to your face?
**29** Stop and rethink this! Don't treat me unfairly! Think again, because I haven't done anything wrong!
**30** Do you really hear lies on my tongue? Don't you think I can taste the difference between right and disaster?"

## Crossings & Lexical Notes

- **v. 2 `והיתי` / `וְהַוָּתִי` (*ve-havvati*):** Ketiv reads `והיתי`, while Qere reads `וְהַוָּתִי` ("and my ruin/calamity," linked with `הַוּוֹת` "calamities" in v. 30). Note how Job plays on Eliphaz's word `כַּעַשׂ` (*ka'as*, "vexation," 5:2 vs 6:2).
- **v. 6 `רִיר חַלָּמוּת` (*rir challamut*):** Literally "slime/juice of purslane/mallow" (a wild mucilaginous plant) or, in later rabbinic tradition, "egg white"—used as an image of insipid, unappetizing counsel.
- **v. 14 `לַמָּס` (*la-mas*):** "To the despairing/melting one," followed by *chesed* ("covenant loyalty/steadfast love"), asserting that friendship's duty overrides theological policing.
- **v. 21 `לא` / `לוֹ` (*lo*):** A key Ketiv/Qere divergence. Ketiv reads `לֹא` ("not / nothing": "you have become nothing"), while Qere reads `לוֹ` ("to it / like it": "you have become just like that dry wadi").
- **v. 29 `ושבי` / `וְשׁוּבוּ` (*ve-shuvu*):** Ketiv reads `ושבי` (perhaps feminine singular or scribal slip), while Qere restores the masculine plural imperative `וְשׁוּבוּ` ("and return / reconsider!"), matching the plural opening imperative `שֻׁבוּ־נָא` ("turn back, please") addressed to all three friends.

## Supplemental Texts & Allusions

- **Jeremiah 15:18** (deceitful brook metaphor): Jeremiah asks God whether he has become like an unreliable wadi whose waters fail in summer—paralleling Job's portrait of fair-weather friends in vv. 15–20.
- **Deuteronomy 32:23–24 & Psalm 38:2 (MT 38:3)** (divine arrows): Depict God's afflictions as arrows piercing the sufferer's body and draining their spirit.
- **1 Kings 19:4 & Jonah 4:3, 8** (weariness of life): Share Job's desperate plea in vv. 8–9 that God grant release through death rather than prolonging unbearable anguish.

## Reference Text (WEB Updated, Public Domain)

**1** Then Job answered,
**2** “Oh that my anguish were weighed, and all my calamity laid in the balances!
**3** For now it would be heavier than the sand of the seas, therefore my words have been rash.
**4** For the arrows of the Almighty are within me. My spirit drinks up their poison. The terrors of God set themselves in array against me.
**5** Does the wild donkey bray when he has grass? Or does the ox low over his fodder?
**6** Can that which has no flavor be eaten without salt? Or is there any taste in the white of an egg?
**7** My soul refuses to touch them. They are as loathsome food to me.
**8** “Oh that I might have my request, that God would grant the thing that I long for,
**9** even that it would please God to crush me; that he would let loose his hand, and cut me off!
**10** Let it still be my consolation, yes, let me exult in pain that doesn’t spare, that I have not denied the words of the Holy One.
**11** What is my strength, that I should wait? What is my end, that I should be patient?
**12** Is my strength the strength of stones? Or is my flesh of bronze?
**13** Isn’t it that I have no help in me, that wisdom is driven away from me?
**14** “To him who is ready to faint, kindness should be shown from his friend; even to him who forsakes the fear of the Almighty.
**15** My brothers have dealt deceitfully as a brook, as the channel of brooks that pass away;
**16** which are black by reason of the ice, in which the snow hides itself.
**17** In the dry season, they vanish. When it is hot, they are consumed out of their place.
**18** The caravans that travel beside them turn away. They go up into the waste, and perish.
**19** The caravans of Tema looked. The companies of Sheba waited for them.
**20** They were distressed because they were confident. They came there, and were confounded.
**21** For now you are nothing. You see a terror, and are afraid.
**22** Did I ever say, ‘Give to me’? or, ‘Offer a present for me from your substance’?
**23** or, ‘Deliver me from the adversary’s hand’? or, ‘Redeem me from the hand of the oppressors’?
**24** “Teach me, and I will hold my peace. Cause me to understand my error.
**25** How forcible are words of uprightness! But your reproof, what does it reprove?
**26** Do you intend to reprove words, since the speeches of one who is desperate are as wind?
**27** Yes, you would even cast lots for the fatherless, and make merchandise of your friend.
**28** Now therefore be pleased to look at me, for surely I will not lie to your face.
**29** Please return. Let there be no injustice. Yes, return again. My cause is righteous.
**30** Is there injustice on my tongue? Can’t my taste discern mischievous things?
