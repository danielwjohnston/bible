## Clear & Faithful

**1** Then Zophar the Naamathite answered and said:
**2** "My agitated thoughts urge me to reply, because of the urgency surging inside me.
**3** I hear a rebuke that tries to put me to shame, yet a spirit born of my understanding prompts my response.
**4** Do you not know this truth from ancient days, ever since humanity was placed upon the earth,
**5** that the triumph of the wicked is short-lived, and the gladness of the godless lasts only an instant?
**6** Even if his pride climbs to the skies and his head brushes the clouds,
**7** like his own dung he vanishes forever; those who once saw him will ask, 'Where did he go?'
**8** He flits away like a dream and cannot be traced, chased off like a vision in the night.
**9** The eye that caught sight of him sees him no more, and his own home never beholds him again.
**10** His children must beg compensation from the poor, while his own hands pay back his ill-gotten wealth.
**11** Though his bones brimmed with youthful strength, it lies down beside him in the dust.
**12** Though evil tastes sweet inside his mouth and he tucks it beneath his tongue,
**13** savoring it so he will not let it go, holding it close against his palate,
**14** his food turns sour inside his stomach—turning into cobra venom within him.
**15** He gulped down riches only to vomit them back up; God forces them right out of his belly.
**16** He sucks on the poison of cobras; the fang of the viper strikes him dead.
**17** He will never feast his eyes on the flowing streams, the rivers surging with honey and cream.
**18** He must return what he labored for without swallowing a bite; for all the wealth he traded, he finds no joy.
**19** Because he crushed the helpless and left them destitute, seizing a home he never built,
**20** because his belly never knew contentment, none of his treasured hoard will save him.
**21** Nothing remained untouched by his greed; therefore his prosperity will not last.
**22** At the peak of his abundance, distress corners him; the full force of misery crashes down on him.
**23** Let him fill his belly if he can—God will unleash his blazing fury against him and rain it down upon him as his meal.
**24** Should he flee from an iron weapon, a bronze-tipped arrow pierces him through.
**25** It is pulled clear out of his back, the flashing blade glistening from his gallbladder; sheer dread descends on him.
**26** Pitch darkness waits in store for his hidden wealth; an unkindled fire devours him and consumes whatever lingers in his tent.
**27** The heavens expose his guilt, and the earth rises up as his accuser.
**28** The wealth of his house is carried off in a flood, swept away on the day of God's wrath.
**29** Such is the portion God assigns to a wicked person, the heritage decreed for him by God."

## Everyday English

**1** Then Zophar from Naamah jumped in:
**2** "My racing thoughts won't let me stay quiet—I'm bursting inside to answer you!
**3** I've had to listen to your insulting lecture, and now my own good sense tells me how to reply.
**4** Surely you know how things have worked from the very beginning, ever since people first walked the earth:
**5** crooked people never get to celebrate for long; a fraud's happiness lasts barely a blink.
**6** Even if his ego towers up to the sky and his head scrapes the clouds,
**7** he'll be tossed out like trash and gone for good; people who knew him will ask, 'Whatever happened to him?'
**8** He vanishes like a dream nobody can recall, chased away like a fleeting night vision.
**9** Eyes that saw him yesterday won't spot him today; his own neighborhood won't see him again.
**10** His kids will have to pay back the poor, and he'll have to hand over his fortune with his own hands.
**11** His body may still feel young and strong, yet all that vitality ends up lying in the dirt with him.
**12** Maybe wrongdoing tastes like candy in his mouth, so he tucks it under his tongue,
**13** savors every bit, and refuses to spit it out, rolling it around in his mouth—
**14** but once he swallows it, his meal curdles in his gut and turns into deadly snake poison!
**15** He gobbled up stolen wealth, and now he throws it all up—God forces it right out of his stomach.
**16** It's like nursing on cobra venom; a viper's bite finishes him off.
**17** He won't live to enjoy streams overflowing with fresh honey and rich cream.
**18** He'll hand back everything he worked for without getting to taste it, and all his profits won't bring him a single smile.
**19** Since he bullied the poor, left them stranded, and stole homes he never built—
**20** since his appetite could never be satisfied—none of his prized possessions will rescue him.
**21** Nothing escaped his greed, so his good fortune won't survive.
**22** Right when he thinks he has more than enough, trouble traps him, and every kind of disaster hits him at once.
**23** Just as he tries to stuff himself, God fires blazing anger at him and rains punishment down for his dinner!
**24** If he dodges an iron sword, a bronze arrow shoots right through him.
**25** Someone yanks the shaft out his back—its shiny point dripping from his guts—and pure terror grips him.
**26** Total darkness waits for his secret stash; a fire nobody had to light burns him up and wipes out whatever is left in his home.
**27** The sky reveals every crime he committed, and the earth itself stands up to testify against him.
**28** Floodwaters wash away everything inside his house on the day God's anger breaks loose.
**29** That's the payoff God gives to a wicked person—the exact inheritance God has lined up for him."

## Crossings & Lexical Notes

- **v. 2 `שְׂעִפַּי` (*se'ippai*) & `חוּשִׁי` (*chushi*):** *Se'ippim* are agitated, branching thoughts (cf. 4:13); *chush* means "haste/agitation" ("my inner urgency/impatience").
- **v. 11 `עֲלוּמָיו` (*alumav*, Qere) vs. `עלומו` (Ketiv):** Qere reads the masculine plural abstract noun "his youth/youthful vigor" (matched in 33:25), while Ketiv writes singular `עלומו`. Notice the feminine singular verb `תִּשְׁכָּב` ("it lies down") treats youth/strength collectively or anticipates "his bone/body."
- **v. 17 `דְּבַשׁ וְחֶמְאָה` (*devash vechem'ah*):** "Honey and curds/cream"—the classic pastoral imagery of lush abundance (echoing "milk and honey"), here denied to the exploiter.
- **v. 23 `בִּלְחוּמוֹ` (*bilchumo*):** Rare noun *lechum* ("food/flesh," related to *lechem* "bread"): God rains down wrath "as his food" (or "into his flesh"). Notice the archaic poetic 3mp suffix `-מוֹ` on `עָלֵימוֹ` (*aleimo*), used poetically here for the singular wicked person (or the wicked collectively).
- **v. 28 `יִגֶל` (*yigel*):** From *galah* ("be carried off into exile/uncovered") or *galal* ("roll away like water"), parallel with `נִגָּרוֹת` (*niggarot*, "torrents poured out").

## Supplemental Texts & Allusions

- **Psalm 37:35–36; 73:18–20** (transience of the wicked): Shares Zophar's image in vv. 6–9 of the arrogant flourishing briefly only to vanish like a fleeting dream so that no trace of them can be found.
- **Proverbs 20:17; 23:31–32** (sweet sin turning to venom): Parallels Zophar's vivid culinary metaphor in vv. 12–16—deceit or indulgence feels sweet on the palate before turning to grit or serpent venom inside.
- **Job 27:13** (verbatim thematic echo): Job will later throw Zophar's closing formula from v. 29 (*"This is the wicked person's portion from God"*) back in the friends' own teeth as he exposes their empty arguments.

## Reference Text (WEB Updated, Public Domain)

**1** Then Zophar the Naamathite answered,
**2** “Therefore my thoughts answer me, even by reason of my haste that is in me.
**3** I have heard the reproof which puts me to shame. The spirit of my understanding answers me.
**4** Don’t you know this from old time, since man was placed on earth,
**5** that the triumphing of the wicked is short, the joy of the godless but for a moment?
**6** Though his height mount up to the heavens, and his head reach to the clouds,
**7** yet he will perish forever like his own dung. Those who have seen him will say, ‘Where is he?’
**8** He will fly away as a dream, and will not be found. Yes, he will be chased away like a vision of the night.
**9** The eye which saw him will see him no more, neither will his place see him any more.
**10** His children will seek the favor of the poor. His hands will give back his wealth.
**11** His bones are full of his youth, but youth will lie down with him in the dust.
**12** “Though wickedness is sweet in his mouth, though he hide it under his tongue,
**13** though he spare it, and will not let it go, but keep it still within his mouth,
**14** yet his food in his bowels is turned. It is cobra venom within him.
**15** He has swallowed down riches, and he will vomit them up again. God will cast them out of his belly.
**16** He will suck cobra venom. The viper’s tongue will kill him.
**17** He will not look at the rivers, the flowing streams of honey and butter.
**18** He will restore that for which he labored, and will not swallow it down. He will not rejoice according to the substance that he has gotten.
**19** For he has oppressed and forsaken the poor. He has violently taken away a house, and he will not build it up.
**20** “Because he knew no quietness within him, he will not save anything of that in which he delights.
**21** There was nothing left that he didn’t devour, therefore his prosperity will not endure.
**22** In the fullness of his sufficiency, distress will overtake him. The hand of everyone who is in misery will come on him.
**23** When he is about to fill his belly, God will cast the fierceness of his wrath on him. It will rain on him while he is eating.
**24** He will flee from the iron weapon. The bronze arrow will strike him through.
**25** He draws it out, and it comes out of his body. Yes, the glittering point comes out of his liver. Terrors are on him.
**26** All darkness is laid up for his treasures. An unfanned fire will devour him. It will consume that which is left in his tent.
**27** The heavens will reveal his iniquity. The earth will rise up against him.
**28** The increase of his house will depart. They will rush away in the day of his wrath.
**29** This is the portion of a wicked man from God, the heritage appointed to him by God.”
