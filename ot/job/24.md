## Clear & Faithful

**1** "Why does the Almighty not set fixed times for judgment? Why do those who know him never see his days of reckoning?
**2** Criminals shift property boundaries; they steal herds and graze them as their own.
**3** They drive off the orphans' only donkey and seize the widow's ox as collateral.
**4** They crowd the needy off the public road, forcing the poor of the land to hide together.
**5** Like wild donkeys in the desert, the poor go out at dawn to forage for scraps; the barren wasteland must yield bread for their children.
**6** They harvest mixed fodder in another's field and glean the leftovers in the wicked owner's vineyard.
**7** They pass the night naked without garments, with nothing to cover them against the chill.
**8** Mountain downpours soak them through, and without shelter they huddle against bare rock.
**9** Exploiting creditors tear the fatherless infant from the breast and take collateral from the destitute.
**10** Stripped bare, the laborers trudge along without clothes; starving, they haul sheaves of grain.
**11** Between the olive-press walls they crush out oil; they tread the winepresses, yet suffer parching thirst.
**12** Out of the city the dying groan, and the throats of the wounded cry for help—yet God charges no one with the crime!
**13** Others belong to those who rebel against the light; they refuse to acknowledge its ways or stay on its paths.
**14** At dawn the murderer gets up to kill the poor and helpless, and after dark he prowls like a thief.
**15** The adulterer's eye watches eagerly for twilight, thinking, 'No eye can spot me,' as he pulls a veil over his face.
**16** In the dark, burglars tunnel into houses; by day they lock themselves indoors, strangers to the daylight.
**17** For pitch darkness is morning to all of them; they are right at home amid the terrors of deep gloom.
**18** Yet swift as foam on the water's surface, their plot of land lies cursed on earth, and no one turns down the road to their vineyards.
**19** Just as drought and heat drink up melted snow, so Sheol swallows those who have sinned.
**20** His own mother's womb forgets him; the worm feasts sweetly on him; his name is remembered no more, and wickedness snaps like a rotten tree—
**21** this man who preyed on the childless woman and showed no kindness to the widow!
**22** Yet God drags off even the mighty by his strength; once he rises up, no one can count on staying alive.
**23** He may grant them security to lean upon, yet his eyes remain fixed on every path they walk.
**24** They rise high for a brief moment—and then vanish! Brought low and gathered in like everyone else, they are snipped off like heads of ripe grain.
**25** If this is not the truth, who can prove me a liar and reduce my words to nothing?"

## Everyday English

**1** "Why doesn't the Almighty schedule clear days for justice? Why do people who know him never get to see him settle the score?
**2** Crooks move boundary markers to steal land, rustle whole herds, and graze them openly.
**3** They haul away an orphan's only donkey and take a widow's ox as security on a loan.
**4** They shove needy people right off the road, leaving the poor nowhere to go except into hiding.
**5** Poor families forage like wild donkeys out in the scrubland, hunting from sunrise just to scrape together food for their kids in the desert.
**6** They have to gather animal feed from someone else's field and pick leftover grapes in a rich bully's vineyard.
**7** They sleep outside half-naked with no clothes and no blanket when the freezing night hits.
**8** Cold mountain rain drenches them; with nowhere to stay dry, they curl up against bare rock.
**9** Cruel lenders snatch a baby right from a widow's arms and demand collateral from people who own nothing.
**10** Workers trudge around in rags without warm clothes, hauling heavy bundles of grain while their own stomachs ache with hunger.
**11** They squeeze olive oil between stone walls and stomp grapes in the vats—yet they're dying of thirst!
**12** Inside the cities, dying people groan and wounded victims scream for help, and God doesn't lift a finger to charge anyone!
**13** Meanwhile, another crowd hates the daylight—they reject honest ways and refuse to stick to bright paths.
**14** A killer wakes up before dawn to murder someone poor and defenseless, then sneaks around at night like a burglar.
**15** An unfaithful spouse waits eagerly for dusk, muttering, 'Nobody will recognize me,' and hides his face behind a mask.
**16** Thieves break through mud-brick walls in the dark and lock themselves inside all day; they hate daylight.
**17** Pitch-black midnight feels like morning to them—they're completely comfortable in the dark!
**18** Yet they vanish as fast as ripples on water; their farms end up cursed, and nobody bothers walking the path to their vineyards.
**19** Just as summer heat dries up snowmelt, the grave swallows sinners whole.
**20** Their own mothers forget them, worms make a sweet meal of them, nobody remembers their names, and cruelty snaps in half like a dead branch—
**21** because they exploited lonely, childless women and never helped a widow!
**22** Even so, God's power can drag away the strongest bullies; when he stands up, nobody's life is guaranteed.
**23** He might let them feel safe for a while, yet his eyes never leave what they're doing.
**24** They climb to the top for a short time, and suddenly they're gone—cut down and gathered up like everyone else, clipped off like tops of wheat.
**25** If I'm wrong about any of this, step up and prove I'm lying! Show that my words don't hold water!"

## Crossings & Lexical Notes

- **v. 1 `וְיֹדְעָיו` (*veyod'av*, Qere) vs. `וידעו` (Ketiv):** Qere supplies the plural participle with 3ms suffix ("and those who know him"), whereas Ketiv reads `וידעו` ("and his knowing ones" / defective spelling).
- **v. 6 `יִקְצוֹרוּ` (*yiqtsoru*, Qere) vs. `יקצירו` (Ketiv):** Qere points the verb as Qal imperfect ("they reap"), while Ketiv `יקצירו` reflects a Hiphil spelling (*yaqtsiru*).
- **v. 12 `תִּפְלָה` (*tiflah*):** Meaning "unsavoriness, folly, moral wrong" (from *tafel*, tasteless/unseemly; cf. `1:22`). Job protests that God does not impute/treat the oppressors' crime as wrong—though a minority vocalization *tefillah* ("prayer": "God does not heed prayer") also exists in ancient versions.
- **v. 22 `בַּחַיִּין` (*bachayyin*):** Uses the Aramaizing masculine plural ending `-in` (matching `מִלִּין` *millin* elsewhere in Job) for "life." Notice vv. 18–24 shift perspective rapidly—some commentators see Job quoting his friends' orthodox view (vv. 18–21) or admitting God *eventually* sweeps tyrants away (vv. 22–24), yet only after unspeakable suffering goes unchecked.

## Supplemental Texts & Allusions

- **Deuteronomy 19:14; 24:12–17** (Torah protections for the vulnerable): Job's indictment in vv. 2–9 directly echoes Covenant law prohibiting moving boundary markers, taking a widow's garment or ox in pledge, and oppressing orphans.
- **Amos 5:11; James 5:4** (oppressed laborers): Parallels vv. 10–12, where workers harvest grain, press oil, and tread wine while starving and crying out against unpaid or abusive employers.
- **Psalm 37:2; 90:5–6** (withering like grain/grass): Shares the closing image of v. 24—human prominence flourishes briefly before being cut down like stalks at harvest.
