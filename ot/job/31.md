## Clear & Faithful

**1** "I have made a covenant with my eyes; how then could I gaze intently at a young woman?
**2** Otherwise, what share does God deal out from heaven above, or what inheritance does the Almighty send from the heights?
**3** Does he not send ruin upon the wicked and strange disaster upon evildoers?
**4** Does he not see my ways and count every single step I take?
**5** If ever I kept company with lies, or my feet rushed swiftly after treachery—
**6** let God weigh me on honest scales and recognize my integrity!
**7** If my steps have turned aside from the path, if my heart has trailed after my eyes, or if any stain has clung to my hands—
**8** then let me sow and another eat, and let my crops be torn up by the roots!
**9** If my heart was ever seduced by another man's wife, or I waited in ambush beside my neighbor's door—
**10** then let my wife grind grain for another man, and let strangers lie with her!
**11** For that would be a shameful scheme, an offense calling for judgment;
**12** it is a fire that consumes all the way down to Abaddon, and would uproot every harvest I own.
**13** If I have dismissed the just complaint of my male or female servant when they brought a grievance against me—
**14** what then could I do when God rises up? When he calls me to account, how could I answer him?
**15** Did not the one who shaped me in the womb also shape them? Did not the same Creator fashion us inside the womb?
**16** If I have denied the poor what they needed, or caused the widow's eyes to grow dim with longing,
**17** or eaten my crust of bread alone without sharing it with the orphan—
**18** yet from my youth I raised him like a father, and from my mother's womb I guided her—
**19** if I have seen anyone perishing for lack of clothing, or a needy person with nothing to cover them,
**20** and their own body did not bless me after being warmed with fleece from my sheep;
**21** if I have raised my hand against the orphan because I knew my influence would back me at the city gate—
**22** then let my shoulder fall from its socket, and let my arm snap from its joint!
**23** For dread of calamity from God held me back, and before his majesty I could not stand.
**24** If I have placed my confidence in gold, or called fine gold my security;
**25** if I have gloated because my wealth was vast and my own hand had amassed a fortune;
**26** if I watched the sun shining in splendor or the moon gliding across in majesty,
**27** and my heart was secretly seduced so that my hand blew a kiss from my mouth—
**28** that too would be a crime worthy of judgment, for I would have disowned God above!
**29** If I have delighted in the ruin of someone who hated me, or exulted when misfortune overtook him—
**30** no, I never allowed my mouth to sin by invoking a curse upon his life—
**31** if the members of my household have not said, 'Who is there who has not been filled with his meat?'—
**32** no stranger ever had to spend the night outside, for I kept my doors open to the traveler—
**33** if I have concealed my transgressions like Adam, hiding my guilt inside my own chest
**34** because I feared the great crowd and dreaded the scorn of the clans, staying silent and refusing to step outside—
**35** Oh, if only I had someone to hear my case! Here is my signature—let the Almighty answer me! And if my accuser has written out an indictment,
**36** I would wear it proudly on my shoulder; I would bind it around my head like a crown!
**37** I would give him a full accounting of all my steps; like a prince I would walk right up to him!
**38** If my own soil raises an outcry against me, and every plowed furrow weeps aloud together,
**39** if I have consumed its yield without paying, or crushed the life out of its owners—
**40** then let thorns sprout instead of wheat, and foul weeds instead of barley!" Thus the speeches of Job come to an end.

## Everyday English

**1** "I made a strict promise to my own eyes never to stare lustfully at a young woman.
**2** After all, what does God above hand out as a reward? What does the Almighty in heaven give in return?
**3** Doesn't he bring ruin on the corrupt and disaster on people who do wrong?
**4** He watches everywhere I go and keeps track of every step I take.
**5** If I've lived a lie or raced off to cheat someone,
**6** then let God put me on fair scales—he'll see that I'm completely innocent!
**7** If I wandered off the right track, let my eyes drag my heart into sin, or got dirty money stuck to my hands,
**8** then let somebody else harvest what I planted, and let everything I own be ripped out by the roots!
**9** If I let myself fall for another man's wife or hung around my neighbor's door waiting for a chance,
**10** then let my wife cook and grind grain for another man, and let other men sleep with her!
**11** Adultery is a disgraceful crime—something the courts ought to punish.
**12** It's a wildfire that burns all the way to the grave and wipes out everything I've worked for.
**13** If I brushed off a fair complaint from my male or female workers when they had a dispute with me,
**14** what excuse would I have when God steps in? What could I possibly say when he puts me on trial?
**15** The same God who formed me in the womb formed them too—one Maker shaped us both!
**16** If I turned away poor people when they begged for help, or left widows staring into empty cupboards,
**17** or kept my food to myself instead of sharing with orphans—
**18** no, I've cared for orphans like a father since I was young, and looked out for widows my whole life!
**19** If I saw someone freezing without a coat or shivering in rags
**20** without warming them up in wool from my own sheep so they blessed me from the heart—
**21** or if I ever bullied an orphan because I knew the judges at the gate were on my side—
**22** then let my arm rip right out of my shoulder socket and snap clean in two!
**23** The thought of God's judgment always kept me in line; I could never face his awesome power if I did those things.
**24** Did I ever make gold my security blanket or tell piles of treasure, 'You're what I trust'?
**25** Did I brag about how huge my bank account was or how much wealth I'd piled up?
**26** Did I ever look up at the blazing sun or the glowing moon sailing across the sky
**27** and feel a secret urge to worship them, blowing them a kiss from my fingertips?
**28** That too would be a punishable offense, because it would mean betraying God in heaven!
**29** Did I ever celebrate when my enemy crashed and burned, or cheer when trouble hit him?
**30** Never—I wouldn't even let my mouth sin by wishing him dead!
**31** Everyone in my household can vouch for me: 'Has anyone ever left Job's table hungry?'
**32** No traveler ever had to sleep out on the street; my front door was always open to visitors.
**33** Have I tried to cover up my sins the way Adam did, burying my guilt deep inside
**34** just because I was terrified of public gossip or scared of my neighbors looking down on me, hiding indoors in silence?
**35** If only someone would actually listen to me! Look—here's my signature on my statement! Now let the Almighty answer me! Let my accuser put his charges in writing!
**36** I'd carry that indictment proudly on my shoulder; I'd wear it on my head like a royal crown!
**37** I'd walk right up to him like a prince and lay out every single step of my life.
**38** If my own fields scream out against me and their plowed rows weep together
**39** because I stole their crops without paying the workers or worked the landowners to death—
**40** then let wild weeds choke out my wheat fields, and rank stinkweed spring up where barley ought to grow!" And with that, Job finished speaking.

## Crossings & Lexical Notes

- **v. 11 `הִיא` (*hi*, Qere) vs. `הוּא` (*hu*, Ketiv) & `וְהוּא` (*ve-hu*, Qere) vs. `וְהִיא` (*ve-hi*, Ketiv):** A mirror-image pronoun swap between the feminine noun `זִמָּה` (*zimmah*, "wicked scheme") and the masculine phrase `עָוֺן פְּלִילִים` (*avon pelilim*, "iniquity for judges").
- **v. 20 `חֲלָצָיו` (*chalatsav*, Qere) vs. `חַלְצוֹ` (*chaltso*, Ketiv):** Plural Qere "his loins" (standard Hebrew dual/plural form for body parts) vs. singular defective Ketiv `חלצו`.
- **v. 27 `וַתִּשַּׁק יָדִי לְפִי` (*va-tishaq yadi le-fi*):** "And my hand kissed my mouth"—an ancient Near Eastern gesture of astral homage (blowing a kiss toward sun or moon deities, prohibited in Deut 4:19).
- **v. 33 `כְּאָדָם` (*ke-adam*):** Either "like Adam" (recalling Gen 3:8–12's hiding in shame) or generically "like [ordinary] mortals/humankind."
- **v. 35 `הֶן־תָּוִי` (*hen-tavi*):** *Tav* is the final letter of the Hebrew alphabet (`ת`, originally shaped like an `X`/cross), used as a legal signature-mark authenticating Job's oath of clearance.
- **v. 40 `תַּמּוּ דִּבְרֵי אִיּוֹב` (*tammu divrey Iyyov*):** Editorial colophon marking the end of Job's formal disputation speeches before Elihu enters (compare Ps 72:20).

## Supplemental Texts & Allusions

- **Genesis 3:8–12** (thematic allusion): Job's refusal to hide guilt "like Adam" (`כְּאָדָם`, v. 33) recalls the primal concealment after transgression.
- **Deuteronomy 4:19 & 17:3** (covenant prohibition): Forbids bowing down or offering homage to the sun, moon, and stars, echoed in Job's denial of astral kiss-worship (vv. 26–28).
- **Exodus 22:21–24 & Deuteronomy 24:17–21** (Torah ethics): Mandates justice and material care for the orphan, widow, and resident alien—standards Job invokes throughout his oath (vv. 16–22, 32).
