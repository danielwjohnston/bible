## Clear & Faithful

**1** Then Elihu spoke again and said:
**2** "Do you regard this as just, when you say, 'My righteousness exceeds God's,'
**3** and ask, 'What advantage is it to you? What do I gain compared with sinning?'
**4** I will reply to you directly, and to your companions along with you.
**5** Look up at the heavens and observe; gaze at the high clouds towering far above you!
**6** If you sin, what harm do you inflict upon him? Even if your rebellions multiply, what can you do to him?
**7** Should you live uprightly, what gift does that bring him? What benefit can he accept from your hands?
**8** Your wickedness affects only a human like yourself, and your righteousness benefits only another mortal!
**9** Beneath the weight of many oppressions people cry out; they plead for rescue from the grip of the powerful.
**10** Yet nobody pauses to ask, 'Where is God who fashioned me, the One who grants melodies in the dark hours,
**11** who instructs us beyond the creatures of the field and grants us greater insight than the birds overhead?'
**12** So they cry out there, yet he does not answer, because of the arrogance of wicked people.
**13** Surely God does not listen to an empty plea, nor does the Almighty take notice of it.
**14** How much less when you claim you cannot see him—your case lies right before him, so wait patiently for him!
**15** Yet now, because his anger has not yet struck in punishment, and he has not taken strict notice of arrogance,
**16** Job opens his mouth wide with empty breath, piling up words without any understanding."

## Everyday English

**1** Elihu continued his response:
**2** "Do you honestly think that's fair, claiming, 'I'm more right than God is,'
**3** or asking, 'What good does obeying God do me? How am I any better off than if I sinned?'
**4** Let me answer that for you—and for your three friends sitting right here with you.
**5** Step outside and stare up at the sky; look how high the clouds drift overhead!
**6** If you mess up, do you actually damage God? Even if you break every rule on the books, does that hurt him?
**7** And if you live an upright life, are you doing him a favor? Does he need anything out of your hand?
**8** Your wrongdoing only hurts ordinary people like you, and your good deeds only help fellow human beings!
**9** Sure, victims groan under brutal oppression and scream for relief from ruthless tyrants,
**10** yet nobody bothers to ask, 'Where is God, the one who created me and puts music in my heart during the darkest night,
**11** who gives us far more insight than wild animals and makes us smarter than the birds flying above?'
**12** People holler for help, and God stays silent because of the smug pride of evil men.
**13** God won't listen to hollow, insincere prayers, and the Almighty refuses to pay attention to them.
**14** So if you complain that you can't see him, remember your lawsuit is already sitting on his desk—wait for his ruling!
**15** Right now, just because God hasn't unleashed his anger yet or cracked down hard on reckless talk,
**16** Job keeps babbling nonsense—filling the air with clueless chatter."

## Crossings & Lexical Notes

- **v. 2 `צִדְקִי מֵאֵל` (*tsidqi me-El*):** Can mean either comparative "my righteousness is greater than God's" (as Elihu characterizes Job's complaint) or forensic "my righteousness before/from God."
- **v. 10 `זְמִרוֹת בַּלָּיְלָה` (*zemirot ba-laylah*):** "Songs in the night"—celebratory/reassuring praise or deliverance granted amid dark affliction (compare Ps 42:8; 77:6).
- **v. 15 `בַּפַּשׁ` (*ba-pash*):** Hapax legomenon of debated etymology—variously understood as "arrogance/rebellion" (from *pash* / *pasha*), "extremity/abundance," or "folly."
- **v. 16 `הֶבֶל יִפְצֶה־פִּיהוּ` (*hevel yiftseh-fihu*):** *Hevel* ("vapor/breath/vanity," echoing Job 7:16 and Ecclesiastes) highlights Elihu's charge that Job multiplies hollow words (`מִלִּין`) lacking knowledge.

## Supplemental Texts & Allusions

- **Psalm 42:8 & 77:6** (night-song motif): Echoes the image of God granting songs and comfort during the night of distress (v. 10).
- **Psalm 8:3–8 & 103:11** (celestial height & human dignity): Mirrors the contrast between the towering heavens (v. 5) and humanity's unique wisdom above animals and birds (v. 11).
- **Isaiah 1:15 & 59:2** (unanswered empty prayer): Parallels Elihu's claim that God turns away from hollow or arrogant cries (vv. 12–13).

## Reference Text (WEB Updated, Public Domain)

**1** Moreover Elihu answered,
**2** “Do you think this to be your right, or do you say, ‘My righteousness is more than God’s,’
**3** that you ask, ‘What advantage will it be to you? What profit will I have, more than if I had sinned?’
**4** I will answer you, and your companions with you.
**5** Look to the skies, and see. See the skies, which are higher than you.
**6** If you have sinned, what effect do you have against him? If your transgressions are multiplied, what do you do to him?
**7** If you are righteous, what do you give him? Or what does he receive from your hand?
**8** Your wickedness may hurt a man as you are, and your righteousness may profit a son of man.
**9** “By reason of the multitude of oppressions they cry out. They cry for help by reason of the arm of the mighty.
**10** But no one says, ‘Where is God my Maker, who gives songs in the night,
**11** who teaches us more than the animals of the earth, and makes us wiser than the birds of the sky?’
**12** There they cry, but no one answers, because of the pride of evil men.
**13** Surely God will not hear an empty cry, neither will the Almighty regard it.
**14** How much less when you say you don’t see him. The cause is before him, and you wait for him!
**15** But now, because he has not visited in his anger, neither does he greatly regard arrogance,
**16** therefore Job opens his mouth with empty talk, and he multiplies words without knowledge.”
