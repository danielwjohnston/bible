## Clear & Faithful

**1** Then Eliphaz the Temanite answered and said:
**2** "Can a mortal be of any benefit to God? At most, a person of insight benefits himself.
**3** Does the Almighty gain pleasure because you are righteous, or profit because you keep your conduct blameless?
**4** Is it out of respect for your piety that he brings charges against you and takes you to court?
**5** Surely your wickedness is enormous, and there is no limit to your sins!
**6** Without cause you took collateral from your own brothers and stripped the poor down to nothing.
**7** You refused water to the exhausted traveler and withheld bread from the starving.
**8** The strong man seized the land for himself, and the privileged settled comfortably inside it.
**9** You turned widows away empty-handed, and you shattered the strength of orphans.
**10** That is why snares surround you on all sides and sudden panic overtakes you,
**11** why darkness blinds your sight and surging floodwaters sweep over you.
**12** Does not God dwell in the highest heavens? Look at the farthest stars, how lofty they stand!
**13** Yet you say, 'What can God really know? Can he render judgment through thick storm clouds?
**14** Dense clouds curtain him off so he cannot see, as he paces along the vault of the sky.'
**15** Will you cling to the ancient path already trampled by evil people—
**16** who were swept away before their time, when a torrent washed away their foundations,
**17** those who kept telling God, 'Leave us alone!' and scoffed, 'What can the Almighty do to us?'
**18** Yet it was he who had filled their homes with good things—though the thinking of the wicked stays far from me!
**19** The righteous watch their downfall and celebrate, and the innocent laugh them to scorn:
**20** 'Look how our foes are wiped out, and fire has devoured all they left behind!'
**21** Come to terms with him now and find peace; through that, prosperity will return to you.
**22** Welcome instruction from his mouth, and store his words away in your heart.
**23** If you turn back to the Almighty, you will be restored; banish wrongdoing from your tent,
**24** toss your gold ore down into the dust and your Ophir gold among the river stones,
**25** and the Almighty himself will become your treasure and your finest silver!
**26** Then you will find sheer delight in the Almighty and lift your face with confidence toward God.
**27** You will pray to him and he will listen to you, and you will fulfill every vow you made.
**28** Whatever course you decide upon will stand firm, and light will brighten your road.
**29** When people are cast down and you call out, 'Lift them up!' he rescues the downcast;
**30** he will even rescue someone who is not innocent—delivered through the purity of your hands."

## Everyday English

**1** Eliphaz from Teman spoke up again:
**2** "Do you really think any human can do God a favor? Even a wise person only helps himself.
**3** Does the Almighty get anything out of your good behavior? Does he profit if you live a spotless life?
**4** Do you imagine God's dragging you into court because you were *too* faithful?
**5** Come on—your wrongdoing is huge, and your sins have piled up without end!
**6** You demanded unfair collateral from your neighbors for nothing and left poor folks without clothes on their backs.
**7** You wouldn't even give a glass of water to someone exhausted, and you held back food from the starving.
**8** You acted like a big shot who owned the whole territory, throwing your weight around as a VIP,
**9** kicking widows out empty-handed and crushing helpless orphans.
**10** No wonder traps are snapping all around you and sudden terror has you cornered!
**11** That's why darkness blinds you so you can't see, and a wall of floodwater swallows you up.
**12** God lives way up above the highest sky—just look at how high the stars sit!
**13** Yet you've been saying, 'What does God actually notice? Can he see through thick storm clouds to judge us?
**14** Heavy clouds hide him so he can't look down while he walks around the rim of heaven.'
**15** Are you really going to follow that old trail worn down by wicked people?
**16** They got wiped out early, their whole foundation washed clean away by a flash flood—
**17** the same crowd who told God, 'Stay out of our lives! What could the Almighty possibly do to us?'
**18** Never mind that God was the one who packed their homes with good things! Still, I want no part of how wicked people think.
**19** Honest folks watch their crash and celebrate, and innocent people laugh right back at them:
**20** 'Our enemies are finished, and fire burned up every last scrap they owned!'
**21** Make peace with God right now and settle things with him—that's how good things will come back to you.
**22** Listen to his teaching and lock his words deep inside your heart.
**23** If you come back to the Almighty, he'll rebuild your life. Just sweep corruption out of your home,
**24** throw your gold nuggets into the dirt and your fancy jewelry out among the creek rocks,
**25** and let the Almighty himself be your gold and your most precious silver!
**26** Then you'll genuinely enjoy the Almighty and look up at God without shame.
**27** When you pray, he'll actually listen, and you'll gladly keep your promises to him.
**28** Whatever you plan will work out, and bright light will shine ahead of your steps.
**29** When others get knocked down and you pray for them to be lifted up, God rescues the humble.
**30** He'll even save people who weren't completely innocent, rescuing them because your hands are clean."

## Crossings & Lexical Notes

- **v. 14 `חוּג שָׁמַיִם` (*chug shamayim*):** "Vault/horizon-circle of the heavens" (*chug* also appears in Isa 40:22 for the dome/circle of the earth and Prov 8:27). Eliphaz accuses Job of claiming God's celestial distance blinds him to earthly injustice—ironically reversing Job's complaint in `21:14` (where the *wicked* say "Leave us alone!").
- **v. 21 `הַסְכֶּן־נָא` (*hasken-na*):** From *sakhan* ("be familiar/acquainted/at peace with" or "be profitable," matching v. 2). Eliphaz urges Job to reconcile himself with God.
- **v. 22 `תּוֹרָה` (*torah*):** "Instruction/teaching" from God's mouth—the sole occurrence of *torah* in the book of Job.
- **v. 24–25 `בָּצֶר` (*batser*):** A rare poetic term for raw gold ore or precious treasure, repeated in v. 25 to create a wordplay: discard earthly *batser* in the dust so the Almighty himself becomes your *betser* ("gold/stronghold").
- **v. 30 `אִי־נָקִי` (*i-naqi*):** A rare negative prefix *i-* ("not-innocent," cf. 1 Sam 4:21 *Ichabod*). Eliphaz promises Job that if he repents, he will become an intercessor whose clean hands win deliverance even for the guilty—a striking foreshadowing of `42:7–9`, though Job will intercede for Eliphaz himself!

## Supplemental Texts & Allusions

- **Isaiah 40:22; Proverbs 8:27** (shared cosmological term *chug*): Uses the same noun for the vault or circular horizon of heaven and the deep that appears in Job 22:14.
- **Psalm 19:10; 119:72** (God's word prized above gold): Parallels Eliphaz's call in vv. 22–25 to treasure God's instruction in the heart above fine gold of Ophir and pure silver.
- **Job 42:8–9** (dramatic reversal): Eliphaz's closing promise in v. 30—that the guilty will be rescued through Job's clean hands—comes true ironically at the end of the book when God commands Eliphaz and his friends to seek Job's prayer for their own forgiveness.

**1** Then Eliphaz the Temanite answered,
**2** “Can a man be profitable to God? Surely he who is wise is profitable to himself.
**3** Is it any pleasure to the Almighty that you are righteous? Or does it benefit him that you make your ways perfect?
**4** Is it for your piety that he reproves you, that he enters with you into judgment?
**5** Isn’t your wickedness great? Neither is there any end to your iniquities.
**6** For you have taken pledges from your brother for nothing, and stripped the naked of their clothing.
**7** You haven’t given water to the weary to drink, and you have withheld bread from the hungry.
**8** But as for the mighty man, he had the earth. The honorable man, he lived in it.
**9** You have sent widows away empty, and the arms of the fatherless have been broken.
**10** Therefore snares are around you. Sudden fear troubles you,
**11** or darkness, so that you can not see, and floods of waters cover you.
**12** “Isn’t God in the heights of heaven? See the height of the stars, how high they are!
**13** You say, ‘What does God know? Can he judge through the thick darkness?
**14** Thick clouds are a covering to him, so that he doesn’t see. He walks on the vault of the sky.’
**15** Will you keep the old way, which wicked men have trodden,
**16** who were snatched away before their time, whose foundation was poured out as a stream,
**17** who said to God, ‘Depart from us!’ and, ‘What can the Almighty do for us?’
**18** Yet he filled their houses with good things, but the counsel of the wicked is far from me.
**19** The righteous see it, and are glad. The innocent ridicule them,
**20** saying, ‘Surely those who rose up against us are cut off. The fire has consumed their remnant.’
**21** “Acquaint yourself with him now, and be at peace. By it, good will come to you.
**22** Please receive instruction from his mouth, and lay up his words in your heart.
**23** If you return to the Almighty, you will be built up, if you put away unrighteousness far from your tents.
**24** Lay your treasure in the dust, the gold of Ophir among the stones of the brooks.
**25** The Almighty will be your treasure, and precious silver to you.
**26** For then you will delight yourself in the Almighty, and will lift up your face to God.
**27** You will make your prayer to him, and he will hear you. You will pay your vows.
**28** You will also decree a thing, and it will be established to you. Light will shine on your ways.
**29** When they cast down, you will say, ‘be lifted up.’ He will save the humble person.
**30** He will even deliver him who is not innocent. Yes, he will be delivered through the cleanness of your hands.”
