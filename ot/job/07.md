## Clear & Faithful

**1** "Does not hard service weigh upon mortals on earth, and are not their days like a hired laborer's term?
**2** Like a slave gasping for evening shade, or a day-worker longing for his pay,
**3** so have I been allotted months of futility, and nights of misery have been counted out to me.
**4** As soon as I lie down, I ask, 'When will I get up?' Yet the night stretches endlessly, and I toss full of restlessness until dawn.
**5** My flesh wears worms and crusts of dirt; my skin scabs over only to break open and seep again.
**6** My days fly faster than a weaver's shuttle and snap off without any thread of hope.
**7** Remember that my life is a mere breath; my eyes will never turn back to look on good again.
**8** The eye that watches me will see me no more; your eyes will search for me, and I will be gone.
**9** Just as a cloud dissolves and drifts off, whoever descends to Sheol never climbs back up;
**10** he returns to his house no more, and his own dwelling forgets who he was.
**11** Therefore I refuse to muzzle my mouth; I will speak out in the anguish of my spirit, and voice my lament in the bitterness of my soul.
**12** Am I the primeval Sea, or the Sea Monster, that you post a sentry over me?
**13** Whenever I think, 'My bed will soothe me, my couch will ease my complaint,'
**14** you ambush me with nightmares and frighten me out of my wits with night visions,
**15** until my soul would rather choose strangulation—death over this skeletal frame!
**16** I am sick of it; I will not live forever. Leave me alone, for my days are a fleeting wisp of air!
**17** What is frail humanity that you magnify him, and fix your obsessive attention upon him—
**18** inspecting him every morning, probing him every single moment?
**19** How long until you glance away from me, until you grant me space just to swallow my own spit?
**20** Suppose I have sinned—what harm can I cause you, O Watcher of humankind? Why have you made me your target practice, until I have turned into a burden to myself?
**21** Why not simply pardon my offense and sweep away my guilt? For soon I will lie down in the dust; you will look for me at dawn, and I will no longer exist."

## Everyday English

**1** "Isn't human life on earth just grueling forced labor—like working a miserable day-laborer's shift?
**2** I'm like an exhausted worker panting for sunset shade, or a hired hand waiting desperately for payday.
**3** That's how I've been stuck with empty month after empty month, and long, agonizing nights assigned to me.
**4** The minute I lie down, I'm counting down: 'When can I get up?' The night drags on forever, and I'm worn out from tossing and turning until sunrise.
**5** My skin is covered with maggots and caked with dirt; it scabs over, then cracks open and oozes all over again.
**6** My days race past faster than a loom shuttle—and then the thread runs out with zero hope left.
**7** God, remember my life is just one puff of wind! These eyes won't ever get to see good days again.
**8** Anyone looking at me now won't see me much longer; you'll glance my way, and I'll already be gone.
**9** Like a cloud that breaks apart and vanishes, nobody who goes down into the grave ever climbs back out.
**10** They never walk back through their front door, and their own neighborhood forgets they were ever there.
**11** So no—I won't keep my mouth shut! My spirit is crushed, my heart is bitter, and I'm going to say my piece!
**12** Wait—am I the roaring Ocean or some giant sea monster that you have to keep me under armed guard?
**13** Every time I tell myself, 'Maybe lying down will give me a break; maybe my bed will calm my pain,'
**14** you hit me with terrifying dreams and panic me with horrifying visions!
**15** I'd honestly rather choke to death than keep living inside this wrecked body.
**16** I hate my life—I don't want to hang on forever! Back off and leave me alone; my days are nothing but smoke!
**17** Why are human beings such a big deal to you that you obsess over us,
**18** checking up on us every single morning and testing us every second of the day?
**19** Won't you look away from me for two seconds—long enough for me just to catch my breath and swallow?
**20** Even if I did sin, what could I possibly do to hurt you, you Spy on humanity? Why use me as your punching bag until I can't even stand carrying myself around?
**21** Why not just forgive my mistake and wipe my record clean? Because any day now I'll be asleep in the dirt—and when you come looking for me, I won't be here."

## Crossings & Lexical Notes

- **v. 1 `על` / `עֲלֵי` (*aley*):** Ketiv uses standard prose `על`, while Qere adopts the archaic poetic form `עֲלֵי` ("upon"), fitting the transition into Job's elevated verse. Note also *tsava* ("forced military service / conscripted toil").
- **v. 5 `וגיש` / `וְגוּשׁ` (*ve-gush*):** Ketiv spells `וגיש`, while Qere points `וְגוּשׁ` ("and clod/crust"), describing hardened scabs of dried earth and pus across Job's skin.
- **v. 6 `אֶרֶג` (*ereg*):** "A weaver's loom/shuttle," whose swift back-and-forth throw symbolizes both the speed of mortality and life's thread running out (`בְּאֶפֶס תִּקְוָה`, where *tiqvah* means both "hope" and "cord/thread").
- **v. 12 `הֲיָם־אָנִי אִם־תַּנִּין` (*ha-Yam-ani im-Tannin*):** "Am I Sea (*Yam*) or Sea-Dragon (*Tannin*)?"—invoking ancient Near Eastern chaos waters and monsters (cf. 3:8; Ps 74:13) that God must keep penned behind cosmic guard (*mishmar*).
- **v. 16 `הֶבֶל` (*hevel*):** "Vapor, breath, fleeting mist"—the signature word of Ecclesiastes (there often "vanity/absurdity"), here expressing life's evanescence.
- **v. 20 `נֹצֵר הָאָדָם` (*notser ha-adam*):** Usually a title of caring protection ("Keeper/Guardian of humanity"), twisted here by Job in bitter irony to mean "Surveillant / Stalker of humanity."

## Supplemental Texts & Allusions

- **Psalm 8:4–5 (MT 8:5–6)** (ironic inversion): Psalm 8 marvels *"What is mortal man (*enosh*) that you remember him?"* as a hymn of royal dignity; Job 7:17–18 parodies the exact wording to complain of suffocating, relentless divine surveillance.
- **Psalm 144:4 & Ecclesiastes 1:2** (*hevel* mortality): Echo the comparison of human days to a fleeting breath (*hevel*) and passing shadow.
- **Isaiah 51:9–10 & Psalm 74:13** (Sea and Tannin): Depict the LORD bounding and conquering primordial *Yam* and *Tannin*, illuminating Job's protest in v. 12 against being guarded like a cosmic threat.

**1** “Isn’t a man forced to labor on earth? Aren’t his days like the days of a hired hand?
**2** As a servant who earnestly desires the shadow, as a hireling who looks for his wages,
**3** so I am made to possess months of misery, wearisome nights are appointed to me.
**4** When I lie down, I say, ‘When will I arise, and the night be gone?’ I toss and turn until the dawning of the day.
**5** My flesh is clothed with worms and clods of dust. My skin closes up, and breaks out afresh.
**6** My days are swifter than a weaver’s shuttle, and are spent without hope.
**7** Oh remember that my life is a breath. My eye will no more see good.
**8** The eye of him who sees me will see me no more. Your eyes will be on me, but I will not be.
**9** As the cloud is consumed and vanishes away, so he who goes down to Sheol will come up no more.
**10** He will return no more to his house, neither will his place know him any more.
**11** “Therefore I will not keep silent. I will speak in the anguish of my spirit. I will complain in the bitterness of my soul.
**12** Am I a sea, or a sea monster, that you put a guard over me?
**13** When I say, ‘My bed will comfort me. My couch will ease my complaint,’
**14** then you scare me with dreams and terrify me through visions,
**15** so that my soul chooses strangling, death rather than my bones.
**16** I loathe my life. I don’t want to live forever. Leave me alone, for my days are but a breath.
**17** What is man, that you should magnify him, that you should set your mind on him,
**18** that you should visit him every morning, and test him every moment?
**19** How long will you not look away from me, nor leave me alone until I swallow down my spittle?
**20** If I have sinned, what do I do to you, you watcher of men? Why have you set me as a mark for you, so that I am a burden to myself?
**21** Why do you not pardon my disobedience, and take away my iniquity? For now will I lie down in the dust. You will seek me diligently, but I will not be.”
