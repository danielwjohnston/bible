## Clear & Faithful

**1** Then Job answered and said:
**2** "Listen carefully to what I say; let at least that much be your consolation for me.
**3** Bear with me while I speak, and once I have spoken, go ahead and mock!
**4** Is my complaint directed merely against a human being? Why then should my spirit not grow impatient?
**5** Look straight at me and be stunned; clamp your hand over your mouth!
**6** Whenever I stop to think of it, I am shaken, and trembling seizes my body.
**7** Why do the wicked go on living, grow old, and even multiply in power?
**8** Their descendants stand firmly established before them, and their offspring thrive right before their eyes.
**9** Their homes stay peaceful and free from dread, and no rod from God ever falls upon them.
**10** Their bull breeds without fail; their cow calves smoothly and never miscarries.
**11** They turn their little ones loose like a flock, and their children skip and dance.
**12** They sing to the beat of tambourine and harp, and celebrate to the music of the flute.
**13** They live out their days in comfort and descend in a single instant into Sheol.
**14** Yet these are the very people who tell God, 'Leave us alone! We have no interest in knowing your ways.
**15** Who is the Almighty that we should serve him? What profit do we get from praying to him?'
**16** Wait—their prosperity does not rest in their own control! Still, the mindset of the wicked stays far from me.
**17** How often does the lamp of the wicked actually go out? How often does disaster strike them, or God deal out agony in his anger?
**18** How often are they like straw swept before the wind, or chaff snatched away by a storm?
**19** You claim, 'God saves up a sinner's punishment for his children.' Let him repay the sinner himself so he feels it!
**20** Let his own eyes witness his downfall; let him drink deeply from the wrath of the Almighty!
**21** What does he care what happens to his household after he is gone, once the tally of his months is cut short?
**22** Can anyone presume to teach knowledge to God, who sits in judgment over the highest beings?
**23** One person dies at the peak of vitality, completely serene and undisturbed—
**24** his vessels brimming with rich milk, the marrow inside his bones fresh and moist.
**25** Another dies with a bitter soul, having never once tasted good fortune.
**26** Yet side by side they lie down in the dust, and worms blanket them both.
**27** Look, I know what you are thinking—I see the twisted arguments you aim at me—
**28** when you ask, 'Where is the mansion of the tyrant now? Where is the tent where the wicked used to live?'
**29** Have you never questioned travelers along the road, or paid attention to the evidence they bring back—
**30** that the wicked person is spared on the day of disaster and escorted safely past the day of fury?
**31** Who dares confront him to his face over his conduct? Who ever pays him back for what he has done?
**32** He is carried with honor to the graveyard, and guards keep watch over his tomb.
**33** Even the soil of the valley lies gently over him; crowds without number march ahead of him, and the whole world follows in his train.
**34** How then can you try to comfort me with empty breath? All that is left of your replies is sheer betrayal!"

## Everyday English

**1** Job answered them:
**2** "Actually listen to what I'm saying—if you want to comfort me, start with that!
**3** Let me have my turn to speak, and once I'm finished, you can laugh all you want.
**4** Look, I'm not complaining about some ordinary human—so why shouldn't I lose patience?
**5** Take one good look at me and be shocked; cover your mouths in silence!
**6** Just thinking about this makes me dizzy, and shivers run right through my skin.
**7** Why do crooked people keep living long lives, growing old and piling up wealth and clout?
**8** They get to watch their kids settle down securely and see their grandkids grow up right in front of them.
**9** Their homes are peaceful and safe from fear; God never brings the rod down on their backs.
**10** Their bulls breed cleanly without failing; their cows calve smoothly without losing a calf.
**11** Their little kids run around outdoors like playful lambs, dancing and skipping everywhere.
**12** They sing along to drums and guitars and party to the sound of flutes.
**13** They enjoy the good life right up to the end, then slip quietly into the grave in a split second.
**14** And these are the very folks who tell God, 'Back off! We don't care to learn your ways!
**15** Why should we bother serving the Almighty? What's in it for us if we pray to him?'
**16** Sure, they aren't really in charge of their own success—and I want nothing to do with their mindset—
**17** but come on: how often does a wicked person's lamp actually go dark? How often does ruin hit them, or God make them suffer in his anger?
**18** How often do they blow away like straw in a breeze or chaff in a tornado?
**19** Maybe you say, 'Well, God punishes their children for their sins.' Let God pay *them* back directly so they actually learn from it!
**20** Let them watch their own ruin happen with their own eyes; make them drink the Almighty's anger themselves!
**21** Once their time is up and they're dead, what do they care what happens to their family later?
**22** Besides, who are you to teach God how to run things—he judges even the highest powers in heaven!
**23** One person dies healthy and strong, totally comfortable and worry-free,
**24** well-fed and thriving with strength in his bones.
**25** Another dies broken and miserable, never having enjoyed a single good thing.
**26** Both of them end up buried side by side in the dirt, covered by the same worms.
**27** I know what you're thinking—I can tell you're twisting things just to attack me!
**28** You ask, 'Where's that rich bully's mansion now? Where are the fancy tents those wicked people lived in?'
**29** Haven't you ever talked to people who travel widely and heard their stories?
**30** They'll tell you that wicked people get protected when disaster hits and skate right past the day of judgment!
**31** Nobody dares call them out to their face or pay them back for the wrongs they've done.
**32** When they die, they get a grand funeral procession and a guard watching over their fancy tomb.
**33** Even the dirt in the valley lies softly on them, with endless crowds marching in front and behind their casket.
**34** So stop offering me empty nonsense as comfort! Every answer you've given me is nothing but a lie."

## Crossings & Lexical Notes

- **v. 13 `יְכַלּוּ` (*yekhallu*, Qere) vs. `יבלו` (Ketiv):** Qere reads *yekhallu* (from *kalah*, "they finish/spend" their days in prosperity), whereas Ketiv reads *yevallu* (from *balah*, "they wear out/enjoy to the full," cf. Isa 65:22).
- **v. 16 `לֹא בְיָדָם טוּבָם` (*lo veyadam tuvam*):** Job pauses to acknowledge that wicked prosperity is ultimately not self-generated (or, read as a rhetorical question echoing the friends: "Is not their prosperity in their own hand?"), while emphatically distancing himself from their godless philosophy.
- **v. 20 `עֵינָיו` (*einav*, Qere) vs. `עינו` (Ketiv):** Qere supplies the plural noun with 3ms suffix ("his eyes") to match the plural verb `יִרְאוּ` (*yir'u*, "let them see"), correcting the defective Ketiv spelling `עינו` ("his eye").
- **v. 20 `כִּידוֹ` (*kido*):** A rare hapax legomenon meaning "his calamity/destruction" (from an Arabic/Semitic root for misfortune or snare).
- **v. 34 `הָבֶל` (*hevel*) & `מָעַל` (*ma'al*):** *Hevel* ("vapor, breath, emptiness") and *ma'al* ("breach of faith, treachery"): Job ends Cycle 2 by indicting his friends' theology not merely as mistaken, but as unfaithful betrayal.

## Supplemental Texts & Allusions

- **Psalm 73:3–12** (shared problem of wicked prosperity): Parallels Job's observation in vv. 7–15 that the arrogant live sleek, pain-free lives untouched by common trouble and scoff at God.
- **Job 18:5–6; 20:29** (direct rebuttal of friends): In vv. 17 and 28–30, Job quotes Bildad's "lamp of the wicked" (18:5–6) and Zophar's "wicked person's tent" rhetorically in order to refute their tidy retribution formula with real-world observation.
- **Ecclesiastes 2:14–16; 9:2–3** (shared mortality equalizer): Reflects the same stark realism as Job 21:23–26—whether one dies in comfort or in bitterness, both lie down alike in the dust.
