## Clear & Faithful

**1** Then Job took up his discourse once more and said:
**2** "As surely as God lives—the one who has denied me justice—and the Almighty, who has filled my soul with bitterness,
**3** for as long as breath remains inside me and the spirit of God breathes in my nostrils,
**4** my lips will never speak what is wrong, nor will my tongue mutter deceit!
**5** Far be it from me to admit that you are right; until my dying breath, I will never surrender my integrity.
**6** I grip my righteousness tightly and refuse to let go; my conscience will not accuse me for a single day of my life.
**7** May my enemy meet the fate of the wicked, and anyone who rises against me share the lot of the unjust!
**8** For what hope does the godless person hold when he is cut off, when God pulls out his life?
**9** Will God listen to his desperate cry when trouble closes in around him?
**10** Will he ever take delight in the Almighty, or call upon God in every season?
**11** Let me instruct you concerning the hand of God; what lies with the Almighty I will not hold back.
**12** Look—all of you have witnessed this with your own eyes! Why then do you keep churning out empty hot air?
**13** Here is the portion God assigns to the wicked person, the inheritance tyrants receive from the Almighty:
**14** Even if his children multiply, they are marked for the sword, and his descendants never get enough bread to eat.
**15** Plague buries whoever survives him, and his widows do not even weep.
**16** Though he stacks up silver like dust and piles up fine robes like mounds of clay,
**17** he piles them up—only for the righteous to wear them, and the innocent to divide his silver!
**18** He builds his house as fragile as a moth's cocoon, flimsy as a watchman's shelter in a field.
**19** He goes to bed rich, yet never wakes to do so again; he opens his eyes, and everything is gone!
**20** Terrors overtake him like a flash flood; a night storm sweeps him away.
**21** The scorching east wind lifts him and carries him off, hurling him straight out of his home.
**22** It pelts him relentlessly without mercy as he scrambles to flee from its grip.
**23** It claps its hands in derision at him and whistles him out of his place."

## Everyday English

**1** Since nobody else spoke, Job picked up his argument again:
**2** "I swear by the living God—even though he's blocked my right to a fair trial—and by the Almighty, who has made my life bitter:
**3** as long as I still have breath in my lungs and God's air in my nostrils,
**4** I won't lie with my lips, and my tongue won't repeat a single dishonest word!
**5** I'll never agree that you're right—no way! Right up to the moment I die, I won't give up my innocence.
**6** I'm holding tight to my integrity and I'm not letting go; my conscience doesn't bother me about any day I've lived.
**7** Let my enemies get what wicked people deserve, and let anyone fighting me end up like a crook!
**8** After all, what future does a fraud have when his time's up and God yanks his life away?
**9** Will God answer his screams when disaster hits?
**10** Did he ever enjoy spending time with the Almighty, or pray to God day in and day out?
**11** Let me give you a lesson about how God actually works—I won't hide anything about the Almighty's ways.
**12** You've all seen this yourselves! So why do you keep repeating empty clichés?
**13** You claim, 'Here's what God hands out to evil people—the payday brutal folks get from the Almighty:
**14** Maybe they have tons of kids, yet the sword cuts them down and their family starves for food.
**15** Deadly disease buries the few survivors, and even their widows won't shed a tear.
**16** Maybe they pile up silver like dirt and stack fancy clothes like mud bricks—
**17** they stock it all up, and honest people end up wearing the clothes and splitting the cash!
**18** They build mansions as flimsy as a moth's cocoon or a temporary shack out in a vineyard.
**19** They go to sleep rich for the very last time; they blink open their eyes, and it's all vanished!
**20** Nightmares hit them like a wall of floodwater; a gale blows them away after dark.
**21** A fierce desert wind picks them up and sweeps them right off their property.
**22** It batters them without pity while they run for their lives from its blast.
**23** Even the wind claps its hands to mock them and hisses them out of town!'"

## Crossings & Lexical Notes

- **v. 1 `שְׂאֵת מְשָׁלוֹ` (*se'et meshalo*):** *Mashal* spans proverb, parable, and elevated poetic discourse. Its reintroduction (`וַיֹּסֶף`, "and he continued") signals Zophar's silence in Cycle 3—Job holds the floor alone through ch. 31.
- **v. 2 `חַי־אֵל` (*chai-El*):** Standard Hebrew oath formula ("As God lives..."). Strikingly, Job swears *by* the very God who has withheld his legal justice (`הֵסִיר מִשְׁפָּטִי`), binding himself in vv. 3–6 to absolute truthfulness.
- **v. 12 `הֶבֶל תֶּהְבָּלוּ` (*hevel tehbalu*):** Cognate accusative verb+noun from *hevel* ("vapor/emptiness"): literally "why do you vaporize vapor / spout empty nonsense?"
- **v. 15 `שְׂרִידָיו` (*seridav*, Qere) vs. `שרידו` (Ketiv):** Qere reads plural "his survivors" to match the plural verb `יִקָּבֵרוּ` ("they will be buried"), whereas Ketiv preserves the singular spelling `שרידו`. Note also `בַּמָּוֶת` ("by death/pestilence," personified lethal plague).
- **v. 13–23 `חֵלֶק־אָדָם רָשָׁע` (*cheleq-adam rasha*):** Because vv. 13–23 closely echo Zophar's wording from `20:29` and Bildad's motifs from ch. 18, interpreters debate whether Job is ironically reciting the friends' own doctrine back at them (cf. v. 12) or conceding that God *sometimes* judges the wicked, even though Job himself is innocent.

## Supplemental Texts & Allusions

- **Genesis 2:7** (*neshamah* / breath of God): Job's oath in v. 3 (*"my breath is within me and the spirit of God in my nostrils"*) draws directly on the creation account of God breathing life into humanity's nostrils.
- **Job 20:29** (Cycle 2 verbatim echo): Job's opening line in v. 13 repeats Zophar's summary verdict almost word for word, turning the friends' own rhetoric into a foil for his defense.
- **Isaiah 1:8; Matthew 7:26–27** (fragile shelter imagery): Isaiah's booth in a vineyard (*sukkah*) and Jesus's house built without enduring foundation resonate with the fragile, temporary dwellings of Job 27:18.

## Reference Text (WEB Updated, Public Domain)

**1** Job again took up his parable, and said,
**2** “As God lives, who has taken away my right, the Almighty, who has made my soul bitter
**3** (for the length of my life is still in me, and the spirit of God is in my nostrils);
**4** surely my lips will not speak unrighteousness, neither will my tongue utter deceit.
**5** Far be it from me that I should justify you. Until I die I will not put away my integrity from me.
**6** I hold fast to my righteousness, and will not let it go. My heart will not reproach me so long as I live.
**7** “Let my enemy be as the wicked. Let him who rises up against me be as the unrighteous.
**8** For what is the hope of the godless, when he is cut off, when God takes away his life?
**9** Will God hear his cry when trouble comes on him?
**10** Will he delight himself in the Almighty, and call on God at all times?
**11** I will teach you about the hand of God. I will not conceal that which is with the Almighty.
**12** Behold, all of you have seen it yourselves; why then have you become altogether vain?
**13** “This is the portion of a wicked man with God, the heritage of oppressors, which they receive from the Almighty.
**14** If his children are multiplied, it is for the sword. His offspring will not be satisfied with bread.
**15** Those who remain of him will be buried in death. His widows will make no lamentation.
**16** Though he heap up silver as the dust, and prepare clothing as the clay;
**17** he may prepare it, but the just will put it on, and the innocent will divide the silver.
**18** He builds his house as the moth, as a booth which the watchman makes.
**19** He lies down rich, but he will not do so again. He opens his eyes, and he is not.
**20** Terrors overtake him like waters. A storm steals him away in the night.
**21** The east wind carries him away, and he departs. It sweeps him out of his place.
**22** For it hurls at him, and does not spare, as he flees away from his hand.
**23** Men will clap their hands at him, and will hiss him out of his place.
