## Clear & Faithful

**1** Then Bildad the Shuhite answered and said:
**2** "Sovereign rule and awesome terror belong to him; he establishes harmony across his celestial heights.
**3** Can anyone tally the ranks of his heavenly armies? Upon whom does his radiance fail to rise?
**4** How then can a frail mortal stand vindicated before God, or anyone born to a mother be counted spotless?
**5** Consider: in his presence not even the moon beams brightly, nor do the stars appear unblemished in his sight—
**6** far less a mortal creature who is merely a maggot, a human offspring who is nothing more than a worm!"

---

## Everyday English

**1** Bildad from Shuah gave this brief reply:
**2** "God holds supreme authority and inspires sheer awe; he keeps order way up in the highest heavens.
**3** Nobody can count his angelic troops, and his sunlight reaches every single person alive.
**4** So how could any ordinary human ever win an argument with God, or claim to be innocent after being born into this world?
**5** Think about it: compared to God, the moon itself looks dim, and even the stars aren't clean enough for his eyes.
**6** What chance does a tiny person have—someone fragile as a grub or a little earthworm?"

## Crossings & Lexical Notes

- **v. 2 `הַמְשֵׁל וָפַחַד` (*hamshel vaphachad*):** *Hamshel* is an infinitive-derived noun from *mashal* ("rule/dominion") paired with *pachad* ("dread/awe"); Bildad compresses all theology into divine cosmic sovereignty.
- **v. 4 `אֱנוֹשׁ` (*enosh*) & `יְלוּד אִשָּׁה` (*yelud ishah*):** Echoes Eliphaz's formula (`4:17`, `15:14`) verbatim—"frail mortal" and "one born of woman"—to assert human moral impossibility before God.
- **v. 5 `יַאֲהִיל` (*ya'ahil*):** From *ahal* ("be bright/shine," or "pitch a tent"—here "even the moon does not shine brightly" when measured against divine glory).
- **v. 6 `רִמָּה` (*rimmah*) & `תּוֹלֵעָה` (*tole'ah*):** Parallel terms for carrion/decay worms, underscoring creaturely fragility and insignificance rather than answering Job's moral complaint.

## Supplemental Texts & Allusions

- **Job 4:17–19; 15:14–16** (Cycle 1–3 structural closure): Bildad's six-verse speech condenses Eliphaz's earlier refrain on human impurity before God, signaling that the friends have run out of fresh arguments.
- **Psalm 22:6** (Hebrew 22:7, shared worm imagery): The lamenting psalmist calls himself "a worm (*tola'at*) and not a human," using the same creaturely contrast Bildad turns polemically against Job in v. 6.
- **Psalm 147:4; Isaiah 40:26** (numbering the heavenly host): Celebrates God's unrivaled command over the countless stars and celestial armies, resonating with Bildad's rhetorical question in v. 3.

## Reference Text (WEB Updated, Public Domain)

**1** Then Bildad the Shuhite answered,
**2** “Dominion and fear are with him. He makes peace in his high places.
**3** Can his armies be counted? On whom does his light not arise?
**4** How then can man be just with God? Or how can he who is born of a woman be clean?
**5** Behold, even the moon has no brightness, and the stars are not pure in his sight;
**6** How much less man, who is a worm, and the son of man, who is a worm!”
