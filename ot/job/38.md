## Clear & Faithful

**1** At last the LORD spoke to Job from within the tempest, saying:
**2** "Who is this that obscures counsel with words devoid of knowledge?
**3** Brace your loins like a warrior; I will question you, and you shall inform me!
**4** Where were you when I laid the earth's foundation? Tell me, if you possess understanding!
**5** Who determined its measurements—surely you know!—or who stretched the measuring line across it?
**6** Upon what were its pedestals sunk, or who laid its cornerstone,
**7** while the stars of dawn raised a song together, and every one of the sons of God shouted triumphantly?
**8** Or who enclosed the sea behind double doors when it burst forth out of the womb,
**9** when I wrapped clouds around it as clothing and thick darkness as its swaddling band,
**10** when I prescribed my boundary upon it and set bars and doors in place,
**11** and declared, 'This far you shall come and no farther, and here your proud waves shall halt'?
**12** Have you ever in your lifetime commanded the morning or assigned the dawn its appointed place,
**13** taking hold of the earth by its furthest corners so that wrongdoers are shaken loose from it?
**14** The earth takes shape like clay beneath a seal, and its features stand out like folds of a garment;
**15** light is withheld from the wicked, and the uplifted arm is shattered.
**16** Have you journeyed to the springs of the sea, or walked through the recesses of the deep?
**17** Have the gates of death been uncovered to you, or have you seen the portals of deep shadow?
**18** Have you surveyed the vast expanses of the earth? Declare it, if you know it all!
**19** Which is the road to the dwelling place of light, and as for darkness—where is its home,
**20** so that you might escort it to its territory and know the paths back to its house?
**21** Surely you know, for you were already born back then, and the count of your days is enormous!
**22** Have you ever stepped inside the vaults where snow is kept, or looked upon the chambers storing hail,
**23** which I have reserved for a time of distress, for the day of battle and war?
**24** Along which way is light distributed, or the east wind dispersed across the earth?
**25** Who carved a channel for the torrential downpour and a path for the thunderbolt,
**26** to bring rain upon an uninhabited land, on a desert where no mortal lives,
**27** to drench the parched wasteland and make the tender green grass sprout?
**28** Can the rain claim a father, or who brought forth the beads of morning dew?
**29** From whose womb came the ice, and who gave birth to the frost of the skies,
**30** when water hardens like stone and the surface of the deep freezes solid?
**31** Can you fasten the chains of the Pleiades, or untie the cords of Orion?
**32** Do you bring forth the star-clusters at their appointed hour, or escort the Great Bear and her young across the sky?
**33** Do you know the ordinances of the heavens, or can you establish their rule over the earth?
**34** Can you lift your voice to the clouds so that a flood of waters covers you?
**35** Can you dispatch lightning bolts so that they go forth and report to you, 'Here we are'?
**36** Who placed wisdom in the inward parts, or gave discernment to the heart?
**37** Who has the wisdom to count the clouds, or who can tip over the waterskins of heaven
**38** when the dust fuses into a solid lump and the clods cling fast together?
**39** Do you hunt prey for the lioness and satisfy the hunger of young lions
**40** while they huddle low inside their lairs and wait hidden in the brush for an ambush?
**41** Who provides food for the raven when its young cry out to God and wander about with nothing to eat?"

## Everyday English

**1** Then the LORD spoke straight out of a roaring storm and answered Job:
**2** "Who is this muddying up my design with uninformed talk?
**3** Tighten your belt and stand up like a fighter—I'm going to ask the questions now, and you give me the answers!
**4** Where were you when I built the earth's foundation? Speak up if you're so insightful!
**5** Who mapped out its dimensions—come on, you must know!—or stretched a builder's tape across it?
**6** What are its pillars anchored into, and who dropped the cornerstone into place
**7** while the morning stars sang in chorus and all the heavenly beings cheered with joy?
**8** Who slammed shut the sea's double doors when it came gushing out of the cosmic womb,
**9** when I dressed it in storm clouds for clothes and wrapped it in dark mist like a newborn blanket,
**10** when I drew a hard boundary around the ocean and bolted its gates shut,
**11** telling it, 'You can come this far and not one inch further—this is where your towering waves stop'?
**12** Have you ever once in your life told morning when to start, or shown sunrise where to report for duty
**13** so daylight grabs the corners of the earth and shakes criminals out of hiding?
**14** At dawn, the landscape pops into focus like clay stamped by a signet ring, standing out in color like pleats on a robe;
**15** wicked people lose the cover of darkness they rely on, and violent raised fists get stopped cold.
**16** Have you ever explored the underground springs feeding the ocean, or strolled across the bottom of the abyss?
**17** Have death's gates ever swung open for you? Have you toured the pitch-black doors of the underworld?
**18** Can you even wrap your head around how wide the earth is? Tell me if you know the whole layout!
**19** Which highway leads to where light lives, and where is darkness's home address—
**20** could you escort either one back to its border or find the trail to its front door?
**21** Of course you know—after all, you were already alive back then, weren't you? You've lived for ages!
**22** Have you ever stepped inside the snow warehouses or inspected the vaults where hail is stored,
**23** stockpiled for times of crisis and days of battle?
**24** Which path does sunlight split along, or where does the scorching east wind scatter across the globe?
**25** Who dug a canal through the sky for heavy rainstorms and cleared a lane for thunderbolts,
**26** sending rain down onto empty wasteland—a desert where not a single human lives—
**27** soaking barren badlands until fresh green grass shoots up?
**28** Does rain have a father? Who gave birth to tiny drops of morning dew?
**29** Out of whose womb did ice emerge, and who delivered the frost falling from the sky,
**30** freezing lakes as hard as granite and locking the surface of the deep sea in ice?
**31** Can you tie the cluster of the Pleiades into a knot, or loosen the belt around Orion?
**32** Can you march the seasonal constellations out on schedule, or guide the Great Bear and her cubs across the night sky?
**33** Do you understand the laws governing the cosmos, or set up how the heavens influence the earth below?
**34** Can you shout up at the storm clouds and make a downpour drench you on demand?
**35** Can you launch bolts of lightning so they salute you and say, 'Reporting for duty, sir!'?
**36** Who put intuition inside living creatures, or gave insight to the heart and mind?
**37** Who is smart enough to tally every cloud, or tilt over the rain-jars of the sky
**38** until dry dust cakes into mud and dirt clods stick together?
**39** Do you go hunting to feed the lioness and fill the bellies of hungry lion cubs
**40** while they crouch low in their caves and hide in the brush waiting to pounce?
**41** Who puts dinner on the table for the raven when its chicks squawk to God and stumble around starving?"

## Crossings & Lexical Notes

- **v. 1 `מִן הַסְּעָרָה` (*min ha-se'arah*, Qere) vs. `מנ הסערה` (Ketiv):** The Ketiv omits the final *nun* vowel/full separation (`מנ`), while Qere reads standard `מִן הַסְּעָרָה` ("out of the whirlwind/tempest"), fulfilling Elihu's storm-theophany buildup in ch. 37.
- **v. 7 `בְּנֵי אֱלֹהִים` (*beney Elohim*):** "Sons of God"—heavenly council beings (compare Job 1:6; 2:1) rejoicing as cosmic spectators at earth's architectural founding.
- **v. 12 `יִדַּעְתָּה הַשַּׁחַר` (*yidda'tah ha-shachar*, Qere) vs. `ידעתה שחר` (Ketiv):** The Qere adds the definite article `הַ` ("the dawn") and points the Piel verb ("have you made the dawn know its place?").
- **v. 31–32 `כִּימָה` (*Kimah*), `כְּסִיל` (*Kesil*), `מַזָּרוֹת` (*Mazzarot*), `עַיִשׁ` (*Ayish*):** Ancient astronomical terms for the Pleiades cluster, Orion, the zodiacal/seasonal constellations, and Ursa Major (the Bear and her stars/cubs).
- **v. 36 `טֻחוֹת` (*tuchot*) & `שֶׂכְוִי` (*sekhvi*):** Rare terms occurring together; *tuchot* means "inward parts/cloud-layers" (compare Ps 51:6), and *sekhvi* can mean either "mind/heart" or "rooster" (heralding dawn) or "celestial appearance."
- **v. 41 `יְלָדָיו` (*yeladav*, Qere) vs. `יַלְדּוֹ` (*yaldo*, Ketiv):** Plural Qere ("his young ones") matches the plural verbs `יְשַׁוֵּעוּ` and `יִתְעוּ` ("cry out / wander"), while defective Ketiv `ילדו` lacks the plural *yod*.

## Supplemental Texts & Allusions

- **Proverbs 8:22–31** (creation architecture): Shares architectural metaphors of laying earth's foundation, setting boundaries for the sea, and cosmic joy at creation (vv. 4–11).
- **Psalm 104:5–9, 21, 27–28** (creation & providence): Parallels setting the earth on its pillars, confining the surging waters behind bounds, and feeding young lions and wild creatures (vv. 6–11, 39–41).
- **Psalm 147:9** (providence for ravens): Directly echoes God providing food for young ravens when they cry out (v. 41).
- **Luke 12:24** (thematic echo): Points to God feeding the ravens as assurance of divine care over creation (v. 41).
