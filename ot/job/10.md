## Clear & Faithful

**1** "My soul grows weary of my own life; I will give full vent to my complaint and speak out from the bitterness of my soul.
**2** I will say to God: Do not pronounce me guilty! Let me know what charge you are bringing against me!
**3** Does it please you to play the oppressor, to spurn the handiwork of your own palms while smiling brightly upon the schemes of the wicked?
**4** Do you possess eyes of mortal flesh, or look at things the way mere humans look?
**5** Are your days fleeting like a mortal's days, or your years measured like a human lifespan,
**6** that you probe for my guilt and hunt down my sin—
**7** even while knowing well that I am innocent, and that nobody can snatch me from your hand?
**8** Your own hands shaped me and crafted every part of me round about—and now you swallow me whole!
**9** Remember, please, that you formed me out of clay; will you really crumble me back into dust right now?
**10** Did you not pour me out like milk and curdle me together like cheese?
**11** You dressed me in skin and muscle, and knitted me fast with bones and sinews;
**12** you granted me life and steadfast kindness, and your watchful care preserved my breath.
**13** Yet all along you hid these darker designs in your heart; I realize now that this was your secret aim:
**14** if I ever stumbled, you kept close watch over me and refused to clear me of my offense.
**15** If I am guilty—woe to me! Yet even if I stand righteous, I dare not raise my head, stuffed full of shame and staring at my own misery.
**16** Should my head lift at all, you stalk me like a fierce lion and display your terrifying wonders against me again.
**17** You bring fresh witnesses against me and heighten your fury toward me; wave after wave of troops attacks me!
**18** Why then did you ever bring me out of the womb? I could have expired before any eye ever spotted me!
**19** I would have been as though I had never existed—carried straight from the womb to the tomb.
**20** Are not my days few enough already? Leave me alone and look away from me, so I may catch a brief moment of relief
**21** before I depart—never to return—to the realm of darkness and deepest shadow,
**22** a land as murky as pitch-black midnight, a realm of death-shadow without order, where even the light gleams like utter gloom."

## Everyday English

**1** "I'm completely sick of living—so I'm letting my complaint pour out and saying exactly what's eating at my soul!
**2** I'll tell God straight up: Quit treating me like a criminal! At least tell me what charges you've filed against me!
**3** Do you actually enjoy being a bully—throwing away the person your own hands built while shining your approval on the plans of wicked people?
**4** Do you have human eyes? Do you see things the limited way people do?
**5** Is your lifespan short like ours, or are your years ticking down like a mortal's,
**6** that you're rushing to dig up my flaws and interrogate me for sins—
**7** even though you already know I'm not guilty, and nobody can pull me out of your grip?
**8** Your own hands carefully sculpted and put me together from head to toe—and now you're turning around to destroy me!
**9** Please remember you molded me out of clay! Are you really going to smash me back into dirt so soon?
**10** Didn't you pour me out like fresh milk and let me thicken like cheese in the womb?
**11** You wrapped me in skin and flesh and wove me together with bones and tendons.
**12** You gave me life and loyal love, and your constant care kept me breathing.
**13** Yet secretly you kept this trap tucked inside your heart—I see now what you were planning all along:
**14** if I messed up once, you'd have me on surveillance and never let me off the hook!
**15** If I actually sinned, I'm doomed—and even when I'm innocent, I'm too humiliated and crushed to lift my chin!
**16** The second I raise my head, you hunt me down like a prowling lion and show off your terrifying power on me again.
**17** You keep lining up new witnesses against me, turning up your anger, and sending fresh waves of soldiers at me!
**18** Why did you even pull me out of my mother's womb? I wish I'd died before anyone ever laid eyes on me!
**19** It would've been like I never existed—carried straight from the delivery room to the graveyard.
**20** Aren't my remaining days short enough already? Step back and leave me alone so I can enjoy one tiny breather
**21** before I leave on a one-way trip to the land of pitch-black shadows—
**22** a chaotic world of midnight gloom and death's shadow, where the brightest shine is still pure darkness."

## Crossings & Lexical Notes

- **v. 1 `נָקְטָה נַפְשִׁי` (*naqtah nafshi*):** Literally "my soul feels loathing/disgust," opening a direct address to God (*Eloah*) that turns the friends' own creation theology into an indictment of the Creator's harshness.
- **v. 8 `עִצְּבוּנִי` (*itstsevuni*):** "Fashioned/shaped me" (like an artisan molding clay, v. 9), though the same root *atsav* also resonates with "grief/pain" (*etsev*), creating an undertone of painful molding before `וַתְּבַלְּעֵנִי` ("yet you swallow me up," echoing 2:3!).
- **v. 10-11 `תַּקְפִּיאֵנִי ... תְּסֹכְכֵנִי` (*taqpi'eni ... tesokhekheni*):** Remarkable embryological poetry: God "pours" the seed like milk, "curdles/coagulates" it like cheese, clothes it with skin/flesh, and "interweaves" (*sakha*) bones and sinews.
- **v. 17 `חֲלִיפוֹת וְצָבָא` (*chalifot ve-tsava*):** Literally "relays/shifts and an army" (hendiadys for "fresh relief troops"), picturing God rotating fresh military units to keep assaulting one worn-out mortal.
- **v. 20 `יחדל` / `וַחֲדָל` & `ישית` / `וְשִׁית` (*va-chadal ... ve-shit*):** Two adjacent Ketiv/Qere variants! Ketiv reads third-person jussives (`יחדל` "let him cease," `ישית` "let him turn away"), whereas Qere switches to direct second-person imperatives (`וַחֲדָל` "and cease!," `וְשִׁית` "and turn away!"), matching Job's direct second-person plea throughout vv. 2–20.
- **v. 22 `וַתֹּפַע כְּמוֹ־אֹפֶל` (*va-tofa kemo-ofel*):** Literally "and it shines like thick darkness"—a chilling oxymoron concluding Job's descent into the chaotic un-creation of Sheol (`וְלֹא סְדָרִים`, "without order").

## Supplemental Texts & Allusions

- **Psalm 139:13–16** (knitting in the womb): Shares the vivid language of God weaving (*sakha*) bones and body together in the secret depths of the womb—celebrated in Psalm 139 as loving intimacy, yet lamented by Job in vv. 8–13 as a prelude to torment.
- **Genesis 2:7 & 3:19** (clay and dust): Job's reminder in v. 9 that God shaped him like clay (*chomer*) and now drives him back to dust (*afar*) recalls humanity's fragile earthen origin.
- **Psalm 39:13 (MT 39:14)** (plea for respite): Closely parallels v. 20's urgent request that God look away so the sufferer may regain a brief smile of relief before departing and being no more.

**1** “My soul is weary of my life. I will give free course to my complaint. I will speak in the bitterness of my soul.
**2** I will tell God, ‘Do not condemn me. Show me why you contend with me.
**3** Is it good to you that you should oppress, that you should despise the work of your hands, and smile on the counsel of the wicked?
**4** Do you have eyes of flesh? Or do you see as man sees?
**5** Are your days as the days of mortals, or your years as man’s years,
**6** that you inquire after my iniquity, and search after my sin?
**7** Although you know that I am not wicked, there is no one who can deliver out of your hand.
**8** “‘Your hands have framed me and fashioned me altogether, yet you destroy me.
**9** Remember, I beg you, that you have fashioned me as clay. Will you bring me into dust again?
**10** Haven’t you poured me out like milk, and curdled me like cheese?
**11** You have clothed me with skin and flesh, and knit me together with bones and sinews.
**12** You have granted me life and loving kindness. Your visitation has preserved my spirit.
**13** Yet you hid these things in your heart. I know that this is with you:
**14** if I sin, then you mark me. You will not acquit me from my iniquity.
**15** If I am wicked, woe to me. If I am righteous, I still will not lift up my head, being filled with disgrace, and conscious of my affliction.
**16** If my head is held high, you hunt me like a lion. Again you show yourself powerful to me.
**17** You renew your witnesses against me, and increase your indignation on me. Changes and warfare are with me.
**18** “‘Why, then, have you brought me out of the womb? I wish I had given up the spirit, and no eye had seen me.
**19** I should have been as though I had not been. I should have been carried from the womb to the grave.
**20** Aren’t my days few? Stop! Leave me alone, that I may find a little comfort,
**21** before I go where I will not return from, to the land of darkness and of the shadow of death;
**22** the land dark as midnight, of the shadow of death, without any order, where the light is as midnight.’”
