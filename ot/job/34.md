## Clear & Faithful

**1** Then Elihu answered and said:
**2** "Listen to my words, you wise ones, and pay attention to me, you who have knowledge;
**3** Surely the ear evaluates speech, much as the tongue savors what we eat.
**4** Let us choose for ourselves what is just; let us determine together what is good.
**5** For Job has claimed, 'I am in the right, yet God has taken away my justice;
**6** despite my right, I am counted a liar; my arrow-wound is incurable, though I committed no transgression.'
**7** Has there ever been a fellow like Job, gulping down mockery like a draught of water,
**8** who travels in company with evildoers and walks alongside wicked people?
**9** For he has said, 'It profits a person nothing to take delight in God.'
**10** Therefore listen to me, you people of understanding: far be it from God to do wickedness, and from the Almighty to commit injustice!
**11** Rather, he repays each person according to their work, and brings upon them what their own conduct deserves.
**12** Truly, God will never act wickedly, nor will the Almighty twist justice.
**13** Who put him in charge of the earth? Who entrusted the entire world to his care?
**14** If he should set his heart upon himself and draw back to himself his spirit and his breath,
**15** every living creature would expire at once, and mortal humankind would crumble back into dust.
**16** If you have discernment, hear this; give ear to the sound of my words.
**17** Could one who hates justice ever govern? And will you condemn the Just and Mighty One—
**18** the one who says to a king, 'Worthless wretch!' and to nobles, 'Wicked!'—
**19** who shows no partiality toward princes and does not favor the wealthy over the poor, because all of them are the work of his hands?
**20** In a single instant they die; at midnight a people are shaken and pass away, and the mighty are removed without a human hand.
**21** For his eyes watch over a person's ways, and he observes their every step.
**22** There is no darkness and no deep shadow where evildoers can hide themselves.
**23** For he does not need to set an extended inquiry before someone comes to God in judgment.
**24** He shatters the mighty without needing investigation, and sets others in their place.
**25** Because he knows their deeds, he overturns them in the night and they are crushed.
**26** He strikes them down as wicked people in full view of onlookers,
**27** because they turned away from following him and gave no regard to any of his ways,
**28** until the plea of the destitute reached him, for he never fails to hear the wail of the oppressed.
**29** When he grants quietness, who can condemn? When he hides his face, who can behold him—whether over a nation or over an individual alike—
**30** so that a godless person may not reign, nor ensnare the people?
**31** Suppose someone says to God, 'I have borne chastisement; I will offend no more;
**32** teach me what I do not see; if I have done wrong, I will do it no longer.'
**33** Should he repay it on your terms because you reject his way—so that you get to choose, and not I? Speak what you know!
**34** People of understanding will agree with me, and every wise person who hears me will say,
**35** 'Job speaks without knowledge, and his words lack insight.'
**36** Oh, that Job might be tested to the utmost, because he answers like wicked men!
**37** For to his sin he adds rebellion; he claps his hands in scorn among us and multiplies his words against God."

## Everyday English

**1** Elihu kept going:
**2** "Listen to me, you wise thinkers! Pay close attention, you educated folks!
**3** Just like your tongue tests the flavor of food, your ear tests whether words ring true.
**4** So let's figure out together what's fair and agree on what's right.
**5** Look at what Job claimed: 'I'm innocent, yet God robbed me of a fair trial!
**6** Even though I'm right, I'm treated like a liar; I'm bleeding from a deadly arrow when I haven't done anything wrong!'
**7** Seriously, is there anyone else like Job, who guzzles mockery like cold water,
**8** teams up with corrupt people, and hangs out with troublemakers?
**9** He actually said, 'Trying to please God doesn't do anybody a bit of good!'
**10** So hear me out, anyone with good sense: God would never do anything evil, and the Almighty would never commit injustice!
**11** Instead, he pays every person back for what they actually do, and gives each one what their lifestyle brings on.
**12** Make no mistake: God never cheats, and the Almighty never bends the law.
**13** Did somebody appoint him to run the earth, or hand him the universe to manage? No!
**14** If God decided to pull back his Spirit and withdraw the breath he gave us,
**15** every living body would drop dead on the spot, and all humanity would turn straight back into dirt.
**16** If you've got any sense at all, listen closely to what I'm saying.
**17** Could someone who hated fairness ever run the world? Are you really going to accuse the all-powerful, righteous Judge of being crooked?
**18** He's the one who calls corrupt kings 'worthless' and tells noblemen, 'You're wicked!'
**19** He doesn't suck up to royalty or side with billionaires over the poor, because he made every single one of them with his own hands.
**20** In one split second they drop dead; at midnight whole nations convulse and vanish, and powerful tyrants are swept away without a human hand lifting a sword.
**21** His eyes track every path a person walks; he sees every single footprint.
**22** There's no pitch-black cave or deep shadow anywhere that wrongdoers can hide in.
**23** God doesn't need to schedule extra hearings to drag someone into his courtroom.
**24** He breaks arrogant rulers without needing a long investigation and puts new leaders in their chairs.
**25** Because he sees every scheme they pull, he flips their world upside down overnight and crushes them.
**26** He punishes them right out in the open where everyone can watch,
**27** because they walked out on him and ignored every principle he taught,
**28** until the cries of poor victims reached his ears—and he always hears desperate people crying out!
**29** Yet if God decides to hold back and keep quiet, who can find fault with him? If he hides his face, who can force him to show up—whether for an entire nation or one individual—
**30** to stop a crooked ruler from taking the throne and trapping the people?
**31** Shouldn't someone say to God, 'I've taken my punishment, and I won't mess up again.
**32** Show me what I'm blind to; if I did wrong, I won't repeat it'?
**33** Should God hand out justice based on your rules just because you don't like his verdict—letting you call the shots instead of him? Go ahead, tell us what you think!
**34** Every thoughtful person here will back me up, and any wise listener will admit:
**35** 'Job doesn't know what he's talking about; his arguments make zero sense.'
**36** Job ought to be cross-examined to the very end, because he talks just like rebellious people do!
**37** He's piling arrogance on top of his sin, clapping his hands defiantly in front of us and rambling on against God."

## Crossings & Lexical Notes

- **v. 6 `אָנוּשׁ חִצִּי` (*anush chitsi*):** "Incurable [is] my arrow[-wound]"—*chets* ("arrow") stands by metonymy for the wound inflicted by divine arrows (compare Job 6:4; 16:13).
- **v. 10 `חָלִלָה לָאֵל` (*chalilah la-El*):** Strongest Hebrew oath of repudiation ("Far be it from God!" / "Profane/unthinkable for God!"), matching Abraham's plea in Gen 18:25.
- **v. 14 `רוּחוֹ וְנִשְׁמָתוֹ` (*rucho ve-nishmato*):** Echoes Gen 2:7 and Ps 104:29: life depends moment-by-moment on God sustaining breath; withdrawing it returns all flesh to `עָפָר` (*afar*, "dust," Gen 3:19).
- **v. 20 `לֹא בְיָד` (*lo ve-yad*):** Literally "not by [human] hand"—signifying sudden divine intervention rather than human warfare (compare Dan 2:34; 8:25).
- **v. 36 `אָבִי` (*avi*):** Rare interjection of wish/desire ("Would that!" / "I wish!"), distinct from the noun "my father" (which some ancient versions nevertheless read).
- **v. 37 `יִסְפּוֹק` (*yispoq*):** "He claps [his hands]" (a gesture of mockery or defiance, matching Job 27:23; Lam 2:15).

## Supplemental Texts & Allusions

- **Genesis 18:25** (theological parallel): Shares the exclamation `חָלִלָה` ("Far be it!") denying that the Judge of all the earth could act unjustly (vv. 10–12).
- **Psalm 104:29–30 & Genesis 3:19** (breath and dust motif): Parallels vv. 14–15, where gathering back God's breath/spirit causes all creatures to expire and return to dust.
- **Daniel 2:34 & 8:25** (idiom parallel): Uses the same "without a [human] hand" (`לֹא בְיָד`, v. 20) phrase for divine overthrow of earthly powers.

**3** For the ear tries words, as the palate tastes food.
**4** Let us choose for us that which is right. Let us know among ourselves what is good.
**5** For Job has said, ‘I am righteous, God has taken away my right.
**6** Notwithstanding my right I am considered a liar. My wound is incurable, though I am without disobedience.’
**7** What man is like Job, who drinks scorn like water,
**8** who goes in company with the workers of iniquity, and walks with wicked men?
**9** For he has said, ‘It profits a man nothing that he should delight himself with God.’
**10** “Therefore listen to me, you men of understanding: far be it from God, that he should do wickedness, from the Almighty, that he should commit iniquity.
**11** For the work of a man he will give to him, and cause every man to find according to his ways.
**12** Yes surely, God will not do wickedly, neither will the Almighty pervert justice.
**13** Who put him in charge of the earth? Or who has appointed him over the whole world?
**14** If he set his heart on himself, if he gathered to himself his spirit and his breath,
**15** all flesh would perish together, and man would turn again to dust.
**16** “If now you have understanding, hear this. Listen to the voice of my words.
**17** Should even one who hates justice govern? Will you condemn him who is righteous and mighty,
**18** who says to a king, ‘Vile!’ or to nobles, ‘Wicked!’?
**19** He doesn’t respect the persons of princes, nor respect the rich more than the poor, for they all are the work of his hands.
**20** In a moment they die, even at midnight. The people are shaken and pass away. The mighty are taken away without a hand.
**21** “For his eyes are on the ways of a man. He sees all his goings.
**22** There is no darkness, nor thick gloom, where the workers of iniquity may hide themselves.
**23** For he doesn’t need to consider a man further, that he should go before God in judgment.
**24** He breaks mighty men in pieces in ways past finding out, and sets others in their place.
**25** Therefore he takes knowledge of their works. He overturns them in the night, so that they are destroyed.
**26** He strikes them as wicked men in the open sight of others;
**27** because they turned away from following him, and wouldn’t pay attention to any of his ways,
**28** so that they caused the cry of the poor to come to him. He heard the cry of the afflicted.
**29** When he gives quietness, who then can condemn? When he hides his face, who then can see him? He is over a nation or a man alike,
**30** that the godless man may not reign, that there be no one to ensnare the people.
**31** “For has any said to God, ‘I am guilty, but I will not offend any more.
**32** Teach me that which I don’t see. If I have done iniquity, I will do it no more’?
**33** Shall his recompense be as you desire, that you refuse it? For you must choose, and not I. Therefore speak what you know.
**34** Men of understanding will tell me, yes, every wise man who hears me:
**35** ‘Job speaks without knowledge. His words are without wisdom.’
**36** I wish that Job were tried to the end, because of his answering like wicked men.
**37** For he adds rebellion to his sin. He claps his hands among us, and multiplies his words against God.”
