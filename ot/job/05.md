## Clear & Faithful

**1** "Call out now—is anyone there to answer you? To which of the holy ones will you turn?
**2** Surely resentment kills the fool, and envy slays the naive.
**3** I myself watched a fool putting down roots, yet suddenly I pronounced his homestead cursed.
**4** His children stand far from safety; they are crushed at the city gate with none to rescue them.
**5** The hungry devour his harvest—snatching it even out from among thorns—and the thirsty gasp after their wealth.
**6** For wickedness does not spring up out of the dust, nor does trouble sprout from the soil;
**7** rather, humanity is born for trouble just as sparks soar upward.
**8** As for me, I would seek God earnestly, and before God I would lay out my cause—
**9** the One who performs mighty deeds past all fathoming, marvels beyond all counting;
**10** who bestows rain upon the face of the earth and sends water out over the open fields;
**11** who raises the lowly to lofty heights and lifts mourners into safety;
**12** who thwarts the schemes of the crafty so their hands achieve no lasting success;
**13** who traps the clever in their own cunning, bringing the plans of the devious to ruinous haste.
**14** In broad daylight they collide with darkness, groping at midday as though it were midnight.
**15** Thus he rescues the needy from the sword of their mouth and from the grip of the mighty.
**16** So the helpless gain hope, and injustice clamps her mouth shut.
**17** Look how blessed is the mortal whom God corrects! Therefore do not despise the discipline of the Almighty.
**18** For though he inflicts wounds, he binds them up; he strikes, yet his own hands bring healing.
**19** Out of six calamities he will rescue you, and in the seventh no harm will touch you.
**20** During famine he will ransom you from death, and in battle from the power of the sword.
**21** From the lash of the tongue you will be sheltered, and you need not dread ruin when it arrives.
**22** You will laugh at devastation and hunger, and have no fear of wild beasts upon the land;
**23** for you hold a covenant with the very stones of the field, and wild creatures live at peace with you.
**24** You will know that wholeness guards your tent, and when you inspect your fold, nothing will be missing.
**25** You will see your descendants grow plentiful, and your offspring like the grass of the earth.
**26** You will enter the grave in full vigor, like a stack of sheaves gathered in at its proper season.
**27** Take note of this: we have examined it carefully, and so it stands. Listen to it and take it to heart for yourself."

## Everyday English

**1** "Go ahead and yell for help—who's going to answer you? Which of the angels do you think will take your side?
**2** Bitter anger destroys a foolish person, and jealousy wipes out the gullible.
**3** I've personally watched foolish people put down deep roots—and then, out of nowhere, their homes were cursed!
**4** Their kids end up helpless, crushed in court at the town gate with nobody to step in and save them.
**5** Starving strangers eat up their crops, grabbing grain right out of the thorn hedges, while greedy rivals swallow up their fortune.
**6** Misery doesn't just pop out of the dirt, and heartache doesn't grow wild from the ground.
**7** Humans are born straight into trouble, as surely as glowing sparks fly up from a fire!
**8** If I were in your shoes, I'd appeal directly to God and put my whole case in his hands.
**9** He pulls off massive wonders nobody can trace and miracles too numerous to count.
**10** He pours rain across the countryside and sends fresh water over the pastures.
**11** He lifts up nobodies to places of honor and pulls grieving people up to safety.
**12** He wrecks the plots of slick schemers so none of their clever plans work out.
**13** He catches smart-alecks in their own traps and trips up crooked advisors before they finish plotting.
**14** Right at noon they run smack into pitch-blackness, feeling their way around in broad daylight like it's night.
**15** That's how God saves vulnerable people from sharp-tongued bullies and from the clutches of powerful tyrants.
**16** So poor people actually have hope, and corrupt bullies get forced to shut their mouths.
**17** Honestly, a person is fortunate when God corrects them—so don't push away the Almighty's discipline!
**18** Yes, he wounds, yet he bandages the cut; he bruises, yet his very own hands heal it.
**19** He'll pull you out of six crises—and even in a seventh, disaster won't lay a hand on you.
**20** When famine strikes, he'll keep you alive; in wartime, he'll shield you from the sword.
**21** You'll stay safe from vicious gossip, and you won't panic when ruin comes marching in.
**22** You'll laugh right in the face of famine and destruction, without worrying about wild animals.
**23** Even the rocks in your fields will be on your side, and wild beasts will leave you in peace.
**24** You'll know your home is completely secure; when you count your flocks, not one animal will be missing.
**25** You'll watch your family multiply, with descendants spreading thick as meadow grass.
**26** You'll reach your grave still strong and full of years, like harvested wheat stacked up right on schedule.
**27** Look, we've researched this thoroughly, and it's a fact. Listen up and apply it to your own life!"

## Crossings & Lexical Notes

- **v. 1 `מִקְּדֹשִׁים` (*miqqedoshim*):** "From the holy ones," referring to heavenly council members or angelic mediators (cf. 15:15; Ps 89:6–8).
- **v. 7 `בְּנֵי־רֶשֶׁף` (*bene-reshef*):** Literally "sons of flame/Reshef." Commonly rendered "sparks" flying upward from fire, though *Reshef* was also an ancient Semitic plague/lightning deity, evoking swift airborne spirits or winged birds of prey.
- **v. 12 `תּוּשִׁיָּה` (*tushiyyah*):** A signature wisdom term in Job and Proverbs meaning "sound judgment, effective capability, or lasting success."
- **v. 18 `וידו` / `וְיָדָיו` (*ve-yadav*):** Ketiv writes defective singular `וידו` ("and his hand"), whereas Qere points plural `וְיָדָיו` ("and his hands") to agree grammatically with the feminine plural imperfect verb `תִּרְפֶּינָה` (*tirpeynah*, "they heal").
- **v. 19 `בְּשֵׁשׁ ... וּבְשֶׁבַע` (*be-shesh ... u-vesheva*):** A classic Hebrew graded numerical proverb ("six ... even seven"), expressing completeness—no matter how many crises arise, God delivers through all of them.
- **v. 24 `לֹא תֶחֱטָא` (*lo techeta*):** The verb *chata* fundamentally means "to miss the mark"; in pastoral inspection of one's homestead (*navekha*), it carries the concrete sense "you will find nothing missing" (cf. Judg 20:16).
- **v. 26 `בְכֶלַח` (*ve-khelach*):** A rare word (*kelach*, recurring only in 30:2) meaning "full vigor / ripe maturity," picturing a life harvested at its prime rather than cut short.

## Supplemental Texts & Allusions

- **1 Corinthians 3:19** (explicit NT citation): Paul quotes Job 5:13 directly—that God catches the wise in their own craftiness—to expose the futility of arrogant human wisdom.
- **Proverbs 3:11–12 & Hebrews 12:5–6** (divine discipline motif): Parallel Eliphaz's exhortation in vv. 17–18 not to despise the Almighty's correction, affirming that divine chastening aims at restoration.
- **Deuteronomy 32:39 & Hosea 6:1** (wounding and healing): Echo the paradox of v. 18 that the same God who strikes and wounds is the One whose hands bind up and heal.

## Reference Text (WEB Updated, Public Domain)

**1** “Call now; is there any who will answer you? To which of the holy ones will you turn?
**2** For resentment kills the foolish man, and jealousy kills the simple.
**3** I have seen the foolish taking root, but suddenly I cursed his habitation.
**4** His children are far from safety. They are crushed in the gate. Neither is there any to deliver them,
**5** whose harvest the hungry eat up, and take it even out of the thorns. The snare gapes for their substance.
**6** For affliction doesn’t come out of the dust, neither does trouble spring out of the ground;
**7** but man is born to trouble, as the sparks fly upward.
**8** “But as for me, I would seek God. I would commit my cause to God,
**9** who does great things that can’t be fathomed, marvelous things without number;
**10** who gives rain on the earth, and sends waters on the fields;
**11** so that he sets up on high those who are low, those who mourn are exalted to safety.
**12** He frustrates the plans of the crafty, so that their hands can’t perform their enterprise.
**13** He takes the wise in their own craftiness; the counsel of the cunning is carried headlong.
**14** They meet with darkness in the day time, and grope at noonday as in the night.
**15** But he saves from the sword of their mouth, even the needy from the hand of the mighty.
**16** So the poor has hope, and injustice shuts her mouth.
**17** “Behold, happy is the man whom God corrects. Therefore do not despise the chastening of the Almighty.
**18** For he wounds and binds up. He injures and his hands make whole.
**19** He will deliver you in six troubles; yes, in seven no evil will touch you.
**20** In famine he will redeem you from death; in war, from the power of the sword.
**21** You will be hidden from the scourge of the tongue, neither will you be afraid of destruction when it comes.
**22** You will laugh at destruction and famine, neither will you be afraid of the animals of the earth.
**23** For you will be allied with the stones of the field. The animals of the field will be at peace with you.
**24** You will know that your tent is in peace. You will visit your fold, and will miss nothing.
**25** You will know also that your offspring will be great, your offspring as the grass of the earth.
**26** You will come to your grave in a full age, like a shock of grain comes in its season.
**27** Behold, we have researched it. It is so. Hear it, and know it for your good.”
