## Clear & Faithful

**1** Then Job answered and said:
**2** "What marvelous help you have offered the powerless! How well you have rescued an arm with no strength!
**3** What brilliant advice you have given someone lacking wisdom! What wealth of insight you have poured out!
**4** Who helped you compose such speeches? Whose spirit has been speaking through your mouth?
**5** The shades of the dead tremble beneath the waters and all that dwell below.
**6** Sheol lies stripped bare before him, and Abaddon has no veil to hide behind.
**7** He spreads the northern sky across empty space and suspends the earth over nothingness.
**8** He wraps the rainwaters inside his dense clouds, yet the cloud does not rip apart under their weight.
**9** He covers the face of his throne—or the full moon—spreading his cloud-bank over it.
**10** He traced a horizon ring across the surface of the deep, right to the border where light meets darkness.
**11** The pillars supporting heaven quake and stand bewildered at his rebuke.
**12** By his strength he stilled the churning sea; by his discernment he shattered Rahab.
**13** By his breath the skies cleared into splendor; his hand ran through the fleeing sea-serpent.
**14** Look—these wonders are merely the outer fringe of his ways! How faint a whisper we catch of him! As for the thunder of his mighty power, who could ever comprehend it?"

**10** He drew the circular horizon across the ocean's surface, marking the exact edge between daylight and night.

## Crossings & Lexical Notes

- **v. 5 `הָרְפָאִים` (*ha-repha'im*):** The departed shades/spirits in the subterranean underworld beneath the cosmic waters (cf. Isa 14:9; Ps 88:10).
- **v. 6 `אֲבַדּוֹן` (*Avaddon*):** From *avad* ("perish/destroy")—"Place of Destruction/Ruin," paired poetically with `שְׁאוֹל` (*She'ol*).
- **v. 7 `צָפוֹן` (*tsaphon*) & `תֹּהוּ` (*tohu*):** *Tsaphon* denotes the northern heavens (and in Canaanite lore, the sacred mountain of the gods), while *tohu* ("formless void") recalls `Gen 1:2`. God suspends earth upon `בְּלִי־מָה` (*beli-mah*, "nothingness").
- **v. 12 `וּבִתְבוּנָתוֹ` (*uvit'vunato*, Qere) vs. `ובתובנתו` (Ketiv):** Ketiv transposes the *vav* (`תובנתו`), while Qere restores the standard form `תְּבוּנָתוֹ` ("his understanding"). Note also `רָגַע` (*raga*), which spans both "stirred up/churned" and "stilled/calmed."
- **v. 12–13 `רָהַב` (*Rahab*) & `נָחָשׁ בָּרִיחַ` (*nachash bariach*):** Mythic chaos-dragon motifs ("Rahab" and "the fleeing/twisting serpent," exact phrasing shared with Isa 27:1 and 51:9), reclaimed here to proclaim God's effortless mastery over cosmic chaos.
- **v. 14 `דְּרָכָיו` (*derakhav*, Qere) vs. `דרכו` (Ketiv) & `גְּבוּרוֹתָיו` (*gevurotav*, Qere) vs. `גבורתו` (Ketiv):** Two Ketiv/Qere pairs in a single verse where Qere pluralizes both "his ways" and "his mighty deeds" from Ketiv singulars (`דרכו`, `גבורתו`).

## Supplemental Texts & Allusions

- **Genesis 1:2, 6–9** (creation over *tohu* and cosmic waters): Echoes Job 26:7–10, depicting God stretching order over the formless void and setting a boundary between waters, light, and darkness.
- **Isaiah 27:1; 51:9; Psalm 89:9–10** (conquering Rahab and the fleeing serpent): Uses the identical Hebrew titles *Rahab* and *nachash bariach* ("fleeing serpent") as Job 26:12–13 to celebrate God's triumph over chaotic sea powers.
- **Psalm 139:8; Proverbs 15:11** (God's gaze into Sheol and Abaddon): Parallels v. 6 in declaring that even the realm of death and destruction lies completely exposed before God.

## Reference Text (WEB Updated, Public Domain)

**1** Then Job answered,
**2** “How have you helped him who is without power! How have you saved the arm that has no strength!
**3** How have you counseled him who has no wisdom, and plentifully declared sound knowledge!
**4** To whom have you uttered words? Whose spirit came out of you?
**5** “The departed spirits tremble, those beneath the waters and all that live in them.
**6** Sheol is naked before God, and Abaddon has no covering.
**7** He stretches out the north over empty space, and hangs the earth on nothing.
**8** He binds up the waters in his thick clouds, and the cloud is not burst under them.
**9** He encloses the face of his throne, and spreads his cloud on it.
**10** He has described a boundary on the surface of the waters, and to the confines of light and darkness.
**11** The pillars of heaven tremble and are astonished at his rebuke.
**12** He stirs up the sea with his power, and by his understanding he strikes through Rahab.
**13** By his Spirit the heavens are garnished. His hand has pierced the swift serpent.
**14** Behold, these are but the outskirts of his ways. How small a whisper do we hear of him! But the thunder of his power who can understand?”
