## Clear & Faithful

**1** Then Job answered and said:
**2** "In truth, I know that this is so—yet how can a mere mortal stand in the right before God?
**3** Should someone desire to argue a case with him, he could not answer him once in a thousand times.
**4** Wise of heart and mighty in power—who has ever defied him and come out unscathed?
**5** He shifts mountains before they even realize it, overturning them in his wrath;
**6** he shakes the earth loose from its foundations until its pillars tremble;
**7** he commands the sun, and it dares not rise, and seals up the stars under lock;
**8** he alone stretches out the skies and marches across the crests of the sea;
**9** he fashioned the Bear, Orion, and the Pleiades, along with the hidden chambers of the southern sky;
**10** he accomplishes mighty deeds past all fathoming and marvels beyond all counting!
**11** Look: he sweeps right past me, and I never see him; he glides by, and I cannot perceive him.
**12** When he seizes his prey, who can force him to give it back? Who dares ask him, 'What are you doing?'
**13** God does not pull back his anger; even the allies of Rahab crouch crushed beneath him.
**14** How then could I ever answer him or pick my words carefully to debate with him?
**15** Even though I am innocent, I could make no reply; I could only plead for clemency from my Judge.
**16** Even if I summoned him and he answered, I would scarcely believe he was truly listening to my voice—
**17** this One who batters me with a gale and multiplies my wounds for no reason!
**18** He refuses to let me catch my breath, cramming me full of bitter misery.
**19** If it is a test of raw strength—look how mighty he is! If it is a matter of legal justice—'Who can serve him a subpoena?'
**20** Though I stand innocent, my own mouth would condemn me; though I am blameless, he would twist me out to be guilty.
**21** I am blameless—yet I no longer care about my own soul; I despise my life!
**22** It is all one and the same; therefore I declare: he destroys both the blameless and the wicked alike!
**23** When a sudden disaster brings swift death, he laughs at the despair of the innocent.
**24** The earth has been handed over to the wicked; he blindfolds the faces of its judges. If it is not him doing this, then who is it?
**25** My days race faster than a courier; they sprint away without ever glimpsing happiness.
**26** They skim past like swift reed skiffs, like an eagle diving onto its prey.
**27** Suppose I decide, 'I will forget my sorrow, drop my grim expression, and put on a cheerful face'—
**28** I still shudder at all my torment, knowing you will never pronounce me innocent.
**29** If I am already judged guilty, why exhaust myself striving in vain?
**30** Even if I scrubbed myself with snow water and scoured my hands with lye,
**31** you would still dunk me into a filthy pit until my own clothes recoiled from me!
**32** For he is no human being like me whom I could cross-examine, so that the two of us could walk into court together.
**33** There is no mediator between us to lay his hand on both our shoulders!
**34** Let him remove his rod from my back and keep his terror from paralyzing me;
**35** then I would speak freely without dread of him—for that is not how things stand inside me!"

## Crossings & Lexical Notes

- **v. 10 `עֹשֶׂה גְדֹלוֹת` (*oseh gedolot*):** Job deliberately quotes Eliphaz's exact hymn line from 5:9 word-for-word, turning Eliphaz's praise of God's unfathomable wonders into terror at an unaccountable cosmic Power.
- **v. 13 `תחתו` / `תַּחְתָּיו` (*tachtav*):** Ketiv lacks the *yod* (`תחתו`), while Qere points standard plural preposition `תַּחְתָּיו` ("beneath him"). `עֹזְרֵי רָהַב` (*ozrei Rahav*, "helpers of Rahab") refers to the allies of the primordial chaos-serpent Rahab crushed by God at creation (cf. 26:12; Ps 89:10).
- **v. 17 `חִנָּם` (*chinnam*):** "For nothing / without cause"—unknowingly echoing the exact word used in the heavenly courtroom in 1:9 and 2:3!
- **v. 30 `במו` / `בְמֵי` (*bemo* / *bemei*):** Ketiv reads poetic `בְּמוֹ־שָׁלֶג` ("with snow"), while Qere reads `בְּמֵי־שָׁלֶג` ("with waters of snow," i.e., melted snow-water), paired with *bor* ("alkali/lye soap").
- **v. 33 `מוֹכִיחַ` (*mokhiach*):** "Umpire, arbiter, mediator" who can lay a hand (*yashet yado*) on both litigants equally—an astonishing longing for a bridge across the Creator-creature abyss.
- **v. 35 `כִּי לֹא־כֵן אָנֹכִי עִמָּדִי` (*ki lo-khen anokhi immadi*):** Literally "for not thus am I with myself"—either "I am not like that [guilty] in my own conscience" or "I am not myself [steady/composed] as things stand."

## Supplemental Texts & Allusions

- **Amos 4:13 & Psalm 104:2–3** (creator of stars and sea): Celebrate God as the One who forms mountains, treads the heights, and stretches out the heavens—imagery Job deploys in vv. 5–9 to underscore God's overwhelming majesty.
- **Isaiah 51:9 & Psalm 89:10 (MT 89:11)** (Rahab): Recall the LORD's victory over the chaos monster Rahab, illuminating v. 13's "helpers of Rahab."
- **1 Timothy 2:5** (longing for a mediator): Job's cry in v. 33 for an impartial *mokhiach* ("arbiter/mediator") who can touch both God and mortal stands as a striking OT precursor to the NT mediator motif.

**3** If he is pleased to contend with him, he can’t answer him one time in a thousand.
**4** God is wise in heart, and mighty in strength. Who has hardened himself against him and prospered?
**5** He removes the mountains, and they don’t know it, when he overturns them in his anger.
**6** He shakes the earth out of its place. Its pillars tremble.
**7** He commands the sun and it doesn’t rise, and seals up the stars.
**8** He alone stretches out the heavens, and treads on the waves of the sea.
**9** He makes the Bear, Orion, and the Pleiades, and the rooms of the south.
**10** He does great things past finding out; yes, marvelous things without number.
**11** Behold, he goes by me, and I don’t see him. He passes on also, but I don’t perceive him.
**12** Behold, he snatches away. Who can hinder him? Who will ask him, ‘What are you doing?’
**13** “God will not withdraw his anger. The helpers of Rahab stoop under him.
**14** How much less will I answer him, and choose my words to argue with him?
**15** Though I were righteous, yet I wouldn’t answer him. I would make supplication to my judge.
**16** If I had called, and he had answered me, yet I wouldn’t believe that he listened to my voice.
**17** For he breaks me with a storm, and multiplies my wounds without cause.
**18** He will not allow me to catch my breath, but fills me with bitterness.
**19** If it is a matter of strength, behold, he is mighty! If of justice, ‘Who,’ says he, ‘will summon me?’
**20** Though I am righteous, my own mouth will condemn me. Though I am blameless, it will prove me perverse.
**21** I am blameless. I don’t respect myself. I despise my life.
**22** “It is all the same. Therefore I say he destroys the blameless and the wicked.
**23** If the scourge kills suddenly, he will mock at the trial of the innocent.
**24** The earth is given into the hand of the wicked. He covers the faces of its judges. If not he, then who is it?
**25** “Now my days are swifter than a runner. They flee away. They see no good.
**26** They have passed away as the swift ships, as the eagle that swoops on the prey.
**27** If I say, ‘I will forget my complaint, I will put off my sad face, and cheer up,’
**28** I am afraid of all my sorrows. I know that you will not hold me innocent.
**29** I will be condemned. Why then do I labor in vain?
**30** If I wash myself with snow, and cleanse my hands with lye,
**31** yet you will plunge me in the ditch. My own clothes will abhor me.
**32** For he is not a man, as I am, that I should answer him, that we should come together in judgment.
**33** There is no umpire between us, that might lay his hand on us both.
**34** Let him take his rod away from me. Let his terror not make me afraid;
**35** then I would speak, and not fear him, for I am not so in myself.
