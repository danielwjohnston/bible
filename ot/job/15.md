## Clear & Faithful

**1** Then Eliphaz the Temanite answered and said:
**2** "Would a wise person answer with windswept opinions, or swell his belly with the east wind,
**3** disputing with talk that achieves nothing and speeches that carry no profit?
**4** Worse still, you undermine the fear of God and erode quiet devotion in his presence.
**5** Your own guilt prompts your mouth, and you adopt the language of the cunning.
**6** Your own mouth pronounces you guilty, not I; your own lips testify against you.
**7** Were you the first human being ever born, brought into birth before the hills took shape?
**8** Have you overheard the secret council of God and hoarded wisdom for yourself alone?
**9** What do you know that escapes us? What insight do you hold that we do not share?
**10** Both the gray-haired and the aged sit among us—elders far older than your own father.
**11** Are the comforts of God too slight for you, and a gentle word spoken in your hearing?
**12** Why does your passion sweep you away, and why do your eyes flash in defiance,
**13** that you vent your anger against God and spill such words from your lips?
**14** What is a mortal, that he should claim purity, or anyone born of woman, that he should stand acquitted?
**15** Look: God does not even trust his holy ones, and the heavens themselves are not spotless in his sight—
**16** how much less someone loathsome and spoiled, a person who gulps down wickedness like water!
**17** Listen to me and I will show you; what I have observed I will declare—
**18** what the sages have handed down without concealing a thing from their forebears,
**19** to whom alone the land was entrusted, before any outsider walked among them:
**20** Every day of the wicked person's life, he twists in dread, throughout the years allotted to the tyrant.
**21** Sounds of panic echo in his ears; even in quiet times, the plunderer descends on him.
**22** He despairs of ever coming back out of darkness; he knows he is marked for the sword.
**23** He roams about begging for bread, asking where any can be found; he knows the day of darkness waits right in his grip.
**24** Distress and torment overwhelm him, overpowering him like a king poised for the assault.
**25** For he raised his fist against God and defied the Almighty in arrogance,
**26** charging at him headlong behind the thick, bossed rim of his shields.
**27** Because he padded his face with fat and gathered layers of flesh around his waist,
**28** he settled in ruined towns and abandoned houses destined to crumble into rubble.
**29** He will not stay wealthy, nor will his fortune endure; his yield will never bend low across the land.
**30** He will never escape the gloom; searing flame will scorch his shoots, and a breath from God's mouth will sweep him away.
**31** Let him not rely on empty illusions, deceiving himself, for emptiness is all he will get in return.
**32** Before his time comes, his reckoning is paid in full, and his crown of leaves will never stay green.
**33** Like a vine he will drop his sour, unripe grapes; like an olive tree he will shed his blossoms.
**34** For the household of the godless yields nothing, and fire consumes the tents built on bribes.
**35** They conceive mischief and give birth to evil, and within them they hatch deceit."

## Everyday English

**1** Eliphaz from Teman spoke up again:
**2** "Would anyone truly wise reply with hot air, or puff himself up with desert wind?
**3** Why argue with useless speeches and empty talk that helps nobody?
**4** Worse yet, you're tearing down reverence for God and brushing off prayer before him.
**5** It's your own sin coaching what you say—you've chosen to talk like a schemer.
**6** I don't even have to condemn you; your own mouth does it. Your own words stand as witness against you.
**7** Were you the very first human on earth? Were you born before the mountains existed?
**8** Did you sit in on God's private meetings and keep all wisdom to yourself?
**9** What do you know that we don't? What secret insight do you have that we've missed?
**10** We have gray-haired elders on our side—men much older than your father!
**11** Aren't God's words of comfort enough for you, spoken so gently to help you?
**12** Why are you letting your temper carry you off, with your eyes blazing like that?
**13** You're turning your anger straight at God and letting reckless complaints pour out!
**14** How could any frail mortal ever be spotless? How could anyone born to a mother be completely innocent?
**15** Think about it: God doesn't even put full trust in his heavenly angels, and even the skies aren't pure to him.
**16** So what chance does a corrupt, twisted person have—someone who drinks in wrongdoing as easily as water?
**17** Hear me out, and I'll tell you what I've seen with my own eyes.
**18** Wise teachers passed this truth down openly from their ancestors—
**19** the ones who owned the land alone, before foreigners ever moved in:
**20** Brutal, wicked people spend their whole lives writhing in anxiety for every year they're given.
**21** Terrifying rumors ring in their ears; right when they feel safe, raiders strike.
**22** They have zero hope of surviving the dark; they know the sword has their name on it.
**23** They wander around hunting for food, wondering where the next bite will come from, sure that disaster is already waiting at hand.
**24** Panic and misery corner them, closing in like a king ready to charge into battle.
**25** Why? Because they shook their fists at God and acted tough against the Almighty—
**26** rushing him headfirst behind thick, heavy shields.
**27** They got sleek and fat, complacent in their luxury,
**28** and moved into cursed, devastated towns—homes nobody should live in, marked to become piles of stone.
**29** Their wealth won't last, their power won't hold up, and their crops won't stretch across the countryside.
**30** They'll never get out of the dark; flames will dry up their young branches, and one puff of God's breath will blow them off.
**31** Nobody who's fooled by worthless lies should count on them—worthless nothing is all they'll get back.
**32** They'll be cut down before their time is up, and their branches will never turn green again.
**33** They'll be like a grapevine that drops its sour buds early, or an olive tree shaking off its flowers.
**34** Godless clans end up barren, and fire burns down every tent paid for with bribes.
**35** They're pregnant with trouble, they give birth to harm, and inside they're cooking up fraud."

## Crossings & Lexical Notes

- **v. 2 `דַעַת־רוּחַ` (*da'at ruach*) & `קָדִים` (*qadim*):** *Ruach* means both "spirit/breath" and "wind," paired with *qadim* (the scorching sirocco from the eastern desert)—Eliphaz mocks Job's speech as hot, hollow air.
- **v. 8 `בְּסוֹד אֱלוֹהַ` (*besod Eloah*):** *Sod* denotes an intimate council or confidential assembly (cf. Jer 23:18); Eliphaz sarcastically asks if Job eavesdropped on God's heavenly deliberations.
- **v. 15 `בִּקְדֹשָׁיו` (*biqdoshav*, Qere) vs. `בקדשו` (Ketiv):** The Qere reads plural "his holy ones" (heavenly beings/angels, matching 4:18 and 5:1), whereas the unpointed Ketiv consonant text `בקדשו` can be read singular ("his holy one").
- **v. 22 `וְצָפוּי` (*vetsafuy*, Qere) vs. `וצפו` (Ketiv):** The Qere points this as a passive participle ("watched/marked out for the sword," from *tsafah*), clarifying the obscure Ketiv spelling `וצפו`.
- **v. 31 `בַּשָּׁיו` / `בַּשָּׁוְא` (*bashav*, Qere) vs. `בשו` (Ketiv):** Ketiv omits the final *yod/aleph* mater; the Qere restores the phonetic glide (`בַּשָּׁיו`), yielding "in emptiness/falsehood," echoing `שָׁוְא` (*shav*) in the second half of the line.
- **v. 34 `חָנֵף` (*chanef*):** Commonly "godless," "profane," or "polluted" (one alienated from covenant faithfulness), often rendered "hypocrite" in older English versions.

## Supplemental Texts & Allusions

- **Job 4:17–19** (self-echo within Eliphaz's speeches): Eliphaz repeats his earlier nocturnal oracle almost verbatim in vv. 14–16—no mortal born of woman stands pure before God, who distrusts even heavenly beings.
- **Psalm 7:14** (Hebrew 7:15, shared metaphor): Expresses the same three-stage birth imagery of wickedness—conceiving trouble, being pregnant with mischief, and giving birth to falsehood—paralleled in Job 15:35.
- **Jeremiah 23:18, 22** (thematic contrast on *sod*): Jeremiah asks which prophet has actually stood in the LORD's *sod* (council) to hear his word, using the same rare council term Eliphaz turns sarcastically on Job in v. 8.

## Reference Text (WEB Updated, Public Domain)

**1** Then Eliphaz the Temanite answered,
**2** “Should a wise man answer with vain knowledge, and fill himself with the east wind?
**3** Should he reason with unprofitable talk, or with speeches with which he can do no good?
**4** Yes, you do away with fear, and hinder devotion before God.
**5** For your iniquity teaches your mouth, and you choose the language of the crafty.
**6** Your own mouth condemns you, and not I. Yes, your own lips testify against you.
**7** “Are you the first man who was born? Or were you brought out before the hills?
**8** Have you heard the secret counsel of God? Do you limit wisdom to yourself?
**9** What do you know that we don’t know? What do you understand which is not in us?
**10** With us are both the gray-headed and the very aged men, much older than your father.
**11** Are the consolations of God too small for you, even the word that is gentle toward you?
**12** Why does your heart carry you away? Why do your eyes flash,
**13** that you turn your spirit against God, and let such words go out of your mouth?
**14** What is man, that he should be clean? What is he who is born of a woman, that he should be righteous?
**15** Behold, he puts no trust in his holy ones. Yes, the heavens are not clean in his sight;
**16** how much less one who is abominable and corrupt, a man who drinks iniquity like water!
**17** “I will show you, listen to me; that which I have seen I will declare
**18** (which wise men have told by their fathers, and have not hidden it;
**19** to whom alone the land was given, and no stranger passed among them):
**20** the wicked man writhes in pain all his days, even the number of years that are laid up for the oppressor.
**21** A sound of terrors is in his ears. In prosperity the destroyer will come on him.
**22** He doesn’t believe that he will return out of darkness. He is waited for by the sword.
**23** He wanders abroad for bread, saying, ‘Where is it?’ He knows that the day of darkness is ready at his hand.
**24** Distress and anguish make him afraid. They prevail against him, as a king ready to the battle.
**25** Because he has stretched out his hand against God, and behaves himself proudly against the Almighty,
**26** he runs at him with a stiff neck, with the thick shields of his bucklers,
**27** because he has covered his face with his fatness, and gathered fat on his thighs.
**28** He has lived in desolate cities, in houses which no one inhabited, which were ready to become heaps.
**29** He will not be rich, neither will his substance continue, neither will their possessions be extended on the earth.
**30** He will not depart out of darkness. The flame will dry up his branches. He will go away by the breath of God’s mouth.
**31** Let him not trust in emptiness, deceiving himself, for emptiness will be his reward.
**32** It will be accomplished before his time. His branch will not be green.
**33** He will shake off his unripe grape as the vine, and will cast off his flower as the olive tree.
**34** For the company of the godless will be barren, and fire will consume the tents of bribery.
**35** They conceive mischief and produce iniquity. Their heart prepares deceit.”
