## Clear & Faithful

**1** "A mortal born of woman lives few days and is stuffed full of turmoil.
**2** Like a wildflower he springs up, then shrivels; he slips away like a passing shadow and never stays.
**3** Yet upon someone so fragile do you fix your watchful eyes, hauling me into court with yourself?
**4** Who can bring something pure out of what is impure? Nobody at all!
**5** Since a human's days are strictly allotted and the count of his months lies in your keeping, you have drawn boundaries he cannot step across.
**6** Turn your gaze away from him and grant him respite, until he finishes out his day like a hired laborer!
**7** For a tree still holds hope: if chopped down, it can sprout once more, and its fresh shoots will never fail.
**8** Even if its roots grow old underground and its stump appears dead in the soil,
**9** at the mere scent of water it buds again and puts out branches like a young sapling.
**10** Yet a human dies and lies powerless; a mortal breathes his last—and where is he then?
**11** Like water evaporating from a lake or a river parching and running dry,
**12** a person lies down and never gets up again; until the skies themselves cease to exist, they will not wake or be stirred from their sleep.
**13** Oh, that you would tuck me safely out of sight in Sheol, shielding me until your wrath subsides, setting an appointed time for me and then calling me back to mind!
**14** If a human dies, could he really live again? All the days of my hard service I would wait patiently until my relief arrived!
**15** You would summon me, and I would answer you; you would yearn for the handiwork of your own hands!
**16** Instead, right now you tally every step I take; do you not keep constant watch over my sin?
**17** My offense lies sealed tight inside a bag, and you plaster over my guilt so nothing escapes.
**18** Yet just as a crumbling mountain wears away and a boulder shifts out of its bed,
**19** as rushing water grinds down stones and cloudbursts sweep away the topsoil of the earth—so you wash away a mortal's hope.
**20** You overpower him permanently, and he passes on; altering his appearance, you send him away.
**21** His children rise to honor, and he never knows it; they sink into disgrace, and he notices nothing of them.
**22** He feels nothing except the ache of his own flesh, and his soul mourns solely over himself."

## Everyday English

**1** "Everyone born to a human mother has a short life packed full of trouble.
**2** We bloom briefly like a wildflower and wilt just as fast; we vanish like a moving shadow and never stick around.
**3** Why keep your magnifying glass trained on someone so weak? Why drag *me* into a courtroom showdown with you?
**4** Who can squeeze clean water out of a dirty rag? Nobody can!
**5** You've already clocked how many days and months each person gets, and set a finish line nobody can cross.
**6** So look away for a minute and give them a break—let them at least finish their shift like a tired day-worker!
**7** A tree at least has something to hope for: chop it down, and it can sprout back up with brand-new shoots.
**8** Even if its roots get old underground and the stump looks dead in the dirt,
**9** just one whiff of water makes it bud and push out green limbs like a freshly planted tree!
**10** Humans aren't like that—when a person dies, they go limp; they take one last breath, and poof, where did they go?
**11** Like a pond drying up in a drought or a riverbed cracking under the sun,
**12** a person lies down and never stands back up. Until the sky itself disappears, they won't wake up or stir from that sleep.
**13** I just wish you'd hide me away down in the grave, tucking me out of sight until your anger cools off—set a timer on it and then remember me!
**14** Wait—if a person dies, can they really come back to life? If so, I'd gladly hold out through my entire miserable shift until my replacement arrived!
**15** You'd call my name, and I'd answer right back—because you'd actually miss the person your own hands shaped!
**16** Instead, you're counting every single step I take and keeping a microscope on my mistakes.
**17** You've locked my sins inside a sealed evidence bag and taped it shut so nothing gets missed!
**18** Look: even a mountain eventually erodes and falls; boulders break loose from cliffs;
**19** water slowly wears away solid rock, and flash floods wash the dirt right off the hills—that's how you wash away human hope!
**20** You overpower someone once and for all, and they're gone; you twist their face in death and send them packing.
**21** If their kids end up famous, they never find out; if their kids fall apart, they never notice.
**22** All they feel left in the end is their own body hurting and their own heart grieving."

## Crossings & Lexical Notes

- **v. 5 `חקו` / `חֻקָּיו` (*chuqqo* / *chuqqav*):** Ketiv reads singular `חקו` ("his prescribed limit/decree"), while Qere reads plural `חֻקָּיו` ("his limits/decrees")—the fixed boundary of human lifespan no mortal can cross (`וְלֹא יַעֲבוֹר`).
- **v. 9 `מֵרֵיחַ מַיִם` (*me-reach mayim*):** Literally "from the scent/aroma of water"—poetic personification of a dormant stump catching the faintest moisture in the soil and springing back to life, sharpening the contrast with human finality in vv. 10–12.
- **v. 13 `בִּשְׁאוֹל תַּצְפִּנֵנִי` (*bi-She'ol tatspineni*):** Remarkably, Job asks to be *hidden in Sheol* as a temporary shelter against God's wrath until God's anger turns back and God sets an appointed time (*choq*) to remember him—a fleeting, conditional hypothesis ("If a mortal dies, could he live again?" v. 14) rather than a settled doctrine yet.
- **v. 14 `צְבָאִי ... חֲלִיפָתִי` (*tseva'i ... chalifati*):** Echoes 7:1 (*tsava*, "conscripted military duty") and 10:17 (*chalifot*, "relief troops"), picturing death-and-renewal as waiting out a sentry shift until one's relief arrives.
- **v. 17 `וַתִּטְפֹּל עַל־עֲוֺנִי` (*va-titpol al-avoni*):** Uses the same verb *tafal* ("smear/plaster over") Job just hurled at his friends in 13:4 (*tofelei-shaqer*): God seals Job's alleged transgressions in a leather pouch (*tseror*) and plasters over the seal so not one flaw slips out!
- **v. 22 `וְנַפְשׁוֹ עָלָיו תֶּאֱבָל` (*ve-nafsho alav te'eval*):** Closes Cycle 1 of the dialogues in stark solitude—an isolated existence aware only of its own bodily pain and inward mourning.

## Supplemental Texts & Allusions

- **Psalm 90:5–6 & Psalm 103:15–16** (flower and shadow): Compare human life to wildflowers that bloom briefly and wither under the wind, and to a fleeting shadow (vv. 1–2).
- **Isaiah 26:19 & 1 Corinthians 15:35–44** (resurrection longing): Job's poignant question *"If a mortal dies, will he live again?"* (v. 14) stands as an early existential ache that later biblical revelation answers directly.
- **Psalm 56:8 (MT 56:9) & Hosea 13:12** (sealed record): Parallel v. 17's image of iniquity bound up and sealed in a storage pouch (*tseror*) awaiting judgment.

## Reference Text (WEB Updated, Public Domain)

**1** “Man, who is born of a woman, is of few days, and full of trouble.
**2** He grows up like a flower, and is cut down. He also flees like a shadow, and doesn’t continue.
**3** Do you open your eyes on such a one, and bring me into judgment with you?
**4** Who can bring a clean thing out of an unclean? Not one.
**5** Seeing his days are determined, the number of his months is with you, and you have appointed his bounds that he can’t pass.
**6** Look away from him, that he may rest, until he accomplishes, as a hireling, his day.
**7** “For there is hope for a tree if it is cut down, that it will sprout again, that the tender branch of it will not cease.
**8** Though its root grows old in the earth, and its stock dies in the ground,
**9** yet through the scent of water it will bud, and sprout boughs like a plant.
**10** But man dies, and is laid low. Yes, man gives up the spirit, and where is he?
**11** As the waters fail from the sea, and the river wastes and dries up,
**12** so man lies down and doesn’t rise. Until the heavens are no more, they will not awake, nor be roused out of their sleep.
**13** “Oh that you would hide me in Sheol, that you would keep me secret until your wrath is past, that you would appoint me a set time and remember me!
**14** If a man dies, will he live again? I would wait all the days of my warfare, until my release should come.
**15** You would call, and I would answer you. You would have a desire for the work of your hands.
**16** But now you count my steps. Don’t you watch over my sin?
**17** My disobedience is sealed up in a bag. You fasten up my iniquity.
**18** “But the mountain falling comes to nothing. The rock is removed out of its place.
**19** The waters wear the stones. The torrents of it wash away the dust of the earth. So you destroy the hope of man.
**20** You forever prevail against him, and he departs. You change his face, and send him away.
**21** His sons come to honor, and he doesn’t know it. They are brought low, but he doesn’t perceive it of them.
**22** But his flesh on him has pain, and his soul within him mourns.”
