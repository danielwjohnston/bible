## Clear & Faithful

**1** Once more the day arrived when the sons of God came to present themselves before the LORD, and the Adversary also entered among them to present himself before the LORD.
**2** The LORD asked the Adversary, "From what quarter do you come?" The Adversary replied to the LORD, "From ranging across the earth and patrolling to and fro within it."
**3** Then the LORD asked the Adversary, "Have you fixed your attention on my servant Job? Nobody on earth matches him—a blameless and upright man who reveres God and shuns evil. He still clings tightly to his integrity, even though you provoked me against him to ruin him without cause."
**4** The Adversary answered the LORD, "Skin for skin! Whatever a mortal owns, he will surrender to preserve his own life.
**5** Yet extend your hand now and strike his very bone and flesh—surely he will curse you straight to your face!"
**6** The LORD told the Adversary, "Very well, he is placed in your power; only spare his life."
**7** So the Adversary departed from the presence of the LORD and afflicted Job with malignant sores from the sole of his foot to the crown of his head.
**8** Job took a piece of broken pottery to scrape himself as he sat among the ashes.
**9** Then his wife addressed him, "Do you still cling to your integrity? Curse God and die!"
**10** Yet he answered her, "You speak like one of the foolish women speaks! Shall we accept good from God, and not also accept adversity?" Throughout all of this, Job did not sin with his lips.
**11** Now Job's three companions heard of every calamity that had befallen him, and each traveled from his homeland—Eliphaz from Teman, Bildad from Shuah, and Zophar from Naamah. They arranged together to come and mourn alongside him and console him.
**12** Looking up from a distance, they could scarcely recognize him; raising their voices, they wept aloud, each tearing his outer garment and flinging dust skyward over their heads.
**13** They sat beside him on the ground for seven days and seven nights, nobody uttering a single word to him, because they perceived how immense his agony had become.

## Everyday English

**1** Another day came when the heavenly beings showed up for roll call before the LORD, and the Accuser turned up among them to stand before him too.
**2** "Where are you coming from this time?" the LORD asked the Accuser. "Just roaming the earth and walking back and forth across it," he replied.
**3** Then the LORD told the Accuser, "Have you been watching my servant Job? Nobody else on earth is like him. He's completely honest and decent, honors God, and stays away from evil. Even after you talked me into wrecking his life for no reason at all, he hasn't let go of his integrity!"
**4** "Skin for skin," the Accuser countered. "People will trade anything they own just to keep breathing!
**5** Reach out and attack his own bones and skin—watch how fast he'll curse you to your face!"
**6** "Go ahead, then," the LORD said to the Accuser. "He's in your hands—just don't take his life."
**7** So the Accuser left the LORD's presence and covered Job with agonizing boils from head to toe.
**8** Sitting out in an ash heap, Job picked up a shard of broken clay to scratch at his sores.
**9** His wife looked at him and said, "Are you seriously still holding onto your integrity? Just curse God and get death over with!"
**10** Job answered her, "You're talking just like a foolish woman! If we gladly take the good things God sends, shouldn't we take the painful things too?" Through it all, Job didn't say one sinful word.
**11** When Job's three friends heard about all the disasters that had crushed him, they set out from their own towns—Eliphaz the Temanite, Bildad the Shuhite, and Zophar the Naamathite—and coordinated a trip to sit with him and comfort him.
**12** While they were still down the road, they spotted him and didn't even know it was him. They broke into loud sobs, ripped their cloaks, and tossed handfuls of dirt into the air over their heads.
**13** For seven straight days and nights they sat silently with him on the dirt, not saying a word, because they could see his pain was unbearable.

## Crossings & Lexical Notes

- **v. 3 `תֻּמָּתוֹ` (*tummato*):** "His integrity/completeness," cognate with *tam* ("blameless") in 1:1, 8 and 2:3; here coupled with *chinnam* ("for nothing / without cause"), answering the Adversary's taunt in 1:9 (*ha-chinnam*, "does Job fear God for nothing?").
- **v. 4 `עוֹר בְּעַד־עוֹר` (*or be'ad-or*):** Literally "skin in exchange for skin," an ancient barter proverb suggesting a person will gladly sacrifice another's hide (family, herds) to save his own skin and life (*nefesh*).
- **v. 7 `עד` / `וְעַד` (*ve-ad*):** Ketiv lacks the prefixed conjunction (`עד` "to"), while Qere supplies `וְעַד` ("and even to"), completing the idiomatic merism "from the sole of his foot even to his crown."
- **v. 9 `בָּרֵךְ אֱלֹהִים` (*barekh Elohim*):** Literally "Bless God," continuing the scribal antiphrasis/euphemism from 1:5, 11 for "Curse God."
- **v. 10 `הַנְּבָלוֹת` (*ha-nevalot*):** Feminine plural of *naval* ("morally senseless, impious, base"), referring not merely to intellectual folly but to irreverent speech that treats God recklessly.
- **v. 11 `לָנוּד־לוֹ וּלְנַחֲמוֹ` (*lanud-lo u-lenachamo*):** *Nud* literally means "to shake the head / sway in grief" (expressing shared sorrow), paired with *nacham* ("to comfort/console"), a root that threads through the entire book (`16:2`, `42:6`, `42:11`).

## Supplemental Texts & Allusions

- **Deuteronomy 28:35** (covenant curse imagery): Uses the identical phrase "severe boils from the sole of your foot to the crown of your head" (*bishechin ra ... mi-kaf raglekha ve-ad qodqodekha*) as a dreaded bodily affliction.
- **Genesis 37:34 & Joshua 7:6** (mourning rites): Tearing robes, casting dust on the head, and sitting on the earth mirror classic Israelite lamentation gestures in times of extreme bereavement or calamity.
- **Psalm 14:1 / 53:1** (*naval* motif): Connects the "foolish" (*naval*) person with inner denial or irreverence toward God, illuminating Job's rebuke in 2:10.

## Reference Text (WEB Updated, Public Domain)

**1** Again, on the day when God’s sons came to present themselves before the LORD, Satan came also among them to present himself before the LORD.
**2** The LORD said to Satan, “Where have you come from?” Satan answered the LORD, and said, “From going back and forth on the earth, and from walking up and down in it.”
**3** The LORD said to Satan, “Have you considered my servant Job? For there is no one like him on the earth, a blameless and an upright man, one who fears God, and turns away from evil. He still maintains his integrity, although you incited me against him, to ruin him without cause.”
**4** Satan answered the LORD, and said, “Skin for skin. Yes, all that a man has he will give for his life.
**5** But stretch out your hand now, and touch his bone and his flesh, and he will renounce you to your face.”
**6** The LORD said to Satan, “Behold, he is in your hand. Only spare his life.”
**7** So Satan went out from the presence of the LORD, and struck Job with painful sores from the sole of his foot to his head.
**8** He took for himself a potsherd to scrape himself with, and he sat among the ashes.
**9** Then his wife said to him, “Do you still maintain your integrity? Renounce God, and die.”
**10** But he said to her, “You speak as one of the foolish women would speak. What? Shall we receive good at the hand of God, and shall we not receive evil?” In all this Job didn’t sin with his lips.
**11** Now when Job’s three friends heard of all this evil that had come on him, they each came from his own place: Eliphaz the Temanite, Bildad the Shuhite, and Zophar the Naamathite; and they made an appointment together to come to sympathize with him and to comfort him.
**12** When they lifted up their eyes from a distance, and didn’t recognize him, they raised their voices, and wept; and they each tore his robe, and sprinkled dust on their heads toward the sky.
**13** So they sat down with him on the ground seven days and seven nights, and no one spoke a word to him, for they saw that his grief was very great.
