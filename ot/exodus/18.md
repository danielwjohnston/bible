## Clear & Faithful

**1** Now Jethro, the priest of Midian, Moses' father-in-law, heard of everything that God had done for Moses and for Israel his people—how the LORD had brought Israel out of Egypt.
**2** So Jethro, Moses' father-in-law, took Zipporah, Moses' wife, after she had been sent back,
**3** along with her two sons. The name of one was Gershom, for Moses had said, "I have been a sojourner in a foreign land,"
**4** and the name of the other was Eliezer, for he had said, "The God of my father was my help, and delivered me from the sword of Pharaoh."
**5** Jethro, Moses' father-in-law, came with Moses' sons and wife to Moses in the wilderness where he was encamped at the mountain of God.
**6** He sent word to Moses: "I, your father-in-law Jethro, am coming to you with your wife and her two sons with her."
**7** Moses went out to meet his father-in-law, bowed low, and kissed him; they asked each other how they were, and went into the tent.
**8** Then Moses told his father-in-law everything that the LORD had done to Pharaoh and to the Egyptians for Israel's sake, all the hardship that had met them along the way, and how the LORD had delivered them.
**9** Jethro rejoiced over all the good that the LORD had done for Israel in delivering them from the hand of the Egyptians.
**10** And Jethro said, "Blessed be the LORD, who has delivered you from the hand of the Egyptians and from the hand of Pharaoh, and who has rescued the people from under the hand of Egypt!
**11** Now I know that the LORD is greater than all gods, because in the very thing in which they acted arrogantly against them, he proved supreme."
**12** Then Jethro, Moses' father-in-law, brought a burnt offering and sacrifices to God; and Aaron and all the elders of Israel came to eat a meal with Moses' father-in-law before God.
**13** The next day, Moses sat down to judge cases for the people, and the people stood around Moses from morning until evening.
**14** When Moses' father-in-law saw all that he was doing for the people, he asked, "What is this that you are doing for the people? Why do you sit alone as judge while all the people stand around you from morning until evening?"
**15** Moses answered his father-in-law, "Because the people come to me to inquire of God.
**16** Whenever they have a dispute, it comes before me, and I judge between one person and another, and I make known the statutes of God and his instructions."
**17** Moses' father-in-law said to him, "What you are doing is not good.
**18** You will surely wear yourself out—both you and these people with you—for the task is too heavy for you; you cannot handle it alone.
**19** Listen now to my advice, and may God be with you: you represent the people before God and bring their disputes to God;
**20** warn and teach them the statutes and the instructions, and show them the way they must walk and the work they must do.
**21** Meanwhile, select from all the people capable men who fear God, trustworthy men who hate dishonest gain, and appoint them as leaders over thousands, hundreds, fifties, and tens.
**22** Let them judge the people at all times; every major case they shall bring to you, while every minor case they themselves shall decide. So lighten the burden from yourself, and let them share it with you.
**23** If you do this, and God so directs you, you will be able to endure, and all these people will also go home in peace."
**24** Moses listened to the voice of his father-in-law and did everything he had said.
**25** Moses chose capable men out of all Israel and appointed them as heads over the people—leaders of thousands, hundreds, fifties, and tens.
**26** They judged the people at all times; the difficult cases they brought to Moses, while every minor case they decided themselves.
**27** Then Moses saw his father-in-law off, and Jethro returned to his own land.

## Everyday English

**1** Moses' father-in-law Jethro, the priest of Midian, heard all about what God had done for Moses and his people Israel—especially how the LORD had rescued Israel from Egypt.
**2** Earlier, Moses had sent his wife Zipporah back home, so Jethro brought her along
**3** together with her two boys. One son was named Gershom (because Moses had said, "I've been living as a foreigner in a strange country"),
**4** and the other was named Eliezer (because Moses had said, "My father's God helped me and saved me from Pharaoh's sword").
**5** Jethro arrived at Moses' camp in the desert near God's mountain, bringing Moses' wife and two sons with him.
**6** He sent a message ahead to Moses: "Your father-in-law Jethro is here to see you, along with your wife and her two sons."
**7** Moses hurried out to welcome his father-in-law, bowed respectfully, and kissed him. After asking how each other was doing, they stepped inside the tent.
**8** There Moses told Jethro the whole story of everything the LORD had done to Pharaoh and Egypt for Israel's sake, every trouble they had faced on the journey, and how the LORD kept rescuing them.
**9** Jethro was thrilled to hear about all the goodness the LORD had shown Israel by freeing them from Egypt's grip.
**10** "Praise the LORD!" Jethro exclaimed. "He rescued you from Pharaoh and the Egyptians, and pulled this whole nation out from under Egyptian control!
**11** Now I'm convinced that the LORD is greater than every other god—especially because he defeated the Egyptians in the very way they tried to bully Israel!"
**12** Then Jethro brought a burnt offering and other sacrifices to God, and Aaron joined all of Israel's elders to share a sacred meal with Moses' father-in-law in God's presence.
**13** The following morning, Moses took his seat to settle legal disputes for the people, and crowds stood waiting around him from sunrise till sunset.
**14** Watching everything Moses was trying to handle, Jethro asked him, "What are you doing to these people? Why are you sitting here all by yourself while everyone lines up around you from dawn until dusk?"
**15** "People come to me to find out what God wants," Moses replied.
**16** "Whenever two people have an argument, they bring it to me. I decide who's right and teach them God's rules and instructions."
**17** "What you're doing isn't smart," Moses' father-in-law told him.
**18** "You're going to burn yourself out completely—and exhaust the people along with you! This job is way too heavy for one person; you can't possibly carry it alone.
**19** Take my advice, and may God guide you: you serve as the people's representative before God, bringing their hard questions to him.
**20** Clearly explain God's rules and teachings to them, and show them how to live and what to do.
**21** At the same time, pick capable leaders from the whole nation—people who revere God, speak the truth, and can't be bribed with crooked money. Put them in charge of groups of a thousand, a hundred, fifty, and ten.
**22** Have them settle everyday disputes full-time. They can refer only the major cases to you and handle all the routine ones themselves. That'll take the weight off your shoulders and let them share the load.
**23** If you set it up this way—and God approves it—you'll actually hold up under the pressure, and all these people can head back to their tents satisfied and at peace."
**24** Moses took his father-in-law's advice and put the entire plan into action.
**25** He picked capable leaders from across Israel and placed them over the people as officials in charge of thousands, hundreds, fifties, and tens.
**26** From then on, they judged everyday cases around the clock—sending only the toughest disputes to Moses while settling all the smaller matters themselves.
**27** Soon afterward, Moses said goodbye to his father-in-law, and Jethro traveled back home to his own country.

## Crossings & Lexical Notes

- **v. 3 `גֵּרְשֹׁם` (*Gershom*):** Wordplay connecting the name to `גֵּר שָׁם` (*ger sham*, "a resident alien / sojourner there"), repeating the etiology from Exod 2:22.
- **v. 4 `אֱלִיעֶזֶר` (*Eliezer*):** Compound name from `אֵלִי` (*Eli*, "my God") + `עֶזֶר` (*ezer*, "help"), marking Moses' deliverance from Pharaoh's pursuit.
- **v. 9 `וַיִּחַדְּ` (*vayyichadd*):** Rare verb from `חָדָה` (*chadah*, "to be glad/rejoice"), expressing deep personal delight at YHWH's deliverance.
- **v. 11 `זָדוּ עֲלֵיהֶם` (*zadu alehem*):** Literally "because in the thing in which they acted presumptuously/arrogantly against them"—an abrupt Hebrew clause implying that Egypt's own arrogant scheme (drowning/enslaving) recoiled upon them.
- **v. 18 `נָבֹל תִּבֹּל` (*navol tibbol*):** Infinitive absolute + imperfect of `נָבֵל` (*navel*, "wilt/wither like a leaf"), vividly picturing exhaustion and burnout.
- **v. 21 `אַנְשֵׁי־חַיִל` (*anshe-chayil*):** "Men of strength/ability/moral character" (*chayil* spans competence, courage, and integrity), defined by three qualifiers: fearing God, truthfulness, and hatred of `בָּצַע` (*batsa*, "dishonest gain/bribery").
