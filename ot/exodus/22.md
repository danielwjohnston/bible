## Clear & Faithful

**1** "When a man steals an ox or a sheep and slaughters it or sells it, he shall pay back five oxen for an ox and four sheep for a sheep.
**2** If a thief is caught breaking in and is struck so that he dies, there shall be no bloodguilt for him;
**3** yet if the sun has risen upon him, there is bloodguilt for him. A thief must make full restitution; if he has nothing, he shall be sold for his theft.
**4** If the stolen animal is found alive in his possession—whether ox or donkey or sheep—he shall pay back double.
**5** When a man lets a field or vineyard be grazed over and lets his livestock loose to graze in another's field, he shall make restitution from the best of his own field and the best of his own vineyard.
**6** When fire breaks out and catches in thorns so that stacked grain, standing grain, or the field itself is consumed, the one who started the fire shall surely make restitution.
**7** When a man gives his neighbor silver or goods for safekeeping, and they are stolen from that man's house, if the thief is caught, he shall pay back double.
**8** If the thief is not caught, the homeowner shall be brought before God to determine whether he has put his hand to his neighbor's property.
**9** In every case of disputed ownership—whether concerning an ox, a donkey, a sheep, a cloak, or any lost item about which someone claims, 'This is it'—the case of both parties shall come before God; whoever God declares guilty shall pay double to his neighbor.
**10** When a man gives his neighbor a donkey, an ox, a sheep, or any beast for safekeeping, and it dies, is injured, or is driven off with no one seeing it,
**11** an oath before the LORD shall settle the matter between the two of them that he has not laid hands on his neighbor's property; the owner shall accept the oath, and he shall not pay restitution.
**12** But if it was actually stolen from him, he shall make restitution to its owner.
**13** If it was torn to pieces by a wild beast, let him bring it as evidence; he shall not make restitution for what was torn.
**14** When a man borrows an animal from his neighbor and it is injured or dies while its owner is not with it, he shall surely make restitution.
**15** If its owner is with it, he shall not make restitution; if it was hired, it came under its hire fee.
**16** When a man seduces a virgin who is not betrothed and lies with her, he shall surely pay the bride-price to make her his wife.
**17** If her father utterly refuses to give her to him, he shall weigh out silver equal to the bride-price for virgins.
**18** You shall not permit a sorceress to live.
**19** Whoever lies with an animal shall surely be put to death.
**20** Whoever sacrifices to any god other than the LORD alone shall be devoted to destruction.
**21** You shall not wrong a sojourner or oppress him, for you were sojourners in the land of Egypt.
**22** You shall not mistreat any widow or fatherless child.
**23** If you ever mistreat them and they cry out to me, I will surely hear their cry;
**24** my wrath will burn, and I will kill you with the sword, so that your own wives become widows and your children fatherless.
**25** If you lend silver to any of my people—to the poor person among you—you shall not act like a creditor toward him, nor shall you charge him interest.
**26** If you ever take your neighbor's cloak as collateral, you shall return it to him before sunset,
**27** for that is his only covering; it is his garment for his body. What else will he sleep in? When he cries out to me, I will hear, for I am gracious.
**28** You shall not revile God, nor shall you curse a ruler of your people.
**29** You shall not delay offering from the fullness of your harvest and the flow of your presses; you shall give me the firstborn of your sons.
**30** You shall do the same with your cattle and your flocks: seven days it shall remain with its mother, and on the eighth day you shall give it to me.
**31** You shall be consecrated people to me; therefore you shall not eat any flesh torn by beasts in the field—throw it to the dogs."

## Everyday English

**1** "If someone steals an ox or a sheep and butchers or sells it, they must pay back five head of cattle for each ox and four sheep for each sheep.
**2** If a burglar is caught breaking into a home at night and gets killed in the struggle, the homeowner isn't guilty of murder.
**3** But if it happens after sunrise, the homeowner is guilty of bloodshed. A thief must always pay full compensation; anyone who owns nothing to pay with must be sold into service to cover the theft.
**4** If the stolen ox, donkey, or sheep is recovered alive in the thief's possession, the thief must pay back twice its value.
**5** If anyone turns their livestock loose to graze and the animals wander into a neighbor's field or vineyard and eat the crops, the owner must repay the loss with the finest produce from their own field or vineyard.
**6** If a fire gets out of hand, spreads through dry brush, and burns up a neighbor's stacked grain, standing crops, or whole field, whoever started the fire must pay for everything destroyed.
**7** Suppose someone leaves money or valuables with a neighbor for safekeeping, and they're stolen from the neighbor's house. If the thief is caught, the thief pays back double.
**8** If the thief isn't caught, the neighbor must appear before God's judges to swear they didn't steal their friend's belongings themselves.
**9** In any dispute over lost property—an ox, donkey, sheep, coat, or anything missing that someone identifies as theirs—both people must bring the case before God's court. Whichever one the court finds guilty must pay the other back double.
**10** Suppose someone leaves a donkey, ox, sheep, or any other animal in a neighbor's care, and it dies, gets crippled, or is rustled away with nobody watching.
**11** A solemn oath before the LORD settles the dispute between them, confirming the caretaker didn't steal the animal. The owner must accept that oath, and the caretaker owes nothing.
**12** However, if the animal was stolen right from the caretaker's property, the caretaker must pay the owner back.
**13** If a wild predator tore it apart, the caretaker must bring the remains as proof and owes nothing for an animal killed by predators.
**14** If someone borrows an animal from a neighbor and it gets injured or dies while the owner isn't present, the borrower must pay for it in full.
**15** If the owner was right there when it happened, the borrower doesn't owe anything; and if it was a rented animal, the rental fee covers the risk.
**16** If a man seduces a young unmarried woman who isn't engaged and sleeps with her, he must pay the full wedding gift and marry her.
**17** If her father flatly refuses to let her marry him, the man still has to pay silver equal to the standard wedding price.
**18** Do not allow any practitioner of sorcery to remain alive.
**19** Anyone who has sex with an animal must be put to death.
**20** Anyone who offers sacrifices to any god other than the LORD alone must be completely destroyed.
**21** Never mistreat or take advantage of foreigners living among you—remember, you yourselves were foreigners in Egypt!
**22** Never abuse any widow or orphan.
**23** If you ever exploit them and they cry out to me for help, I promise I will hear their cry!
**24** My anger will blaze up, and I'll strike you down by the sword—leaving your own wives as widows and your own children as orphans!
**25** Whenever you loan money to anyone among my people who is poor, don't act like a ruthless moneylender and don't charge them interest.
**26** If you take your neighbor's coat as security for a loan, give it back before sundown,
**27** because that coat is their only blanket—the wrap over their bare skin! What else will they sleep in? If they cry out to me, I will listen, because I am compassionate.
**28** Never slander God, and never curse a leader among your people.
**29** Don't hold back offerings from your grain harvests or your wine and olive presses. Dedicate your firstborn sons to me,
**30** and do the exact same with your cattle and sheep: let the newborn stay with its mother for seven days, then give it to me on the eighth day.
**31** You are to be my sacred people. So never eat meat from an animal mauled in the field—toss it to the dogs."

## Crossings & Lexical Notes

- **v. 1 `כִּי יִגְנֹב־אִישׁ` (*ki yignov-ish*, WLC 21:37 = English 22:1):** Versification shift across the entire chapter: WLC attaches `Exod 21:37` to the end of chapter 21 and numbers `Exod 22:1–30` one verse lower than English `22:2–31` (so English `22:1` = WLC `21:37`, and English `22:2–31` = WLC `22:1–30`).
- **v. 2 `בַּמַּחְתֶּרֶת` (*ba-machteret*):** Literally "in the tunneling/digging through" (mud-brick walls at night), distinguishing nocturnal self-defense from daytime pursuit (`v. 3`).
- **v. 5 `בְּעִיר֔וֹ` (*be'iro*):** *Ketiv/Qere* variant (`WLC 22:4`): written *Ketiv* is `בעירה` (older/dialectal 3ms suffix `-ה`), while *Qere* is `בְּעִיר֔וֹ` ("his grazing livestock/beasts," from `בְּעִיר`).
- **v. 8 `אֶל־הָאֱלֹהִים` (*el ha-Elohim*):** "To God" (or judicial authorities/sanctuary court acting in God's name, as in `v. 9` and `21:6`; cf. `v. 28`).
- **v. 16 `מֹהַר` (*mohar*):** Traditional marriage-gift/bride-price transferred from the groom's family to the bride's household (`v. 17`), distinct from a dowry.
- **v. 20 `יָחֳרָם` (*yochoram*):** Hophal of `חָרַם` (*charam*), "devoted to irrevocable destruction/ban" for high treason against exclusive loyalty to YHWH.
- **v. 25 `נֶשֶׁךְ` (*neshech*):** "Interest/usury" (lit. "a bite," from *nashakh* "to bite"), forbidden on subsistence loans to the Israelite poor.
- **v. 27 `כְּסוּתוֹ` (*kesuto*):** *Ketiv/Qere* variant (`WLC 22:26`): written *Ketiv* is `כסותה` (`-ה` archaic 3ms suffix), while *Qere* is `כְסוּתוֹ` ("his covering/outer garment"), paired with YHWH's self-description `חַנּוּן` (*channun*, "gracious/compassionate," anticipating `34:6`).
- **v. 29 `מְלֵאָתְךָ וְדִמְעֲךָ` (*mele'atkha ve-dim'akha*):** Literally "your fullness [grain tithe] and your tear/trickle [pressed wine and olive oil]"—poetic agricultural terms for firstfruits.

## Supplemental Texts & Allusions

- **Luke 19:8** (echoes Exod 22:1, 4, 7): *"Zacchaeus pledges fourfold restitution for anything he has defrauded—mirroring the Covenant Code's fourfold penalty for a stolen sheep."*
- **2 Samuel 12:6** (echoes Exod 22:1): *"David pronounces a fourfold restitution penalty on the rich man in Nathan's parable who stole a poor neighbor's single lamb."*
- **Acts 23:5** (cites Exod 22:28): *"Paul quotes the prohibition against speaking evil of a ruler of the people after realizing he has addressed the high priest."*
- **James 5:4; Deuteronomy 24:12–15** (echoes Exod 22:23, 26–27): *"Affirms that God hears the outcry of the poor and exploited whose wages or evening cloaks are wrongfully withheld."*

**3** If the sun has risen on him, he is guilty of bloodshed. He shall make restitution. If he has nothing, then he shall be sold for his theft.
**4** If the stolen property is found in his hand alive, whether it is ox, donkey, or sheep, he shall pay double.

**6** “If fire breaks out, and catches in thorns so that the shocks of grain, or the standing grain, or the field are consumed; he who kindled the fire shall surely make restitution.
**7** “If a man delivers to his neighbor money or stuff to keep, and it is stolen out of the man’s house, if the thief is found, he shall pay double.
**8** If the thief isn’t found, then the master of the house shall come near to God, to find out whether or not he has put his hand on his neighbor’s goods.
**9** For every matter of trespass, whether it is for ox, for donkey, for sheep, for clothing, or for any kind of lost thing, about which one says, ‘This is mine,’ the cause of both parties shall come before God. He whom God condemns shall pay double to his neighbor.

**19** “Whoever has sex with an animal shall surely be put to death.
**20** “He who sacrifices to any god, except to the LORD only, shall be utterly destroyed.
**21** “You shall not wrong an alien or oppress him, for you were aliens in the land of Egypt.
**22** “You shall not take advantage of any widow or fatherless child.
