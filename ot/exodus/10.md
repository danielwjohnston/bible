## Clear & Faithful

**1** Then the LORD said to Moses, "Go in to Pharaoh, for I have made his heart and the hearts of his officials heavy so that I may perform these signs of mine among them,
**2** and so that you may tell in the hearing of your children and your grandchildren how decisively I dealt with Egypt and the signs I displayed among them—that you all may know that I am the LORD."
**3** So Moses and Aaron went in to Pharaoh and declared to him, "Thus says the LORD, the God of the Hebrews: 'How long will you refuse to humble yourself before me? Release my people so they may worship me.
**4** For if you refuse to release my people, tomorrow I will bring locusts into your territory.
**5** They will blanket the surface of the land so that no one can see the ground; they will devour whatever survived the hailstorm, consuming every tree growing for you in the fields.
**6** They will fill your palace, the homes of all your officials, and the homes of every Egyptian—a sight neither your fathers nor your grandfathers ever witnessed from the day they appeared on the earth until today.'" With that, Moses turned and walked out from Pharaoh's presence.
**7** Pharaoh's officials pleaded with him, "How long will this man remain a trap for us? Let the men go so they may worship the LORD their God! Do you not yet realize that Egypt is ruined?"
**8** So Moses and Aaron were brought back to Pharaoh, who said to them, "Go, worship the LORD your God. Exactly who is going?"
**9** Moses replied, "We will go with our young and our old; with our sons and daughters, and with our flocks and herds we must go, because we are holding a pilgrim festival to the LORD."
**10** Pharaoh retorted, "The LORD certainly had better be with you if I ever let you and your children go! Look out—trouble is staring you in the face!
**11** No indeed! Only the adult men may go and worship the LORD, since that is what you were asking for." And they were driven out from Pharaoh's presence.
**12** Then the LORD told Moses, "Stretch out your hand over the land of Egypt for the locusts, so they may swarm over Egypt and devour every plant in the land—everything the hail spared."
**13** Moses stretched out his staff over the land of Egypt, and the LORD drove an east wind across the country all that day and all night; by morning the east wind had carried in the locusts.
**14** The locusts descended upon the entire land of Egypt and settled within every Egyptian border in an unprecedented swarm—never before had there been such a locust plague, nor will there ever be again.
**15** They covered the surface of the whole country until the ground turned dark; they devoured every plant on the land and every piece of tree fruit left after the hail, leaving nothing green on any tree or field plant anywhere in Egypt.
**16** Pharaoh hastily summoned Moses and Aaron and confessed, "I have sinned against the LORD your God and against you.
**17** Please forgive my sin just this once, and pray to the LORD your God to remove at least this deadly plague from me."
**18** Moses left Pharaoh and prayed to the LORD.
**19** Then the LORD shifted the wind to a fierce west wind off the sea, which lifted up the locusts and swept them into the Sea of Reeds; not a single locust remained anywhere within Egypt's borders.
**20** Still the LORD hardened Pharaoh's heart, and he did not release the Israelites.
**21** Next the LORD told Moses, "Stretch out your hand toward the sky, and darkness will fall over the land of Egypt—a darkness thick enough to touch."
**22** Moses stretched out his hand toward the sky, and pitch-black gloom enveloped the entire land of Egypt for three days.
**23** Nobody could see anyone else, nor did anyone rise from his place for three whole days; yet all the Israelites had light where they lived.
**24** Pharaoh called for Moses and said, "Go, worship the LORD; only your flocks and herds must stay behind. Even your children may go with you."
**25** Moses replied, "You must also place sacrifices and burnt offerings in our hands for us to offer to the LORD our God.
**26** Our livestock must go with us too—not a single hoof shall remain behind—because from them we must take offerings to worship the LORD our God, and we do not know what we are to offer the LORD until we arrive there."
**27** Yet the LORD hardened Pharaoh's heart, and he was unwilling to let them go.
**28** Pharaoh snapped at him, "Get out of my sight! Watch yourself—do not ever appear before my face again, for the day you see my face you will die!"
**29** Moses answered, "You have spoken accurately: I will never see your face again."

## Everyday English

**1** Next, the LORD told Moses, "Go back to Pharaoh. I'm the one who has made him and his advisors stubborn, so I can put these miracles on display right in front of them—
**2** and so you can tell your kids and grandkids the story of how I humbled Egypt and all the wonders I performed there. Then every one of you will know that I am the LORD."
**3** Moses and Aaron went back to Pharaoh and announced, "Here is what the LORD, the God of the Hebrews, asks: 'How long are you going to refuse to bow before me? Let my people go so they can worship me!
**4** If you still refuse to release my people, watch out—tomorrow I'm sending a massive swarm of locusts into your country.
**5** They'll blanket the entire landscape so thickly you won't even be able to see the dirt. They'll strip whatever little was left after the hailstorm, chewing down every tree sprouting in your fields.
**6** They'll pack your palace, your officials' houses, and every Egyptian home—more than your parents or grandparents ever laid eyes on since the day people first lived on earth!'" Without waiting for an answer, Moses spun around and walked out.
**7** Pharaoh's own advisors begged him, "How long are we going to let this man trap us in disaster? Let the men leave so they can worship the LORD their God! Can't you see Egypt is already destroyed?"
**8** So Pharaoh had Moses and Aaron brought back in. "Fine, go worship the LORD your God," he said. "Just tell me: who exactly is going?"
**9** "Everyone!" Moses replied. "We're taking our kids and our elderly, our sons and our daughters, plus all our sheep, goats, and cattle—because we're celebrating a sacred festival for the LORD."
**10** Pharaoh sneered, "The LORD really would have to be on your side if I ever let you walk off with your families! Watch out—I see evil intentions written all over your faces!
**11** Forget it! Only the grown men can go worship the LORD, since that's what you asked for." With that, Pharaoh had them thrown out of his palace.
**12** Then the LORD instructed Moses, "Reach your hand out over Egypt to summon the locusts, so they swarm across the country and eat every green plant left standing after the hail."
**13** Moses raised his staff over Egypt, and the LORD blew a steady east wind across the land all day and all night; by sunrise, that east wind carried in the locusts.
**14** They invaded all of Egypt and carpeted every square mile—a staggering plague! Nothing like it had ever hit Egypt before, and nothing like it will ever happen again.
**15** The swarm covered the ground until the whole countryside looked black. They devoured every crop and every piece of fruit the hail had missed, leaving not a single green leaf on any tree or plant across Egypt.
**16** Pharaoh scrambled to call Moses and Aaron back and pleaded, "I've sinned against the LORD your God and against you!
**17** Please forgive my sin just this once, and beg the LORD your God to call off this deadly disaster!"
**18** Moses walked out from Pharaoh's palace and prayed to the LORD.
**19** Immediately the LORD swung the wind around into a roaring west wind off the sea, which scooped up the locusts and dumped them into the Sea of Reeds—not one locust survived anywhere in Egypt.
**20** Still, the LORD stiffened Pharaoh's heart, and he wouldn't let the Israelites go.
**21** Then the LORD told Moses, "Raise your hand toward the sky so pitch darkness falls over Egypt—a darkness so thick you can practically touch it."
**22** Moses held out his hand toward the sky, and suffocating darkness blanketed Egypt for three straight days.
**23** People couldn't even see the person next to them, and nobody got up to go anywhere for three whole days—meanwhile, every Israelite neighborhood still had light.
**24** Pharaoh called Moses back and said, "All right, go worship the LORD! Even your children can go with you—just leave your sheep, goats, and cattle behind."
**25** "No," Moses answered, "you'll actually have to hand us animals for our sacrifices and burnt offerings to present to the LORD our God!
**26** Every bit of our livestock has to come along too—not even a single hoof stays behind—because we need animals from our herds to worship the LORD our God, and we won't know which offerings he wants until we get there."
**27** Once again, the LORD made Pharaoh's heart unyielding, and he flatly refused to release them.
**28** Pharaoh exploded at Moses, "Get out of my sight! Don't ever set foot in front of me again—the very day you see my face again, you're a dead man!"
**29** "Just as you say," Moses replied. "You'll never see my face again."

## Crossings & Lexical Notes

- **v. 1 `הִכְבַּדְתִּי` (*hikhbadti*), v. 20 `וַיְחַזֵּק` (*vayyechazzeq*):** Continues the alternating heart-hardening verbs (`כָּבֵד` "make heavy/dull" in v. 1 and `חָזַק` "make strong/rigid" in vv. 20, 27), now explicitly framed around generational memory (`v. 2`) so Israel's descendants will recount God's mighty deeds.
- **v. 2 `הִתְעַלַּלְתִּי` (*hit'allalti*):** Hitpael of *alal* ("deal ruthlessly/make a mockery of"), depicting God dismantling Pharaoh's pretensions of divine kingship.
- **v. 5 `עֵין הָאָרֶץ` (*ein ha-arets*):** Literally "the eye of the land," an idiom for the visible face or surface of the countryside (repeated in v. 15).
- **v. 19 `יָמָּה סּוּף` (*yammah suf*):** Literally "toward the Sea of Reeds" (*Yam Suf*, traditionally rendered "Red Sea" via the Septuagint *Erythra Thalassa*), marking the eastern water boundary where the west wind dumps the locusts.
- **v. 21 `וְיָמֵשׁ חֹשֶׁךְ` (*veyamesh choshekh*):** Verb *mashash* ("feel/grope") alongside *choshekh* ("darkness")—either "darkness so dense one can feel it" or "people grope in darkness," striking directly at Egypt's sun-god cult.
- **v. 26 `פַּרְסָה` (*parsah*):** Literally "a hoof," an emphatic synecdoche meaning not even the tiniest fraction of Israel's herds will stay in Egypt.

## Supplemental Texts & Allusions

- **Psalm 78:46; 105:34–35** (locust plague): *The psalms remember locusts without number devouring every green plant and crop in Egypt.*
- **Joel 1:4; 2:2–11** (unprecedented locust swarm): *Joel adapts the Exodus 10 motif of an unmatched locust invasion that darkens the sky and strips the land bare.*
- **Revelation 9:2–3** (darkness and locusts): *Apocalyptic judgment visions combine smoke-darkened air and tormenting locusts, recalling Egypt's eighth and ninth plagues.*
