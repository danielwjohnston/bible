## Clear & Faithful

**1** Moses objected, "What if they do not believe me or listen to my voice, and instead say, 'The LORD did not appear to you'?"
**2** The LORD asked him, "What is that in your hand?" "A staff," he replied.
**3** "Throw it on the ground," he commanded. So Moses threw it on the ground, and it turned into a snake, and Moses recoiled from it.
**4** Then the LORD told Moses, "Reach out your hand and grab it by the tail." Moses reached out, took hold of it, and it became a staff again in his grip.
**5** "This is so they may believe that the LORD, the God of their ancestors—the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob—has appeared to you."
**6** Next the LORD told him, "Put your hand inside your cloak." He tucked his hand inside his cloak, and when he pulled it out, his hand was covered with white skin disease, like snow.
**7** "Put your hand back inside your cloak," he said. Moses returned his hand to his cloak, and when he drew it out, it was restored like the rest of his flesh.
**8** "If they do not trust you or heed the message of the first sign, they will trust the message of the second sign.
**9** And if they remain unconvinced by even these two signs and refuse to listen to you, draw some water from the Nile and pour it out on dry ground; the water you take from the river will become blood on the dry ground."
**10** Still Moses pleaded with the LORD, "Pardon me, Lord, I am not an eloquent speaker—not in the past, nor even since you began speaking to your servant. My mouth is heavy and my tongue is slow."
**11** The LORD replied to him, "Who gave human beings a mouth? Who makes someone mute or deaf, sighted or blind? Is it not I, the LORD?
**12** Now go; I will be with your mouth and instruct you in what to say."
**13** Yet Moses persisted, "Pardon me, Lord—please send someone else!"
**14** Then the LORD's anger flared against Moses, and he said, "What about your brother Aaron the Levite? I know he speaks fluently. In fact, he is already on his way to meet you, and his heart will be glad when he sees you.
**15** You shall speak to him and put the words in his mouth; I will be with both your mouth and his, and I will teach you both what you are to do.
**16** He will speak for you to the people; he will serve as your mouthpiece, and you will stand in God's place for him.
**17** Take this staff in your hand, for with it you will perform the signs."
**18** So Moses went back to his father-in-law Jether and said, "Please let me return to my kinsmen in Egypt to see whether they are still alive." Jethro answered Moses, "Go in peace."
**19** Meanwhile the LORD told Moses in Midian, "Go back to Egypt, for all the men who wanted you dead are gone."
**20** Moses took his wife and sons, set them on a donkey, and headed back toward Egypt, carrying the staff of God in his hand.
**21** The LORD instructed Moses, "As you return to Egypt, be sure to perform before Pharaoh all the wonders I have placed in your power. Yet I will harden his heart so that he refuses to let the people go.
**22** Then you shall declare to Pharaoh, 'Thus says the LORD: Israel is my firstborn son.
**23** I told you, "Let my son go so he may worship me," yet you have refused to release him. Therefore I am about to kill your own firstborn son.'"
**24** On the journey, at an overnight stopping place, the LORD confronted Moses and sought to take his life.
**25** At once Zipporah took a sharp flint, cut off her son's foreskin, touched Moses' feet with it, and declared, "Truly you are a bridegroom of blood to me!"
**26** So the LORD relented and spared him. That was when she said, "A bridegroom of blood," referring to the circumcision.
**27** Meanwhile the LORD said to Aaron, "Go into the wilderness to meet Moses." So Aaron went, met him at the mountain of God, and kissed him.
**28** Moses recounted to Aaron everything the LORD had spoken when sending him, along with all the signs he had commanded him to perform.
**29** Together Moses and Aaron went and convened all the elders of Israel.
**30** Aaron relayed every word the LORD had spoken to Moses and performed the signs in full view of the people.
**31** The people believed; and when they heard that the LORD had visited the Israelites and seen their suffering, they bowed down low in worship.

## Everyday English

**1** "Wait," Moses protested. "What if they don't believe me or listen to a word I say? What if they claim, 'The LORD never appeared to you'?"
**2** The LORD asked him, "What's that you're holding?" "A shepherd's staff," Moses answered.
**3** "Throw it down on the ground," God said. Moses tossed it down, and instantly it turned into a snake—Moses jumped back out of its way!
**4** Then the LORD told him, "Reach out and grab it by the tail." Moses reached down and caught it, and right in his hand it turned back into a wooden staff.
**5** "Do that so they'll believe that the LORD—the God of their ancestors Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob—really appeared to you."
**6** Next the LORD told him, "Slide your hand inside your coat." Moses tucked his hand into his robe, and when he pulled it back out, his skin was white and flaky with a terrible skin disease, like snow.
**7** "Put your hand back inside your coat," God said. Moses slipped it back in, and when he drew it out again, his skin looked completely healthy like the rest of his body.
**8** God explained, "If they aren't convinced by the first miracle and won't listen, the second miracle will get through to them.
**9** And if they still refuse to trust you or listen after seeing both signs, scoop up some water from the Nile River and dump it onto dry land—the river water will turn into blood right on the ground."
**10** Even then, Moses argued with the LORD: "Please, Lord, I've never been good with words—not before, and not even since you started talking to me! I stumble over my speech and my tongue gets tied up."
**11** The LORD snapped back, "Who made the human mouth in the first place? Who decides whether someone can speak or hear, see clearly or be blind? Isn't it me, the LORD?
**12** Now get going! I'll stand behind every word you say and teach you exactly how to speak."
**13** Still Moses begged, "Oh, Lord, please just send someone else!"
**14** At that, the LORD grew angry with Moses and said, "Isn't your brother Aaron, the Levite, right there? I know he's a great speaker. Better yet, he's already heading out to meet you, and he'll be thrilled when he sees you.
**15** You'll talk things over with him and tell him what to say, and I'll guide both of you in speaking and show you both what to do.
**16** He'll act as your spokesperson to the crowd—he'll be your voice, and you'll represent God to him.
**17** Don't forget to carry that staff in your hand, because you're going to use it to perform those miracles."
**18** Moses went back to his father-in-law Jether and asked, "Please let me go back to my relatives in Egypt to check if they're still alive." "Go safely, with my blessing," Jethro told him.
**19** Before he left Midian, the LORD assured Moses, "Go ahead and return to Egypt—everyone who was hunting you down to kill you is dead."
**20** So Moses loaded his wife and sons onto a donkey and started back toward Egypt, gripping God's staff in his hand.
**21** Along the way, the LORD reminded Moses, "Once you get back to Egypt, be sure to demonstrate before Pharaoh every wonder I've put in your power. Still, I'm going to make his heart stubborn so he won't release the people.
**22** Then tell Pharaoh, 'Here is the LORD's message: Israel is my firstborn son.
**23** I ordered you to let my son go so he can worship me, and you refused—so now I'm going to take the life of your own firstborn son.'"
**24** On the trip, at a campsite where they stopped for the night, the LORD confronted Moses and was about to kill him.
**25** Quickly Zipporah grabbed a sharp flint stone, circumcised her son, touched Moses' feet with the foreskin, and cried out, "You're a bridegroom bought with blood to me!"
**26** Then God backed off and spared Moses' life. That's why she called him "a bridegroom of blood"—because of that circumcision.
**27** Meanwhile the LORD told Aaron, "Head into the desert to meet Moses." Aaron went out, met his brother at God's mountain, and greeted him with a kiss.
**28** Moses filled Aaron in on everything the LORD had told him and all the miraculous signs God had instructed him to perform.
**29** Then Moses and Aaron went together and called a meeting of all the Israelite leaders.
**30** Aaron passed along every word the LORD had given Moses and performed the miraculous signs right in front of the crowd.

## Crossings & Lexical Notes

- **v. 2 `מַה־זֶּה` (*mah-zeh*, Ketiv `מזה`):** Textual variant in WLC/OSHB: the written *ketiv* joins the two words into `מזה` (*mazzeh*), while the traditional vocalized *qere* reads two linked words `מַה־זֶּה` ("What is this?").
- **v. 6 `מְצֹרַעַת כַּשָּׁלֶג` (*metsora'at ka-shaleg*):** *Tsara'at* refers to a scaly skin condition causing ritual impurity, rather than modern Hansen's disease; "like snow" describes its sudden bleached appearance.
- **v. 10 `אֲדֹנָי` (*Adonai*) vs. `יְהוָה` (*YHWH*):** Here and in v. 13 Moses addresses God as `אֲדֹנָי` ("my Lord/Master"), whereas the narrator and vv. 11, 14 use the personal covenant name `יְהוָה` (*YHWH*), underlining Moses' deference as he tries to decline the commission.
- **v. 10 `כְּבַד־פֶּה וּכְבַד לָשׁוֹן` (*kevad-peh u-khevad lashon*):** Literally "heavy of mouth and heavy of tongue," an idiom for slow, hesitant, or labored speech; shares the root *k-v-d* ("heavy") later applied to Pharaoh's stubborn heart.
- **v. 21 `אֲחַזֵּק אֶת־לִבּוֹ` (*achazzeq et-libbo*):** First occurrence of the heart-hardening theme in Exodus, using the verb `חָזַק` (*chazaq*, "make strong/firm/rigid"), one of three distinct Hebrew verbs (`חָזַק` firm/strong, `כָּבֵד` heavy/unmoved, `קָשָׁה` hard/stiff) used across Exodus 4–14.
- **v. 25 `לְרַגְלָיו` (*le-raglav*):** Literally "to his feet," a Hebrew idiom that can also euphemistically refer to the lower body/groin; Zipporah's blood-rite averts judgment by enacting covenant circumcision on the way back to Egypt.

## Supplemental Texts & Allusions

- **Hosea 11:1** (Israel as God's son): *Hosea echoes Exod 4:22–23 when recalling that God loved Israel as a child and called his son out of Egypt.*
- **Matthew 2:19–20** (return after enemies die): *The angelic instruction to Joseph after Herod's death—that those seeking the child's life have died—deliberately echoes God's assurance to Moses in Exod 4:19.*
- **1 Corinthians 1:27–29** (God using weak messengers): *Paul's reflection that God chooses unimpressive speakers to confound the powerful resonates with Moses' reluctant confession of slow speech in Exod 4:10–12.*
