## Clear & Faithful

**1** These are the names of the sons of Israel who entered Egypt with Jacob, each man arriving with his household:
**2** Reuben, Simeon, Levi, and Judah;
**3** Issachar, Zebulun, and Benjamin;
**4** Dan and Naphtali; Gad and Asher.
**5** All the persons who descended from Jacob numbered seventy in all, while Joseph was already in Egypt.
**6** Then Joseph died, along with all his brothers and that entire generation.
**7** Yet the Israelites were fruitful and teemed, multiplying and growing exceedingly strong, so that the land became filled with them.
**8** Now a new king arose over Egypt who had no regard for Joseph.
**9** He spoke to his people: "Look, the Israelite population has become too numerous and strong for us.
**10** Come, let us act shrewdly toward them, lest they multiply further and, if war breaks out, join forces with our enemies, fight against us, and march out of the country."
**11** So they appointed taskmasters over them to oppress them with heavy labor, and the Israelites built supply cities for Pharaoh—Pithom and Raamses.
**12** Yet the more they oppressed them, the more they multiplied and spread out, until the Egyptians felt dread because of the Israelites.
**13** The Egyptians therefore subjected the Israelites to ruthless slavery.
**14** They made their lives bitter with harsh toil—working with mortar and brick and performing every kind of agricultural labor—all the forced work they imposed on them ruthlessly.
**15** Then the king of Egypt spoke to the Hebrew midwives, one named Shiphrah and the other Puah.
**16** He instructed them, "When you assist the Hebrew women in childbirth and look at the birthstool, if it is a boy, put him to death; if it is a girl, let her live."
**17** The midwives, however, feared God and did not carry out what the king of Egypt had ordered them; instead, they let the newborn boys live.
**18** So the king of Egypt summoned the midwives and demanded of them, "Why have you acted this way and spared the boys?"
**19** The midwives answered Pharaoh, "Hebrew women are not like Egyptian women; they are full of vigor and give birth before the midwife can even reach them."
**20** And God dealt kindly with the midwives, while the people continued to multiply and grow very strong.
**21** Because the midwives revered God, he established households of their own for them.
**22** Then Pharaoh issued an edict to all his people: "Every newborn boy must be thrown into the Nile, but every girl you may spare."

## Everyday English

**1** Here is the list of Jacob's sons who moved to Egypt with their families:
**2** First were Reuben, Simeon, Levi, and Judah,
**3** followed by Issachar, Zebulun, and Benjamin,
**4** along with Dan, Naphtali, Gad, and Asher.
**5** Counting everyone born in Jacob's line, there were seventy people altogether—Joseph was already living in Egypt.
**6** Eventually Joseph died, as did all his brothers and everyone else from that generation.
**7** Meanwhile, the Israelites had huge families. Their numbers exploded, and they grew so powerful that the whole region was packed with them.
**8** Later, a new king took the throne in Egypt—one who knew nothing about Joseph's legacy.
**9** He warned his citizens, "Look at these Israelites! There are far too many of them, and they're stronger than we are.
**10** We need a clever plan to hold them down. Otherwise they'll keep growing, and if a war starts, they'll side with our enemies, fight against us, and escape from the country."
**11** So the Egyptians put brutal slave drivers over them to crush their spirits with backbreaking work, forcing them to build the royal storage cities of Pithom and Raamses.
**12** Yet the harsher the abuse became, the faster the Israelites grew and spread across the land, leaving the Egyptians terrified of them.
**13** The Egyptians drove the Israelites without mercy,
**14** turning their lives into a nightmare of bitter toil—mixing mud for bricks, hauling heavy loads, and working every kind of field job under cruel supervision.
**15** Next, the Egyptian king called in the Hebrew midwives—one named Shiphrah and the other Puah—
**16** and ordered them, "When you help Hebrew women deliver their babies on the birthing stool, check the child: if it's a boy, kill him; if it's a girl, let her live."
**17** Because the midwives honored God, they refused to obey the king's decree and kept the baby boys alive.
**18** Pharaoh summoned them back and asked, "Why did you disobey me and spare those boys?"
**19** The midwives replied, "Hebrew women aren't like Egyptian women. They're so strong that they deliver their babies before we even arrive!"
**20** God rewarded the midwives for their courage, and the Israelites kept growing larger and stronger.
**21** Because those women respected God, he blessed them with families of their own.
**22** Finally Pharaoh gave an order to his entire nation: "Throw every newborn Hebrew boy into the Nile River, but let every baby girl live."

## Crossings & Lexical Notes

- **v. 5 `יֶרֶךְ־יַעֲקֹב` (*yerekh-Ya'aqov*):** Literally "thigh/loins of Jacob," an anatomical idiom for physical procreation and lineage.
- **v. 7 `וַיִּשְׁרְצוּ` (*vayyishretsu*):** "And they swarmed/teemed," echoing the creation blessing of Gen 1:20–21 (`שָׁרַץ`) and Gen 9:7 to show Israel fulfilling the primal command to fill the earth despite Egyptian hostility.
- **v. 8 `לֹא־יָדַע` (*lo-yada*):** "Did not know"—in Hebrew narrative, "knowing" implies covenantal recognition or gratitude, not mere factual ignorance; the new ruler chose to disregard Joseph's service.
- **v. 13 `עֲבֹדָה` (*avodah*) & `בְּפָרֶךְ` (*befarekh*):** *Avodah* means both "forced labor/slavery" (vv. 13–14) and "sacred worship/service" (Exod 3:12; 12:25), setting up the book's central conflict over whom Israel will serve; *parekh* denotes crushing severity.
- **v. 16 `הָאָבְנָיִם` (*ha-ovnayim*):** Dual noun literally meaning "the two stones," referring to a two-part birthing stool or support stones on which a woman squatted during delivery.
- **v. 19 `חָיוֹת` (*chayot*):** "Lively," "vigorous," or "full of vitality" (related to *chayyah*, living creature/vital force), contrasting with Pharaoh's death decree.
- **v. 21 `בָּתִּים` (*battim*):** "Houses" in the sense of enduring households, lineages, and descendants—ironically answering Pharaoh's attempt to extinguish Israelite households.
- **v. 22 `הַיְאֹרָה` (*ha-ye'orah*):** *Ye'or* is an Egyptian loanword (*itrw*, "great river/channel") used in Hebrew specifically for the Nile River and its waterways.

## Supplemental Texts & Allusions

- **Genesis 1:28; 9:7** (creation-mandate fulfillment): *The rapid multiplication of Israel in Exod 1:7 deliberately echoes the verbs "be fruitful," "swarm," and "multiply" spoken at creation and after the flood.*
- **Genesis 15:13–14** (covenant prophecy): *God's earlier word to Abram foretold that his descendants would live as strangers in a foreign land, endure enslavement and oppression, and afterward depart with great wealth.*
- **Acts 7:17–19** (Stephen's historical summary): *Stephen recounts how the promise to Abraham neared fulfillment as the people multiplied in Egypt until a new king arose who exploited the nation and forced parents to abandon their infants.*
