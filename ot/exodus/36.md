## Clear & Faithful

**1** "So Bezalel, Oholiab, and every wise-hearted artisan to whom the LORD has granted wisdom and discernment to know how to execute all the work of the sanctuary service shall carry out everything just as the LORD has commanded."
**2** Then Moses summoned Bezalel and Oholiab and every wise-hearted artisan into whose heart the LORD had put wisdom—everyone whose heart moved him to come forward to perform the work.
**3** They received from Moses all the sacred contributions that the Israelites had brought to carry out the work of the sanctuary service, while the people continued bringing him freewill offerings morning after morning.
**4** At length all the skilled artisans who were performing every task of the sanctuary work came, each man from the task he was doing,
**5** and told Moses, "The people are bringing far more than enough for the work that the LORD has commanded to be done."
**6** So Moses gave an order, and they sent a proclamation throughout the camp, saying, "Let neither man nor woman prepare any more material as a sacred contribution for the sanctuary." Thus the people were restrained from bringing more,
**7** because the materials already on hand were more than sufficient for all the work to be done—in fact, there was a surplus.
**8** Then all the wise-hearted artisans among those doing the work constructed the tabernacle with ten curtains of finely twisted linen and blue, purple, and crimson yarn; he made them with cherubim worked in by a master weaver.
**9** The length of each curtain was twenty-eight cubits and the width of each curtain four cubits; all the curtains had the same dimensions.
**10** He joined five curtains together into one set, and the other five curtains together into a second set.
**11** He made loops of blue yarn along the edge of the last curtain in the first set, and did the same along the edge of the outermost curtain in the second set.
**12** Fifty loops he made on the first curtain, and fifty loops on the edge of the curtain in the second set, with the loops aligned opposite one another.
**13** He fashioned fifty gold clasps and joined the curtains one to another with the clasps, so that the tabernacle became a single whole.
**14** Next, he made curtains of goats' hair for a tent over the tabernacle; eleven curtains he made.
**15** The length of each curtain was thirty cubits and the width of each curtain four cubits; all eleven curtains shared one measurement.
**16** He joined five of the curtains together by themselves and six of the curtains together by themselves.
**17** He made fifty loops along the edge of the outermost curtain in the first set, and fifty loops along the edge of the curtain in the second joining set.
**18** He made fifty bronze clasps to join the tent together so that it would be one unit.
**19** For the tent he made a covering of tanned rams' skins and an outer covering of fine leather skins above that.
**20** He made the upright frames for the tabernacle out of acacia wood.
**21** Each frame was ten cubits long and one and a half cubits wide.
**22** Each frame had two tenons joined to each other by cross-pieces; he made all the frames of the tabernacle this way.
**23** He constructed the frames for the tabernacle: twenty frames for the south side facing the Negev,
**24** and he cast forty silver socket-bases beneath the twenty frames—two bases under one frame for its two tenons, and two bases under the next frame for its two tenons.
**25** For the second side of the tabernacle, on the north side, he made twenty frames
**26** with their forty silver socket-bases—two bases under one frame and two bases under the next frame.
**27** For the rear of the tabernacle, facing west, he made six frames.
**28** He also made two frames for the rear corners of the tabernacle.
**29** They were paired together from the bottom and joined together tightly at the top into a single ring; so he made both of them for the two corners.
**30** Thus there were eight frames and sixteen silver socket-bases—two bases beneath each frame.
**31** He made crossbars of acacia wood: five for the frames on one side of the tabernacle,
**32** five crossbars for the frames on the second side of the tabernacle, and five crossbars for the frames at the rear of the tabernacle facing west.
**33** He made the center crossbar to pass straight through the middle of the frames from one end to the other.
**34** He overlaid the frames with gold, made their rings of gold to hold the crossbars, and overlaid the crossbars with gold.
**35** He made the veil of blue, purple, and crimson yarn and finely twisted linen, skillfully woven with cherubim.
**36** For it he made four pillars of acacia wood and overlaid them with gold, with gold hooks, and cast four silver socket-bases for them.
**37** He also made a screen for the entrance of the tent out of blue, purple, and crimson yarn and finely twisted linen, the work of an embroiderer,
**38** along with its five pillars and their hooks; he overlaid their capitals and bands with gold, while their five socket-bases were bronze.

## Everyday English

**1** "So Bezalel, Oholiab, and every talented craftsperson whom the LORD has gifted with skill and insight to know how to build everything for the sanctuary must do the work exactly as the LORD ordered."
**2** Moses called together Bezalel, Oholiab, and every skilled artisan whom the LORD had endowed with creative talent—everyone eager to step up and tackle the project.
**3** Moses handed over to them all the donations the Israelites had contributed for building the sanctuary. Meanwhile, people kept bringing in more voluntary gifts every single morning!
**4** Finally, all the craftspeople working on the sanctuary paused their individual tasks, walked over to Moses,
**5** and reported, "The people are donating far more supplies than we actually need to finish the project the LORD commanded us to build!"
**6** So Moses issued an order that was announced throughout the camp: "Men and women, please stop bringing any more donations for the sanctuary!" Only then did the people stop bringing gifts,
**7** because the materials already piled up were more than enough to finish everything—with plenty left over.
**8** Next, the most talented artisans on the crew crafted the sacred tent itself out of ten tapestry panels of fine twisted linen and blue, purple, and scarlet yarn, skillfully weaving cherubim figures right into the fabric.
**9** Every single panel measured 42 feet long by 6 feet wide—all ten were identical.
**10** They stitched five panels side by side into one large sheet, and the other five into a second sheet.
**11** Then they sewed loops of blue yarn along the outer edge of the first sheet and along the matching edge of the second sheet—
**12** fifty blue loops on the first edge and fifty lining up directly across on the second edge.
**13** They forged fifty gold clasps and clipped the two sheets together so the sacred tent formed one seamless roof and wall canopy.
**14** Over that, they sewed eleven panels of woven goat hair to form an outer protective tent.
**15** Each goat-hair panel measured 45 feet long by 6 feet wide—all eleven matched in size.
**16** They stitched five panels into one section and six panels into the second section,
**17** sewing fifty loops along the joining edge of each section.
**18** They cast fifty bronze clasps to hook the two sections together into a single outer tent.
**19** Above the goat-hair tent, they added a cover of red-dyed ram hides and an outermost weatherproof cover of tough fine-grained hides.
**20** For the structural walls of the sacred tent, they built upright frames out of acacia timber.
**21** Each frame stood 15 feet tall and 27 inches wide,
**22** fitted with two matching pegs at the bottom joined by cross-rungs—every wall frame was built the same way.
**23** They built 20 frames for the south wall facing the desert,
**24** resting on 40 heavy silver mounting bases—two bases under each frame to lock in its two bottom pegs.
**25** For the opposite north wall, they built another 20 frames
**26** with 40 silver mounting bases—again, two bases supporting each frame.
**27** Across the back wall facing west, they crafted 6 frames,
**28** plus 2 extra corner frames to brace the rear corners of the tent.
**29** Those corner frames were joined side by side at the bottom and locked tightly together at the top with a single ring; both rear corners were built identically.
**30** In all, the back wall used 8 frames resting on 16 silver mounting bases—two bases beneath every frame.
**31** They also shaped acacia-wood crossbars: five bars for one long wall,
**32** five bars for the opposite long wall, and five bars across the back western wall.
**33** The middle crossbar ran continuously inside the frames from one end of the wall all the way to the other.
**34** They plated all the wall frames with gold, forged gold rings on them to hold the crossbars, and plated the crossbars with gold too.
**35** For the inner divider, they wove a curtain from blue, purple, and bright red yarn with fine twisted linen, featuring cherubim woven by a master artist.
**36** They hung it on four acacia posts plated with gold, fitted with gold hooks and seated in four cast-silver bases.
**37** Finally, they embroidered an entrance curtain for the front doorway of the tent using blue, purple, and scarlet yarn with fine twisted linen,
**38** hanging it on five acacia posts with gold-plated tops, gold bands, gold hooks, and five bronze bases.

## Crossings & Lexical Notes

- **v. 2 `בְּצַלְאֵל` (*Betsal'el*):** WLC preserves Codex Leningradensis vocalization/accentuation on `בְּצַלְאֵל` (flagged by OSIS note `n="1"` in `Exod.xml`), whose name literally means "in the shadow of God."
- **v. 5 `מִדֵּי הָעֲבֹדָה` (*midde ha-avodah*):** "More than enough for the work"—literally "from sufficiency of the service," expressing an unprecedented surplus of freewill generosity that forces Moses to halt donations (v. 6).
- **v. 20 `קְרָשִׁים` (*qerashim*):** "Planks" or "upright frames"—27-inch-wide (1.5-cubit) acacia structures fitted with twin bottom tenons (`יָדוֹת`, literally "hands," v. 22) seated into heavy 75-pound silver socket-bases (`אֲדָנִים`).
- **v. 29 `תוֹאֲמִם` (*to'amim*):** "Twinned/matched/doubled"—from the root of "twin," describing the two rear corner posts engineered to interlock at both base and top ring (`הַטַּבַּעַת הָאֶחָת`) for structural rigidity.
- **v. 35 `הַפָּרֹכֶת` (*ha-parokhet*):** The inner woven veil separating the Holy Place from the Most Holy Place, emblazoned with guardian cherubim (cf. 26:31–33).

## Supplemental Texts & Allusions

- **Exodus 26:1–37** (original architectural blueprint): *Chapters 26 and 36 correspond detail-for-detail as command and faithful execution of the tabernacle curtains, frames, crossbars, and inner veil.*
- **2 Chronicles 24:10–14 & 31:5–10** (abundant temple contributions): *Echo Exodus 36:5–7 when Israelites bring more than enough offerings for temple repair and priestly support.*
- **Matthew 27:51, Mark 15:38 & Hebrews 10:19–20** (the torn sanctuary veil): *The inner cherubim-woven veil (parokhet) separating the Most Holy Place is torn top-to-bottom at Jesus' crucifixion, interpreted in Hebrews as opening direct access to God.*
