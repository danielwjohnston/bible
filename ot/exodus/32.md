## Clear & Faithful

**1** When the people saw that Moses was taking so long to come down from the mountain, the people gathered around Aaron and said to him, "Get up and make us a god who will go ahead of us! As for this Moses, the man who brought us up out of the land of Egypt, we have no idea what has become of him."
**2** Aaron said to them, "Take off the gold rings that are in the ears of your wives, your sons, and your daughters, and bring them to me."
**3** So all the people took off the gold rings from their ears and brought them to Aaron.
**4** He took the gold from their hands, shaped it with a carving tool, and fashioned it into a cast calf. Then they declared, "Here are your gods, O Israel, who brought you up out of the land of Egypt!"
**5** When Aaron saw this, he built an altar before it, and Aaron made a proclamation, saying, "Tomorrow shall be a festival to the LORD!"
**6** Early the next morning they rose, offered burnt offerings, and presented peace offerings; then the people sat down to eat and drink, and got up to indulge in revelry.
**7** Meanwhile the LORD said to Moses, "Go, descend at once, for your people whom you brought up out of the land of Egypt have acted corruptly.
**8** They have quickly turned aside from the path I commanded them: they have cast for themselves a molten calf, bowed down to it, sacrificed to it, and proclaimed, 'Here are your gods, O Israel, who brought you up out of the land of Egypt!'"
**9** The LORD said further to Moses, "I have watched this people, and indeed they are a stiff-necked people.
**10** Leave me alone now, so that my wrath may blaze against them and consume them; and I will make of you a great nation."
**11** But Moses sought to appease the LORD his God and pleaded, "Why, O LORD, should your anger blaze against your people whom you brought out of the land of Egypt with mighty power and a strong hand?
**12** Why should the Egyptians be able to say, 'He led them out with malicious intent, to slaughter them in the mountains and wipe them off the face of the earth'? Turn from your fierce wrath and relent concerning this disaster against your people.
**13** Remember Abraham, Isaac, and Israel, your servants, to whom you swore by your own self and promised them, 'I will multiply your descendants like the stars of heaven, and all this land of which I spoke I will give to your descendants, and they shall inherit it forever.'"
**14** So the LORD relented concerning the disaster he had threatened to bring upon his people.
**15** Then Moses turned and went down the mountain with the two tablets of the testimony in his hand—tablets inscribed on both sides, written on the front and on the back.
**16** The tablets were the handiwork of God, and the script was the writing of God, engraved upon the tablets.
**17** When Joshua heard the clamor of the people shouting, he said to Moses, "There is a sound of battle in the camp!"
**18** But Moses replied, "It is not the sound of shouts of victory, nor the sound of cries of defeat; it is the sound of singing that I hear."
**19** As soon as he approached the camp and saw the calf and the dancing, Moses' anger blazed; he hurled the tablets from his hands and shattered them at the foot of the mountain.
**20** He seized the calf they had made, burned it in fire, ground it down to fine powder, scattered it over the surface of the water, and forced the Israelites to drink it.
**21** Then Moses said to Aaron, "What did this people do to you that you have brought such a grave sin upon them?"
**22** Aaron answered, "Do not let my lord's anger blaze! You know this people yourself—how bent on evil they are.
**23** They said to me, 'Make us a god who will go ahead of us; as for this Moses, the man who brought us up out of the land of Egypt, we have no idea what has become of him.'
**24** So I said to them, 'Whoever has gold, take it off.' They gave it to me, I threw it into the fire, and out popped this calf!"
**25** Moses saw that the people had broken loose—for Aaron had let them run wild, turning them into an object of mockery among their foes—
**26** so Moses stood at the entrance of the camp and called out, "Whoever is on the LORD's side, come to me!" And all the sons of Levi rallied to him.
**27** He told them, "Thus says the LORD, the God of Israel: 'Each of you strap his sword to his side, go back and forth from gate to gate throughout the camp, and strike down brother, friend, and neighbor alike.'"
**28** The Levites did as Moses commanded, and about three thousand men among the people fell that day.
**29** And Moses said, "Today you have ordained yourselves to the LORD—each man at the cost of his son and his brother—so that he may bestow a blessing upon you this day."
**30** The next morning Moses said to the people, "You have committed a terrible sin. Now I will go back up to the LORD; perhaps I can make atonement for your sin."
**31** So Moses returned to the LORD and said, "Alas, this people has committed a grave sin by making gods of gold for themselves.
**32** Yet now, if you will forgive their sin—but if not, please erase me from the scroll that you have written."
**33** The LORD answered Moses, "Whoever has sinned against me, him alone I will erase from my scroll.
**34** Now go, lead the people to the place I told you about; see, my angel will go ahead of you. Nevertheless, on the day I call them to account, I will punish them for their sin."
**35** And the LORD struck the people with a plague because of the calf they had made—the one Aaron had fashioned.

## Everyday English

**1** When the people saw how long Moses was taking to come back down the mountain, they crowded around Aaron and demanded, "Come on! Make us a god to lead us forward. We don't know what's happened to this fellow Moses who pulled us out of Egypt!"
**2** Aaron told them, "Pull off the gold earrings your wives, sons, and daughters are wearing and hand them over to me."
**3** Everyone took off their gold earrings and brought them to Aaron.
**4** He collected the gold, worked it with an engraving tool, and cast a metal statue of a young bull. Then the crowd shouted, "Israel, here is your god who rescued you from Egypt!"
**5** Seeing their excitement, Aaron built an altar right in front of the idol and announced, "Tomorrow we'll hold a festival honoring the LORD!"
**6** Early the next day, they got up, sacrificed burnt offerings, and brought peace offerings. Then everyone sat down to feast and drink, and soon broke out into wild partying.
**7** Up on the mountain, the LORD told Moses, "Hurry back down! Your people—the ones you brought up out of Egypt—have ruined everything.
**8** They didn't waste any time abandoning the path I commanded them to follow. They've cast themselves a metal calf, bowed down to worship it, offered sacrifices to it, and announced, 'Israel, here are the gods who brought you out of Egypt!'"
**9** The LORD added, "I've watched these people closely, and they're hopelessly stubborn.
**10** Don't try to stop me now—let my fury burn against them and wipe them out! Then I'll turn you into a great nation instead."
**11** But Moses pleaded earnestly with the LORD his God: "LORD, why unleash your anger against your own people, whom you rescued from Egypt with immense power and a mighty hand?
**12** Why give the Egyptians an excuse to mock you and say, 'He only led them out with wicked intentions—to slaughter them in the mountains and erase them from the earth'? Please cool your blazing anger and hold back this disaster from your people!
**13** Remember your servants Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob—remember the solemn oath you swore by your own name when you promised them, 'I will make your descendants as countless as the stars in the sky, and I will give all this land I promised to your offspring to own forever.'"
**14** So the LORD relented and held back the disaster he had warned he'd bring on his people.
**15** Moses turned around and hiked down the mountain carrying the two covenant tablets—stone slabs inscribed on both the front and the back.
**16** God himself had crafted those slabs, and God himself had carved the writing right into the stone.
**17** When Joshua heard the roar of the crowd shouting, he said to Moses, "It sounds like war down in the camp!"
**18** Moses answered, "That's not the cheering of victory or the groaning of defeat—I hear the sound of singing."
**19** As soon as Moses neared the camp and spotted the golden calf and the dancing, he exploded with anger. He threw the tablets out of his hands and smashed them to pieces at the base of the mountain.
**20** Grabbing the calf they had made, he melted and burned it in the fire, ground it into fine powder, sprinkled the dust onto the water supply, and made the Israelites drink it.
**21** Then Moses confronted Aaron: "What did these people ever do to you to make you drag them into such an awful sin?"
**22** "Please don't be furious with me, my lord," Aaron replied. "You know yourself how bent on trouble these people are.
**23** They came to me and said, 'Make us gods to march ahead of us, because we don't know what happened to this Moses who led us out of Egypt.'
**24** So I told them, 'Anyone wearing gold, take it off.' They handed it to me, I tossed it into the fire, and out came this calf!"
**25** Moses saw that the people were completely out of control—Aaron had let them run wild, making them a laughingstock to all their enemies.
**26** So Moses stood at the camp's main entrance and shouted, "Everyone who stands with the LORD, come over here to me!" Every man from the tribe of Levi gathered around him.
**27** Moses told them, "Here is what the LORD, the God of Israel, commands: 'Strap your swords to your sides, sweep back and forth through the camp from one gate to the other, and execute even your own brothers, friends, and neighbors.'"
**28** The Levites obeyed Moses' order, and roughly three thousand men died that day.
**29** Afterward Moses told the Levites, "Today you've consecrated yourselves to the LORD—even at the cost of your own sons and brothers—so he is granting you a blessing today."
**30** The following day Moses told the crowd, "You have committed a massive sin. I'm going back up to the LORD now; maybe I can somehow make atonement for what you did."
**31** So Moses climbed back up to the LORD and prayed, "Please—these people have committed a terrible sin by crafting gods of gold for themselves.
**32** Now, if you'll forgive their sin—great! But if not, cross my name right out of the record book you've written."
**33** The LORD answered Moses, "I will cross out only the person who sinned against me from my book.
**34** Now go and lead the people to the place I told you about. My messenger will walk ahead of you. Still, when the day of reckoning comes, I will hold them accountable for their sin."
**35** And the LORD struck the people with a plague because of the golden calf Aaron had made for them.

## Crossings & Lexical Notes

- **v. 4 `עֵגֶל מַסֵּכָה` (*egel massekah*):** "A calf of molten/cast metal"—either solid cast gold or a wooden core overlaid with hammered gold from the earrings, likely intended not as a pagan rival to YHWH (note Aaron's proclamation "Feast to YHWH tomorrow!" in v. 5) but as a visible pedestal/emblem violating the second commandment.
- **v. 16 `חָרוּת` (*charut*):** "Engraved/incised"—a biblical hapax legomenon (used only here), famous in later rabbinic midrash as a wordplay with *cherut* ("freedom").
- **v. 17 `בְּרֵעֹה` (*be-re'oh*):** "In its/their shouting"—WLC follows Codex Leningradensis by printing `בְּרֵעֹה` directly with an archaic 3ms suffix (*-oh*) without adding a Ketiv/Qere apparatus (marked with OSIS note `n="q"` and `n="1"` in `Exod.xml`).
- **v. 19 `מִיָּדָיו` (*miyyadav*, Qere) / `מידו` (Ketiv):** `Exod.xml` records unpointed Ketiv `מידו` ("from his hand", singular) while the Masoretic Qere reads dual/plural `מִיָּדָיו` ("from his hands"), fitting Moses holding the two stone tablets in both hands.
- **v. 25 `פָרֻעַ` (*faru'a*):** "Unrestrained/let loose"—from *para* ("let hang loose, uncover, cast off restraint"), depicting moral and cultic disorder that leaves Israel exposed to enemy ridicule.
- **v. 32 `מְחֵנִי נָא מִסִּפְרְךָ` (*mecheni na mi-sifrekha*):** "Blot me out, please, from your book"—Moses offers his own covenant standing in solidarity with Israel, anticipating the principle of vicarious intercession while YHWH insists on individual moral accountability (v. 33).

## Supplemental Texts & Allusions

- **Deuteronomy 9:8–21** (Moses' parallel retelling): *Recounts the forty-day fast, the golden calf, smashing the tablets, pulverizing the idol, and interceding specifically for Aaron as well.*
- **1 Corinthians 10:7** (warning against idolatry): *Paul cites Exodus 32:6's wording about sitting down to eat and drink and rising up to indulge in revelry as a cautionary example for the Corinthian church.*
- **Romans 9:3** (intercessory self-sacrifice): *Paul's wish to be cut off for the sake of his kinspeople parallels Moses' plea in Exodus 32:32 to be blotted out of God's book.*
- **Psalm 106:19–23 & Acts 7:39–41** (Israel's calf at Horeb): *Summarize the episode of exchanging God's glory for the image of an ox and Moses standing in the breach.*
