## Clear & Faithful

**1** Now the LORD spoke to Moses and Aaron in the land of Egypt:
**2** "This month shall stand as the beginning of months for you; it shall be the first month of your year.
**3** Tell the whole congregation of Israel that on the tenth day of this month, every man must select a lamb for his ancestral household—one lamb per household.
**4** If a household is too small for a whole lamb, the householder and his nearest neighbor shall share one, counting the number of people and apportioning the lamb according to what each person eats.
**5** Your animal must be an unblemished year-old male; you may choose it from either the sheep or the goats.
**6** Keep it under watch until the fourteenth day of this month, when the entire assembled congregation of Israel shall slaughter it at twilight.
**7** They shall take some of its blood and smear it on the two doorposts and the overhead lintel of the houses where they eat it.
**8** That very night they must eat the meat, roasted over fire, together with unleavened bread and bitter herbs.
**9** Do not eat any of it raw or boiled in water, but only roasted over fire—head, legs, and inner organs intact.
**10** Leave none of it until morning; anything remaining until morning you shall burn up in the fire.
**11** This is how you must eat it: with your belt fastened, your sandals on your feet, and your staff in your hand. Eat it in urgent haste—it is the LORD's Passover.
**12** For I will pass through the land of Egypt that night and strike down every firstborn in Egypt, both human and animal, and I will execute judgments against all the gods of Egypt. I am the LORD.
**13** The blood will serve as a sign for you on the houses where you stay; when I see the blood, I will pass over you, and no destructive plague will strike you when I strike the land of Egypt.
**14** This day shall become a memorial for you; you shall celebrate it as a festival to the LORD throughout your generations, keeping it as a perpetual statute.
**15** For seven days you shall eat unleavened bread. On the very first day remove all yeast from your homes, for anyone who eats anything leavened from the first day until the seventh day shall be cut off from Israel.
**16** On the first day you shall hold a sacred assembly, and on the seventh day another sacred assembly. No ordinary work may be done on those days, except preparing whatever each person needs to eat—that alone may be done.
**17** Observe the Feast of Unleavened Bread, because on this very day I brought your divisions out of the land of Egypt; therefore observe this day throughout your generations as a perpetual statute.
**18** From the evening of the fourteenth day of the first month until the evening of the twenty-first day of the month, you are to eat unleavened bread.
**19** For seven days no yeast may be found in your houses; anyone who eats anything fermented shall be cut off from the community of Israel, whether resident alien or native-born.
**20** Eat nothing leavened; wherever you dwell, eat unleavened bread."
**21** Then Moses summoned all the elders of Israel and instructed them: "Go select lambs for your families and slaughter the Passover offering.
**22** Take a bunch of hyssop, dip it into the blood in the basin, and brush the lintel and both doorposts with the blood from the basin. None of you may step outside the doorway of his house until morning.
**23** When the LORD passes through to strike Egypt and sees the blood on the lintel and the two doorposts, the LORD will pass over that doorway and will not allow the destroyer to enter your homes to strike you.
**24** Keep this command as a lasting ordinance for you and your descendants forever.
**25** And when you enter the land that the LORD is giving you just as he promised, continue to observe this sacred service.
**26** When your children ask you, 'What does this ceremony mean to you?'
**27** you shall reply, 'It is the Passover sacrifice to the LORD, who passed over the houses of the Israelites in Egypt when he struck Egypt and spared our households.'" At this, the people bowed low and worshiped.
**28** Then the Israelites went and carried out everything the LORD had commanded Moses and Aaron.
**29** At midnight the LORD struck down every firstborn in the land of Egypt, from the firstborn of Pharaoh sitting on his throne to the firstborn of the prisoner in the dungeon, as well as every firstborn of the livestock.
**30** Pharaoh got up in the night—he, all his officials, and every Egyptian—and a great wail of grief swept through Egypt, for there was not a single house without someone dead.
**31** In the night he summoned Moses and Aaron and told them, "Get up, leave from among my people—both you and the Israelites! Go worship the LORD just as you asked!
**32** Take your flocks and your herds as you said, and be gone—and bless me too!"
**33** The Egyptians urgently pressed the people to hurry out of the country, crying, "Otherwise we will all die!"
**34** So the people carried their unrisen dough, wrapping their kneading bowls in their cloaks over their shoulders.
**35** Meanwhile the Israelites acted on Moses' instruction and asked the Egyptians for silver and gold articles and clothing.
**36** The LORD granted the people favor in the eyes of the Egyptians, who gladly gave what they asked; thus they stripped Egypt of its wealth.
**37** The Israelites journeyed from Raamses to Succoth, about six hundred thousand men on foot, besides children.
**38** A large mixed crowd also went up with them, along with vast herds and flocks of livestock.
**39** With the dough they had brought out of Egypt they baked unleavened flatbreads, for it had not fermented—they had been driven out of Egypt with no time to delay and had not even prepared travel provisions.
**40** The length of time the Israelites lived in Egypt was 430 years.
**41** At the exact conclusion of 430 years, on that very day, all the hosts of the LORD marched out of the land of Egypt.
**42** That night was a night of solemn vigil for the LORD to bring them out of Egypt; this same night belongs to the LORD as a vigil to be kept by all the Israelites across their generations.
**43** The LORD further instructed Moses and Aaron: "This is the statute of the Passover: no foreigner may partake of it.
**44** Any slave bought with money may eat of it once you have circumcised him.
**45** No temporary resident or hired laborer may eat of it.
**46** It must be eaten inside a single house; you must not carry any of the meat outside the house, nor break any of its bones.
**47** The entire community of Israel shall celebrate it.
**48** If a resident alien living among you wishes to celebrate the Passover to the LORD, every male in his household must first be circumcised; then he may draw near to celebrate it and shall be treated like a native of the land. No uncircumcised person may eat of it.
**49** One and the same law shall apply to the native-born and to the resident alien living among you."
**50** All the Israelites did precisely as the LORD had commanded Moses and Aaron.
**51** And on that very day, the LORD brought the Israelites out of the land of Egypt, company by company.

## Everyday English

**1** While they were still in Egypt, the LORD told Moses and Aaron:
**2** "From now on, this month is the start of your calendar—the very first month of your year!
**3** Tell the whole Israelite community that on the tenth day of this month, every family leader must pick out a lamb for his household—one animal per home.
**4** If a family is too small to finish a whole lamb, they should share one with their nearest neighbor, dividing the cost based on the number of people and how much each person can eat.
**5** Choose a healthy, defect-free one-year-old male, either a sheep or a goat.
**6** Care for it until the fourteenth day of the month, and then at dusk the whole community of Israel will slaughter their lambs together.
**7** Dip into the blood and brush it across the two side doorframes and the top beam of every house where the meal is eaten.
**8** Roast the meat over an open fire and eat it that very night along with flatbread made without yeast and bitter greens.
**9** Never eat it raw or boiled in water—roast the whole animal over fire, head, legs, and organs together.
**10** Don't leave any leftovers until morning; anything not eaten by dawn has to be burned up in the fire.
**11** And eat it ready to run—belt cinched tight, sandals strapped on, walking stick in hand. Wolf it down in a hurry, because this is the LORD's Passover!
**12** That very night I'll sweep through Egypt and strike down every oldest son in the country, human and animal alike, and I'll bring judgment crashing down on all Egypt's gods. I am the LORD!
**13** The blood painted on the homes where you're staying will mark you as safe. As soon as I see the blood, I'll pass right over you, and the deadly plague won't touch you when I strike Egypt.
**14** Remember this date forever! Celebrate it every year as a sacred festival honoring the LORD—make it a permanent tradition for all future generations.
**15** For seven days, eat only yeast-free flatbread. On day one, clean every trace of yeast out of your homes. Anyone who eats anything with yeast from the first day through the seventh day will be cut off from Israel.
**16** Hold a sacred worship gathering on day one and again on day seven. Don't do any regular work on those two days—only cook what everyone needs to eat.

**24** Keep this practice as a binding tradition for you and your children forever.
**25** Even after you settle in the land the LORD promised to give you, keep celebrating this worship ritual.
**26** And when your children ask you, 'Why do we do this ceremony?'

**29** At midnight, the LORD struck down every oldest son across Egypt—from Pharaoh's crown prince sitting on his throne down to the oldest son of the prisoner locked in a dungeon, plus every firstborn animal.
**30** Pharaoh leaped out of bed in the middle of the night—along with all his officials and every Egyptian—and a piercing wail of grief shook Egypt, because there wasn't a single house without a body inside.
**31** Right then in the dark, Pharaoh called for Moses and Aaron and yelled, "Get up and get out of my country—you and all the Israelites! Go worship the LORD just like you wanted!
**32** Take your sheep, goats, and cattle like you asked, and go! And ask God to bless me too!"
**33** Ordinary Egyptians practically shoved the Israelites out of the country to get them moving faster, panicking, "We're all going to drop dead!"
**34** So the people grabbed their bread dough before it could even rise, bundled their mixing bowls into their cloaks, and slung them over their shoulders.
**35** Following Moses' instructions, the Israelites also asked the Egyptians for silver and gold jewelry and fine clothes.
**36** The LORD softened the Egyptians' hearts toward them so they handed over whatever they asked for—and that's how Israel walked away with Egypt's wealth.

## Crossings & Lexical Notes

- **v. 6 `בֵּין הָעַרְבָּיִם` (*bein ha-arbayim*):** Literally "between the two evenings," referring to late afternoon/twilight between sunset and full dark.
- **v. 11 `פֶּסַח` (*pesach*) & v. 13 `וּפָסַחְתִּי` (*ufasachti*):** From the root `פָּסַח` (*pasach*), which carries a rich dual nuance: both "pass over / spare" (v. 13, 27) and "leap/hover protectively over" (v. 23, blocking the destroyer *ha-mashchit* from entering the doorway; cf. Isa 31:5).
- **v. 12 `שְׁפָטִים` (*shefatim*):** "Acts of judgment" (matching 6:6; 7:4), framing the tenth plague as a decisive judicial triumph over Egypt's pantheon.
- **v. 17 `בְּעֶצֶם הַיּוֹם הַזֶּה` (*be-etsem ha-yom ha-zeh*):** Literally "in the bone/substance of this day" (also vv. 41, 51), a Hebrew idiom for "on this very day" (marking chronological exactness).
- **v. 38 `עֵרֶב רַב` (*erev rav*):** "A great mixed multitude"—non-Israelite captives, intermarried families, and fellow sufferers who joined the exodus (cf. v. 49's single *torah* for native and *ger*).
- **v. 42 `לֵיל שִׁמֻּרִים` (*leil shimmurim*):** Plural intensive noun meaning "a night of watching/vigils"—both YHWH keeping protective watch over Israel to bring them out and Israel keeping annual night-watch in remembrance.
- **v. 46 `וְעֶצֶם לֹא תִשְׁבְּרוּ־בוֹ` (*ve-etsem lo tishberu-vo*):** "And a bone you shall not break in it"—preserving the integrity of the whole roasted sacrifice, later applied to Christ on the cross.

## Supplemental Texts & Allusions

- **1 Corinthians 5:7–8** (Christ our Passover): *Paul identifies Christ as the sacrificed Passover lamb and urges believers to sweep out old yeast and celebrate with unleavened sincerity and truth.*
- **John 1:29; 19:36** (Lamb and unbroken bones): *John presents Jesus as the Lamb of God and explicitly notes that none of his bones were broken, linking his crucifixion to the Passover ordinance of Exod 12:46.*
- **1 Peter 1:18–19** (unblemished lamb): *Peter describes redemption through the precious blood of a lamb without blemish or spot, echoing Exod 12:5.*
- **Galatians 3:17** (430-year span): *Paul cites the 430-year chronology associated with Exod 12:40–41 when contrasting the Abrahamic promise with the later Sinai law.*

**13** The blood shall be to you for a token on the houses where you are. When I see the blood, I will pass over you, and no plague will be on you to destroy you when I strike the land of Egypt.
**14** This day shall be a memorial for you. You shall keep it as a feast to the LORD. You shall keep it as a feast throughout your generations by an ordinance forever.
**15** “‘Seven days you shall eat unleavened bread; even the first day you shall put away yeast out of your houses, for whoever eats leavened bread from the first day until the seventh day, that soul shall be cut off from Israel.
**16** In the first day there shall be to you a holy convocation, and in the seventh day a holy convocation; no kind of work shall be done in them, except that which every man must eat, only that may be done by you.
**17** You shall observe the feast of unleavened bread; for in this same day I have brought your armies out of the land of Egypt. Therefore you shall observe this day throughout your generations by an ordinance forever.
**18** In the first month, on the fourteenth day of the month at evening, you shall eat unleavened bread, until the twenty first day of the month at evening.
**19** There shall be no yeast found in your houses for seven days, for whoever eats that which is leavened, that soul shall be cut off from the congregation of Israel, whether he is a foreigner, or one who is born in the land.
**20** You shall eat nothing leavened. In all your habitations you shall eat unleavened bread.’”
**21** Then Moses called for all the elders of Israel, and said to them, “Draw out, and take lambs according to your families, and kill the Passover.
**22** You shall take a bunch of hyssop, and dip it in the blood that is in the basin, and strike the lintel and the two door posts with the blood that is in the basin. None of you shall go out of the door of his house until the morning.
**23** For the LORD will pass through to strike the Egyptians; and when he sees the blood on the lintel, and on the two door posts, the LORD will pass over the door, and will not allow the destroyer to come in to your houses to strike you.
**24** You shall observe this thing for an ordinance to you and to your sons forever.
**25** It shall happen when you have come to the land which the LORD will give you, as he has promised, that you shall keep this service.
**26** It will happen, when your children ask you, ‘What do you mean by this service?’
**27** that you shall say, ‘It is the sacrifice of the LORD’s Passover, who passed over the houses of the children of Israel in Egypt, when he struck the Egyptians, and spared our houses.’” The people bowed their heads and worshiped.
**28** The children of Israel went and did so; as the LORD had commanded Moses and Aaron, so they did.
**29** At midnight, the LORD struck all the firstborn in the land of Egypt, from the firstborn of Pharaoh who sat on his throne to the firstborn of the captive who was in the dungeon, and all the firstborn of livestock.
**30** Pharaoh rose up in the night, he, and all his servants, and all the Egyptians; and there was a great cry in Egypt, for there was not a house where there was not one dead.
**31** He called for Moses and Aaron by night, and said, “Rise up, get out from among my people, both you and the children of Israel; and go, serve the LORD, as you have said!
**32** Take both your flocks and your herds, as you have said, and be gone; and bless me also!”
**33** The Egyptians were urgent with the people, to send them out of the land in haste, for they said, “We are all dead men.”
**34** The people took their dough before it was leavened, their kneading troughs being bound up in their clothes on their shoulders.
**35** The children of Israel did according to the word of Moses; and they asked of the Egyptians jewels of silver, and jewels of gold, and clothing.
**36** The LORD gave the people favor in the sight of the Egyptians, so that they let them have what they asked. They plundered the Egyptians.
**37** The children of Israel traveled from Rameses to Succoth, about six hundred thousand on foot who were men, in addition to children.
**38** A mixed multitude went up also with them, with flocks, herds, and even very much livestock.
**39** They baked unleavened cakes of the dough which they brought out of Egypt; for it wasn’t leavened, because they were thrust out of Egypt, and couldn’t wait, and they had not prepared any food for themselves.
**40** Now the time that the children of Israel lived in Egypt was four hundred thirty years.
**41** At the end of four hundred thirty years, to the day, all of the LORD’s armies went out from the land of Egypt.
**42** It is a night to be much observed to the LORD for bringing them out from the land of Egypt. This is that night of the LORD, to be much observed by all the children of Israel throughout their generations.
**43** The LORD said to Moses and Aaron, “This is the ordinance of the Passover. No foreigner shall eat of it,
**44** but every man’s servant who is bought for money, when you have circumcised him, then shall he eat of it.
**45** A foreigner and a hired servant shall not eat of it.
**46** It must be eaten in one house. You shall not carry any of the meat outside of the house. Do not break any of its bones.
**47** All the congregation of Israel shall keep it.
**48** When a stranger lives as a foreigner with you, and would like to keep the Passover to the LORD, let all his males be circumcised, and then let him come near and keep it. He shall be as one who is born in the land; but no uncircumcised person shall eat of it.
**49** One law shall be to him who is born at home, and to the stranger who lives as a foreigner among you.”
**50** All the children of Israel did so. As the LORD commanded Moses and Aaron, so they did.
**51** That same day, the LORD brought the children of Israel out of the land of Egypt by their armies.
