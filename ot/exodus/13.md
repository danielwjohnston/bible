## Clear & Faithful

**1** Then the LORD spoke to Moses:
**2** "Set apart to me every firstborn—the first offspring to open every womb among the Israelites, both human and animal; it belongs to me."
**3** Moses declared to the people, "Remember this very day on which you departed from Egypt, from the house of slavery, because by sheer strength of hand the LORD brought you out from there. Nothing leavened may be eaten.
**4** Today you are departing, in the month of Aviv.
**5** When the LORD brings you into the land of the Canaanites, Hittites, Amorites, Hivites, and Jebusites—which he swore to your ancestors to give you, a land flowing with milk and honey—you shall keep this sacred service in this very month.
**6** For seven days eat unleavened bread, and on the seventh day celebrate a festival to the LORD.
**7** Unleavened bread shall be eaten throughout the seven days; nothing leavened may be seen with you, nor any yeast anywhere within your territory.
**8** On that day explain to your child, 'This is because of what the LORD did for me when I came out of Egypt.'
**9** It shall serve you as a sign upon your hand and a reminder between your eyes, so that the instruction of the LORD may remain on your lips, for with a mighty hand the LORD brought you out of Egypt.
**10** Keep this statute at its appointed season from year to year.
**11** Furthermore, when the LORD brings you into the land of the Canaanites, as he swore to you and your ancestors, and gives it to you,
**12** you must devote every firstborn that opens the womb to the LORD; every firstborn male among your livestock belongs to the LORD.
**13** Every firstborn donkey you shall redeem with a lamb, or if you do not redeem it, break its neck; and every human firstborn among your sons you must redeem.
**14** When your child asks you in days to come, 'What does this mean?' answer him, 'With a strong hand the LORD brought us out of Egypt, out of the house of slavery.
**15** When Pharaoh stubbornly refused to release us, the LORD struck down every firstborn in Egypt, both human firstborn and animal firstborn. That is why I sacrifice to the LORD every male that first opens the womb, yet redeem every firstborn among my sons.'
**16** So this shall stand as a sign on your hand and a frontlet emblem between your eyes, because with strength of hand the LORD brought us out of Egypt."
**17** When Pharaoh released the people, God did not lead them by the route through the Philistine coast, even though it was shorter; for God reasoned, "Otherwise the people may change their minds when they face warfare and turn back to Egypt."
**18** Instead, God routed the people around by the desert road toward the Sea of Reeds; and the Israelites marched up out of Egypt equipped in ordered ranks.
**19** Moses also took Joseph's bones along with him, because Joseph had placed the Israelites under a solemn oath: "God will surely visit you, and you must carry my bones up from here with you."
**20** They set out from Succoth and camped at Etham on the edge of the wilderness.
**21** And the LORD traveled ahead of them by day in a pillar of cloud to guide their way, and by night in a pillar of fire to give them light, so they could travel day and night.
**22** Neither the pillar of cloud by day nor the pillar of fire by night ever left its place before the people.

## Everyday English

**1** The LORD also gave Moses these instructions:
**2** "Dedicate every firstborn male to me—every oldest son and every firstborn male animal among the Israelites belongs to me!"
**3** Moses told the crowd, "Never forget this day—the day you walked out of Egypt and out of slavery! The LORD used his raw strength to pull you free from that place, so don't eat anything with yeast today.
**4** Today, right here in the spring month of Aviv, you are marching out.
**5** Once the LORD brings you into the country of the Canaanites, Hittites, Amorites, Hivites, and Jebusites—the rich land overflowing with milk and honey he promised your ancestors—you must repeat this worship ceremony in this same month.
**6** For a full week eat only yeast-free flatbread, and cap off the seventh day with a joyful festival honoring the LORD.
**7** Eat nothing except unleavened bread all seven days; don't let a single loaf of raised bread or trace of yeast show up anywhere in your country.
**8** On that day tell your kids, 'We do this because of what the LORD did for me personally when I walked out of Egypt!'
**9** This practice will work like a wristband on your arm and a badge across your forehead to keep the LORD's teaching constantly on your tongue—because the LORD used his mighty power to rescue you from Egypt.
**10** Stick to this yearly appointment at the exact same time every single year.
**11** And after the LORD brings you into the Canaanites' land—just as he swore to you and your ancestors—and hands it over to you,
**12** set apart every oldest male child and firstborn male livestock for the LORD.
**13** Since a donkey isn't a sacrificial animal, buy back every firstborn donkey by substituting a lamb—or break its neck if you choose not to redeem it. And always redeem every oldest human son!
**14** Later on, when your child asks you, 'Why do we do this?' explain it like this: 'With unstoppable strength the LORD rescued us from Egypt, our slave prison.
**15** When Pharaoh stubbornly fought letting us go, the LORD killed every firstborn across Egypt, from oldest sons to newborn animals. That's why we sacrifice every firstborn male livestock to the LORD, while paying a redemption price for our oldest sons.'
**16** Let this serve like a reminder tied to your hand and a headband between your eyes, because the LORD rescued us from Egypt with unmistakable power."
**17** Once Pharaoh let the people leave, God didn't steer them along the coastal road through Philistine territory, even though that was the fastest route. God knew, "If they run straight into battle, they'll lose heart and head back to Egypt."
**18** So God detoured the people onto the desert trail heading toward the Sea of Reeds, and the Israelites marched out of Egypt organized like troops in formation.
**19** Moses carried Joseph's coffin of bones along with them, since Joseph had made the Israelites swear an oath: "God is certain to come help you—when he does, carry my bones out of here with you!"
**20** Leaving Succoth behind, they set up camp at Etham right where the desert begins.
**21** Ahead of them marched the LORD himself—inside a tall column of cloud during the day to show them the trail, and inside a column of fire at night to light their way, so they could keep moving both day and night.
**22** Never once did the daytime cloud column or the nighttime fire column disappear from in front of the people.

## Crossings & Lexical Notes

- **v. 2 `פֶּטֶר כָּל־רֶחֶם` (*peter kol-rechem*):** Literally "opener of every womb," defining the *bekhor* ("firstborn") consecrated to YHWH in recognition that Israel's own firstborn were spared during the tenth plague.
- **v. 4 `הָאָבִיב` (*ha-Aviv*):** "Month of the fresh barley ears" (spring, March–April), tying Israel's liturgical calendar to agricultural renewal and liberation.
- **v. 9 `זִכָּרוֹן` (*zikkaron*) & v. 16 `טוֹטָפֹת` (*totafot*):** Sign on the hand and memorial/frontlets (*totafot*, later linked to *tefillin*/phylacteries) between the eyes—either figurative devotion (Deut 6:8) or tangible wearable reminders of God's *torah* ("instruction").
- **v. 15 `הִקְשָׁה` (*hiqshah*):** Hiphil of `קָשָׁה` (*qashah*, "make hard/stiff/obstinate"), echoing YHWH's initial forecast in 7:3 (`אַקְשֶׁה`) and attributing the stubborn refusal to Pharaoh himself.
- **v. 18 `חֲמֻשִׁים` (*chamushim*):** Often rendered "armed" or "in battle array/groups of fifty" (related to *chamesh*, "five," or military gear), showing an orderly march rather than a panicked flight.
- **v. 19 `עַצְמוֹת יוֹסֵף` (*atsmot Yosef*):** "Bones of Joseph," directly fulfilling Joseph's dying oath in Gen 50:25 (`פָּקֹד יִפְקֹד`, "God will surely visit you").

## Supplemental Texts & Allusions

- **Genesis 50:24–25** (Joseph's oath): *Moses taking Joseph's bones in Exod 13:19 directly fulfills Joseph's deathbed charge that Israel carry his remains out of Egypt when God visits them.*
- **Luke 2:22–24** (consecration of the firstborn): *Mary and Joseph bring the infant Jesus to the Jerusalem temple to present him to the Lord in explicit obedience to the firstborn law of Exod 13:2, 12.*
- **1 Corinthians 10:1–2** (under the cloud): *Paul recalls how Israel's ancestors traveled beneath the guiding cloud of God's presence as they departed Egypt.*
- **Hebrews 11:22** (Joseph's faith): *Hebrews highlights Joseph's instructions concerning his bones as a supreme act of faith in God's promised exodus.*

## Reference Text (WEB Updated, Public Domain)

**1** The LORD spoke to Moses, saying,
**2** “Sanctify to me all the firstborn, whatever opens the womb among the children of Israel, both of man and of animal. It is mine.”
**3** Moses said to the people, “Remember this day, in which you came out of Egypt, out of the house of bondage; for by strength of hand the LORD brought you out from this place. No leavened bread shall be eaten.
**4** Today you go out in the month Abib.
**5** It shall be, when the LORD brings you into the land of the Canaanite, and the Hittite, and the Amorite, and the Hivite, and the Jebusite, which he swore to your fathers to give you, a land flowing with milk and honey, that you shall keep this service in this month.
**6** Seven days you shall eat unleavened bread, and in the seventh day shall be a feast to the LORD.
**7** Unleavened bread shall be eaten throughout the seven days; and no leavened bread shall be seen with you. No yeast shall be seen with you, within all your borders.
**8** You shall tell your son in that day, saying, ‘It is because of that which the LORD did for me when I came out of Egypt.’
**9** It shall be for a sign to you on your hand, and for a memorial between your eyes, that the LORD’s law may be in your mouth; for with a strong hand the LORD has brought you out of Egypt.
**10** You shall therefore keep this ordinance in its season from year to year.
**11** “It shall be, when the LORD brings you into the land of the Canaanite, as he swore to you and to your fathers, and will give it to you,
**12** that you shall set apart to the LORD all that opens the womb, and every firstborn that comes from an animal which you have. The males shall be the LORD’s.
**13** Every firstborn of a donkey you shall redeem with a lamb; and if you will not redeem it, then you shall break its neck; and you shall redeem all the firstborn of man among your sons.
**14** It shall be, when your son asks you in time to come, saying, ‘What is this?’ that you shall tell him, ‘By strength of hand the LORD brought us out from Egypt, from the house of bondage.
**15** When Pharaoh stubbornly refused to let us go, the LORD killed all the firstborn in the land of Egypt, both the firstborn of man, and the firstborn of livestock. Therefore I sacrifice to the LORD all that opens the womb, being males; but all the firstborn of my sons I redeem.’
**16** It shall be for a sign on your hand, and for symbols between your eyes; for by strength of hand the LORD brought us out of Egypt.”
**17** When Pharaoh had let the people go, God didn’t lead them by the way of the land of the Philistines, although that was near; for God said, “Lest perhaps the people change their minds when they see war, and they return to Egypt”;
**18** but God led the people around by the way of the wilderness by the Red Sea; and the children of Israel went up armed out of the land of Egypt.
**19** Moses took the bones of Joseph with him, for he had made the children of Israel swear, saying, “God will surely visit you, and you shall carry up my bones away from here with you.”
**20** They took their journey from Succoth, and encamped in Etham, in the edge of the wilderness.
**21** The LORD went before them by day in a pillar of cloud, to lead them on their way, and by night in a pillar of fire, to give them light, that they might go by day and by night:
**22** the pillar of cloud by day, and the pillar of fire by night, didn’t depart from before the people.
