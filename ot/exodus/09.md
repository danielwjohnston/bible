## Clear & Faithful

**1** Then the LORD directed Moses, "Go in to Pharaoh and declare to him, 'Thus says the LORD, the God of the Hebrews: Release my people so they may worship me.
**2** If you refuse to release them and keep holding onto them,
**3** behold, the hand of the LORD will fall upon your livestock out in the fields—upon horses, donkeys, camels, cattle, and sheep and goats—with a devastating plague.
**4** Yet the LORD will make a distinction between the livestock of Israel and the livestock of Egypt, so that nothing belonging to the Israelites will die.'"
**5** And the LORD fixed a definite time, saying, "Tomorrow the LORD will carry out this deed in the land."
**6** The next day the LORD did just that: all the livestock of Egypt died, while not a single animal belonging to the Israelites perished.
**7** Pharaoh sent to check, and indeed not one of Israel's livestock had died; yet Pharaoh's heart grew obstinate, and he did not release the people.
**8** Next the LORD told Moses and Aaron, "Take handfuls of soot from a furnace, and let Moses toss it into the air right before Pharaoh's eyes.
**9** It will spread as fine dust across the whole land of Egypt, causing festering boils to break out on people and animals everywhere in Egypt."
**10** So they took furnace soot and stood before Pharaoh; Moses tossed it up toward the sky, and festering boils erupted on humans and animals alike.
**11** Even the magicians could not stand before Moses because of the boils, for the boils afflicted the magicians along with all the Egyptians.
**12** Still the LORD hardened Pharaoh's heart, and he would not listen to them, just as the LORD had told Moses.
**13** Then the LORD instructed Moses, "Get up early in the morning, stand before Pharaoh, and tell him, 'Thus says the LORD, the God of the Hebrews: Release my people so they may worship me.
**14** For this time I will unleash the full force of my plagues against your heart, your officials, and your people, so that you may know there is no one like me on all the earth.
**15** By now I could have stretched out my hand and struck you and your people with pestilence, wiping you off the earth;
**16** yet for this very purpose I have kept you standing: to display my power to you and to proclaim my name throughout all the earth.
**17** You are still blocking my people and refusing to release them!
**18** Therefore tomorrow at this very hour I will rain down a catastrophic hailstorm, unlike anything Egypt has seen from the day of its founding until now.
**19** So send word right now to bring your livestock and everything you own out in the fields under shelter; every person and animal left out in the open and not brought indoors will be struck by the hail and die.'"
**20** Those among Pharaoh's officials who revered the word of the LORD hurried their servants and livestock indoors,
**21** while those who disregarded the LORD's warning left their servants and livestock out in the fields.
**22** Then the LORD said to Moses, "Stretch out your hand toward the sky, and hail will fall across all the land of Egypt—on people, on animals, and on every plant growing in the fields of Egypt."
**23** Moses raised his staff toward the sky, and the LORD sent thunder and hail, with lightning darting to the ground; the LORD rained down hail upon the land of Egypt.
**24** Hail fell accompanied by continuous flashes of fire within the hailstorm—more severe than anything that had ever struck Egypt since it became a nation.
**25** Throughout Egypt the hail struck down everything in the open fields, both human and beast; the hail battered every field crop and shattered every tree in the countryside.
**26** Only in the region of Goshen, where the Israelites lived, did no hail fall.
**27** Then Pharaoh sent for Moses and Aaron and confessed to them, "This time I have sinned. The LORD is in the right, while I and my people are in the wrong.
**28** Pray to the LORD—we have had enough of God's thunder and hail! I will release you; you need stay no longer."
**29** Moses answered him, "As soon as I step outside the city, I will lift my hands in prayer to the LORD. The thunder will stop and the hail will fall no more, so that you may know that the earth belongs to the LORD.
**30** Yet as for you and your officials, I know very well that you still do not stand in awe of the LORD God."
**31** Now the flax and the barley were ruined, since the barley was in the ear and the flax was in bloom;
**32** the wheat and the spelt, however, were not ruined, because they ripen later.
**33** So Moses left Pharaoh, walked outside the city, and spread his hands toward the LORD; immediately the thunder and hail stopped, and rain no longer poured onto the ground.
**34** Yet when Pharaoh saw that the rain, hail, and thunder had ceased, he sinned all over again and hardened his heart—both he and his officials.
**35** Pharaoh's heart remained unyielding, and he refused to release the Israelites, just as the LORD had foretold through Moses.

## Everyday English

**1** Next, the LORD told Moses, "Go straight back to Pharaoh and deliver this message: 'Here is what the LORD, the God of the Hebrews, says: Let my people go so they can worship me!
**2** If you keep refusing and hanging onto them,
**3** the LORD's hand is about to strike all your livestock out in the pastures—your horses, donkeys, camels, cattle, sheep, and goats—with a horrific plague.
**4** But the LORD will draw a boundary between Israel's herds and Egypt's herds, so not one animal belonging to the Israelites will die.'"
**5** The LORD even set an exact schedule: "Tomorrow the LORD will make this happen across the country."
**6** True to his word, the next day the LORD struck Egypt's herds, and all their livestock died, while not a single animal owned by an Israelite died.
**7** Pharaoh sent scouts to investigate, and sure enough, not one Israelite animal was dead. Even so, Pharaoh stayed stubborn and refused to let the people leave.
**8** Then the LORD instructed Moses and Aaron, "Scoop up handfuls of ash from a brick kiln, and have Moses throw it up into the air while Pharaoh watches.
**9** It will blow across Egypt as a fine dust and break out into painful, blistering boils on every person and animal in the country."
**10** They gathered kiln ash and stood in front of Pharaoh. Moses tossed it skyward, and oozing sores erupted all over both humans and livestock.
**11** The court magicians couldn't even stand in front of Moses because they were covered with boils just like everyone else in Egypt.
**12** Yet the LORD made Pharaoh's heart unyielding, and he wouldn't listen to them, just as the LORD had told Moses.
**13** Early the following morning, the LORD sent Moses back to confront Pharaoh: "Tell him, 'Here is what the LORD, the God of the Hebrews, says: Let my people go so they can worship me!
**14** This time I'm aiming all my blows straight at your heart—and at your advisors and citizens—so you'll learn there is nobody anywhere on earth like me.
**15** By now I could've raised my hand and wiped you and your whole nation off the map with disease.
**16** The only reason I've let you stay standing this long is to show you my power and make my name famous across the entire world.
**17** Yet you're still blocking my people and refusing to release them!
**18** So at this exact time tomorrow, I'm going to unleash the worst hailstorm Egypt has ever experienced since the day the nation began.
**19** Send riders out right now to bring your herds and everything else from the fields under cover! Any worker or animal still caught outside and not brought indoors will be pounded by the hail and killed.'"
**20** Pharaoh's officials who took the LORD's warning seriously rushed their workers and livestock indoors,
**21** while those who brushed off the LORD's word left their workers and animals out in the open.
**22** Then the LORD told Moses, "Reach your hand toward the sky so hail hammers all of Egypt—hitting people, livestock, and every green crop in the fields."
**23** Moses lifted his staff skyward, and the LORD unleashed booming thunder and hail, with streaks of fire racing down to the ground—a torrent of hail pounding Egypt.
**24** Hail poured down with blazing lightning dancing inside the storm, far worse than anything Egypt had suffered since becoming a country.
**25** Everywhere across Egypt, the hail smashed everything left outdoors—people, livestock, field crops—and snapped every tree in the countryside.
**26** The only place spared from the hail was Goshen, where the Israelites lived.
**27** Pharaoh urgently called Moses and Aaron back and admitted, "This time I've blown it and sinned! The LORD is right, and my people and I are in the wrong.
**28** Please pray to the LORD! We can't take any more of God's thunder and hail! I'll let you leave—you don't have to stay another day."
**29** Moses answered, "As soon as I walk outside the city gates, I'll lift my hands in prayer to the LORD. The thunder will quiet down and the hail will stop, proving to you that the whole earth belongs to the LORD.
**30** Still, I know very well that neither you nor your advisors truly fear the LORD God yet."
**31** (The flax and barley crops were destroyed because the barley had already headed out and the flax was blooming,
**32** though the wheat and spelt survived because they sprout later in the season.)
**33** Moses walked out of the city away from Pharaoh and lifted his hands to the LORD. At once the thunder and hail stopped, and rain quit pouring down on the ground.
**34** As soon as Pharaoh saw that the rain, hail, and thunder were over, he went right back to sinning—both he and his advisors dug in their heels.
**35** Pharaoh's heart stayed hard, and he refused to let the Israelites leave, just as the LORD had said through Moses.

## Crossings & Lexical Notes

- **v. 7 `וַיִּכְבַּד` (*vayyikhbad*), v. 12 `וַיְחַזֵּק` (*vayyechazzeq*), vv. 34–35 `וַיַּכְבֵּד` / `וַיֶּחֱזַק`:** Note the interplay of heart-hardening verbs in this chapter: Pharaoh's heart "grows heavy/unresponsive" (`כָּבֵד`, vv. 7, 34), while YHWH "makes firm/strengthens" (`חָזַק`, v. 12) and Pharaoh's heart "grows rigid" (`חָזַק`, v. 35), illustrating both human obstinacy and divine judicial hardening.
- **v. 14 `מַגֵּפֹתַי` (*maggepotai*):** From *nagaf* ("strike/smite"), meaning "my blows/plagues," aimed at Pharaoh's *lev* ("heart/mind/will") to dismantle Egyptian royal ideology.
- **v. 16 `הֶעֱמַדְתִּיךָ` (*he'emadtikha*):** Hiphil of *amad* ("cause to stand/keep standing/maintain in place")—God spared Pharaoh's life during earlier plagues specifically to display divine sovereignty globally.
- **v. 17 `מִסְתּוֹלֵל` (*mistolel*):** Hitpoel participle of *salal* ("heap up a mound/wall against"), depicting Pharaoh acting like an arrogant barrier or siege rampart obstructing Israel's release.
- **v. 27 `חָטָאתִי` (*chatati*) & `הַצַּדִּיק` (*ha-tsaddiq*):** Pharaoh's legal confession ("I have sinned; YHWH is the righteous one") uses forensic courtroom vocabulary, though v. 34 exposes its merely tactical sincerity.
- **v. 31 `אָבִ֣יב` (*aviv*):** Means "fresh green ear/ripening head" of barley, which also gives its name to the spring month *Ha-Aviv* (Exod 13:4; 23:15).
