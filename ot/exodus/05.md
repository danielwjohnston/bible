## Clear & Faithful

**1** Afterward Moses and Aaron came and declared to Pharaoh, "Thus says the LORD, the God of Israel: 'Release my people so that they may celebrate a pilgrim festival to me in the wilderness.'"
**2** Pharaoh retorted, "Who is the LORD, that I should obey his voice and release Israel? I do not acknowledge the LORD, nor will I release Israel."
**3** They replied, "The God of the Hebrews has met with us. Please let us travel three days into the wilderness and offer sacrifices to the LORD our God, lest he strike us with plague or sword."
**4** But the king of Egypt answered them, "Moses and Aaron, why are you pulling the people away from their tasks? Get back to your forced labor!"
**5** Pharaoh added, "Look how numerous the native populace has become, and you want to make them cease from their heavy labor!"
**6** That very day Pharaoh issued orders to the Egyptian taskmasters over the people and their Israelite supervisors:
**7** "You shall no longer supply the people with straw for brickmaking as you did previously; let them go and gather straw for themselves.
**8** Yet you shall still require from them the same daily quota of bricks they made before, without reducing it at all. They are lazy—that is why they keep crying out, 'Let us go and sacrifice to our God!'
**9** Make the labor heavier on these men so they stay occupied with it and stop paying attention to deceptive words."
**10** So the Egyptian taskmasters and their Israelite supervisors went out and announced to the people, "Thus says Pharaoh: 'I will not supply you with straw.
**11** Go gather straw for yourselves wherever you can find it, yet not a single thing will be subtracted from your work quota.'"
**12** The people therefore dispersed throughout the entire land of Egypt to collect stubble to substitute for straw.
**13** Meanwhile the taskmasters kept pressing them, "Finish your daily output each single day, just as when straw was provided."
**14** The Israelite supervisors whom Pharaoh's taskmasters had placed over them were beaten and interrogated: "Why have you not fulfilled your brickmaking quota yesterday or today as you did before?"
**15** Then the Israelite supervisors went and appealed to Pharaoh: "Why are you treating your servants like this?
**16** No straw is being supplied to your servants, yet they keep ordering us, 'Make bricks!' Look, your servants are being beaten, while the fault lies with your own officials."
**17** Pharaoh answered, "Lazy—you are nothing but lazy! That is why you keep saying, 'Let us go and sacrifice to the LORD.'
**18** Now get back to work! No straw will be given to you, yet you must still deliver the full tally of bricks."
**19** The Israelite supervisors saw that they were in desperate trouble when told, "You must not reduce your daily brick output at all."
**20** As they left Pharaoh's presence, they met Moses and Aaron waiting for them.
**21** They confronted them, "May the LORD look upon you and judge you! You have made us utterly repulsive to Pharaoh and his officials, putting a sword right into their hands to slay us."
**22** Then Moses turned back to the LORD and pleaded, "Lord, why have you brought trouble on this people? Why did you ever send me?
**23** Ever since I went to Pharaoh to speak in your name, he has treated this people worse—and you have done nothing at all to rescue your people!"

## Everyday English

**1** After that, Moses and Aaron marched in to see Pharaoh and told him, "Here is what the LORD, the God of Israel, says: 'Let my people go so they can hold a sacred festival for me out in the desert.'"
**2** "Who's this LORD that I should take orders from him and let Israel go?" Pharaoh scoffed. "I don't recognize the LORD, and I'm certainly not letting Israel leave!"
**3** Moses and Aaron persisted, "The God of the Hebrews appeared to us. Please let us walk three days out into the desert to sacrifice to the LORD our God—otherwise he might hit us with a deadly disease or war."
**4** The king of Egypt snapped back, "Moses and Aaron, why are you distracting the workers from their jobs? Get back to your own chores!"
**5** Then Pharaoh said, "There are already way too many of these common workers across the land, and now you're trying to stop them from working!"
**6** That same day, Pharaoh gave new orders to the Egyptian slave bosses and the Israelite crew leaders:
**7** "Stop handing out straw to the workers for making bricks like you used to. Let them go out and scavenge straw on their own!
**8** But don't lower their daily brick quota by a single brick—make them produce the exact same amount as before. They're just slacking off! That's why they're yelling, 'Let us go sacrifice to our God!'
**9** Pile more backbreaking work on these men so they stay too busy to listen to lies."
**10** The Egyptian bosses and Israelite crew leaders went out and broke the news to the workers: "Pharaoh says, 'I'm not giving you any more straw.
**11** Go hunt down your own straw wherever you can find it—and don't think your daily brick count is going down one bit.'"
**12** So the Israelites scattered all over Egypt scrounging for dry stalks to use instead of straw,
**13** while the bosses hounded them nonstop: "Finish each day's full quota on time, just like when we gave you straw!"
**14** Whenever they fell short, Pharaoh's slave bosses flogged the Israelite crew leaders and yelled, "Why didn't you hit your brick quota yesterday or today like you used to?"
**15** Finally, the Israelite crew leaders went to Pharaoh himself and begged, "Why are you doing this to your own workers?
**16** Nobody gives us straw anymore, yet your bosses keep shouting at us, 'Make bricks!' Now we're getting beaten, and your own people are the ones at fault!"
**17** Pharaoh exploded, "You're lazy! Completely lazy! That's the only reason you keep whining, 'Let us go sacrifice to the LORD!'
**18** Get out there and work! You won't get a shred of straw, and you'll still hand over every single brick!"
**19** The crew leaders realized they were trapped in a hopeless mess when they heard, "Don't come up short on even a single day's bricks."
**20** Walking out of Pharaoh's palace, they ran straight into Moses and Aaron, who were waiting to hear what happened.
**21** They lashed out at them: "May the LORD see what you've done and judge you! You've made us stink in front of Pharaoh and his advisors—you practically handed them a sword to slaughter us!"
**22** Moses went straight back to the LORD and cried out, "Lord, why have you made life so miserable for these people? Why did you send me here in the first place?
**23** Ever since I stepped in front of Pharaoh to speak your message, he's only treated them more cruelly—and you haven't lifted a finger to rescue your people!"

## Crossings & Lexical Notes

- **v. 1 `וְיָחֹגּוּ` (*veyachoggu*):** From *chagag* ("to celebrate a pilgrim festival / dance in sacred procession"), introducing the festival motif later fulfilled at Passover (`חַג`, Exod 12:14).
- **v. 5 `וְהִשְׁבַּתֶּם` (*vehishbattem*):** Hiphil of *shabat* ("cause to rest/cease"); Pharaoh accuses Moses and Aaron of imposing an unauthorized "Sabbath" on his enslaved workforce.
- **v. 9 `תִּכְבַּד` (*tikhbad*):** "Let the labor grow heavy," from `כָּבֵד` (*kaved*, "heavy/severe")—anticipating the same root used for Pharaoh's "heavy/unyielding" heart (`7:14`, `8:15`).
- **v. 21 `הִבְאַשְׁתֶּם אֶת־רֵיחֵנוּ` (*hiv'ashtem et-rechenu*):** Literally "you have caused our scent to stink," a vivid Hebrew idiom for ruining someone's reputation or standing (cf. Gen 34:30; the same verb `בָּאַשׁ` describes the stinking Nile in Exod 7:18).
- **v. 22 `אֲדֹנָי` (*Adonai*):** As in 4:10, 13, Moses addresses God as *Adonai* ("Lord/Master") in painful protest, contrasting his own disappointment with the covenant name `יְהוָה` (*YHWH*).
- **v. 23 `וְהַצֵּל לֹא־הִצַּלְתָּ` (*vehatsel lo-hitsalta*):** Infinitive absolute plus finite Hiphil of *natsal* ("delivering, you have not delivered"), emphatically sharpening Moses' charge against God's seeming inaction.

## Supplemental Texts & Allusions

- **Genesis 34:30** (same stench idiom): *Jacob uses the identical phrase "you have made my scent foul" among the Canaanites after Simeon and Levi's raid on Shechem.*
- **Jeremiah 12:1; Habakkuk 1:2–4** (covenant lament): *Moses' blunt complaint in Exod 5:22–23 inaugurates the prophetic tradition of questioning God's timing when obedience worsens suffering.*
- **1 Corinthians 5:8** (festival imagery): *Paul draws on the language of keeping a sacred festival (`chag`/feast) rooted in Israel's exodus deliverance.*

**12** So the people were scattered abroad throughout all the land of Egypt to gather stubble for straw.
**13** The taskmasters were urgent saying, “Fulfill your work quota daily, as when there was straw!”
**14** The officers of the children of Israel, whom Pharaoh’s taskmasters had set over them, were beaten, and were asked, “Why haven’t you fulfilled your quota both yesterday and today, in making brick as before?”
**15** Then the officers of the children of Israel came and cried to Pharaoh, saying, “Why do you deal this way with your servants?
**16** No straw is given to your servants, and they tell us, ‘Make brick!’ and behold, your servants are beaten; but the fault is in your own people.”
**17** But Pharaoh said, “You are idle! You are idle! Therefore you say, ‘Let’s go and sacrifice to the LORD.’
**18** Go therefore now, and work; for no straw shall be given to you; yet you shall deliver the same number of bricks!”
**19** The officers of the children of Israel saw that they were in trouble when it was said, “You shall not diminish anything from your daily quota of bricks!”
**20** They met Moses and Aaron, who stood along the way, as they came out from Pharaoh.
**21** They said to them, “May the LORD look at you and judge, because you have made us a stench to be abhorred in the eyes of Pharaoh, and in the eyes of his servants, to put a sword in their hand to kill us!”
**22** Moses returned to the LORD, and said, “Lord, why have you brought trouble on this people? Why is it that you have sent me?
**23** For since I came to Pharaoh to speak in your name, he has brought trouble on this people. You have not rescued your people at all!”
