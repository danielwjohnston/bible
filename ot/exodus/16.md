## Clear & Faithful

**1** Setting out from Elim, the entire congregation of the Israelites came into the wilderness of Sin, which lies between Elim and Sinai, on the fifteenth day of the second month after their departure from the land of Egypt.
**2** There in the wilderness the entire congregation of the Israelites grumbled against Moses and Aaron.
**3** The Israelites said to them, "If only we had died by the LORD's hand in the land of Egypt, when we sat by pots of meat and ate bread until we were full! Instead, you have brought us out into this wilderness to starve this entire assembly to death."
**4** Then the LORD said to Moses, "Behold, I am about to rain bread from heaven for you. The people shall go out and gather a day's portion each day, so that I may test them to see whether they will walk in my law or not.
**5** On the sixth day, when they prepare what they bring in, it will be twice as much as they gather each day."
**6** So Moses and Aaron said to all the Israelites, "At evening you will know that it was the LORD who brought you out of the land of Egypt,
**7** and in the morning you will see the glory of the LORD, because he has heard your grumblings against the LORD. For what are we that you grumble against us?"
**8** And Moses said, "This will happen when the LORD gives you meat to eat in the evening and bread to the full in the morning, since the LORD hears your grumblings that you utter against him. What are we? Your grumblings are not against us, but against the LORD."
**9** Then Moses told Aaron, "Say to the whole congregation of the Israelites, 'Come near before the LORD, for he has heard your grumblings.'"
**10** While Aaron was speaking to the whole congregation of the Israelites, they looked toward the wilderness, and behold, the glory of the LORD appeared in the cloud.
**11** And the LORD spoke to Moses, saying,
**12** "I have heard the grumblings of the Israelites. Speak to them and say, 'At twilight you shall eat meat, and in the morning you shall be filled with bread; then you will know that I am the LORD your God.'"
**13** That evening, quail came up and covered the camp, and in the morning a layer of dew lay around the camp.
**14** When the layer of dew lifted, there on the surface of the wilderness was a fine, flaky substance, as fine as frost on the ground.
**15** When the Israelites saw it, they said to one another, "What is it?"—because they did not know what it was. Moses said to them, "It is the bread that the LORD has given you to eat.
**16** This is what the LORD has commanded: 'Let each one gather from it as much as he needs to eat, an omer per person according to the number of people in your households; take it for everyone who is in your tent.'"
**17** The Israelites did so; some gathered more, and some gathered less.
**18** Yet when they measured it by the omer, whoever gathered much had nothing left over, and whoever gathered little had no lack; each had gathered just what he needed to eat.
**19** Moses told them, "Let no one leave any of it over until morning."
**20** But they did not listen to Moses; some left part of it until morning, and it bred worms and rotted. So Moses grew angry with them.
**21** Morning after morning they gathered it, each person according to what he could eat, and when the sun grew hot, it melted away.
**22** On the sixth day they gathered twice as much bread, two omers for each person. When all the leaders of the congregation came and reported this to Moses,
**23** he said to them, "This is what the LORD has spoken: 'Tomorrow is a solemn rest, a holy Sabbath to the LORD. Bake what you wish to bake, and boil what you wish to boil; whatever remains, set aside for yourselves to be kept until morning.'"
**24** So they set it aside until morning, just as Moses had commanded, and it neither stank nor had any worms in it.
**25** Moses said, "Eat it today, for today is a Sabbath to the LORD; today you will not find it out in the field.
**26** Six days you shall gather it, but on the seventh day, the Sabbath, there will be none."
**27** Nevertheless, on the seventh day some of the people went out to gather, and they found nothing.
**28** Then the LORD said to Moses, "How long will you refuse to keep my commandments and my instructions?
**29** See! The LORD has given you the Sabbath; therefore on the sixth day he gives you bread for two days. Let every person stay where he is; no one is to leave his place on the seventh day."
**30** So the people rested on the seventh day.
**31** The house of Israel named it manna; it was white like coriander seed, and its taste was like wafers made with honey.
**32** Moses said, "This is what the LORD has commanded: 'Keep an omer-full of it throughout your generations, so that they may see the bread with which I fed you in the wilderness when I brought you out of the land of Egypt.'"
**33** And Moses said to Aaron, "Take a jar, put a full omer of manna inside it, and place it before the LORD to be preserved for your generations."
**34** Just as the LORD commanded Moses, Aaron placed it before the testimony to be preserved.
**35** The Israelites ate the manna for forty years until they arrived at an inhabited land; they ate the manna until they reached the border of the land of Canaan.
**36** Now an omer is one-tenth of an ephah.

## Everyday English

**1** The entire Israelite community packed up and left Elim, arriving in the Desert of Sin—between Elim and Sinai—on the fifteenth day of the second month after they had left Egypt.
**2** Out in the desert, the whole community started complaining against Moses and Aaron.
**3** "We wish the LORD had struck us dead back in Egypt!" the Israelites grumbled. "At least there we sat next to big pots of meat and ate all the bread we wanted! You dragged us out into this wasteland just to starve our whole community to death!"
**4** Then the LORD told Moses, "Watch—I'm going to rain bread down from the sky for you. Each day the people are to go out and pick up only enough for that single day. This will test them to see if they'll follow my instructions or not.
**5** On the sixth day, when they prepare what they bring home, it'll turn out to be twice what they collect on other days."
**6** So Moses and Aaron announced to all the Israelites, "Tonight you'll realize it was the LORD who rescued you from Egypt,
**7** and tomorrow morning you'll see the LORD's shining glory, because he has heard how you've been complaining against him. Who do you think we are that you're blaming us?"
**8** Moses went on, "You'll see this when the LORD gives you meat to eat tonight and all the bread you can eat in the morning—because the LORD has heard your complaints against him. Who are we? You aren't really complaining against us; you're complaining against the LORD!"
**9** Then Moses instructed Aaron, "Tell the whole Israelite community, 'Come stand before the LORD, because he has heard your complaints.'"
**10** As Aaron spoke to the entire assembly, they turned to look out toward the desert—and there in the cloud appeared the LORD's radiant glory!
**11** The LORD told Moses,
**12** "I've heard the Israelites' complaints. Tell them, 'At dusk you'll eat meat, and in the morning you'll fill up on bread. Then you'll know for sure that I am the LORD your God.'"
**13** Sure enough, that evening flocks of quail flew in and blanketed the camp, and by morning a layer of dew covered the ground all around them.
**14** When the dew evaporated, thin, flaky bits—fine as frost on the ground—lay scattered across the desert floor.
**15** When the Israelites saw it, they asked each other, "What is this stuff?" because they had no idea what it was. Moses answered them, "This is the bread the LORD has given you to eat!
**16** Here are the LORD's orders: 'Everyone is to collect as much as each person needs—about two quarts per person, matching the number of people living in your tent.'"
**17** The Israelites did just that; some picked up a lot, and some picked up a little.
**18** Yet when they measured it out in two-quart portions, whoever gathered plenty had nothing extra left over, and whoever gathered little didn't come up short. Every person had gathered just the right amount to eat.
**19** Moses warned them, "Don't save any of it overnight until morning."
**20** Some of them ignored Moses and kept part of it until the next day, and it turned maggoty and smelled awful. Moses was furious with them.
**21** So morning after morning they collected what they needed, each person taking what they could eat; once the sun grew hot, whatever remained on the ground melted away.
**22** On the sixth day they collected twice as much bread—about four quarts per person. All the community leaders came and reported this to Moses,
**23** and he explained, "This is just what the LORD promised: 'Tomorrow is a sacred day of complete rest, a holy Sabbath for the LORD. Bake whatever you want to bake and boil whatever you want to boil today, then store the leftovers for tomorrow morning.'"
**24** They put the extra food aside until morning, just as Moses instructed, and it didn't spoil or grow worms.
**25** "Go ahead and eat it today," Moses told them, "because today is a Sabbath honoring the LORD. You won't find any out on the ground today.
**26** You may collect it for six days, but the seventh day is the Sabbath, so none will appear then."
**27** Even so, on the seventh day a few people went out looking to gather some, and they came back empty-handed.
**28** The LORD asked Moses, "How much longer will you people refuse to follow my commands and teachings?
**29** Pay attention! The LORD gave you the Sabbath—that's why on the sixth day he provides two days' worth of food. Everyone must stay put; nobody should leave camp on the seventh day."
**30** So the people took the seventh day off from work and rested.
**31** The Israelites named the bread "manna." It looked white like coriander seeds and tasted like thin honey wafers.
**32** Moses declared, "Here is what the LORD commands: 'Save a two-quart measure of manna for future generations, so they can see the bread I fed you in the desert after I brought you out of Egypt.'"
**33** So Moses told Aaron, "Take a jar, fill it with two quarts of manna, and place it before the LORD to be kept safe for future generations."
**34** Following the LORD's command to Moses, Aaron placed the jar in front of the covenant chest for safekeeping.
**35** The Israelites ate manna for forty years until they reached settled territory; they ate it right up to the border of Canaan.
**36** (An omer is one-tenth of an ephah—roughly two dry quarts.)

## Crossings & Lexical Notes

- **v. 2 `וַיִּלּוֹנוּ` (*vayyillonu*):** *Ketiv/Qere* variant in WLC: the written consonantal *Ketiv* is `וילינו` (Hiphil, "caused murmuring"), while the traditional reading *Qere* is `וַיִּלּוֹנוּ` (Niphal, "they grumbled/murmured").
- **v. 7 `תַלִּינוּ` (*tallinu*):** Second *Ketiv/Qere* variant in this chapter: *Ketiv* is `תלונו` (Niphal, "you grumble"), while *Qere* is `תַלִּינוּ` (Hiphil, "you raise grumbling / complain").
- **v. 10 `כְּבוֹד יְהוָה` (*kevod YHWH*):** "The glory of YHWH" (*kavod*, lit. "weightiness/splendor"), first appearing here in the desert cloud as a visible manifestation of God's presence.
- **v. 15 `מָן הוּא` (*man hu*):** "What is it?" (an archaic/dialectal interrogative akin to Aramaic *man* or Hebrew *mah*), yielding the popular etymology for `מָן` (*man*, "manna," v. 31).
- **v. 16 `עֹמֶר` (*omer*):** A dry-volume measure equal to one-tenth of an *ephah* (v. 36), roughly 2.2 liters or about two quarts—a daily grain ration.
- **v. 23 `שַׁבָּתוֹן שַׁבַּת־קֹדֶשׁ` (*shabbaton shabbat-qodesh*):** "A complete rest, a holy Sabbath"—linking the seventh-day rest (`שָׁבַת` *shavat*, v. 30) to Genesis 2:2–3 before Sinai.
- **v. 34 `לִפְנֵי הָעֵדֻת` (*lifne ha-edut*):** "Before the testimony" (anticipating the Ark containing the covenant tablets, cf. Exod 25:16, 21).
