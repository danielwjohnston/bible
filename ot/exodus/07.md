## Clear & Faithful

**1** The LORD answered Moses, "Look, I have made you like God to Pharaoh, and your brother Aaron will serve as your prophet.
**2** You are to speak everything I command you, and your brother Aaron will tell Pharaoh to release the Israelites from his land.
**3** Yet I will stiffen Pharaoh's heart, and I will multiply my miraculous signs and wonders in the land of Egypt.
**4** Pharaoh will not listen to you; then I will lay my hand upon Egypt and bring out my divisions—my people the Israelites—from the land of Egypt through mighty acts of judgment.
**5** Then the Egyptians will know that I am the LORD when I stretch out my hand over Egypt and lead the Israelites out from among them."
**6** Moses and Aaron did exactly as the LORD commanded them.
**7** Moses was eighty years old and Aaron was eighty-three when they addressed Pharaoh.
**8** Now the LORD spoke to Moses and Aaron:
**9** "When Pharaoh demands of you, 'Perform a wonder for yourselves,' tell Aaron, 'Take your staff and throw it down before Pharaoh,' and it will turn into a serpent."
**10** So Moses and Aaron went in to Pharaoh and did just as the LORD had commanded. Aaron threw his staff down before Pharaoh and his officials, and it turned into a serpent.
**11** Pharaoh then summoned his wise men and sorcerers, and the Egyptian magicians also reproduced the same thing through their occult arts.
**12** Each of them threw down his staff, and they turned into serpents—yet Aaron's staff swallowed up their staffs.
**13** Even so, Pharaoh's heart grew hard, and he refused to listen to them, just as the LORD had foretold.
**14** Then the LORD told Moses, "Pharaoh's heart is stubbornly heavy; he refuses to release the people.
**15** Go to Pharaoh tomorrow morning as he goes out to the water, and wait to meet him on the bank of the Nile, taking in your hand the staff that turned into a snake.
**16** Tell him, 'The LORD, the God of the Hebrews, has sent me to you with this message: Release my people so they may worship me in the wilderness. Until now, you have refused to listen.
**17** Thus says the LORD: By this you shall know that I am the LORD—watch as I strike the waters of the Nile with the staff in my hand, and they will turn into blood!
**18** The fish in the Nile will die, the river will reek, and the Egyptians will struggle in disgust to drink water from the Nile.'"
**19** The LORD also told Moses, "Tell Aaron: 'Take your staff and stretch out your hand over the waters of Egypt—over their rivers, canals, ponds, and every reservoir—so they turn to blood.' There will be blood throughout the entire land of Egypt, even in vessels of wood and stone."
**20** Moses and Aaron carried out everything the LORD commanded. In full view of Pharaoh and his officials, Aaron raised the staff and struck the Nile's waters, and all the water in the Nile turned into blood.
**21** The fish in the Nile died, the river grew foul, and the Egyptians could no longer drink Nile water; blood stood everywhere across the land of Egypt.
**22** Yet the Egyptian magicians duplicated this with their secret arts. So Pharaoh's heart remained obstinate, and he would not listen to them, just as the LORD had spoken.
**23** Pharaoh turned on his heel and walked back into his palace, refusing to take even this to heart.
**24** Meanwhile all the Egyptians had to dig along the banks of the Nile for drinking water, since they could not drink from the river itself.
**25** Seven full days passed after the LORD struck the Nile.

## Everyday English

**1** The LORD told Moses, "Listen carefully: I'm putting you in the role of God before Pharaoh, and your brother Aaron will act as your spokesperson.
**2** You'll tell Aaron every single thing I order you to say, and Aaron will demand that Pharaoh let the Israelites leave his country.
**3** Still, I'm going to make Pharaoh's heart rigid, and I'll perform wave after wave of astonishing wonders across Egypt.
**4** Pharaoh won't listen to you. Then I'll bring my power down hard on Egypt and march my organized people, the Israelites, out of that country through crushing judgments.
**5** When I raise my hand against Egypt and lead the Israelites out from among them, the Egyptians will finally realize that I am the LORD."
**6** Moses and Aaron carried out every instruction the LORD gave them.
**7** At the time they spoke to Pharaoh, Moses was eighty years old and Aaron was eighty-three.
**8** The LORD gave Moses and Aaron these instructions:
**9** "When Pharaoh challenges you, 'Prove yourselves with a miracle!' tell Aaron, 'Take your walking stick and throw it on the ground in front of Pharaoh,' and it will turn into a huge serpent."
**10** Moses and Aaron walked into Pharaoh's court and did just what the LORD had said. Aaron tossed his staff down in front of Pharaoh and his advisors, and right away it became a serpent.
**11** Not to be outdone, Pharaoh called in his sages and sorcerers, and Egypt's court magicians pulled off the same trick using their secret spells.
**12** Every magician threw his staff down, and they all turned into serpents—but Aaron's staff swallowed all of theirs whole!
**13** Even after seeing that, Pharaoh's heart stayed rock-hard, and he wouldn't listen to a word they said, exactly as the LORD had predicted.
**14** Then the LORD told Moses, "Pharaoh is digging in his heels and flatly refusing to let the people go.
**15** Catch him first thing in the morning when he walks down to the river. Stand waiting on the bank of the Nile, holding the staff that turned into a snake.
**16** Tell him, 'The LORD, the God of the Hebrews, sent me to tell you: Let my people go so they can worship me in the desert. Up until now, you haven't listened at all.
**17** So here is what the LORD says: This next sign will prove to you that I am the LORD—watch me strike the Nile River with the staff in my hand, and the water will turn to blood!
**18** Every fish in the river will die, the Nile will rot and stink, and Egyptians won't be able to stomach drinking from it.'"
**19** Then the LORD instructed Moses, "Tell Aaron, 'Grab your staff and wave your hand over all of Egypt's water—their rivers, irrigation canals, marshes, and every water tank—and it will turn to blood.' There will be blood all over Egypt, even inside wooden buckets and stone jars."
**20** Moses and Aaron obeyed every word the LORD commanded. Right before Pharaoh and his officials, Aaron raised the staff and hit the Nile, and the entire river turned into blood.
**21** The fish died off, the river stank horribly, and nobody in Egypt could drink Nile water—blood filled every corner of the land.
**22** Yet Egypt's magicians copied that too with their hidden arts, so Pharaoh's heart hardened even more and he ignored them, just as the LORD had warned.
**23** Pharaoh simply turned around and went back inside his palace without giving it a second thought.
**24** Meanwhile, Egyptians everywhere were forced to dig wells along the Nile's banks looking for clean drinking water, because nobody could drink from the river.
**25** Seven whole days went by after the LORD struck the Nile.

## Crossings & Lexical Notes

- **v. 1 `נְבִיאֶךָ` (*nevi'ekha*):** "Your prophet/spokesperson," clarifying the biblical meaning of *navi*—not primarily a predictor of the future, but an authorized speaker who delivers another's words (parallel to "mouth" in 4:16).
- **v. 3 `אַקְשֶׁה` (*aqsheh*), v. 13 `וַיֶּחֱזַק` (*vayyechezaq*), & v. 14 `כָּבֵד` (*kaved*):** This chapter showcases all three Hebrew verbs for heart-hardening in Exodus: `קָשָׁה` (*qashah*, "make hard/stiff/unbending," v. 3), `חָזַק` (*chazaq*, "grow firm/strong/resolute," vv. 13, 22), and `כָּבֵד` (*kaved*, "be heavy/dull/unmovable," v. 14, echoing Pharaoh's heavy labor in 5:9 and contrasting with God's *kavod* "glory").
- **v. 9 `תַּנִּין` (*tannin*):** Unlike `נָחָשׁ` (*nachash*, ordinary snake in 4:3 and 7:15), *tannin* denotes a large serpent, crocodile, or chaos dragon (Gen 1:21; Ps 74:13), heightening the royal-cosmic clash with Pharaoh's cobra-crown emblem.
- **v. 25 `אַחֲרֵי הַכּוֹת־יְהוָה אֶת־הַיְאֹר` (*acharei hakkot-YHWH et-ha-ye'or*):** English versification ends Exodus 7 at v. 25; in the Westminster Leningrad Codex (WLC), however, chapter 7 continues with four additional verses (`Exod 7:26–29`), which English Bibles place at the opening of chapter 8 (`Exod 8:1–4`).

## Supplemental Texts & Allusions

- **Psalm 78:44; 105:29** (plague remembrance): *Israel's liturgical psalms celebrate how God turned Egypt's rivers and streams into blood so their enemies could not drink.*
- **2 Timothy 3:8** (royal magicians tradition): *Paul names Jannes and Jambres as the court figures who opposed Moses, drawing on Jewish tradition surrounding the Egyptian magicians of Exod 7:11–12.*
- **Revelation 16:4–6** (waters turned to blood): *John's vision of rivers and springs turning to blood echoes the first Egyptian plague as an act of divine judgment.*
