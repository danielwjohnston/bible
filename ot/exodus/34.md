## Clear & Faithful

**1** The LORD said to Moses, "Chisel two stone tablets like the first ones, and I will write upon the tablets the words that were on the first tablets, which you shattered.
**2** Be ready by morning, and come up in the morning to Mount Sinai and present yourself to me there on the summit of the mountain.
**3** No one shall come up with you, nor let anyone be seen anywhere on the mountain; neither let flocks nor herds graze in front of that mountain."
**4** So Moses chiseled two stone tablets like the first ones, rose early in the morning, and went up Mount Sinai as the LORD had commanded him, carrying the two stone tablets in his hand.
**5** Then the LORD descended in the cloud, stood beside him there, and proclaimed the name of the LORD.
**6** The LORD passed before his face and proclaimed: "The LORD, the LORD, a God compassionate and gracious, slow to anger, and abounding in steadfast love and faithfulness,
**7** keeping steadfast love for thousands, forgiving iniquity and transgression and sin, yet by no means leaving the guilty unpunished, visiting the iniquity of the parents upon the children and the children's children, to the third and the fourth generation."
**8** Moses immediately bowed low to the ground and worshiped.
**9** He said, "If I have indeed found favor in your sight, O Lord, please let my Lord go in our midst, even though this is a stiff-necked people; pardon our iniquity and our sin, and claim us as your own inheritance."
**10** And he said, "Behold, I am making a covenant. Before all your people I will perform wonders that have never been created in all the earth or among any of the nations, and all the people among whom you dwell shall see the work of the LORD, for what I am doing with you is awesome.
**11** Observe carefully what I command you today: see, I am driving out before you the Amorite, the Canaanite, the Hittite, the Perizzite, the Hivite, and the Jebusite.
**12** Take care not to make a covenant with the inhabitants of the land into which you are entering, lest it become a trap in your midst.
**13** Rather, you shall tear down their altars, shatter their standing stones, and cut down their Asherah poles—
**14** for you shall not bow down to any other god, because the LORD, whose name is Jealous, is a jealous God—
**15** lest you make a covenant with the inhabitants of the land, and when they prostitute themselves after their gods and sacrifice to their gods, someone invites you and you eat from his sacrifice,
**16** and you take some of their daughters for your sons, and their daughters prostitute themselves after their gods and lead your sons into prostitution after their gods.
**17** You shall not make for yourselves cast metal gods.
**18** Keep the Feast of Unleavened Bread: for seven days you shall eat unleavened bread, as I commanded you, at the appointed time in the month of Aviv, for in the month of Aviv you came out of Egypt.
**19** Every first offspring of the womb belongs to me, including all your male livestock, the firstborn of cattle and sheep.
**20** Redeem the firstborn of a donkey with a lamb, or if you do not redeem it, break its neck. Every firstborn of your sons you shall redeem, and no one shall appear before me empty-handed.
**21** Six days you shall labor, but on the seventh day you shall rest; even in plowing time and in harvest you shall rest.
**22** Observe the Feast of Weeks with the firstfruits of the wheat harvest, and the Feast of Ingathering at the turn of the year.
**23** Three times a year all your males shall appear before the Sovereign LORD, the God of Israel.
**24** For I will dispossess nations before you and enlarge your territory, and no one will covet your land when you go up to appear before the LORD your God three times a year.
**25** Do not offer the blood of my sacrifice with anything leavened, nor let the sacrifice of the Passover feast remain overnight until morning.
**26** Bring the choicest firstfruits of your soil to the house of the LORD your God. Do not boil a young goat in its mother's milk."
**27** Then the LORD said to Moses, "Write down these words, for in accordance with these words I have made a covenant with you and with Israel."
**28** Moses remained there with the LORD forty days and forty nights; he neither ate bread nor drank water. And he wrote upon the tablets the words of the covenant, the Ten Commandments.
**29** When Moses came down from Mount Sinai with the two tablets of the testimony in his hand as he descended the mountain, Moses did not realize that the skin of his face shone because he had spoken with him.
**30** Aaron and all the Israelites looked at Moses, and behold, the skin of his face was shining, so they were afraid to come near him.
**31** But Moses called out to them; so Aaron and all the leaders of the congregation returned to him, and Moses spoke with them.
**32** Afterward all the Israelites drew near, and he commanded them everything the LORD had spoken with him on Mount Sinai.
**33** When Moses finished speaking with them, he placed a veil over his face.
**34** Whenever Moses went in before the LORD to speak with him, he would remove the veil until he came out; then he would come out and tell the Israelites whatever he had been commanded.
**35** The Israelites would see the face of Moses, that the skin of Moses' face shone, and Moses would put the veil back over his face until he went in to speak with him.

## Everyday English

**1** The LORD told Moses, "Carve two new stone slabs just like the first set, and I'll write the same words on them that were on the first slabs you smashed.
**2** Be ready at dawn, hike back up Mount Sinai, and stand before me on the mountaintop.
**3** Nobody else may climb up with you—in fact, nobody should even be seen anywhere on the mountain, and don't let sheep or cattle graze near the base."
**4** So Moses chiseled two stone slabs like the originals, got up early the next morning, and climbed Mount Sinai with the two slabs in hand, just as the LORD had ordered.
**5** The LORD came down in the cloud, stood there beside him, and announced his own name—the LORD.
**6** As the LORD passed right in front of Moses, he proclaimed: "The LORD, the LORD—the compassionate and kindhearted God! Patient and slow to lose his temper, overflowing with unfailing love and truthfulness,
**7** maintaining faithful love for thousands of generations, forgiving wrongdoing, rebellion, and sin. Yet he never leaves guilt unpunished, holding children and grandchildren accountable for their ancestors' sins to the third and fourth generations."
**8** Immediately Moses dropped to the ground and bowed in worship.
**9** He prayed, "My Lord, if I truly have your favor, please travel right in our midst. Even though these people are stubborn, forgive our wrongdoing and our sin, and claim us as your very own possession!"
**10** God answered, "Look, I am establishing a covenant right now. In full view of all your people, I'll perform astonishing deeds never seen anywhere on earth or among any nation. Every person around you will witness the LORD's awesome work that I'm doing with you.
**11** Pay close attention to what I command you today: I'm going to clear out the Amorites, Canaanites, Hittites, Perizzites, Hivites, and Jebusites ahead of you.
**12** Be careful never to sign treaties with the people living in the land you're entering, or they'll become a deadly trap right in your midst.
**13** Instead, smash their altars, break their sacred stone pillars, and chop down their Asherah poles.
**14** Never bow down to any rival god, because the LORD—whose very name is Jealous—is a deeply jealous God.
**15** If you make treaties with the locals, when they chase after their own gods and offer sacrifices to them, they'll invite you over and you'll end up eating their sacrificial meals.
**16** Worse yet, you'll let your sons marry their daughters, and when their daughters worship their gods, they'll lure your sons into chasing those gods too.
**17** Never cast metal idols for yourselves.
**18** Celebrate the Festival of Yeast-Free Bread: eat bread without yeast for seven days, just as I ordered, at the set time in the month of Aviv—because Aviv was the month you left Egypt.
**19** Every firstborn male belongs to me, including the firstborn males of all your cattle and sheep.
**20** Buy back every firstborn donkey by sacrificing a lamb; if you refuse to redeem it, break its neck. You must also redeem every firstborn son. Nobody may appear before me empty-handed.
**21** Do your work for six days, and rest on the seventh day—even during busy plowing and harvest seasons, take your rest.
**22** Celebrate the Harvest Festival of Weeks with the first crops of your wheat harvest, and the Festival of Final Harvest at the close of the year.
**23** Three times every year, all your men must come into the presence of the Sovereign LORD, the God of Israel.
**24** I will drive out nations ahead of you and expand your territory, and nobody will try to raid your land while you're away appearing before the LORD your God three times a year.
**25** Never offer the blood of my sacrifice alongside anything with yeast, and don't leave any meat from the Passover sacrifice sitting overnight until morning.
**26** Bring the very best first crops from your fields to the house of the LORD your God. Never boil a young goat in its own mother's milk."
**27** Then the LORD instructed Moses, "Write all these words down, because these are the exact terms on which I've made a covenant with you and with Israel."
**28** Moses stayed up there with the LORD for forty days and forty nights without eating food or drinking water. And God wrote the words of the covenant—the Ten Commandments—onto the stone slabs.
**29** When Moses finally hiked down Mount Sinai carrying the two covenant slabs, he had no idea his face was glowing brightly because he had been talking with God.
**30** Aaron and all the Israelites saw Moses—and noticed his face radiating light—and they were terrified to step close to him.
**31** Moses called out to them, so Aaron and the community leaders walked over, and Moses spoke with them.
**32** Soon after, the entire Israelite community came closer, and Moses passed on every instruction the LORD had given him on Mount Sinai.
**33** Once Moses finished speaking with them, he covered his face with a veil.
**34** Whenever Moses stepped into the LORD's presence to speak with him, he took the veil off until he came back out. Then he would come out and announce to Israel whatever God had commanded him.
**35** The Israelites would see how brightly Moses' face was shining, and then Moses would put the veil back over his face until the next time he went in to talk with God.

## Crossings & Lexical Notes

- **v. 6 `יְהוָה יְהוָה אֵל רַחוּם וְחַנּוּן` (*YHWH YHWH El rachum ve-channun*):** The core self-revelation of God's character (often called the Thirteen Attributes in Jewish tradition); *rachum* is rooted in *rechem* ("womb-compassion"), *channun* means "favor-bestowing," *erekh appayim* literally means "long of nostrils" (slow to flare in anger), and *rav-chesed ve-emet* means "abounding in covenant loyalty and fidelity." WLC includes OSIS note `n="c"` at v. 6 marking a minor accent difference between Codex Leningradensis and BHS.
- **v. 7 `וְנַקֵּה לֹא יְנַקֶּה` (*ve-naqqeh lo yenaqqeh*):** Infinitive absolute plus finite Piel of *naqah* ("acquit/clear"), expressing emphatic refusal to leave unrepentant guilt unpunished—balancing mercy with justice.
- **v. 14 `קַנָּא` (*qanna*):** "Jealous/zealous"—denoting covenant exclusivity (like marital fidelity in vv. 15–16), not petty envy; here treated as a title of YHWH himself.
- **v. 28 `עֲשֶׂרֶת הַדְּבָרִים` (*aseret ha-devarim*):** Literally "the Ten Words"—the Hebrew designation for the Decalogue. Though Moses writes the covenant summary (v. 27), the grammar of v. 28 leaves "he wrote" pointing back to YHWH (cf. v. 1 and Deut 10:4).
- **v. 29 `קָרַן עוֹר פָּנָיו` (*qaran or panav*):** "The skin of his face shone/sent forth rays"—denominative verb from *qeren* ("horn / ray of light," cf. Hab 3:4); the Latin Vulgate's literal rendering *cornuta* ("horned") famously inspired later Renaissance iconography, though the Hebrew idiom denotes radiant beams of reflected divine glory.

## Supplemental Texts & Allusions

- **Numbers 14:18, Joel 2:13, Jonah 4:2 & Psalm 103:8** (echoes of Exod 34:6–7): *Repeatedly invoke YHWH's Sinai self-proclamation as compassionate, gracious, slow to anger, and abounding in steadfast love during moments of national crisis or repentance.*
- **Deuteronomy 10:1–5** (second stone tablets): *Recounts chiseling the replacement tablets, YHWH inscribing the Ten Words as before, and placing them inside the acacia-wood ark.*
- **2 Corinthians 3:7–18** (Moses' shining face and veil): *Paul reflects extensively on the fading glory on Moses' face and the veil he wore (Exod 34:29–35) to contrast the ministry of the letter with the Spirit's enduring transformation.*
- **John 1:14, 17** (abounding in grace and truth): *Alludes to the Hebrew pair chesed ve-emet ("steadfast love and faithfulness") proclaimed to Moses in Exodus 34:6.*
