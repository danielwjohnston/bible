## Clear & Faithful

**1** Then the LORD instructed Moses, "Go in to Pharaoh and tell him, 'Thus says the LORD: Release my people so they may worship me.
**2** If you refuse to release them, behold, I am about to strike your entire territory with frogs.
**3** The Nile will teem with frogs, and they will climb up and enter your palace, your bedroom, and your bed—into the houses of your officials and your people, even into your ovens and kneading bowls.
**4** Upon you, your people, and all your officials the frogs will climb.'"
**5** The LORD further said to Moses, "Tell Aaron: 'Stretch out your hand with your staff over the rivers, canals, and marshes, and bring up the frogs onto the land of Egypt.'"
**6** So Aaron stretched out his hand over Egypt's waters, and frogs surged up and blanketed the land of Egypt.
**7** Yet the magicians did the same with their occult arts, bringing up frogs onto the land of Egypt.
**8** Then Pharaoh summoned Moses and Aaron and pleaded, "Pray to the LORD to remove the frogs from me and my people, and I will release the people to sacrifice to the LORD."
**9** Moses replied to Pharaoh, "You may have the honor of setting the time for me: when should I pray for you, your officials, and your people to clear away the frogs from you and your homes, leaving them only in the Nile?"
**10** "Tomorrow," Pharaoh answered. Moses said, "Let it be just as you say, so that you may know there is no one like the LORD our God.
**11** The frogs will depart from you, your houses, your officials, and your people; they will remain only in the Nile."
**12** So Moses and Aaron left Pharaoh's presence, and Moses cried out to the LORD regarding the frogs he had brought upon Pharaoh.
**13** The LORD acted just as Moses asked, and the frogs died inside the houses, courtyards, and open fields.
**14** They gathered them into huge heaps, and the whole land reeked.
**15** Yet when Pharaoh saw that relief had come, he hardened his heart and refused to listen to them, just as the LORD had foretold.
**16** Then the LORD told Moses, "Tell Aaron: 'Stretch out your staff and strike the dust of the ground, and throughout the land of Egypt it will become swarms of gnats.'"
**17** They did so: Aaron stretched out his hand with his staff and struck the dust of the ground, and gnats infested both people and livestock—all the dust of the earth turned into gnats throughout Egypt.
**18** The magicians tried with their occult arts to produce gnats as well, but they could not; the gnats remained on people and animals alike.
**19** Then the magicians told Pharaoh, "This is the finger of God!" Still, Pharaoh's heart grew hard, and he would not listen to them, just as the LORD had spoken.
**20** Next the LORD said to Moses, "Rise early in the morning and stand before Pharaoh as he goes out to the water, and tell him, 'Thus says the LORD: Release my people so they may worship me.
**21** For if you do not release my people, behold, I am sending dense swarms of biting insects upon you, your officials, your people, and your homes; Egyptian houses and the very ground they stand on will be packed with swarms.
**22** Yet on that day I will set apart the region of Goshen, where my people reside, so that no swarms will appear there—that you may know that I, the LORD, am present in the midst of this earth.
**23** Thus I will set a clear distinction between my people and your people. Tomorrow this sign will take place.'"
**24** The LORD did just that: oppressive swarms poured into Pharaoh's palace, his officials' homes, and all across Egypt, so that the land was devastated by the swarms.
**25** Pharaoh summoned Moses and Aaron and said, "Go ahead and sacrifice to your God right here within the land."
**26** Moses objected, "It would not be right to do that, for what we sacrifice to the LORD our God is deeply offensive to Egyptians. If we offer what Egyptians revere right before their eyes, won't they stone us?
**27** We must travel three days into the wilderness and sacrifice to the LORD our God as he directs us."
**28** Pharaoh replied, "I will let you go to sacrifice to the LORD your God in the wilderness, provided you do not go very far away. Pray for me!"
**29** Moses said, "As soon as I leave you, I will pray to the LORD so that tomorrow the swarms depart from Pharaoh, his officials, and his people—only let Pharaoh not act deceitfully again by refusing to release the people to sacrifice to the LORD."
**30** Moses left Pharaoh and prayed to the LORD,
**31** and the LORD did as Moses asked, removing the swarms from Pharaoh, his officials, and his people; not a single one remained.
**32** Yet Pharaoh hardened his heart this time as well and did not release the people.

## Everyday English

**1** Next, the LORD sent Moses back inside: "Go to Pharaoh and tell him, 'This is what the LORD says: Let my people go so they can worship me!
**2** If you refuse to release them, watch out—I'm going to overrun your entire country with frogs.
**3** The Nile will boil over with frogs! They'll hop right into your palace, into your bedroom, and onto your bed—into your officials' houses, your citizens' homes, even inside your baking ovens and dough bowls.
**4** Frogs will be crawling all over you, your people, and every one of your advisors!'"
**5** Then the LORD instructed Moses, "Tell Aaron, 'Wave your staff over the rivers, canals, and marshes and bring the frogs up across Egypt.'"
**6** Aaron stretched his hand over Egypt's waterways, and out came the frogs, covering the entire country.
**7** Pharaoh's magicians copied that too with their secret spells, bringing even more frogs up onto the land.
**8** Then Pharaoh called Moses and Aaron in and begged, "Please ask the LORD to take these frogs away from me and my people! I'll let your people go offer sacrifices to the LORD."
**9** "I'll give you the honor of picking the exact time," Moses answered Pharaoh. "When do you want me to pray for you, your advisors, and your citizens so the frogs disappear from your homes and stay only in the Nile?"
**10** "Tomorrow!" Pharaoh said. Moses replied, "It'll happen just like you asked, so you'll learn there is nobody like the LORD our God.
**11** The frogs will clear out of your palace, your homes, your officials, and your people—only the Nile will have them left."
**12** Moses and Aaron walked out from Pharaoh's palace, and Moses pleaded with the LORD about the frogs he had sent against Pharaoh.
**13** The LORD did exactly what Moses asked: the frogs dropped dead everywhere—inside the houses, in the courtyards, and out in the fields.
**14** People shoveled them into rotting piles, and the whole country reeked.
**15** But the moment Pharaoh saw they had some breathing room, he turned stubborn again and wouldn't listen to Moses and Aaron, just as the LORD had said.
**16** Then the LORD told Moses, "Have Aaron raise his staff and strike the dust on the ground—all the dust in Egypt will turn into stinging gnats."
**17** They obeyed: Aaron held out his staff and struck the ground, and swarms of gnats attacked people and animals alike—every speck of dust across Egypt turned into gnats.
**18** Egypt's magicians tried their secret spells to conjure up gnats too, but they failed completely, while the gnats kept swarming over people and livestock.
**19** Finally the magicians admitted to Pharaoh, "God's own finger did this!" Yet Pharaoh stayed rock-stubborn and refused to listen, just as the LORD had predicted.
**20** Early the next morning, the LORD told Moses, "Get up and intercept Pharaoh when he walks out to the river. Tell him, 'Here is what the LORD says: Let my people go so they can worship me!
**21** Because if you don't let them leave, I'm going to send thick swarms of biting flies on you, your advisors, your citizens, and your houses. Every home in Egypt—and the ground right outside—will be crawling with flies.
**22** But on that day I'll wall off the region of Goshen, where my people live, so not a single swarm touches them. That way you'll know that I, the LORD, rule right here in the middle of the land.
**23** I'll draw a clear line of rescue between my people and yours. Expect this sign tomorrow.'"
**24** And the LORD followed through: thick, suffocating swarms of flies invaded Pharaoh's palace, his advisors' homes, and every corner of Egypt until the country was in ruins.
**25** Pharaoh summoned Moses and Aaron and offered a compromise: "Fine—go offer sacrifices to your God right here inside Egypt."
**26** "We can't possibly do that," Moses replied. "The animals we sacrifice to the LORD our God are sacred—sacrificing them would disgust the Egyptians! If we slaughter what Egyptians consider sacred right in front of them, they'll stone us to death.
**27** We have to hike three days into the desert and sacrifice to the LORD our God the way he instructs us."
**28** "All right," Pharaoh agreed. "I'll let you go into the desert to sacrifice to the LORD your God—just don't wander too far away. And pray for me!"
**29** Moses answered, "As soon as I leave here, I'll pray to the LORD, and tomorrow the flies will leave you, your officials, and your citizens. Just stop playing games with us by going back on your word and refusing to let the people go sacrifice to the LORD."
**30** Moses walked out and prayed to the LORD,
**31** and the LORD did what Moses asked—every single fly disappeared from Pharaoh, his officials, and his people, leaving not even one behind.
**32** Yet once again Pharaoh hardened his heart and refused to let the people leave.

## Crossings & Lexical Notes

- **v. 1–4 `בֹּא אֶל־פַּרְעֹה` (*bo el-Par'oh*, WLC `7:26–29`):** Versification shift between the Hebrew Masoretic Text (WLC) and English Bibles: WLC includes the frog-plague warning (`vv. 1–4` here) at the end of chapter 7 (`Exod 7:26–29`), and numbers WLC `8:1–28` as English `8:5–32`, yielding 32 verses in English Exodus 8.
- **v. 15 `הָרְוָחָה` (*ha-revachah*) & `וְהַכְבֵּד` (*vehakhbed*):** *Revachah* means "breathing space/relief"; Pharaoh exploits that reprieve by making his heart "heavy" (`כָּבֵד`, *kaved*, infinitive absolute *hakhbed*, repeated at v. 32 `וַיַּכְבֵּד`), whereas in v. 19 the heart-verb shifts to `חָזַק` (*chazaq*, "grew rigid/strong").
- **v. 19 `אֶצְבַּע אֱלֹהִים` (*etsba Elohim*):** "Finger of God"—an acknowledgment by Egypt's own court specialists that the third plague surpasses human magic and reflects authentic divine power (cf. Luke 11:20).
- **v. 21 `הֶעָרֹב` (*he-arov*):** Collective noun from a root meaning "mixture/swarm," denoting dense swarms of biting insects or stinging flies.
- **v. 23 `פְדֻת` (*pedut*):** Literally "ransom/redemption," used here for the protective line of deliverance separating Goshen from Egypt (anticipating the Passover ransom).
- **v. 26 `תּוֹעֲבַת מִצְרַיִם` (*to'avat Mitsrayim*):** "Abomination of Egypt"—sacrificing cattle or sheep revered in Egyptian cultic life would provoke violent outrage among local onlookers.

## Supplemental Texts & Allusions

- **Psalm 78:45; 105:30–31** (frog and insect plagues): *The historical psalms recall frogs invading royal chambers and swarms of flies and gnats filling Egypt's territory.*
- **Luke 11:20** ("finger of God"): *Jesus echoes the magicians' confession in Exod 8:19 ("If it is by the finger of God that I cast out demons...") as proof of God's sovereign kingdom breaking in.*
- **Revelation 16:13** (frogs as unclean spirits): *The imagery of frogs emerging from unclean mouths draws on the revulsion of Egypt's second plague.*
