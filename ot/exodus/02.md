## Clear & Faithful

**1** Now a man from the house of Levi went and married a Levite woman.
**2** The woman conceived and gave birth to a son; seeing how goodly he was, she kept him hidden for three months.
**3** When she could no longer conceal him, she took a papyrus basket for him, waterproofed it with bitumen and pitch, placed the child inside, and set it among the reeds along the bank of the Nile.
**4** His sister stood at a distance to watch what would happen to him.
**5** Soon Pharaoh's daughter came down to bathe at the Nile while her attendants walked along the riverbank. She noticed the basket among the reeds and sent her servant girl to fetch it.
**6** Opening it, she saw the child—a baby boy crying. She felt compassion for him and remarked, "This must be one of the Hebrew children."
**7** Then his sister spoke up to Pharaoh's daughter, "Should I go and summon a Hebrew wet nurse to nurse the child for you?"
**8** "Go," Pharaoh's daughter told her. So the young woman went and called the child's own mother.
**9** Pharaoh's daughter said to her, "Take this child away and nurse him for me, and I will pay your wages." So the woman took the child and nursed him.
**10** When the boy had grown, she brought him to Pharaoh's daughter, and he became her son. She named him Moses, explaining, "For I drew him out of the water."
**11** In the course of time, when Moses had grown to adulthood, he went out to his kinsmen and observed their hard labor. There he saw an Egyptian beating a Hebrew, one of his own people.
**12** Glancing this way and that and seeing no one around, he struck the Egyptian dead and buried him in the sand.
**13** The next day he went out again and saw two Hebrew men fighting. He asked the aggressor, "Why are you striking your fellow Hebrew?"
**14** The man retorted, "Who appointed you ruler and judge over us? Do you mean to kill me just as you killed the Egyptian?" Moses was seized with fear and thought, "Clearly the affair has become known."
**15** When Pharaoh heard about this incident, he sought to execute Moses. So Moses fled from Pharaoh's presence and settled in the land of Midian, where he sat down beside a well.
**16** Now the priest of Midian had seven daughters, who came to draw water and fill the troughs for their father's flock.
**17** Some shepherds arrived and chased them away, but Moses stood up, rescued them, and watered their flock.
**18** When they returned to Reuel their father, he asked, "How is it that you are back so early today?"
**19** They answered, "An Egyptian man rescued us from the shepherds; in fact, he even drew plenty of water for us and watered the flock!"
**20** "Then where is he?" he asked his daughters. "Why did you leave the man behind? Invite him in so he can share a meal."
**21** Moses agreed to stay with the man, who gave his daughter Zipporah to Moses in marriage.
**22** She gave birth to a son, and Moses named him Gershom, saying, "I have been a resident alien in a foreign land."
**23** After many years passed, the king of Egypt died. Still the Israelites groaned under their slavery and cried out, and their plea for rescue rose up to God from their bondage.
**24** God heard their groaning, and God remembered his covenant with Abraham, with Isaac, and with Jacob.
**25** God looked upon the Israelites, and God took full notice of them.

## Everyday English

**1** A man from the tribe of Levi married a Levite woman,
**2** and she became pregnant and had a son. When she saw what a wonderful, healthy baby he was, she hid him for three months.
**3** Once she couldn't hide him any longer, she got a basket woven from papyrus reeds, sealed it with tar and pitch so it wouldn't leak, laid the baby inside, and tucked it among the tall reeds at the edge of the Nile.
**4** The baby's older sister stayed back a little way to see what would become of him.
**5** Before long, Pharaoh's daughter came down to the river to bathe while her maids strolled along the shore. Spotting the basket in the reeds, she sent one of her attendants to bring it over.
**6** When she lifted the lid and saw the crying baby boy, her heart went out to him. "This has to be one of the Hebrew babies," she said.
**7** Right then his sister stepped up and asked Pharaoh's daughter, "Would you like me to go find a Hebrew woman who can nurse the baby for you?"
**8** "Yes, go ahead," the princess replied. So the girl ran and brought back the baby's own mother.
**9** Pharaoh's daughter told her, "Take this baby and nurse him for me, and I'll pay you for your work." The mother took her baby home and nursed him.
**10** After the boy was weaned, she brought him back to Pharaoh's daughter, who adopted him as her own son. She named him Moses, saying, "Because I pulled him out of the water."
**11** Years later, after Moses had grown up, he went out to check on his fellow Hebrews and saw how brutally they were forced to work. He watched an Egyptian beating one of his Hebrew kinsmen.
**12** Looking both ways to make sure nobody was watching, Moses killed the Egyptian and hid his body in the sand.
**13** The following day he went back out and found two Hebrew men fighting each other. He asked the one in the wrong, "Why are you hitting your own neighbor?"
**14** The man snapped back, "Who put you in charge of us as our boss and judge? Are you planning to murder me like you murdered that Egyptian?" Suddenly Moses was terrified—he realized people already knew what he'd done.
**15** Sure enough, Pharaoh heard about it and tried to have Moses executed. Moses ran for his life, escaped into Midian, and stopped to rest by a well.
**16** Now the priest of Midian had seven daughters. They came to that well to draw water and fill the troughs for their father's sheep and goats.
**17** Other shepherds showed up and bullied them away, so Moses jumped up, defended the women, and watered their animals himself.
**18** When they got home to their father, Reuel, he asked, "How'd you finish up so fast today?"
**19** They said, "An Egyptian stepped in and rescued us from those shepherds! He even drew water for us and watered the whole flock."
**20** "Well, where is he?" Reuel asked them. "Why'd you leave him standing out there? Go invite him over for dinner!"
**21** Moses decided to stay on with Reuel, who gave Moses his daughter Zipporah as his wife.
**22** She gave birth to a son, and Moses named him Gershom, explaining, "I've been an outsider living in a foreign country."
**23** Many years went by, and the king of Egypt died. Still, the Israelites groaned under their crushing slavery and cried out for help, and their desperate cries reached God.
**24** God listened to their groans and remembered the solemn covenant he had made with Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob.
**25** God saw what the Israelites were enduring, and he knew it was time to act.

## Crossings & Lexical Notes

- **v. 2 `טוֹב` (*tov*):** "Good," echoing Gen 1:4, 31 ("God saw that it was good"), signaling that this child belongs to God's preserving purpose.
- **v. 3 `תֵּבַת גֹּמֶא` (*tevat gome*) & `סוּף` (*suf*):** *Tevah* ("ark/chest") appears in the Hebrew Bible solely here and for Noah's ark in Gen 6–9, linking Moses' survival through water to Noah's deliverance; *suf* ("reeds/rushes") anticipates *Yam Suf* ("Sea of Reeds").
- **v. 10 `מֹשֶׁה` (*Mosheh*):** Associated with the Hebrew verb *mashah* ("to draw out," *meshitihu*, "I drew him out"), while also sounding like the Egyptian element *ms/mose* ("born/child of").
- **v. 18 `רְעוּאֵל` (*Re'u'el*):** "Friend of God"; called Reuel here and later Jethro (*Yitro*, Exod 3:1; 4:18) or Hobab (Num 10:29), reflecting personal name and title/clan usage in Midianite tradition.
- **v. 22 `גֵּרְשׁוֹם` (*Gershom*):** Wordplay on *ger sham* ("a resident alien there") and *ger* ("sojourner/foreigner"), capturing Moses' displacement before Israel's own exodus.
- **v. 25 `וַיֵּדַע אֱלֹהִים` (*vayyeda Elohim*):** Ends abruptly without an explicit grammatical object—"and God knew"—conveying intimate covenantal awareness and readiness to intervene.

## Supplemental Texts & Allusions

- **Genesis 6:14** (ark motif): *Both Noah's vessel and Moses' reed basket share the rare noun tevah and waterproof coating, portraying deliverance through perilous waters.*
- **Acts 7:20–29** (Moses' early life): *Stephen summarizes Moses' birth, upbringing in Pharaoh's palace, defense of an oppressed Israelite, and forty-year exile in Midian.*
- **Hebrews 11:23–26** (faith of Moses and his parents): *Hebrews highlights how Moses' parents hid him without fearing the royal edict and how Moses later chose solidarity with God's mistreated people over Egyptian privilege.*
