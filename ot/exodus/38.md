## Clear & Faithful

**1** He made the altar of burnt offering out of acacia wood, five cubits long and five cubits wide—square—and three cubits high.
**2** He made its horns on its four corners, of one piece with it, and overlaid it with bronze.
**3** He crafted all the utensils of the altar—the pots, the shovels, the tossing basins, the meat forks, and the firepans—making all its utensils of bronze.
**4** For the altar he fashioned a grating of bronze mesh network beneath its ledge from below, reaching halfway up.
**5** He cast four rings at the four corners of the bronze grating to serve as holders for the poles.
**6** He made the poles of acacia wood and overlaid them with bronze,
**7** and inserted the poles into the rings on the sides of the altar for carrying it; he built the altar hollow out of planks.
**8** He also made the bronze basin and its bronze stand from the mirrors of the women who served at the entrance of the tent of meeting.
**9** Next, he constructed the courtyard. On the south side facing the Negev, the courtyard hangings were of finely twisted linen, one hundred cubits long,
**10** with their twenty pillars and twenty bronze socket-bases, while the pillar hooks and their bands were silver.
**11** On the north side they were one hundred cubits long, with their twenty pillars and twenty bronze socket-bases, and silver hooks and bands for the pillars.
**12** On the west side there were hangings of fifty cubits, with their ten pillars and ten socket-bases, and silver hooks and bands for the pillars.
**13** On the east side toward the sunrise it was fifty cubits wide:
**14** hangings of fifteen cubits on one wing, with their three pillars and three socket-bases,
**15** and on the second wing—on either side of the courtyard gate—hangings of fifteen cubits, with their three pillars and three socket-bases.
**16** All the hangings surrounding the courtyard were of finely twisted linen.
**17** The socket-bases for the pillars were bronze, the hooks and bands of the pillars were silver, and the overlays of their capitals were silver; thus all the courtyard pillars were banded with silver.
**18** The screen for the courtyard gate was the work of an embroiderer in blue, purple, and crimson yarn and finely twisted linen; it was twenty cubits in length, and its height along the breadth was five cubits, matching the courtyard hangings.
**19** Its four pillars and four socket-bases were bronze; their hooks were silver, and the overlays of their capitals and their bands were silver.
**20** All the tent pegs for the tabernacle and for the surrounding courtyard were bronze.
**21** Here is the inventory of the tabernacle—the tabernacle of the testimony—as compiled at Moses' command by the work of the Levites under the direction of Ithamar son of Aaron the priest.
**22** Bezalel son of Uri, son of Hur, of the tribe of Judah, made everything that the LORD had commanded Moses;
**23** and with him was Oholiab son of Ahisamach, of the tribe of Dan, an engraver, a master designer, and an embroiderer in blue, purple, and crimson yarn and fine linen.
**24** All the gold used for the work throughout the sanctuary project—the gold of the wave offering—came to twenty-nine talents and seven hundred thirty shekels, according to the sanctuary shekel.
**25** The silver from the registered men of the congregation amounted to one hundred talents and one thousand seven hundred seventy-five shekels, according to the sanctuary shekel:
**26** a beka per person—that is, half a shekel by the sanctuary shekel—for every man who passed into the census from twenty years old and upward, numbering six hundred three thousand five hundred fifty men.
**27** The hundred talents of silver were used to cast the socket-bases of the sanctuary and the socket-bases of the veil: one hundred socket-bases from the hundred talents, one talent per socket-base.
**28** From the remaining one thousand seven hundred seventy-five shekels he made hooks for the pillars, overlaid their capitals, and banded them.
**29** The bronze of the wave offering came to seventy talents and two thousand four hundred shekels.
**30** With it he made the socket-bases for the entrance of the tent of meeting, the bronze altar with its bronze grating and all the altar utensils,
**31** the socket-bases all around the courtyard, the socket-bases for the courtyard gate, and all the tent pegs for the tabernacle and for the courtyard all around.

## Everyday English

**1** Next, Bezalel built the burnt-offering altar from acacia wood as a square—7.5 feet long, 7.5 feet wide, and 4.5 feet tall.
**2** He carved four corner horns out of the frame itself and plated the entire altar with bronze.
**3** Then he made all the altar tools out of bronze: the ash buckets, shovels, sprinkling bowls, meat forks, and coal pans.
**4** He wove a bronze mesh grate and mounted it beneath the altar's ledge, halfway down inside the frame.
**5** He cast four rings at the four corners of the bronze grate to hold the carrying poles,
**6** shaped the poles from acacia wood, and plated them with bronze.
**7** He slipped the poles through the side rings for transporting the altar, which was built as a hollow wooden frame.
**8** He also cast the bronze washbasin and its bronze stand using the polished bronze mirrors donated by the women who served at the doorway of the meeting tent.
**9** Then he set up the outer courtyard. Along the south side facing the desert, he hung 150 feet of fine twisted linen curtains,
**10** supported by 20 posts seated in 20 bronze bases, using silver hooks and silver bands on the posts.
**11** Along the north side, he hung another 150 feet of curtains on 20 posts in 20 bronze bases, again with silver hooks and bands.
**12** Across the western end, he hung 75 feet of curtains on 10 posts in 10 bronze bases, fitted with silver hooks and bands.
**13** The eastern front, facing sunrise, also measured 75 feet wide:
**14** 22.5 feet of curtains on one side of the entrance held by 3 posts in 3 bases,
**15** and 22.5 feet of curtains on the opposite side of the courtyard gate held by 3 posts in 3 bases.
**16** Every curtain enclosing the courtyard was woven of fine twisted linen.
**17** The bases under the posts were bronze, while the hooks, trim bands, and plated tops of every courtyard post were silver.
**18** For the courtyard gate itself, an embroiderer wove a 30-foot-wide curtain from blue, purple, and bright red yarn with fine twisted linen, standing 7.5 feet tall to match the surrounding courtyard curtains.
**19** It hung on 4 bronze-based posts fitted with silver hooks, silver-plated tops, and silver bands.
**20** All the tent stakes used around both the sacred tent and the courtyard were bronze.
**21** Here is the official inventory of materials used for the sacred tent—the tent housing the covenant tablets—recorded under Moses' orders by the Levites led by Ithamar son of Aaron the priest.
**22** Bezalel son of Uri, grandson of Hur, from the tribe of Judah, built everything the LORD had commanded Moses,
**23** assisted by Oholiab son of Ahisamach, from the tribe of Dan—an engraver, master designer, and embroiderer in blue, purple, and scarlet yarn and fine linen.
**24** The total gold donated as a dedicated offering for all the sanctuary work weighed roughly 2,193 pounds (nearly 1,000 kilograms)—twenty-nine talents and 730 sanctuary shekels.
**25** The silver collected from the registered men of the community weighed about 7,544 pounds (roughly 3,440 kilograms)—one hundred talents and 1,775 sanctuary shekels.
**26** Every enrolled man aged twenty and older paid a standard coin weight—one-fifth of an ounce (half a sanctuary shekel)—across all 603,550 registered men.
**27** The hundred talents—7,500 pounds of silver—were melted down to cast the 100 mounting bases for the sacred tent walls and the inner divider curtain, using about 75 pounds of silver per base.
**28** The remaining 44 pounds of silver (1,775 shekels) went toward forging hooks for the posts, plating their tops, and making their decorative bands.
**29** The dedicated bronze offering totaled about 5,310 pounds (roughly 2,420 kilograms)—seventy talents and 2,400 shekels.
**30** With that bronze they cast the mounting bases for the meeting tent entrance, the burnt-offering altar with its bronze grate and all its tools,
**31** all the bases circling the courtyard, the bases for the courtyard gate, and every tent stake for both the sacred tent and the courtyard.

## Crossings & Lexical Notes

- **v. 8 `בְּמַרְאֹת הַצֹּבְאֹת` (*be-mar'ot ha-tsove'ot*):** "From the mirrors of the women who served/kept watch"—bronze hand-mirrors polished to a bright reflection were sacrificed by the women ministering at the tent doorway (`צָבָא`, used of organized Levitical/sanctuary duty in Num 4:23; 8:24) to forge the basin of purification.
- **v. 21 `פְּקוּדֵי הַמִּשְׁכָּן` (*pequde ha-mishkan*):** "The inventory/reckonings of the tabernacle"—from *paqad* ("count/audit/appoint"), linking the census-silver accounting (30:12–16) with transparent public stewardship led by Ithamar.
- **v. 26 `בֶּקַע` (*beqa*):** Literally "a split/half"—specifically one-half of a sanctuary shekel (10 gerahs, about 5.7 grams or 0.2 ounces). Multiplying 603,550 men by half a shekel yields 301,775 shekels, which at 3,000 shekels per talent equals exactly 100 talents + 1,775 shekels (v. 25).
- **v. 27 `כִּכָּר לָאָדֶן` (*kikkar la-aden*):** "One talent per socket-base"—each heavy silver base weighed roughly 75 pounds (34 kg), anchoring the 15-foot acacia frames securely into desert sand.
