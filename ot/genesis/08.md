## Clear & Faithful

**1** God remembered Noah and all the wild and domestic animals with him in the ark. God sent a wind over the earth, and the waters subsided.
**2** The springs of the deep and the windows of the heavens were closed, and the rain from the heavens was held back.
**3** The waters steadily receded from the earth. At the end of one hundred and fifty days, the waters had diminished.
**4** On the seventeenth day of the seventh month, the ark came to rest on the mountains of Ararat.
**5** The waters continued to diminish until the tenth month. On the first day of the tenth month, the mountaintops became visible.
**6** At the end of forty days, Noah opened the window he had made in the ark
**7** and sent out the raven. It went back and forth until the waters had dried from the earth.
**8** Then he sent out the dove to see whether the waters had abated from the surface of the ground.
**9** But the dove found no resting place for its feet and returned to him in the ark, because water covered the surface of the whole earth. He reached out his hand, took it, and brought it back into the ark with him.
**10** He waited another seven days and again sent the dove out of the ark.
**11** The dove returned to him toward evening, and there in its beak was a freshly plucked olive leaf. Noah knew that the waters had abated from the earth.
**12** He waited another seven days and sent out the dove. This time it did not return to him.
**13** In the six-hundred-and-first year, on the first day of the first month, the waters had dried from the earth. Noah removed the ark’s covering and looked: the surface of the ground had dried.
**14** By the twenty-seventh day of the second month, the earth was dry.
**15** God said to Noah,
**16** “Come out of the ark with your wife, your sons, and your sons’ wives.
**17** Bring out all the living creatures with you—all flesh, birds, domestic animals, and everything that crawls along the earth—so they may swarm over the earth, be fruitful, and multiply upon it.”
**18** Noah came out with his sons, his wife, and his sons’ wives.
**19** Every living creature, every crawling thing, and every bird—everything moving upon the earth—came out of the ark by their families.
**20** Noah built an altar to the LORD. He took some of every clean animal and every clean bird and offered burnt offerings on the altar.
**21** The LORD smelled the pleasing aroma and said in his heart, “I will never again curse the ground because of humankind, for the inclination of the human heart is evil from youth. I will never again strike down every living thing as I have done.
**22** As long as the earth endures, seedtime and harvest, cold and heat, summer and winter, day and night will not cease.”

## Everyday English

**1** God remembered Noah and all the wild and farm animals with him in the ark. He sent a wind across the earth, and the floodwaters began to settle.
**2** The springs of the deep and the sky’s windows closed, and the rain stopped.
**3** The water kept draining away. After one hundred and fifty days, it had gone down.
**4** On the seventeenth day of the seventh month, the ark settled on the mountains of Ararat.
**5** The water kept dropping until the tenth month. On the first day of that month, the mountaintops could be seen.
**6** Forty days later, Noah opened the window he had made in the ark
**7** and let the raven out. It flew back and forth until the floodwaters had dried from the earth.
**8** He also let the dove out to find out whether the water had gone down from the ground.
**9** The dove could find nowhere to land, because water still covered the ground everywhere. It came back to Noah in the ark. He held out his hand, took the dove, and brought it inside.
**10** Noah waited seven more days, then let the dove out again.
**11** It came back that evening with a freshly plucked olive leaf in its beak. Then Noah knew the water had gone down.
**12** He waited another seven days and let the dove out once more. It did not come back.
**13** On the first day of the first month of Noah’s six-hundred-and-first year, the water had dried from the earth. Noah took the covering off the ark and looked out. The ground’s surface had dried.
**14** By the twenty-seventh day of the second month, the land was dry.
**15** Then God told Noah,
**16** “Leave the ark with your wife, your sons, and their wives.
**17** Take all the living creatures with you—the birds, the farm animals, and everything that crawls along the ground. Let them spread over the earth, have offspring, and grow in number.”
**18** So Noah left the ark with his sons, his wife, and his sons’ wives.
**19** The animals, the creatures that crawled, and the birds—everything that moved on the earth—left the ark in their family groups.
**20** Noah built an altar to the LORD. He chose animals and birds from every clean kind and offered them on the altar as burnt offerings.
**21** The LORD smelled the pleasing aroma and said to himself, “I will never again curse the ground because of human beings, for their hearts turn toward evil from youth. I will never again destroy every living thing as I have done.
**22** For as long as the earth lasts, planting and harvest, cold and heat, summer and winter, day and night will keep coming.”

## Crossings & Lexical Notes

- **8:1 — Remembered:** *Zakar* is retained as “remembered.” The narrative immediately follows this with God’s action for Noah and the animals. The draft does not paraphrase it as an assertion that God previously lost knowledge of them.
- **8:1 — Wind:** *Ruach* is rendered “wind” in the action of the waters subsiding. The same Hebrew word can elsewhere be rendered “breath” or “spirit”; its occurrence here does not by itself settle a theological identification. Compare the fuller breath expression in Genesis 7:22.
- **8:3, 5, 7 — Repeated movement:** Verse 3 has “going and returning,” verse 5 “going and diminishing,” and verse 7 “going out and returning.” The more natural registers use steadily receding, continued diminution, and back-and-forth flight respectively. The raven’s movements are not replaced with the dove’s later sequence.
- **8:4 — Mountains of Ararat:** *Harei* is plural. The draft preserves “mountains of Ararat”; it does not identify one modern summit as the location specified by the Hebrew.
- **8:6–12 — Birds and intervals:** The source names a raven, then the dove, followed by two waits of another seven days and further releases of the dove. Verse 6 says “at the end of forty days”; Everyday English reads this in the sequence after verse 5. The translation does not insert an unstated date for the first release or an extra numbered waiting period before it.
- **8:9, 11 — Foot and mouth:** Word-for-Word retains “sole of its foot” and “in its mouth.” The readable registers use “feet” and “beak” for natural English. The olive leaf is described as plucked; “freshly plucked” is the rendering adopted here, not a statement about an independently established botanical timetable.
- **8:13–14 — Two observations of drying:** The first day of the first month in Noah’s six-hundred-and-first year concerns the disappearance of water and the visible ground surface. The twenty-seventh day of the second month is separately named as the time when the earth was dry. The draft keeps both dates and does not treat the first observation as the command to leave.
- **8:17, 19 — Fruitfulness and families:** Verse 17 combines swarming, fruitfulness, and multiplication. Verse 19 uses *mishpechot*, “families,” rather than the “kind” wording of Genesis 7:14. The registers preserve that difference without claiming either term is a modern scientific rank.
- **8:20 — Burnt offerings:** *‘Olot* is rendered “burnt offerings.” Noah takes from clean animals and clean birds. The passage does not list the individual species or the number of offerings, so this draft does not supply them.
- **8:21 — Pleasing aroma and speech to the heart:** *Reach hannichoach* is rendered “pleasing aroma.” “Said to his heart” becomes “said in his heart” or “said to himself” in the readable registers. These renderings retain the narrative’s portrayal; they do not establish a claim about divine physical anatomy.
- **8:21 — For / although:** The Hebrew *ki* introduces the statement about the human heart’s evil inclination from youth. This draft retains “for,” leaving the stated reason visible. A concessive rendering, “although,” is an interpretive alternative requiring review. Neither option makes this verse an assertion that human wrongdoing has ended.
- **8:21–22 — Scope of the promise:** The wording concerns cursing the ground on account of humanity, striking every living thing “as I have done,” and the continuing cycles of the earth. The draft does not expand it into a claim that no future judgment or local disaster can occur. Verse 22’s paired seasons and times are retained without an added climate or calendar theory.

## Supplemental Texts & Allusions

These are explicit later uses of the Noah account. Their inclusion does not claim that Genesis quotes them. The excerpts below are from **WEB Updated (Public Domain)**, separate from the draft registers.

### Isaiah 54:9 — A comparison with the waters of Noah

> **9** “For this is like the waters of Noah to me; for as I have sworn that the waters of Noah will no more go over the earth, so I have sworn that I will not be angry with you, nor rebuke you.

The later passage compares a promise to the waters-of-Noah pledge. Read Genesis 8:21–22 with the further covenant wording in Genesis 9; the reference does not make the two chapters a single undifferentiated speech.

### 1 Peter 3:20–21 — Later use of preservation through water

> **20** who before were disobedient when God waited patiently in the days of Noah while the ship was being built. In it, few, that is, eight souls, were saved through water.
> **21** This is a symbol of baptism, which now saves you—not the putting away of the filth of the flesh, but the answer of a good conscience toward God—through the resurrection of Jesus Christ,

These verses are an excerpt from a continuing discussion. They explicitly connect Noah’s preservation with baptism in the later author’s argument. Genesis 8 itself does not use baptism vocabulary, and this cross-reference does not settle competing interpretations of 1 Peter’s wording.
