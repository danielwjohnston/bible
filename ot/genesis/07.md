## Clear & Faithful

**1** The LORD said to Noah, “Enter the ark, you and your whole household, because I have seen that you are righteous before me in this generation.
**2** Take seven pairs of every clean animal, each male with its mate, and one pair of every unclean animal, a male with its mate.
**3** Take seven pairs of the birds of the heavens as well, male and female, to preserve their offspring throughout the earth.
**4** In seven more days I will send rain upon the earth for forty days and forty nights. I will wipe from the surface of the ground every living thing that I have made.”
**5** Noah did all that the LORD commanded him.
**6** Noah was six hundred years old when the floodwaters came upon the earth.
**7** Noah entered the ark with his sons, his wife, and his sons’ wives because of the floodwaters.
**8** From the clean animals and the unclean animals, from the birds, and from everything that moves along the ground,
**9** they came to Noah in the ark two by two, male and female, as God had commanded Noah.
**10** After the seven days, the floodwaters came upon the earth.
**11** In the six-hundredth year of Noah’s life, on the seventeenth day of the second month, on that very day all the springs of the great deep burst open, and the windows of the heavens opened.
**12** Rain fell upon the earth for forty days and forty nights.
**13** On that very day Noah entered the ark with his sons Shem, Ham, and Japheth, his wife, and his sons’ three wives.
**14** With them came every kind of wild animal, every kind of domestic animal, every kind of creature that crawls along the earth, and every kind of bird—every bird and winged creature.
**15** They came to Noah in the ark two by two, from all flesh that had the breath of life.
**16** Those that entered were male and female from all flesh, as God had commanded him. Then the LORD shut him in.
**17** The flood continued upon the earth for forty days. The waters increased and lifted the ark until it rose above the ground.
**18** The waters grew powerful and increased greatly upon the earth, and the ark floated on the surface of the waters.
**19** The waters rose more and more upon the earth until all the high mountains under the whole heavens were covered.
**20** The waters rose fifteen cubits above, covering the mountains.
**21** All flesh that moved upon the earth perished: birds, domestic animals, wild animals, every creature that swarmed upon the earth, and all humankind.
**22** Everything on dry ground that had the breath of life in its nostrils died.
**23** He wiped away every living thing on the surface of the ground, from humans to animals, crawling creatures, and birds of the heavens. They were wiped from the earth. Only Noah and those with him in the ark remained.
**24** The waters prevailed upon the earth for one hundred and fifty days.

## Everyday English

**1** The LORD told Noah, “Go into the ark with everyone in your household. In this generation, I have seen that you live rightly before me.
**2** Bring seven pairs of each clean animal, a male with its mate. Bring a single pair of each animal that is not clean, a male with its mate.
**3** Bring seven pairs of each kind of bird too, male and female, so their offspring can continue across the earth.
**4** Seven days from now I will send rain on the earth. It will fall for forty days and forty nights. I will wipe every living thing I have made from the ground.”
**5** Noah did everything the LORD told him to do.
**6** He was six hundred years old when the flood came.
**7** To escape the floodwaters, Noah went into the ark with his sons, his wife, and his sons’ wives.
**8** Clean animals, animals that were not clean, birds, and creatures that moved along the ground
**9** came to Noah inside the ark in pairs, male and female, just as God had told him.
**10** When the seven days had passed, the floodwaters came over the earth.
**11** It happened in Noah’s six-hundredth year, on the seventeenth day of the second month. That very day, all the springs of the great deep broke open, and the sky’s windows opened.
**12** Rain poured onto the earth for forty days and forty nights.
**13** On that day Noah went into the ark with his sons Shem, Ham, and Japheth, his wife, and the three wives of his sons.
**14** They were joined by every kind of wild animal, farm animal, creature that crawls on the ground, and bird—every kind of winged creature.
**15** Animals with the breath of life came to Noah in the ark, two by two.
**16** Males and females entered, just as God had told Noah. Then the LORD closed the ark behind him.
**17** For forty days the flood spread over the earth. The rising water lifted the ark off the ground.
**18** The water became powerful and kept rising, and the ark floated on it.
**19** The water rose higher and higher until it covered all the high mountains under the sky.
**20** It rose another fifteen cubits, covering the mountains.
**21** Every living creature moving on the earth died: birds, farm animals, wild animals, small creatures swarming over the ground, and every human being.
**22** Everything living on dry ground that breathed through its nostrils died.
**23** God wiped every living thing from the ground—people, animals, crawling creatures, and birds. They were swept from the earth. Only Noah and those with him in the ark survived.
**24** The floodwaters held their power over the earth for one hundred and fifty days.

## Crossings & Lexical Notes

- **7:1, 5, 9, 16 — Divine names:** The Hebrew uses *YHWH* in verses 1, 5, and the closing clause of 16; it uses *Elohim* in verses 9 and 16. Word-for-Word retains YHWH; the other registers use LORD. Both render *Elohim* as God. These are visible wording distinctions, not a settled account of the text’s composition.
- **7:2–3 — “Seven seven”:** The distributive wording *shiv‘ah shiv‘ah* is followed by “a male and its mate” in verse 2 and “male and female” in verse 3. The readable registers take this as seven pairs. The more literal register leaves the repeated number visible. The relationship between these totals and the “two two” entry descriptions in verses 9 and 15 deserves review rather than silent deletion of either expression.
- **7:2, 8 — Clean / not clean:** *Tahor* and the negated expression distinguish categories of animals; they do not describe animals as washed or dirty. Genesis 8:20 subsequently connects clean animals and birds with Noah’s offerings. This draft does not import a full later list of animal categories into the verse.
- **7:3 — Offspring:** *Zera‘*, literally “seed,” is rendered “offspring” here because the stated purpose is preserving animal life. It does not identify a modern biological classification.
- **7:4, 23 — Wipe away:** The repeated verb from *machah* links the announced action with its narration. “Living existence” in Word-for-Word represents *yequm*; the readable registers use “living thing.” Verse 23 supplies the narrative’s human-and-animal scope.
- **7:11 — Great deep / windows of the heavens:** The draft retains the source’s paired imagery of water below and above. It does not replace the imagery with an asserted modern geological mechanism. “On that very day” renders the emphatic expression also used in verse 13.
- **7:14 — Kinds and animal groupings:** *Min* is rendered “kind”; *chayyah* and *behemah* are differentiated here as wild and domestic animals. Everyday English uses “farm animals” for accessibility. These choices require lexical review and do not claim equivalence with modern taxonomic ranks.
- **7:19–20 — Extent and measurement:** The Hebrew says all the high mountains under all the heavens were covered, then gives fifteen cubits “upward/above.” The readable registers understand the measurement in relation to the covered mountains. They retain cubits rather than insert a falsely exact modern conversion. Debates about geographical scope belong in a sourced interpretive layer, not an unmarked alteration of the verse.
- **7:21–22 — Flesh and breath:** *Basar* (“flesh”) includes humans and animals in the listed groups. Verse 22 has the fuller expression *nishmat ruach chayyim*, represented as “breath of the spirit of life” in Word-for-Word and “breath of life” in the readable registers. It also explicitly says “on dry ground.”
- **7:12, 17, 24 — Durations:** The chapter states forty days and nights of rain and one hundred and fifty days during which the waters prevailed. This draft preserves the stated durations without adding them together or settling their overlap; compare the recession narrative in Genesis 8:3–5.

## Supplemental Texts & Allusions

These are later passages that explicitly refer to Noah and the flood. Their inclusion does not claim that Genesis quotes them. The excerpts below are from **WEB Updated (Public Domain)**, separate from the draft registers.

### Luke 17:26–27 — A later comparison with Noah’s days

> **26** As it was in the days of Noah, even so it will also be in the days of the Son of Man.
> **27** They ate, they drank, they married, and they were given in marriage until the day that Noah entered into the ship, and the flood came and destroyed them all.

The comparison explicitly recalls entry into the ark and the arrival of the flood. Its later application should not be inserted into Genesis as if it were part of the Hebrew wording.

### 2 Peter 2:5 — Noah and preservation

> **5** and didn’t spare the ancient world, but preserved Noah with seven others, a preacher of righteousness, when he brought a flood on the world of the ungodly,

This is an excerpt from a continuing sentence. It adds the later description “a preacher of righteousness”; Genesis 7:1 calls Noah righteous but does not contain that preaching description.
