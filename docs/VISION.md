# Long-term product vision

## Purpose

Create a comprehensive, open, extensible Scripture study platform supporting every meaningful modality of learning, investigation, interpretation, comprehension, spiritual formation, and practical application, built upon a genuinely copyleft Bible corpus.

The corpus is foundational infrastructure. The founder's goal is to create a fresh translation from the original languages whose covered adaptations remain available under reciprocal terms. A public-domain Bible can be a useful source or comparison text, but public-domain availability alone does not achieve that reciprocity goal. Open application code alone also does not establish rights to redistribute, adapt, narrate, annotate, or transform a Bible text. See [corpus and licensing](CORPUS_AND_LICENSING.md).

The existing project begins with sequential Genesis-to-Revelation translation, three English registers, lexical notes, inline allusions, and a self-contained offline reader. Preserve that work while building an ecosystem in which those same passage identities and rights records can support much more than reading.

## Scope is comprehensive and extensible

All 82 categories from the preceding study taxonomy, every method listed under them, and meaningful combinations of those methods belong to the long-term product requirement. This is not a menu of optional ideas that can silently disappear after an initial reader release. Delivery can be phased; scope reductions require an explicit recorded decision and a coverage impact assessment.

There is no finite enumeration of every possible study. New methods, media, contexts, and combinations will continue to emerge. Preserve the original 82-category baseline, then extend it with versioned identifiers and relationships. Do not promise that an arbitrary count such as 100,000 entries exhausts the space. Support discovery, authoring, and composition so the space can grow without redesigning the platform.

The [taxonomy source record](STUDY_TAXONOMY.md) preserves all 82 categories and 4,588 entries in the supplied reconstruction, plus diagrams, lifecycle, objective mappings, recommendations, calculator axes, and generator choices. The [coverage register](TAXONOMY_COVERAGE.md) maps each category and every entry by inheritance to requirements and milestones. All 743 available original-excerpt entries match exactly; fidelity to the remainder of the direct original transcript remains unverified.

## Product pillars

| Pillar | Intended outcome |
| --- | --- |
| Copyleft corpus | Read, quote, print, adapt, translate, narrate, transform, and redistribute covered original work with attribution and reciprocal licensing. |
| Scripture data architecture | Versioned text, stable passage and token identities, register alignment, language metadata, source editions, and explicit provenance. |
| Universal study taxonomy | Discoverable modalities, methods, frameworks, lenses, cognitive operations, representations, contexts, objectives, applications, evaluation, and integration. |
| Study composition | Combine independent dimensions into reusable, inspectable study recipes. |
| Knowledge architecture | Connect passages, words, people, places, events, themes, doctrines, arguments, and relationships without confusing assertions with evidence. |
| Multimodal presentation | Text, audio, visual explanation, interactive exploration, embodied activities, and accessible alternatives. |
| Study authoring | Turn authored studies into portable content and reusable structures rather than isolated hand-maintained HTML files. |
| Intellectual transparency | Distinguish Scripture, translation, quotation, observation, interpretation, inference, speculation, and application. |
| Accessibility | Support different abilities, cognitive needs, languages, experience levels, and user-selected presentation preferences. |
| Spiritual formation | Support reflection, prayer, remembrance, practice, and community, with consent and privacy. |
| Extensibility | Add tools, datasets, pedagogies, renderers, and study types without changing the core passage model. |
| Open ecosystem | Preserve remixability, portable exports, attribution, contribution history, and the ability to continue the project independently. |

## Independent dimensions of a study

| Dimension | Question | Illustrative values; not an exhaustive vocabulary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Subject and scope | What is studied? | Word, phrase, verse, passage, book, whole canon, theme, person, event, argument. |
| Modality | How is it encountered? | Visual, auditory, reading, writing, verbal, embodied, multisensory. |
| Method | What does the learner do? | Close read, annotate, compare, ask, research, discuss, retrieve, rehearse, create. |
| Framework | How is understanding organized? | Observation–interpretation–application, guided inquiry, argument structure, narrative, thematic organization. |
| Lens | What perspective is examined? | Linguistic, literary, historical, cultural, canonical, theological, Christological, ethical, reception history. |
| Cognitive operation | What thinking is exercised? | Observe, comprehend, analyze, reason, evaluate, synthesize. |
| Representation | What form expresses it? | Prose, table, outline, diagram, timeline, geographic map, argument map, knowledge graph, audio, performance. |
| Environment | Under what conditions? | Offline, print, mobile, home, classroom, congregation, field setting. |
| Participation | Who is involved? | Individual, pair, family, small group, facilitated class, public collaboration. |
| Objective | What outcome is sought? | Familiarity, comprehension, retention, interpretation, explanation, discernment, formation, practical action. |
| Application | What changes in practice? | Reflection, prayer, service, reconciliation, habit, teaching, authored response. |
| Evaluation | What provides feedback? | Recall, explanation, reconstruction, source review, discussion, evidence critique, voluntary reflection. |
| Integration | What does it connect to? | Other passages, prior knowledge, historical sources, concepts, practices, other studies. |

These axes overlap without being interchangeable. A lens is not a medium; a memory score is not a measure of spiritual maturity. Preferences must remain selectable rather than assigning people fixed “learning styles.” A theological perspective must be identified, not presented as a neutral property of the text.

## Composition and examples

A study recipe references its subject, corpus revision, one or more values from each relevant dimension, ordered activities, evidence, representations, accessibility alternatives, and intended evaluation. Dimensions can be omitted when not relevant; a recipe need not force every possible axis into every session. Prerequisites and incompatible combinations must be explained. Composing audio and a map does not automatically produce a useful learning activity.

Examples of future recipes, offered as requirements illustrations rather than recovered taxonomy entries:

- Study Romans through an historical lens, compare register renderings, build a visual argument map, discuss its premises in a group, and explain the reasoning with passage citations.
- Study a theme across passages, compare vocabulary and contexts, listen to selected text, practice progressive recall, and write a private reflection with a chosen next action.
- Open a personal declaration statement, inspect its cited passages and lexical evidence, compare interpretations and cautions, then revise the statement while preserving its supporting sources.
- Study a journey with an accessible geographic map and textual itinerary, distinguish attested locations from uncertain reconstructions, and reconstruct the route from memory.

New recipes should reuse capabilities demonstrated by the [three prototypes](PROTOTYPES.md), while making their content, tools, and presentation independently replaceable. The supplied taxonomy adds a six-axis generator with 82 selectable options and four depth choices, a configurable combination calculator, and a learning cycle from encounter through reflection. These are requirements evidence for composable authoring, not proof that every generated tuple is useful. Preserve the 24 objective-to-method pairings and 10 recommended approaches as discoverable starting points, with explicit provenance and room for alternatives.

## Boundaries and success

Success means that a contributor can add a study method or representation without editing every study; a reader can trace a claim to a source and a corpus revision; an author can export an intelligible study with its evidence and license notices; and a downstream contributor can adapt covered original corpus material under its reciprocal terms.

No prototype establishes that every taxonomy category is implemented, every quotation is cleared, every source claim is correct, or every interaction is accessible. The roadmap requires evidence at each stage. A feature's status must be one of planned, designed, implemented, verified, or deferred with a reason. Long-term completeness requires the full baseline, extensible coverage, and continued review rather than a single “supports everything” badge.
